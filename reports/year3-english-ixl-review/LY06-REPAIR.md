## Concrete coverage

64 original tasks: 48 Practice and 16 Test. All 56 legacy IDs retained and eight Practice IDs added. Sixteen applied MCQs assess meaningful edits, paragraph focus, relevant reasons/examples, tense, spelling, map/text agreement and helpful visual placement. Correct positions are balanced 3/3/3/3 Practice and 1/1/1/1 Test.

The other 48 tasks require actual products. Each of the three purposes receives twelve Practice and four independent Test briefs. Each asks for a plan, two short paragraphs, an actual learner-made visual, a focused useful edit, a final version and sharing with a partner, adult or class. Publishing means sharing a finished copy; no public internet posting, login or database is required. The specific edit can refine a correct feature rather than manufacture an error.

The imaginative briefs use different problems, choices, settings and resolutions. Informative briefs supply facts where factual knowledge is needed, or explicitly invite an invented model/collection; they do not assess inaccessible background knowledge. Persuasive briefs require a relevant opinion, reason, example and reader-facing request, while allowing different supported preferences. Every visual has a purpose: route, labelled parts, checklist, before/after scene, information box, display plan, diagram, speech bubble or response card. No blank visual placeholder is treated as a completed multimodal text.

Rubrics require the actual plan, draft, visual, identifiable edit and shared final copy. They check the prompt-specific content plus paragraph organisation, mostly consistent tense, suitable vocabulary, high-frequency and regular spelling, and sentence punctuation. The model fields offer possible approaches; they are not mandatory creative answers or full exemplar products. A typed completion statement alone does not establish the writing task was performed.
