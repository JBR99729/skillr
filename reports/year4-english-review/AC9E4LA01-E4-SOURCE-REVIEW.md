## Bounded factual observations and drafting implications

The guide describes community-recognised Elders and cautions that protocols differ. Section 2 reports that First Nations people often address Elders as Aunty or Uncle, but not in every place or situation; non-Indigenous people may need an invitation. Section 3 describes first meetings that establish connections through questions about place and belonging. Section 5 describes attention to Elders' comfort. These are attributed examples, not universal prescriptions. A source-based discussion may examine how respectful address and establishing connections develop relationships. Preserve variation, individual preferences and teacher-supported local guidance. Do not turn the account into a compulsory greeting script, identity disclosure, invented language, or a claim that consultation occurred.

## Scope and limits for independent approval

This account can close the earlier absence of any actually inspected cultural-greeting/relationship source if the author adds a bounded, attributed discussion and accurate model about what this account describes. It does not authorise copying cultural material, asserting that a local community uses these conventions, having children contact Elders, or equating an everyday greeting with Welcome to Country. Existing distinctions between conversation, recording and sharing permission must remain. A source link alone is insufficient: final teaching must actually explore the account and its limits.
