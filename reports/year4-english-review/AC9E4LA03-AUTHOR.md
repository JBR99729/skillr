## Exact curriculum boundary

Descriptor: **identify how texts across the curriculum have different language features and are typically organised into characteristic stages depending on purposes**.

Must teach: purpose, text structure/stage, language feature and form; actual original examples of all nine E1 text types; E2 sequential/causal explanations and comparative/part-whole information reports; E3 story/description/reflection poems; E4 form versus purpose/stages. Assess visible textual evidence and the relationship between organisation and reader purpose.

Prerequisites: reading short age-suitable paragraphs, recognising a sentence, familiar time words and basic differences between informing, entertaining and persuading. Support vocabulary within each text. Do not make advanced grammar labels, exact genre formulas or research knowledge necessary for answering.

May support: procedures, investigation reports, headings, a small text-structure table, oral rehearsal and brief composition that reveals a deliberate organisation choice. The descriptor's `identify` target does not require extended essays; composing is concise supporting performance evidence.

Must not overteach: universal stages for every poem/poster; every text in one subject uses one genre; all reports are investigation reports; one word/tense proves text type; formal literary analysis of hybrid genres. A factual recount/biography must be clearly distinguished from a fictional or imaginary sample; invented practice characters/events must not masquerade as historical sources.

## Proposed coverage and resource model

All items below are original drafting plans, not source observations. Root's IXL brief may refine the question design while ACARA remains the scope authority.

| Required strand | Static teaching/model plan | Assessment/performance evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Narrative | Short original orientation → problem → response/resolution sample; show action/dialogue words | Identify the problem or resolution from supplied text; explain why an event sits there; plan a short ending |
| Factual recount | Supplied classroom event notes and a chronological report, explicitly within an invented assessment scenario | Order actual event sentences; select time/language evidence without asserting all recounts use identical openings |
| Imaginative recount | Clearly imagined first-person account of an impossible journey | Compare its time-ordered recount structure with imagined content; identify viewpoint and meaningful stages |
| Biography | Clearly labelled invented practice profile, or supplied biographical fact card used only within the scenario | Relate life-event chronology and third-person reference to informing readers about a life; never claim a fictional person is historical |
| Information report | General classification and grouped facts; short comparative and part-whole examples | Identify grouping criteria and language; compare two objects on the same aspects; show whole-to-parts organisation |
| Explanation | One sequential process and one causal chain, using supplied facts | Distinguish what happens in sequence from why it happens; explain links using evidence, not connector recognition alone |
| Book talk | Original mini-book invented for the task; brief identification, spoiler-safe gist, response/recommendation with a reason | Choose a useful opening or evidence-based recommendation; rehearse/create a short talk rather than naming a rubric |
| Poetry | Original short story, descriptive and reflective poems | Explain sequence, image grouping or thought development and quote a clue; no universal stanza/rhyme requirement |
| Argument | Clear position, connected reasons/evidence and concluding recommendation using supplied facts | Identify claim/reason/evidence; explain the order's persuasive purpose; compose a short supported case |
| Form versus purpose | Two posters/emails/lists with different social purposes; same purpose in different forms | Name form separately from purpose; compare stages/content choices; redesign a short message without treating format as genre |

## Eight separate homework tasks — planned purposes

1. Read and annotate a new short narrative's stages, then explain the ending's role.
2. Compare a supplied factual-scenario recount with an imaginative recount, identifying shared sequence and different content.
3. Organise a clearly labelled practice life-profile fact card into a short biography with an explanation of ordering.
4. Build a comparative or part-whole information report from supplied facts and justify the grouping.
5. Read short sequential and causal explanations; explain how their organisation differs and write a short missing stage.
6. Prepare/rehearse a brief book talk from an original mini-story supplied in the worksheet, using a reason rather than unsupported praise.
7. Compare original descriptive and reflective poems, then create a few lines for one stated purpose and explain the arrangement.
8. Adapt an evidence-supported argument into a poster or email, naming form separately from purpose and preserving its main point.

These will be separate original tasks, not eight sampled Practice questions.
