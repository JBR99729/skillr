## AC9E4LA02 — subjective/objective wording

**Read:** NSW Department of Education, [Stage 2 reading — Fact and opinion](https://education.nsw.gov.au/teaching-and-learning/curriculum/literacy-and-numeracy/teaching-and-learning-resources/literacy/teaching-strategies/stage-2/reading/stage-2-fact-and-opinion), updated **3 July 2026**. Inspected learning intention, progression and the three public task outlines. These address vocabulary clues, making a poster/jingle explaining the distinction, checking the reliability of purported facts, and asking a peer panel questions that elicit factual/opinion statements. The full resource is **staff-only and was not opened**; no worksheet, complete lesson or assessment key from that resource is claimed reviewed.

**Original drafting implications:** retain one event/data set while changing evaluative wording; ask learners to identify the words contributing a judgement. Include an original report/reaction pair and a supported findings-to-conclusion task. Separate factual-reporting wording from independently verified truth. Reading an attributed opinion can establish whose view was expressed without establishing the opinion as fact. These implications combine the source's limited conceptual support with the exact E1/E2 requirements; the source alone does not fully teach thinking/summary verbs.

**Khan limitation:** [Objective summary: facts vs opinions](https://www.khanacademy.org/ela/5th-grade-english-foundations/xc1f7df3d387fd7d1:1st-quarter/xc1f7df3d387fd7d1:untitled-19/a/objective-summary-facts-vs-opinions-reading) was located and opened, but returned **zero readable body lines**. The progression includes using text conventions to build meaning and assessing features against purpose/audience. Full staff-only activities were not inspected; the outline does not supply all nine ACARA genres or all poem/report/explanation variants.

**Original drafting implications:** show actual short texts; relate the order and language of their parts to what the reader needs. Use evidence from multiple features rather than a single cue word. Preserve typical rather than universal stage patterns. Include distinct original sequential/causal explanations, comparative/part-whole reports and story/descriptive/reflective poems because ACARA requires these, not because the public NSW outline demonstrates all of them.

## AC9E4LA04 — cohesion and connectives

**Read:** NSW Department of Education, [Stage 2 reading — Connecting ideas in text](https://education.nsw.gov.au/teaching-and-learning/curriculum/literacy-and-numeracy/teaching-and-learning-resources/literacy/teaching-strategies/stage-2/reading/stage-2-connecting-ideas-in-text), updated **3 July 2026**. The public outline covers pronoun/noun reference, scanning then rereading/checking informative text, connections among related/opposite key words, and different relationships between images and language. Its progression explicitly includes connective tracking and topic word associations. The full staff-only activities and keys were not inspected.

**Original drafting implications:** provide a whole short paragraph so learners can trace a pronoun to a clear referent and explain a connective's relationship. Include an ambiguous-reference repair, coherent topic-word chain and same-situation alternatives distinguishing addition/result/contrast. Check temporal sequence against the actual events, and conditional meaning against a stated condition. Do not assess “if” versus “when” in a context where both interpretations are plausible. These are original applications, not copied NSW tasks. Conditional words and temporal links between sections remain explicit ACARA coverage even though the public outline is only partial support.

## AC9E4LA05 — navigation and readability

**Read:** Australian Government Style Manual, [Links](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/structuring-content/links), sections through the clear-destination worked comparisons and keyword-first links; [Headings](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/structuring-content/headings), sections on scanning, specific headings, hierarchy and related subheadings. These are professional writing/accessibility guidance, not a Year 4 classroom course. No curriculum-equivalence claim is made. The opened text teaches that links should support a user need, clear labels predict destinations, headings organise/scaffold scanning, and poorly chosen/excessive links can increase reading effort. No update date was independently retained from these opens; use the access date above.

**Read:** W3C WAI, [Fly-out Menus](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/menus/flyout/), overview and sections on indicating submenus, mouse users and keyboard users. It explains how drop-downs expose hierarchy and why users need other ways to reach destinations. Its example menu labels/hierarchy were present in extracted text; **no interactive menu was operated or visually usability-tested** by this researcher. Technical implementation is author background, not student lesson content.

**Original drafting implications:** create two supplied same-topic page models and give a concrete information-finding task. Let learners trace Home → category → subpage using visible labels, distinguish a heading from a link, improve a vague label and explain why a relevant graphic/caption helps. A drop-down groups choices but is not automatically easier for every reader. Model accessible alternate links. Use native static disclosure/anchors only within the already authorised architecture; inert sketches must be labelled as sketches, not represented as clicked navigation. More graphics/links and the longest heading are not automatically better.

**Boundary check:** NSW's [Stage 2 Text features](https://education.nsw.gov.au/teaching-and-learning/curriculum/literacy-and-numeracy/teaching-and-learning-resources/literacy/teaching-strategies/stage-2/reading/stage-2-text-features), updated 3 July 2026, was opened/read but chiefly addresses fonts, brackets and punctuation. It is **not sufficient evidence for digital home/subpage/drop-down coverage**. No suitably inspected Khan digital-navigation lesson is claimed.

## AC9E4LA06 — complex sentences and dependent clauses

**Read:** NSW Department of Education, [English 3–6 complex-sentence posters](https://education.nsw.gov.au/content/dam/main-education/documents/teaching-and-learning/curriculum/english/english-3-6-sentence-forms-a3-posters-04-complex-sentences.pdf), **all four pages' extracted text**. Pages 1–2 distinguish adverbial dependent clauses, main-clause meaning, reversed clause order and introductory commas; pages 3–4 address adjectival clauses modifying a noun/pronoun. The text includes the cat/dog before-and-after ordering model and a noun-description example. This is text review, not certification of poster visual layout; no source artwork is being reused.

**Source caution:** do not inherit a blanket comma rule from the poster's broad adjectival wording or teach *although* as a manner connective simply because of its table placement. Preserve accurate context-dependent punctuation and contrast/concession meaning. The source's simplification does not override ACARA or the examples' grammar.

**Read and cross-checked:** British Council LearnEnglish [Defining relative clauses](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/b1-b2/relative-clauses-defining-relative-clauses), explanatory sections through relative-pronoun omission; and [Non-defining relative clauses](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/b1-b2/relative-clauses-non-defining-relative-clauses), explanatory sections through place/time examples. These are B1–B2 EAL grammar lessons, not Year 4 equivalence. Actual explanation/examples were read; embedded exercises were not attempted. Defining clauses identify the referent; extra-information clauses are separated with commas. Both *who/that* can refer to people and *which/that* to things in appropriate defining examples; *that* is not used in non-defining examples. Therefore do not create single-answer items whose only distinction is two valid pronouns. Formal omission/non-defining taxonomy need not become a new Year 4 assessed gate.

**Original drafting implications:** use original clear finite-clause examples and preserve their intended meaning when combining/reordering. Include relative/adjectival description in substantial teaching and assessment, not only adverbial time/reason clauses. Have learners identify what the relative clause describes, then compose a complete sentence and explain the added meaning. Contrast a phrase, a fragment and a complete clause; length alone does not determine complexity. Adult-reviewed work should check completeness and meaning, accepting grammatically valid alternatives.

**Khan limitations:** opened [Dependent and independent clauses](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/grammar/syntax-sentences-and-clauses/phrases-and-clauses/v/dependent-and-independent-clauses-syntax-khan-academy), [Independent and dependent clauses article](https://www.khanacademy.org/ela/5th-and-6th-grade-grammar/x90cef5375e9bcad6:sentences-clauses-and-phrases-5/x90cef5375e9bcad6:phrases-and-clauses-5/a/independent-and-dependent-clauses-5?lang=en), [Noun, relative and adverbial clauses](https://www.khanacademy.org/ela/9th-grade-reading-and-vocabulary/xd45453bfd2ae8614:sentences-clauses-and-phrases-9-grammar/xd45453bfd2ae8614:clauses-9-grammar/a/noun-relative-and-adverbial-clauses-5), and [Subordinating conjunctions](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/class-6-english-grammar/x374ee5ebbe9de1c4:friendship/x374ee5ebbe9de1c4:the-unlikely-best-friends/v/subordinating-conjunctions).
