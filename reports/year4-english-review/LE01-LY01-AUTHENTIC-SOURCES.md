## Evidence and reuse rules

“Read” below means the named text actually opened and inspected. “Viewed” means an actual image was visually inspected, not merely its alt text. Video transcripts, curator descriptions and metadata are distinguished from actual audiovisual viewing. Root must supply actual video observations. Public accessibility does not authorise republication. Link to the providers for class reading/viewing; do not rehost copyrighted poems, book interiors, videos or transcripts. Production should use original questions and brief attributed references. Do not copy publisher worksheet wording or imitate culturally specific art. Named texts portray particular voices and contexts, not all First Nations Peoples. Whole-book reading must not be claimed from previews or notes.

## AC9E4LE04: fully inspected First Nations poetry

### Kirli Saunders, “Wildflower” (19 June 2017)

- Actual complete poem read: https://redroompoetry.org/poets/kirli-saunders/wildflower/
- Author identity inspected at https://redroompoetry.org/poet/kirli-saunders/: Gunai woman. Red Room Poetry is the publishing/project source, not an unauthorised repost.
- Observed specific evidence: the speaker offers flowers as encouragement; the contrast between their valued status and their humble growing place links natural growth with difficulty. The exact words **“struggles”** and **“precious”** are useful contrasting emotional vocabulary. The poem addresses another person. Embedded audio was not played.
- Original teaching implications: Have students read the source. Ask which word communicates value; ask how the flowers' growing place supports the encouraging message; accept hopeful, caring or reassuring readings with textual evidence. Do not insist all readers have the same emotion. Explain that a poem can carry feeling without rhyme. This is a genuine named First Nations poem for LE04's emotive-language component.
- Reuse boundary: the poem is very short. Link to it and discuss two words; do not reproduce most or all of it in a question bank or worksheet.

### Kirli Saunders, “Mother” (19 June 2017)

- Actual complete poem read: https://redroompoetry.org/poets/kirli-saunders/mother/
- Observed evidence: the speaker addresses land as Mother, notices tidal water and earth, links the present with earlier people and ends with belonging. Exact short references: **“sand dance”**, **“like tea”**, **“where I belong”**.
- Original teaching implications: identify one personifying choice and its effect; contrast the sense of rooted belonging with Wildflower's encouragement. Scaffold unfamiliar vocabulary such as tannins and tessellate. Do not call the poem simply about a human mother, or invent community-specific symbolism.
- Author context inspected separately: https://centreforstories.com/stories/between-the-lines/kirli-saunders/ contains the author's interview explaining the poem's connection with Dharawal Country and colonial history. Keep that historical context teacher mediated; a nature-appreciation reading alone should not erase it.
- Reuse boundary: source-linked reading, short references and original commentary; not a full copied poem.

## AC9E4LE01: concrete comparison options

### First Nations works

Compare Saunders' **Mother** with Morgan/Malibirr's **Little Bird's Day** after reading/viewing the specified works. Supported shared idea: closeness to the natural world. Meaningful difference: Mother links a human speaker's identity and ancestral belonging with Country; Little Bird's Day follows a bird's daily encounters with natural elements. Do not reduce Country to generic scenery. A source-linked task should require one actual detail from each work. Alternatively, **Mother + Wildflower** are two complete, actually read First Nations poems by the same author: both connect nature with human feeling, but belonging and encouragement differ. Add the second author's book for breadth; do not count two same-author poems as wide-ranging authors.

### Australian author: Mem Fox, *Time for Bed* (1993)

- Author's own page actually read: https://memfox.au/time-for-bed
- The page supplies the opening couplet addressing a little mouse as darkness falls, plus the author's account of revising the repeating structure. Jane Dyer is illustrator. The author identifies a bedtime purpose. Full author audio is available but was not played; book not read in full.
- Supported comparison with Little Bird's Day: both connect rest/activity with the daily cycle; supplied Fox opening is evening/rest while viewed Little Bird preview shows sunrise/resting creatures. A meaningful difference concerns time and action, not one culture being more natural or loving. Teacher should read the named books before comparing full story patterns.
- Useful LE05 authoring implication: the author reports removing an inconsistent fish detail and making repeated phrases orderly. Can discuss revision without copying the draft or imposing literal realism on all fantasy.

### World author: Louisa May Alcott, *Little Women*, chapter2 “A Merry Christmas”

- Actual primary literary text read, breakfast-giving section and surrounding context: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/514/pg514-images.html#chap02
- Observed events: the March sisters agree to give their Christmas breakfast to a neighbouring family; they help warm and feed the children, then accept simple food themselves. Their generosity and family cooperation are evidenced by actions, not an abstract moral label.
- Pair with *Bindi*'s named family-meal poem below after actual class reading: food and family care connect both, but Alcott's holiday episode involves sharing with neighbours while Bindi's poem shows family closeness during dinner. Do not claim that a historical fictional household represents all nineteenth-century American families.
- Scope: teacher-selected short passage, glossing older words. Full chapter includes a later theatrical plot unsuitable as accidental required context; use the breakfast passage. Link original public-domain text; preserve attribution if making an adaptation.

### Kirli Saunders, *Bindi* (2020), illustrated Dub Leffler

- Publisher page read: https://www.magabala.com/products/bindi
- Official notes read: https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0092/5043/9268/files/Bindi_Magabala_Books_Teacher_Notes.pdf

## AC9E4LY01: real dated family communication

### Samuel Valentine to his brother, 1860

- Official NLA exhibition and caption read: https://www.library.gov.au/whats-on/exhibitions/hopes-and-fears-australian-migration-stories/hopes-and-fears-exhibition-themes
- Actual manuscript detail visually inspected: https://www.library.gov.au/sites/default/files/styles/media_carousel/public/images/2024-11/collection-correspondence-samuel-valentine-gold-1860-nla-cat-vn125634-carousel.jpg?h=e9fe5cfc&itok=9js4538P
- Clearly visible greeting **“My dear Brother”**, dated manuscript in the upper right, handwriting, printed city panorama, folded/worn paper. Legible opening reassures the brother that the writer is alive. Do not use a conjectural full transcription for assessment. The printed panorama's **1854** is the stationery date; NLA dates this letter **1860**, not1854.
- Original task: identify the family audience from the greeting and compare affectionate/reassuring purpose, what is depicted, and medium with the current example below. The picture supports a place description; do not infer a postal delivery time from the paper alone.

### Lucy and “Papa Dan”, reported by Jessica van Vonderen, ABC, 3 May2021

- Article actually read: https://www.abc.net.au/news/2021-05-03/qld-coronavirus-families-international-travel/100110838
- The opening reports one-year-old Lucy greeting her grandfather in a video call, kissing the screen and saying **“Mwah!”**. It names FaceTime and describes overseas family separation. Caption identifies Dana holding a phone with Lucy on her lap. The photograph itself was not visually inspected in this subtask.
- Evidence supports sound/gesture in the reported greeting and family affection despite separation. This is a published news report of communication, not a primary recording of the whole call. Do not falsely present the article as a direct family transcript, nor generalise that digital messages are always brief/private.
- Use as an attributed optional real-life comparison alongside the authored fictional dated letter/message pair, which remains the self-contained core exercise. Do not require pupils' private messages or personal family disclosures.
