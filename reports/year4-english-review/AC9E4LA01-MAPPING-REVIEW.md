## Cultural responsibility and inclusion review checks

Independent source checks support the safeguards already in the root's source brief:

- NSW Department of Education, updated 22 July 2026, opened 8 September: https://education.nsw.gov.au/teaching-and-learning/curriculum/english/planning-programming-and-assessing-english-k-6/oral-language-and-communication
- NESA curriculum overview, protocols section, opened 8 September: https://curriculum.nsw.edu.au/learning-areas/english/english-k-10-2022/overview

The NSW sources recommend working with local communities/appropriate knowledge holders or endorsed resources, and recognise varying protocols and non-verbal conventions. Accordingly the final LA01 review will require:

1. First Nations Elders/greeting learning is explicit, not erased because it is sensitive; the teacher follows local community guidance and the person's preferred form of address.
2. No invented Indigenous-language greeting, universal Aunty/Uncle rule, or claim that one example represents every Country/Place or community.
3. No grading respect by mandatory eye contact, touch, loudness, immediate response or a single body-language norm. Evaluate the language choice, meaning and respectful relationship-building evidence actually required.
4. No child asked to represent all members of a culture, disclose private family identity, or role-play being a cultural authority. General greeting rehearsal must be clearly distinct from a simulated cultural ceremony or culturally restricted practice.
5. Inclusive language invites participation without assumptions about gender, family structure, culture, disability or preferred communication mode. Offer accessible speaking/communication alternatives without turning a speaking outcome into a self-tick.
6. Formality is contextual and graded, not a synonym for politeness, moral worth, longer words or an absolute ban on contractions. Informal language can be respectful and effective.
7. Age, expertise, familiarity and role are distinguished. A younger person may have relevant expertise; a familiar person can participate in a formal event. Do not teach automatic deference solely from age or status.
