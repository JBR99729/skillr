## Exact scope and preservation decisions

The target is identifying subjective opinion/feeling language and objective factual-reporting language. E1 explicitly requires thinking verbs such as **I think / I believe** and summary verbs such as **we concluded**. E2 requires controlled comparisons of similar information expressed subjectively/objectively.

Preserve the current playground contrast, useful opinion-with-reason model, concrete bean-growth/temperature/race examples and source-versus-reaction distinction identified by the audit. Give each a clear limited teaching purpose, not eight paraphrased assessment repetitions. Preserve the optional-video block and its authentic report/opinion follow-up, metadata, native-details shell, related routes and resource navigation.

Additional same-code preservation source inspected: `assets/year4-english-data-la1.js`, LA02 object. Its useful content includes comparison tables, verifiable versus evaluative wording, attribution, the reminder that reviews may contain facts, and the misconceptions that every adjective is subjective or that quoting an opinion makes it factual. Incorporate useful material directly in static HTML; do not reactivate a legacy lesson renderer. Tighten comparison models so changed wording does not silently replace the original factual information.

Keep this Year 4 boundary: short original sentences/passages, specific word evidence and simple supported conclusions. Avoid turning this into an advanced bias/media-framing analysis unit or requiring extended essays. Objective **wording** does not prove a claim true. A reporting verb does not establish supporting evidence; a first-person pronoun does not automatically mean opinion. A reviewer may correctly include both checkable facts and personal evaluation, so every question must state the requested distinction.

## Proposed resource coverage

| Coverage strand | Teaching/model requirement | Assessment evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opinion and feeling language | Clearly identify evaluative/emotional wording in a supplied sentence, including mixed factual/evaluative sentences | Quote/select the exact words and explain their contribution, not only label a whole topic as opinion |
| Objective factual-reporting language | Count/measure/event information with a stated possible checking source; distinguish a checkable claim from a verified one | Choose wording for an explicitly factual-reporting purpose; identify what evidence would be needed |
| E1 thinking verbs | I think / I believe signal a viewpoint in actual original opinion examples; read the whole claim | Identify and compare how wording presents a judgement; avoid the shortcut “every I is opinion” |
| E1 summary/reporting verbs | We concluded linked to supplied findings, plus a contrasting unsupported conclusion | Select the conclusion supported by the actual supplied evidence and explain the limit |
| E2 controlled comparisons | Preserve people/events/counts/times while adding or removing evaluation | Compare, revise and annotate changed words without inventing facts |
| Attribution and mixed passages | State whose opinion is being reported while keeping the opinion distinct from verified fact | Read a short report/reaction passage; identify reporting, quoted viewpoint and source-check boundaries |

Proposed bank mix: **40 selected-response +8 adult-reviewed Practice =48**, and **12 selected-response +4 adult-reviewed Test =16**. Selected responses will have four plausible distinct options and balanced keys (10 per Practice position, 3 per Test position). Difficulty will grow through close wording/evidence distinctions and transfer, not teacher-rubric jargon. Adult tasks will require actual short reading annotations, controlled revisions or paired reporting/opinion responses with specific models and acceptance criteria. This is a proposed design pending the source brief, not a fixed requirement to force inappropriate task forms.

Eight separate homework tasks will use new original data/passages rather than bank copies: language annotation; same-information comparison; report/reaction pair; thinking-verb explanation; findings-to-conclusion work; attribution repair; a short mixed passage; and an independently edited report with a separate opinion. Each needs response space, an illustrative model and adult checking criteria.
