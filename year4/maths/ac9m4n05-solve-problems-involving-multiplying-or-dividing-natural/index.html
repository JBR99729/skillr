AC9M4N05: Multiply and divide by powers of 10

Solve problems involving multiplying or dividing natural numbers by multiples and powers of 10 using place-value relationships.

Topic guideTeacher SlidesWorksheetPracticeTest
What students learn in AC9M4N05

Students use place-value structure to explain and calculate multiplication and division by 10, 100 and 1000, then apply those patterns to solve problems without a calculator.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Content description: solve problems involving multiplying or dividing natural numbers by multiples and powers of 10 without a calculator, using the multiplicative relationship between the place value of digits.

  • E1: use physical or virtual materials to demonstrate multiplicative relationships between places.
  • E2: use place-value charts, numeral expanders or sliders to explain why multiplying by 10 shifts digits one place left and dividing by 10 shifts them one place right.
  • E3: use calculators or digital tools to identify patterns for ×/÷ 10, 100 and 1000, record patterns and generalise them for mental calculation.

Common mistakes to watch for
  • Saying “add a zero” instead of reasoning with place value.
  • Moving the decimal point rather than understanding digit value.
  • Applying a power-of-10 pattern to an unrelated multiplier.
