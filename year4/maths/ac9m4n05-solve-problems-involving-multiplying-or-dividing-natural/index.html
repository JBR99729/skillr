AC9M4N05: Multiply and divide by powers of 10

Solve problems involving multiplying or dividing natural numbers by multiples and powers of 10 using place-value relationships.

Topic guideClassroom ViewWorksheetPracticeTest
What students learn in AC9M4N05

Multiplying by 10, 100 or 1 000 makes each digit worth 10, 100 or 1 000 times as much. Dividing reverses this relationship when the quotient is a natural number.

Learning routine: Identify factor or divisor → Track place-value shift → Calculate → Interpret units → Check inverse

Success looks like

  • Multiply by powers of 10
  • Divide by powers of 10
  • Factor multiples of 10
  • Use inverse checks
  • Explain digit-value changes
Concept models and worked thinking

Track the value of digits through ×10 and ×100

thousandshundredstensones373703700
37 × 10 = 370→37 × 100 = 3 700

The digits shift to places with greater value; zeros fill empty positions. This is more reliable than memorising 'add zeros'.

  1. Identify the labels, quantities or evidence shown.
  2. Explain the relationship represented by the model.
  3. Check that the conclusion answers the original question.

Use multiples of 10 by separating factors

24 × 30→24 × 3 × 10→72 × 10→720

Rewrite 30 as 3 × 10. For division, use inverse factor relationships such as 4 800 ÷ 60 = 480 ÷ 6 = 80.

Transfer the relationship to the new situation and justify the result using precise vocabulary and evidence.

Guided learning activities

1. Digit-value tracker

Use place-value cards to show how the value of each digit changes in 406 × 10 and 406 × 100.

10001001014064060
406 → 4 060

2. Factor the multiple

Solve ×20, ×40 and ×300 problems by separating the single-digit factor and power of 10.

18 × 2018 × 2 × 10
35 × 4035 × 4 × 10
12 × 30012 × 3 × 100

3. Inverse-chain check

Check multiplication and division problems using related inverse facts.

28 × 50 = 1 400→1 400 ÷ 50 = 28→1 400 ÷ 28 = 50
Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Content description: solve problems involving multiplying or dividing natural numbers by multiples and powers of 10 without a calculator, using the multiplicative relationship between the place value of digits.

  • E1: use physical or virtual materials to demonstrate multiplicative relationships between places.
  • E2: use place-value charts, numeral expanders or sliders to explain why multiplying by 10 shifts digits one place left and dividing by 10 shifts them one place right.
  • E3: use calculators or digital tools to identify patterns for ×/÷ 10, 100 and 1000, record patterns and generalise them for mental calculation.
Quick check and feedback
  1. There are 6 bags with 24 counters in each. How many counters? 144 counters, because 6 x 24 = 6 x 20 + 6 x 4.
  2. Share 96 cards equally among 4 players. 24 cards each, because 96 divided by 4 = 24.
  3. How can you check a division answer? Multiply the quotient by the divisor and add any remainder.

Mastery evidence: Students choose multiplication or division from the story, use place-value partitioning or known facts, and check that the answer matches the context.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Add zeros rule used blindly — Place-value reasoning explains when and why placeholder zeros appear.
  • 30 treated as 3 — Remember the extra factor of 10.
  • Division shift in wrong direction — Division by 10 makes each digit worth one tenth as much.
  • Units ignored — Interpret whether the answer counts items, cost, length or groups.
