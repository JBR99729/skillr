What students learn in AC9M4N06

Efficiency means choosing a method that is accurate, understandable and well suited to the numbers and context. Mental strategies, written methods and digital tools each have appropriate uses.

Learning routine: Estimate → Analyse numbers → Choose strategy/tool → Solve → Check → Compare efficiency

Success looks like

  • Choose efficient strategies
  • Use all four operations
  • Use digital tools appropriately
  • Check with inverse/estimate
  • Compare methods

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Content description: develop efficient strategies and use appropriate digital tools for solving problems involving addition and subtraction, and multiplication and division where there is no remainder.

  • E1: choose efficient addition and subtraction strategies such as place-value partitioning, inverse relationships, compatible numbers, bridging, splitting and algorithms.
  • E2: use doubling, halving and associative strategies for multiplication.
  • E3: use arrays to connect groups, rows, columns and multiplication number sentences.
  • E4: solve multiplication or division problems with materials or diagrams and explain each number in the number sentence.
  • E5: represent multiplicative situations with materials, arrays or bar models and identify the missing quantity.
  • E6: use place-value partitioning, basic facts and region models to solve multiplication problems.
  • E7: use materials or diagrams to develop and explain division strategies, including inverse relationships.
Quick check and feedback
  1. Estimate 398 + 206 before calculating. About 600, so an exact answer near 604 is reasonable.
  2. Which strategy suits 25 x 16? Use factors: 25 x 4 x 4 = 100 x 4 = 400.
  3. When is a digital tool appropriate? When the calculation is long or data-heavy, after students can explain the operation and estimate the size of the answer.

Mastery evidence: Students select an efficient strategy, explain why it fits the numbers, and use estimation to detect unreasonable answers.

Avoid this trap
  • One method used for every problem — Analyse the numbers before choosing a strategy.
  • Calculator answer accepted without checking — Estimate and interpret the result.
  • Partial products omitted — Record every partitioned part before recombining.
  • Efficiency confused with speed only — A method should also be reliable and communicable.
