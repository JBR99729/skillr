AC9M4M02: Perimeter and area of shapes and enclosed spaces

Recognise ways of measuring and approximating perimeter and area using appropriate formal and informal units.

Topic guideClassroom ViewWorksheetPracticeTest
What students learn in AC9M4M02

Perimeter measures distance around a boundary in linear units. Area measures surface coverage in square units. Shapes can share an area but have different perimeters, or share a perimeter but have different areas.

Learning routine: Identify boundary or surface → Choose unit → Measure/count → Calculate or approximate → Label units → Compare

Success looks like

  • Distinguish perimeter/area
  • Calculate rectangles
  • Use formal/informal units
  • Approximate irregular regions
  • Compare shapes and justify
Concept models and worked thinking

Compare perimeter and area on a grid

perimeter 20 unitsarea 24 square units

A 4 by 6 rectangle has area 24 square units and perimeter 4 + 6 + 4 + 6 = 20 units. Square units and linear units communicate different attributes.

Guided learning activities

1. Same area, new perimeter

Build several rectangles with 24 square tiles and compare their perimeters.

2. String and tiles

Measure an object’s boundary with string and cover its surface with square units, then describe the difference.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Content description: recognise ways of measuring and approximating the perimeter and area of shapes and enclosed spaces, using appropriate formal and informal units.

  • E1: recognise perimeter as the sum of boundary lengths; choose suitable units and use string or rope for irregular or curved boundaries.
  • E2: create rectangles on grid paper and compare efficient methods for finding boundary length.
  • E3: recognise area as enclosed space; measure and compare area with tiles or squares, including part units.
  • E4: repeatedly use one unit to measure area, avoiding gaps and overlaps and explaining use of partial units.
  • E5: investigate how First Nations Ranger Groups and others measure land areas when making decisions about caring for Country/Place (teaching context).

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Perimeter and area formulas confused — Ask whether the problem concerns around or covering.
  • Area unit written as cm — Area uses square units such as cm².
  • Internal grid lines counted in perimeter — Only the outer boundary contributes.
  • Approximation presented as exact — Use approximately and describe the method or range.
