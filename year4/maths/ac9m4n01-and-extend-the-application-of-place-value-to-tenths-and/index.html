Learn the skill

Six learning focuses

Learning goal: The decimal point separates whole-number places from fractional places. One tenth is one of 10 equal parts of a whole, one hundredth is one of 100 equal parts, and 10 hundredths equal 1 tenth.

1. One whole, tenths and hundredths

Keep one whole the same size. Split it into ten equal parts: each is one tenth. Split each tenth into ten equal pieces: each is one hundredth. Ten hundredths cover exactly one tenth.

Build the whole, then split a tenth

Exchange rule: 1 whole = 10 tenths = 100 hundredths; 1 tenth = 10 hundredths. One bundle of 10 equal straws can stand for a whole. Each straw is a tenth; splitting one straw into ten equal pieces makes hundredths.

Quick check: 14 tenths = 1.4 because ten of the tenths make one whole.

E1: divide a whole into 10 equal parts and connect tenths to decimal notation such as 0.1 and 0.2.

E2: use materials to show the multiplicative relationship between wholes, tenths and hundredths.

Try it: How many hundredths make three tenths?

Check the answer

30 hundredths. Each of the three tenths contains ten hundredths.

2. Read and write decimal places

In 2.08, the 2 means two ones, the 0 means no extra tenths, and the 8 means eight hundredths. The zero holds the tenths place; 2.8 would mean two ones and eight tenths.

Represent 3.47 using a place-value chart

tenths4
hundredths7
.

3.47 = 3 ones + 4 tenths + 7 hundredths

Read 3.47 as three and forty-seven hundredths, or three ones, four tenths and seven hundredths. The 4 is worth 0.4, not 4.

  1. Put 3 in the ones column, 4 in the tenths column and 7 in the hundredths column.
  2. Read the amount as three point four seven, or three and forty-seven hundredths.
  3. Check the values: 3 + 0.4 + 0.07 = 3.47. The digit 4 is worth four tenths.

Try it: Write four ones and six hundredths as a decimal.

Check the answer

4.06. The empty tenths place needs a zero.

3. Show decimals in different ways

For 0.35, shade three full tenths and five more hundredths of a fixed whole. On a line from 0.3 to 0.4 split into ten equal intervals, each interval is 0.01 and 0.35 is halfway.

Try it: A line goes from 0.6 to 0.7 in ten equal intervals. What is each interval worth?

Check the answer

0.01, because the gap of 0.1 has been divided into ten equal parts.

4. Rename without changing the amount

0.4 and 0.40 name the same amount: four tenths are forty hundredths. A final zero can record the smaller unit without adding any value. This differs from putting a zero between the point and a nonzero digit.

Adding a zero at the end of the decimal part does not change the value: 0.6 = 0.60. Use place value rather than digit count to compare decimals.

Six tenths can be split into sixty hundredths, so 0.6 = 0.60. A zero inside a numeral can matter: 0.06 is six hundredths, one tenth of 0.6.

E3: recognise one as ten tenths and one tenth as ten hundredths and use these relationships to rename decimals.

Try it: Are 0.7 and 0.07 equal? Explain with named units.

Check the answer

No. Seven tenths are seventy hundredths, which is more than seven hundredths.

5. Read decimal measurements

Always name the unit. A length of 1.25 m is one whole metre and twenty-five hundredths of a metre. The decimal part describes part of that metre; it is not an unrelated count.

Read decimal measures

One metre is 100 centimetres, so 7 cm is 0.07 m. A length of 2 m and 35 cm is 2.35 m, read “two point three five metres”. Keep the zero when reading 2.05 m: “two point zero five metres”.

E4: model decimal measures such as 1.5 m and 1.75 units.

Try it: Explain 0.09 m in hundredths of a metre.

Check the answer

Nine hundredths of a metre. It is less than one tenth of a metre.

6. Connect dollars and cents

One dollar is the whole and one cent is one hundredth of a dollar. $3.05 means three dollars and five cents; $3.50 means three dollars and fifty cents. Coin size does not tell you its value.

Count money and connect the place values

Count each denomination before combining it. The three 20c coins make 60c; adding 5c makes 65 hundredths of the next dollar.

Compare the readings: $2.75 is “two dollars and seventy-five cents”; 2.75 m is “two point seven five metres”. In both, 7 means seven tenths and 5 means five hundredths of the named unit. Money uses cents; a measurement keeps its measurement unit.

E5: count mixed notes and coins and write totals using dollars and cents.

E6: compare how decimal money amounts and decimal measurements are read and interpreted.

Try it: Write two dollars and seven cents in dollars.

Check the answer

$2.07. Seven cents is seven hundredths of a dollar.

Guided learning activities

1. Decimal grid build

Shade 36 hundredths on a 10 × 10 grid, then write it as a fraction, decimal and place-value statement.

36/100 = 0.36

2. Place-value rename

Use counters or cards to exchange 10 hundredths for 1 tenth and explain why 0.30 and 0.3 are equal.

30 hundredths→exchange 10 hundredths × 3→3 tenths→0.30 = 0.3

3. Decimal comparison

Use place value to compare 0.8, 0.08, 0.80 and 0.18. From least to greatest: 0.08, 0.18, then 0.8 = 0.80. Eight tenths is eighty hundredths; it is not smaller just because it has fewer decimal digits.

4. Count and say the total

Use play money: two $20 notes, one $10 note, three $2 coins, one 50c coin and one 5c coin. Count the total and write it in dollars and cents.

Expected answer: $56.55. The notes make $50, the dollar coins make $6 and the cent coins make 55c. Say “fifty-six dollars and fifty-five cents”. Then read 56.55 m as “fifty-six point five five metres”. The digit values agree but the units differ.

Common misconceptions

  • Longer decimal is larger — 0.75 is not automatically larger than 0.8; compare tenths first by writing 0.8 as 0.80.
  • Decimal point read as punctuation only — The point separates whole-number and fractional place values.
  • Tenths and hundredths reversed — Tenths are larger parts than hundredths: 1 tenth = 10 hundredths.
  • Trailing zero changes value — 0.6 and 0.60 name the same quantity.
