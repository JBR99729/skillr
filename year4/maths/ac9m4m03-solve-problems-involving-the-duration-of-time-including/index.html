AC9M4M03: Duration, am/pm and time conversions

Solve problems involving duration, situations using am and pm, and conversions between units of time.

Topic guideClassroom ViewWorksheetPracticeTest
What students learn in AC9M4M03

Duration measures elapsed time, not the difference between clock digits. Breaking a journey across whole hours, noon or midnight reduces errors, and units must be converted consistently.

Learning routine: Identify start/end → Mark am or pm → Bridge to friendly time → Add intervals → Convert units → Check context

Success looks like

  • Use am/pm correctly
  • Calculate elapsed time
  • Cross noon/midnight
  • Convert time units
  • Interpret schedules

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Content description: solve problems involving the duration of time including situations involving “am” and “pm” and conversions between units of time.

  • E1: calculate time between events such as movies, journeys or flights, including am/pm starting and finishing times.
  • E2: use relationships such as 60 minutes in an hour and 60 seconds in a minute to solve timetable and routine problems.
  • E3: explore First Nations Australians’ explanations of the passing of time through cyclic phenomena involving the sun, moon and stars (teaching context).

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Subtracting clock digits directly — Use elapsed intervals; 1:10 − 9:35 is not ordinary place-value subtraction.
  • 12 am and 12 pm reversed — 12:00 pm is noon and 12:00 am is midnight.
  • Minutes treated as hundredths of an hour — An hour has 60 minutes.
  • am/pm omitted — Without it, a duration may be ambiguous.
