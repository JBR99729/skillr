AC9M4N04: Count by fractions and mixed numerals

Count by fractions including mixed numerals; locate and represent these fractions as numbers on number lines.

Topic guideClassroom ViewWorksheetPracticeTest
What students learn in AC9M4N04

Fractions are numbers that can be counted and located on a continuous number line. An improper fraction and a mixed numeral can represent the same point, such as 7/4 = 1 3/4.

Learning routine: Choose step size → Partition each whole equally → Count beyond one → Rename → Locate and compare

Success looks like

  • Count by fractions
  • Cross whole numbers
  • Use mixed numerals
  • Rename improper fractions
  • Locate and compare on number lines

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Content description: count by fractions including mixed numerals; locate and represent these fractions as numbers on number lines.

  • E1: count by quarters beyond one whole and connect names such as five-quarters with one-and-one-quarter.
  • E2: subdivide parallel number lines into halves, quarters and thirds and notice equivalent positions.
  • E3: convert mixed numerals to improper fractions and vice versa and represent them on number lines.
  • E4: count in tenths and recognise 10 tenths as one whole.
Quick check and feedback
  1. Count by quarters from 3/4 to 7/4. 3/4, 4/4, 5/4, 6/4, 7/4.
  2. Rename 7/4 as a mixed numeral. 1 3/4, because 4/4 makes one whole and 3/4 remains.
  3. Where is 5/2 on a number line? At 2 1/2, halfway between 2 and 3.

Mastery evidence: Students keep equal spacing on the number line and can rename between improper fractions and mixed numerals without changing the value.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Denominator changes after one whole — The denominator keeps naming the same-sized parts.
  • Mixed numeral treated as multiplication — 1 3/4 means one whole and three quarters, not 1 × 3/4.
  • Unequal number-line intervals — Equal fraction steps require equal distances.
  • Improper fractions cannot be numbers — They are valid numbers greater than or equal to one.
