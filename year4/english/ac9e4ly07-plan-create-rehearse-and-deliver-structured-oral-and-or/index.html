What students learn

1. Outcome Overview & Core Concepts

Students learn to plan, create, rehearse and deliver structured oral and/or multimodal presentations to report on a topic, tell a story, recount events or present an argument using subjective and objective language, complex sentences, visual features, tone, pace, pitch and volume. They identify the relevant language or text feature, point to evidence and explain how it contributes to meaning or purpose. Key concepts include audience, context, language, vocabulary, sentence, visual.

Learning target: plan, create, rehearse and deliver structured oral and/or multimodal presentations to report on a topic, tell a story, recount events or present an argument using subjective and objective language, complex sentences, visual features, tone, pace, pitch and volume

2. Subject-Specific Content & Key Examples

2. Subject-Specific Content & Key Examples

Build the sentence

Start with “Birds fly.” Add precise information: “Small shorebirds fly quickly across the inlet.” Identify what each added word or group contributes.

Clause relationship

Compare “We stayed inside because it rained” with “It rained, so we stayed inside.” Both connect ideas, but the clause structure foregrounds the relationship differently.

Grammar changes meaning

Compare “The dog chased the ball” with “The ball was chased by the dog.” The event is similar, but the sentence foregrounds different information.

Transfer

Students change one grammatical feature in a new sentence, then explain what changed in meaning, emphasis, time or detail.

3. Common Student Misconceptions

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • Feature spotting: Naming a camera angle, colour or sound is not analysis; explain its effect.
  • One fixed effect: Interpret the feature in context and alongside other modes.
  • Separate-mode lists: Explain how image, words and sound reinforce, contradict or qualify one another.
  • Retelling: Analyse how the representation was constructed, not only what it shows.
4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence

4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence

Step 1: Explicit Teaching & Modelling

Define the outcome-specific metalanguage (audience, context, language) and model how to identify the feature, select evidence and explain or use it.

Step 2: Guided Analysis

Work through a second example together: What is the choice or feature? Where is the evidence? What does it do here?

Step 3: Collaborative / Independent Practice

Students annotate, compare, read, edit, speak or compose with a fresh example, then apply the concept independently.

Step 4: Formative Assessment

Use an unseen micro-text, oral task or creation task. Require precise evidence plus a justified explanation or deliberate crafting choice.

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: reporting on a topic in an organised manner, providing relevant facts and descriptive detail to support audience understanding, and using references to reliable sources to support claims
  • E2: exploring the effects of changing tone, volume, pitch and pace in formal and informal contexts
  • E3: choosing a variety of appropriate words and phrases, including descriptive words and some technical vocabulary, to communicate meaning accurately
  • E4: rehearsing a presentation with a peer and sharing feedback about tone, pace, pitch and volume appropriate to audience
Practice Thought & Formative Assessment

Practice Thought & Formative Assessment

Use a fresh example that has not been modelled. Ask the student to show the exact clue, feature or language choice and explain or use it independently.
