Year 4 English · AC9E4LY04

Accurate, fluent and self-correcting reading

Use print, word parts, meaning, grammar and text structure together.

1. Curriculum target

Read, monitor and repair

Students read increasingly complex texts using established word-identification strategies, topic knowledge, text structure and language features. They reread and self-correct when necessary.

Success criteria: I notice when a reading does not fit; I can explain which clues I checked; I correct the word; I reread smoothly.

2. Retrieval warm-up

Prefixes

re- = again · pre- = before · mis- = incorrectly

Suffixes

-ful full of · -less without · -ly in a way · -ness state of being · -able able to be · -ment action/result

Context

Definition · example · contrast · consequence · restatement

3. The four checks

Print + sound

Look through the whole word. Do not guess from the first letter.

Word parts

Split base word, prefix and suffix. Recombine their meanings.

Meaning

Use topic knowledge and nearby clues. Ask, “Does this make sense here?”

Grammar + structure

Check the sentence pattern, punctuation and organisation of the text.

4. Teacher think-aloud: self-correction

Text: “The geologist inspected the layers of rock.”

First attempt: “The geologist insected…”

“That does not make sense. I look through the whole word: in-s-p-e-c-t-e-d. I can see inspect. A geologist can inspect rock. I correct to inspected, then reread the whole sentence smoothly.”

Routine: Notice → Check → Correct → Reread.

5. Teacher think-aloud: context clues

Text: “The creek was turbid. Mud and fine soil floated through the water, so the stones below could not be seen.”

“I do not know turbid. I keep reading. Mud and soil make water hard to see through. So turbid probably means cloudy or muddy. I can point to the exact evidence that supports my inference.”

6. Word-part strategy

Model: rediscover → re- + discover → discover again.

Model: breathless → breath + -less → without breath / short of breath.

Model: silently → silent + -ly → in a silent way.

Important: morphology makes a prediction. Context confirms or rejects it.

7. Fluency

Accuracy

Read every printed word.

Pace

Not too fast to understand; not word-by-word.

Phrasing

Group words into meaningful chunks.

Expression

Let punctuation and meaning shape the voice.

Try: “Could the storm reach the coast by evening?” How should the question mark change the reading?

8. Guided practice
  1. “Neither of the gates was open.” A reader says were. Which grammatical clue should trigger correction?
  2. “First rinse the seeds… Finally place the tray by the window.” Why would reading finally as first be a problem?
  3. “The ranger cautiously approached the injured wallaby.” Why is casually a weak first guess?

Answers: the “Neither … was” pattern; procedure sequence; both the printed letters and serious context point to cautiously.

9. Independent check

Read: “The museum displayed several prehistoric tools.” Explain what pre- contributes, what the whole word most likely means, and why you should still check the sentence after using the prefix.

Expected: pre- means before; prehistoric means from before recorded history; context/topic knowledge confirms the morphological prediction.

10. Misconceptions to correct
  • “Fluent means fast.” No: fluency includes accuracy, pace, phrasing and expression.
  • “If it makes sense, it is fine.” No: it must also match the print.
  • “Prefixes always solve the word.” No: use them to predict, then confirm in context.
  • “Going back is bad reading.” No: effective readers self-correct and reread.
