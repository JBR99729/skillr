Year 4 English · AC9E4LY05

Build, link and check meaning

Use comprehension strategies when listening, viewing and reading to build literal and inferred meaning, expand content knowledge, integrate and link ideas, and understand texts.

Learning intentionKnow the reading job

Today we will

Use clues and connections to build meaning, then check whether our understanding still fits.

Success criteria: I can point to evidence, explain my thinking, connect ideas and correct myself when evidence changes.

Model 1 · inferenceClue → meaning → inference

Think aloud

Text: “The bottle lid was loose. The book was damp. A puddle sat at the bottom of the bag.”

Clue 1: loose lid → water could escape.

Clue 2: damp book + puddle → water has escaped inside the bag.

Inference: the bottle probably leaked.

Teacher prompt: What tempting answer is possible but not actually supported?

Model 2 · main ideaCover all the details

Do not choose one detail

Details: sunflower seeds can be eaten, made into a spread and pressed for oil.

Main idea: sunflower seeds can be used in several foods and products.

Reject: “Sunflower oil can be used in baking.” It is true, but too narrow.

Model 3 · evidenceClaim → evidence → reasoning

Is the support strong enough?

Claim: Every Year 4 student concentrates better outdoors.

Evidence: One student read two pages faster outside yesterday.

Judgement: weak support for such a broad claim. One experience does not represent every student.

Improve it: compare many students in similar indoor and outdoor reading conditions.

Model 4 · monitorNotice → pause → check → correct

Repair meaning

“Although the creek looked shallow near the bank, the centre channel was deeper than it appeared.”

If your first note says “the creek is shallow”, although and the second clause should trigger a check.

Corrected understanding: the bank may look shallow, but the centre can be deeper.

Guided practiceConnect modes

Words + image + sound

Words warn walkers to turn back if dark clouds build. An image shows thick clouds over a ridge. A warning tone sounds before the spoken advice.

  1. What does each mode contribute?
  2. Which idea is supported by all three?
  3. What would change if the sound were cheerful birdsong?
Independent checkShow the evidence

Exit ticket

  1. Write one inference and quote or point to the clue that supports it.
  2. State the main idea in one sentence that covers all important details.
  3. Name one self-question you would ask if two details seemed to conflict.
