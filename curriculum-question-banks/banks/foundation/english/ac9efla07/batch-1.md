# AC9EFLA07 — Pass 1 question bank

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Information in an illustration

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Describe an illustration of “a girl in gumboots holding an umbrella while rain falls”. Read the options aloud.

**Question:** What meaning does the image communicate even without a written sentence?

A. It is raining.

B. The girl is baking bread.

C. It is midnight indoors.

D. The umbrella is a musical instrument.

**Correct answer:** A — The gumboots, umbrella and raindrops visually show wet weather.

**Coverage:** Identifying meaning contributed by an image.

### P02 — Information in the words

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say, “A page shows a plain picture of a cave. The words say, ‘Milo heard a soft squeak inside.’”

**Question:** What do the words tell that the picture of the cave alone does not show?

**Marking key:** They tell that Milo hears a soft squeak and/or that a sound comes from inside. Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Isolating meaning supplied by written language rather than the image.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Matching caption and image

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the caption “The seed sends its first root down into the soil” and the options.

**Question:** Which image would best support this informative caption?

A. A fully grown tree with fruit

B. A seed with a small root growing downward underground

C. A bird flying above a roof

D. An empty plant pot on a shelf

**Correct answer:** B — It visually represents the exact stage and direction described.

**Coverage:** Selecting an image that adds visual evidence to informative words.

### P04 — How a changed image changes meaning

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say, “Both story pages have the words ‘Tara opened the box.’ One picture shows Tara smiling. The other shows Tara stepping back with wide eyes.”

**Question:** How does each picture make the same sentence feel different?

**Marking key:** Identifies happiness/excitement/pleasant expectation in the smiling version (1 mark) and surprise/fear/worry in the wide-eyed, stepping-back version (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Comparing emotional meaning contributed by alternative illustrations.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Combining two clues

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “The words say, ‘Pip searched everywhere.’ The picture shows Pip looking under an empty dog bed while holding a lead.”

**Question:** What is Pip probably searching for? Use one clue from the words or picture.

**Marking key:** Pip is probably searching for a dog (1 mark); gives a clue such as dog bed, lead, or “searched everywhere” with the dog-related image (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Inferring story meaning by integrating verbal and visual clues.

### P06 — Completing an information page

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Read the sentence “A turtle’s hard shell helps protect its body.” Provide drawing materials.

**Question:** Draw or describe an image that would help a young reader understand this fact. Explain what your image adds.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for clearly representing a turtle and shell/body relationship; 1 mark for an image choice that highlights protection, such as the turtle partly withdrawing; 1 mark for explaining how the image supports the words; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Designing an image to clarify an informative statement.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Finding a mismatch

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “A page says, ‘The tiny boat drifted quietly on the calm pond.’ The picture shows a large ship in crashing ocean waves.”

**Question:** Identify two ways the image and words do not match. Suggest one change that would make the page clearer.

**Marking key:** Up to 2 marks for mismatches such as tiny/large, boat/ship, pond/ocean, calm/crashing waves or quietly/stormily; 1 mark for a logical change to either words or image that restores agreement; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Evaluating consistency between verbal and visual information.

### P08 — Planning a multimodal story moment

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “Create one page about a child discovering a hidden door in a garden wall.”

**Question:** Give one or two sentences for the page, describe the illustration, and explain something the image communicates that your words do not state directly.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for coherent original words; 1 mark for a relevant illustration; 1 mark for a distinct visual contribution such as the child’s expression, the door’s size, surrounding clues or mood; 1 mark for explaining how words and image work together; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Composing complementary verbal and visual meaning in a story.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — A picture supporting a sentence

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the sentence “The nocturnal possum wakes after sunset” and the options.

**Question:** Which picture best supports the information?

A. A possum sleeping while the sun rises

B. A butterfly resting on a flower at noon

C. A possum climbing at night under a dark sky

D. A fish swimming beneath ice

**Correct answer:** C — It shows the animal active after sunset.

**Coverage:** Matching an informative image to explicit verbal meaning.

### E02 — What the illustration adds

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say, “The sentence says, ‘The family reached the beach.’ The picture shows them carrying towels, a bucket and a striped shade tent.”

**Question:** Name one detail learned from the picture but not stated in the sentence.

**Marking key:** Any pictured detail such as the towels, bucket or striped shade tent. Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Distinguishing visual elaboration from written content.

### E03 — Reading words and symbols

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Describe a diagram labelled “Water cycle” with arrows from sea to cloud and cloud to rain.

**Question:** What do the arrows contribute to the diagram’s meaning?

A. They tell the reader to close the page.

B. They decorate the empty spaces only.

C. They name every type of cloud.

D. They show movement or sequence between parts.

**Correct answer:** D — The arrows visually connect stages and directions.

**Coverage:** Interpreting a visual convention in an informative text.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Inferring an event

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “The words say, ‘Nina stared at the empty hook.’ The picture shows an open gate and muddy pawprints leading away.”

**Question:** What has probably happened? Give two clues.

**Marking key:** Infers that an animal/dog has escaped or gone through the gate (1 mark); identifies any two relevant clues among empty hook, open gate and pawprints (1 mark each); 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Building an inference from complementary story modes.

### E05 — Improving an unclear diagram

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “A picture shows three stages of a butterfly’s life, but there are no words, numbers or arrows, and the pictures are not arranged in order.”

**Question:** Suggest two additions or changes that would help a Foundation reader understand the sequence.

**Marking key:** 1 mark each for two useful improvements such as ordering the images, adding arrows, numbering stages or adding simple labels; 1 mark for explaining how one change supports sequence/meaning; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Revising visual-verbal organisation in an information text.

### E06 — Choosing whether words are needed

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Describe “a drawing of two identical bottles, one safe drinking water and one soapy water, with no labels”.

**Question:** Why are words or clear symbols needed here? Suggest one useful label.

**Marking key:** Explains that appearance alone does not safely distinguish the contents (1 mark) and gives a clear label such as “Drinking water” or “Soapy water—do not drink” (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Reasoning about when images alone cannot communicate precise information.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Comparing two illustrated versions

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “Both pages say, ‘A sound came from behind the curtain.’ Version A shows a kitten’s tail. Version B shows a dark claw-shaped shadow.”

**Question:** Explain the different prediction and feeling each image creates. Which visual clue causes each response?

**Marking key:** 1 mark for predicting a kitten/friendly animal in A; 1 mark for linking that to the tail; 1 mark for predicting something unknown/frightening in B; 1 mark for linking that to the dark claw-shaped shadow; 1 mark for contrasting the likely gentle versus suspenseful mood; 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Analysing how visual details shape prediction and mood alongside identical words.

### E08 — Creating a clear safety poster

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “Design a poster reminding children to wash and dry their hands before eating.”

**Question:** State the words you would include, describe two image features, and explain how the words and images divide the work of communicating the message.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for concise accurate wording; 1 mark each for two useful image features such as ordered handwashing/drying actions, soap, water or food context; 1 mark for explaining what the words make precise; 1 mark for explaining how images make the actions quick/easy to understand; 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Designing and analysing complementary modes for an informative purpose.
