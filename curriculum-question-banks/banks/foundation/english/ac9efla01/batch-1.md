# AC9EFLA01 — Batch 1 question bank

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Greeting a teacher

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the question and options aloud.

**Question:** Mia sees her teacher at the classroom door in the morning. Which greeting best suits this school situation?

A. Move!

B. Hey, you!

C. Good morning, Ms Chen.

D. Give me my bag.

**Correct answer:** C — It is a clear, respectful greeting for a teacher.

**Coverage:** Selecting a greeting that suits a student–teacher relationship.

### P02 — Words for a family member

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the question and options aloud.

**Question:** Noah wants his dad to help fasten his helmet. What could Noah say at home?

A. Students must line up now.

B. Class, open your books.

C. Excuse me, Principal Singh.

D. Dad, can you please help me?

**Correct answer:** D — Noah names his family member and asks for help politely.

**Coverage:** Recognising language suited to a child speaking with a parent.

### P03 — A polite school request

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the question and options aloud.

**Question:** Which sentence is a polite request a student could make to a teacher?

A. May I please get a drink?

B. That book is blue.

C. I played at the park.

D. Shut the door now.

**Correct answer:** A — “May I please” makes the request respectful.

**Coverage:** Identifying polite request language used at school.

### P04 — Speaking to a friend

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the question and options aloud.

**Question:** Ava is building with her friend Leo. Which words invite Leo to join her?

A. Mr Leo, report to the office.

B. Leo, would you like to build with me?

C. Children, sit on the mat.

D. Leo is absent today.

**Correct answer:** B — Ava uses Leo’s name and a friendly invitation.

**Coverage:** Recognising friendly language between classmates.

### P05 — Who might say it?

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Read each sentence aloud. The child may point, draw lines or answer orally.

**Question:** Match each sentence to the person most likely to say it: **teacher** or **family member**. (a) “Please place your work in the tray.” (b) “Your pyjamas are on your bed.” (c) “Let us meet on the reading mat.” (d) “Dinner is ready.”

**Marking key:** Teacher: (a) and (c). Family member: (b) and (d). Award 1 mark for each correct match; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Distinguishing familiar home and classroom language by context.

### P06 — Choosing a name or title

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read the prompt aloud and accept an oral answer.

**Question:** At school, Ben calls his teacher “Mr Patel”. At home, he calls his brother “Sam”. Why does Ben use different names?

**Marking key:** The child explains that the people have different relationships or roles; for example, “Mr Patel is his teacher, but Sam is his brother.” Award 1 mark for identifying the different roles.

**Coverage:** Connecting forms of address to relationships.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P07 — Improving a command

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read the prompt aloud and scribe the response if needed.

**Question:** Zara says to her teacher, “Give me scissors.” Change Zara’s words into a polite school request.

**Marking key:** A suitable answer includes a request and polite language, such as “Could I please have the scissors?” Award 1 mark for a request form and 1 mark for respectful wording; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Changing a direct command into a polite request.

### P08 — Same need, different listener

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say, “You cannot reach a book. First ask your older sister at home. Then ask the librarian at school.”

**Question:** What could you say to each person?

**Marking key:** Two context-appropriate requests, such as “Sophie, can you reach that for me?” and “Excuse me, could you please help me reach that book?” Award 1 mark for each suitable request; 2 marks total. Exact wording is not required.

**Coverage:** Adapting a request for a family relationship and a school relationship.

### P09 — Volume in two places

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the scenario and options aloud.

**Question:** Eli is talking to Mum in the kitchen. Later, he answers during quiet reading time. What is the best choice?

A. Shout in both places.

B. Whisper so no one can hear in either place.

C. Use a comfortable voice at home and a quiet classroom voice during reading.

D. Say nothing whenever an adult speaks.

**Correct answer:** C — Speakers adjust volume to suit the place and situation.

**Coverage:** Adjusting voice volume across home and school contexts.

### P10 — Sorting spoken messages

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Place two labels, **home** and **school**, in front of the child. Read each message aloud.

**Question:** Sort these messages by where they would most likely be heard: (a) “Please pack your lunchbox in your schoolbag.” (b) “Can we read one more story before bed?” (c) “Take out your pencils for writing.” (d) “Grandma is calling us for breakfast.”

**Marking key:** School: (a), (c). Home: (b), (d). Award 1 mark per correct placement; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Using vocabulary and routines to identify the likely setting.

### P11 — Asking to join

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Role-play as a classmate using blocks. Invite the child to ask to join the activity.

**Question:** Show how you could ask a classmate if you may build with them.

**Marking key:** The child gains attention appropriately and uses a friendly request, such as “Can I build with you, please?” Award 1 mark for an understandable request and 1 mark for friendly/respectful delivery; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Using relationship-appropriate language to enter peer play.

### P12 — A teacher and a parent

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read both statements aloud.

**Question:** Listen: “Mum, I finished my picture!” and “Mrs Rao, I have finished my work.” Tell one way the sentences are different.

**Marking key:** Identifies a meaningful difference, such as the name used (“Mum”/“Mrs Rao”), “picture”/“work”, or the more formal school phrasing. Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Comparing language used with a parent and a teacher.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P13 — Welcoming a new student

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the scenario and options aloud.

**Question:** A new student is standing alone near the class puzzle table. Which sentence would help the student feel welcome?

A. You cannot stand there.

B. I do not know you.

C. The teacher owns that table.

D. Would you like to do the puzzle with us?

**Correct answer:** D — It is a friendly invitation between classmates.

**Coverage:** Selecting inclusive peer language in a school situation.

### P14 — Calling from another room

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read the scenario aloud and accept an oral response.

**Question:** At home, Luca needs help but Nan is in another room. What can Luca say instead of yelling, “Come here!”?

**Marking key:** A respectful call or request, such as “Nan, could you please help me when you are free?” Award 1 mark for addressing Nan and 1 mark for polite request language; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Repairing an impolite home command while retaining familiar language.

### P15 — Speaking in the library

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the scenario and options aloud.

**Question:** In the school library, Harper cannot find the animal books. Which combination of words and voice best suits the librarian?

A. Quiet voice: “Excuse me, could you help me find the animal books?”

B. Loud voice: “Where are the animal books?”

C. Silly voice: “Books! Come to me!”

D. No words: take books from another child.

**Correct answer:** A — The words are respectful and the volume suits a library.

**Coverage:** Coordinating polite wording and suitable volume with a school adult.

### P16 — Two ways to borrow

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Read the prompt in two parts and allow planning time.

**Question:** First, ask your brother at home to borrow his red pencil. Next, ask a classmate at school to borrow their ruler. Make each request suit the person.

**Marking key:** Gives two clear requests directed to the correct listeners. Both should ask permission; at least one should use polite wording. Example: “Max, can I use your red pencil?” and “Could I please borrow your ruler, Ruby?” Award 1 mark per suitable request and 1 mark for a clear difference in names/details; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Producing distinct requests for home and classroom relationships.

### P17 — Explaining a classroom choice

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read the two choices aloud.

**Question:** The teacher is helping someone else. Kai could say, “Listen to me now!” or wait and say, “Excuse me, may I ask a question?” Which should Kai choose, and why?

**Marking key:** Chooses the second sentence (1 mark) and explains that it is polite, waits for a turn or respects the teacher helping someone else (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Justifying respectful turn-taking language with a teacher.

### P18 — Responding to an instruction

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the dialogue and options aloud.

**Question:** Ms Bell says, “Please put the paintbrushes in the sink.” Which reply shows that Arlo understood the classroom instruction?

A. “My dog has a red ball.”

B. “Okay, I will put them in the sink.”

C. “You do it.”

D. “What paintbrushes?” while hiding them.

**Correct answer:** B — It acknowledges the teacher and repeats the required action.

**Coverage:** Choosing an appropriate verbal response to a teacher’s instruction.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P19 — One message for two people

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read the scenario aloud and scribe if needed.

**Question:** Riley has spilled water. At home, Riley tells an older sister. At school, Riley tells the teacher. Give Riley suitable words for each place and explain why the words might be different.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for a clear home message, such as “Ella, can you help me wipe this up?”; 1 mark for a clear school message, such as “Mr Jones, I spilled some water. Could you please help me?”; 1 mark for explaining that the listener or relationship is different; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Creating and explaining language choices for the same problem across relationships.

### P20 — Comparing two conversations

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read both conversations with expression.

**Question:** Conversation 1: “Aunty, look at my tower!” Conversation 2: “Mrs Green, may I show the class my model?” What clues tell you that one conversation is at home and the other is at school?

**Marking key:** Identifies at least two clues: “Aunty” signals a family relationship; “Mrs Green” signals a teacher; “the class” signals school; “may I” is a formal request. Award 1 mark per valid clue, up to 2 marks.

**Coverage:** Inferring setting and relationship from vocabulary and forms of address.

### P21 — Visiting the school office

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say, “You have taken a lost hat to the school office. The office worker has not met you.”

**Question:** What could you say so the office worker knows who you are and why you came?

**Marking key:** A complete, courteous message with a greeting or attention-getter, the child’s name/class if appropriate, and the reason for visiting; for example, “Excuse me, I’m Priya from Foundation Blue. I found this hat near the swings.” Award 1 mark for courteous opening, 1 for identifying self/context and 1 for explaining the purpose; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Planning clear language for an unfamiliar school adult.

### P22 — Working out the relationship

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the line and options aloud.

**Question:** A child says, “Good afternoon, Dr Evans. Mum is waiting for me at the gate.” Who is Dr Evans most likely to be in this conversation?

A. The child’s baby brother

B. The child’s pet

C. A school adult the child addresses by title

D. The child speaking to themself

**Correct answer:** C — The title “Dr Evans” and the school-gate clue suggest a school adult.

**Coverage:** Inferring a relationship from title and context clues.

### P23 — Fixing a misunderstanding

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read the scenario aloud.

**Question:** At home, Sam calls, “Miss, can I have an apple?” Sam’s older cousin does not know Sam is speaking to her. How could Sam change the message so the listener is clear? Explain your change.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for using the cousin’s name or family term, such as “Layla” or “Cousin Layla”; 1 mark for a clear request; 1 mark for explaining that “Miss” usually addresses a different relationship and did not identify the cousin; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Revising a form of address to clarify a family relationship.

### P24 — Deciding when to speak

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read the scenario aloud and accept an oral explanation.

**Question:** During a class discussion, Theo remembers something exciting from breakfast. Should Theo call it out straight away, tell the person beside him, or wait for a suitable time? Choose an action and give words Theo could use.

**Marking key:** Selects waiting for a suitable turn (1 mark), gives suitable words such as “May I share something connected to our discussion?” or saves the unrelated story for later (1 mark), and explains that classroom talk requires listening and turn-taking (1 mark); 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Reasoning about timing and language in a structured school interaction.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Requesting help from a teacher

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the scenario and options aloud.

**Question:** Olivia’s zip is stuck before the class goes outside. What is the best way to ask her teacher for help?

A. “Everyone must stop.”

B. “Fix this.”

C. “Mum, the class is going outside.”

D. “Mrs Ali, could you please help me with my zip?”

**Correct answer:** D — It names the teacher, explains the need and asks politely.

**Coverage:** Independently selecting appropriate teacher-directed request language.

### E02 — Identifying the setting

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read the sentence aloud: “Dad, may we feed the fish before bedtime?”

**Question:** Is this most likely spoken at home or at school? Give one clue.

**Marking key:** Home (1 mark), with a valid clue such as “Dad” or “before bedtime” (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Identifying a home interaction from relationship and routine clues.

### E03 — Matching language to listeners

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Read the listeners and messages aloud.

**Question:** Match each listener to the most suitable message. Listeners: (1) younger brother, (2) classroom teacher, (3) new classmate. Messages: (a) “Hello, I’m Nia. Would you like to sit with me?” (b) “Could you please explain this part, Mr Wood?” (c) “Tom, let us pack the cars away together.”

**Marking key:** 1–c, 2–b, 3–a. Award 1 mark for each correct match; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Matching forms of address and purposes to three relationships.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Rewriting words for school

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read the prompt aloud and scribe if required.

**Question:** At the art table, Finn says to the teacher, “I want the glue.” Rewrite Finn’s message so it suits the teacher and makes his request clear.

**Marking key:** A complete polite request such as “Ms King, may I please use the glue?” Award 1 mark for a request form, 1 mark for respectful wording and 1 mark for making the required item clear; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Transforming an abrupt statement into an effective school request.

### E05 — Choosing words for a grandparent and a principal

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Tell the child, “You have made a drawing. First show it to Grandpa at home. Then show it to the principal during a school visit.”

**Question:** Give one sentence for each person that suits your relationship with them.

**Marking key:** Two clear, appropriate sentences with distinguishable forms of address or level of formality. Example: “Grandpa, look what I drew!” and “Excuse me, Principal Lee, may I show you my drawing?” Award 1 mark for each suitable sentence and 1 mark for a meaningful difference; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Independently adapting one communicative purpose for familiar and formal relationships.

### E06 — Repairing classroom talk

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read the scenario aloud.

**Question:** During group work, Amy keeps calling, “No, do it my way!” Give Amy better words that help the group work together. Explain why your words are better for classmates.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for offering an idea rather than commanding, such as “Could we try it this way?”; 1 mark for inviting or listening to others, such as “What do you think?”; 1 mark for explaining that friendly, cooperative language respects classmates or helps everyone contribute; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Repairing peer language and explaining its effect on collaboration.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Same question in two settings

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read the situation aloud and record the child’s exact words.

**Question:** You want to know when it is time to leave. At home you ask your aunt. At school you ask a relief teacher you have just met. Say what you would ask each person, then explain one language choice you changed.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for a clear home question; 1 mark for a clear question to the relief teacher; 1 mark for an appropriate difference such as the name/title, greeting, “please”, or fuller wording; 1 mark for explaining that the relationship or familiarity changed; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Generating, comparing and explaining language across two relationships.

### E08 — Solving a communication problem

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read the scenario aloud. The child may answer orally or act it out.

**Question:** Jay finds a lunchbox under the playground seat. Jay first tells a close friend, but the friend does not know whose lunchbox it is. What should Jay say next, and to whom, so the problem can be solved at school? Explain why that language suits the person.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for choosing a suitable school adult such as the teacher or office worker; 1 mark for a courteous attention-getter/form of address; 1 mark for clearly reporting where the lunchbox was found; 1 mark for asking what to do or handing it in; 1 mark for explaining that a school adult can help locate the owner and should be addressed clearly/respectfully; 5 marks total. Sample: “Excuse me, Mr Diaz. I found this lunchbox under the seat near the playground. Could you help me return it?”

**Coverage:** Selecting a listener, composing a complete school report and justifying relationship-appropriate language.
