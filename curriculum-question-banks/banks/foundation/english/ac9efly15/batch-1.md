# AC9EFLY15 — Pass 1 question bank

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Two meaningful parts in dogs

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Show **dog + s → dogs** and pictures of one dog and three dogs.

**Question:** What does **dog** mean? What new meaning does **s** add in **dogs**?

**Marking key:** **dog** names the animal/one dog (1 mark); **s** shows more than one dog (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Identifying a base word and a plural marker as meaningful parts.

### P02 — Finding the word with an added part

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Show the options and read each naturally.

**Question:** Which word is made from **cup** plus a part meaning “more than one”?

A. cup

B. cub

C. cap

D. cups

**Correct answer:** D — **cups** is **cup + s**, and **s** adds the plural meaning.

**Coverage:** Recognising a familiar base word with an inflectional ending.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Building an action happening now

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Provide the cards **jump**, **ing** and a picture of a child in mid-jump.

**Question:** Join the cards to make a word for the action happening now. Read the word and tell what each part contributes.

**Marking key:** Makes **jumping** (1 mark); explains **jump** names the action (1 mark) and **ing** shows it is happening/continuing now (1 mark). 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Combining a base with an ending that changes its time meaning.

### P04 — Matching endings to meanings

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Show **cat + s** and **walk + ed**. Read: “more than one cat” and “the walking already happened”.

**Question:** Match each built word to its meaning, then underline the added part.

**Marking key:** **cats** → more than one cat, underlines **s** (1 mark); **walked** → the walking already happened, underlines **ed** (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Distinguishing plural and past-time meanings added to base words.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Joining two words in sunhat

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Show separate pictures of the sun and a hat, then a picture of a sunhat.

**Question:** Join **sun** and **hat** to write the word for the pictured object. Explain how both parts help with its meaning.

**Marking key:** Writes **sunhat** (1 mark); explains it is a hat used for protection in/from the sun or a hat for sunny conditions (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Constructing and interpreting a transparent compound word.

### P06 — Comparing cat and cats

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Show one cat beside **cat** and four cats beside **cats**.

**Question:** What stays the same in the two words? What changes in the word and in the picture?

**Marking key:** Identifies **cat** as the shared base/animal meaning (1 mark); identifies added **s** (1 mark); explains the quantity changes from one to more than one (1 mark). 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Relating a visible word-part change to a predictable meaning change.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Three meaningful parts in raincoats

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Show **raincoats** with moveable dividers and a picture of two raincoats.

**Question:** Split **raincoats** into three meaningful parts. Explain the meaning of each part and how they work together.

**Marking key:** Splits **rain + coat + s** (2 marks); explains **rain** gives the weather/use clue (1 mark), **coat** names the clothing item (1 mark), and **s** means more than one (1 mark). 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Analysing a compound base with a plural ending.

### P08 — Choosing an ending for the intended meaning

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Provide **play**, **s**, **ing** and **ed** cards. Read: (a) “The game happened yesterday.” (b) “The game is happening now.”

**Question:** Build one word for each sentence, then explain why you chose each ending.

**Marking key:** Builds **played** for yesterday (1 mark) and **playing** for now (1 mark); links **ed** with a completed/past action (1 mark) and **ing** with an action in progress (1 mark). Accept correctly written forms even if the child needs support removing the silent **e** issue is not relevant here. 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Selecting meaningful word endings according to time context.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Splitting books

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Show **books** beside a picture of several books.

**Question:** Draw a line between the two meaningful parts. What does the final part tell you?

**Correct answer:** **book | s**; **s** tells that there is more than one book.

**Coverage:** Segmenting a plural word into base and suffix.

### E02 — Reading past-time meaning

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Show **looked** and read the options aloud.

**Question:** What meaning does **ed** add to **look** in this word?

A. The looking already happened.

B. More than one person looks.

C. The looking is happening now.

D. The word names a place.

**Correct answer:** A — **ed** marks the action as completed in the past in this regular word.

**Coverage:** Interpreting a common past-tense ending.

### E03 — Understanding bedroom

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Show the word cards **bed** and **room**, then join them.

**Question:** Read **bedroom** and explain how the meanings of the two smaller words help you know what it is.

**Marking key:** Reads **bedroom** (1 mark) and explains it is a room containing/used for a bed or sleeping (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Inferring a compound word’s meaning from its components.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Building two different meanings from paint

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Provide **paint**, **ing** and **ed** cards with two pictures: one person painting now and one finished picture from yesterday.

**Question:** Build the word that matches each picture and state the time clue.

**Marking key:** **painting** matches the action in progress/now (2 marks); **painted** matches the completed/yesterday action (2 marks). 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Applying suffix meanings to distinguish ongoing and completed actions.

### E05 — Meaningful parts are not just syllables

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say **rabbit** in two beats, then show **rabbits** beside pictures of one rabbit and two rabbits.

**Question:** Which split shows the meaningful parts that explain “more than one rabbit”?

A. rab + bits

B. rabbit + s

C. rab + bit + s

D. ra + bbits

**Correct answer:** B — **rabbit** carries the animal meaning and **s** adds “more than one”; syllable beats do not necessarily mark meaningful parts.

**Coverage:** Distinguishing morphological parts from arbitrary or syllabic splits.

### E06 — Explaining a plural compound

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Show **bedrooms** in the sentence “The house has three bedrooms.”

**Question:** Split the word into all its meaningful parts and explain the final **s**.

**Marking key:** Splits **bed + room + s** (2 marks); explains that **s** shows more than one bedroom (1 mark). 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Analysing a compound word carrying a plural suffix in context.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Comparing a word family

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Show **jump**, **jumped**, **jumping** beside pictures of the action, a completed jump and a person mid-jump.

**Question:** Circle the part shared by all three words. Explain how each added ending changes the meaning.

**Marking key:** Identifies shared **jump** base (1 mark); explains unmarked **jump** names the action/base idea (1 mark); **ed** shows that the jump already happened (1 mark); **ing** shows the action in progress (1 mark); relates the explanations to the pictures or gives correct examples (1 mark). 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Comparing meaning across related forms built from one base.

### E08 — Analysing snowballs

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “The children rolled three snowballs.” Show the word **snowballs**.

**Question:** Divide **snowballs** into three meaningful parts. Explain each part, then say how the sentence would change if the final part were removed.

**Marking key:** Splits **snow + ball + s** (2 marks); explains **snow** as material/context and **ball** as shape/object (2 marks); explains **s** means more than one and removing it would give singular **snowball**, requiring a one-item meaning rather than “three” (1 mark). 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Analysing layered morphology and predicting the effect of removing a suffix.
