# AC9EFLA02 — Batch 1 question bank

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Saying what you like

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the prompt and options aloud.

**Question:** Which sentence tells that the speaker likes drawing?

A. The paper is square.

B. Where are the crayons?

C. Put the drawing away.

D. I enjoy drawing pictures.

**Correct answer:** D — “I enjoy” expresses a like.

**Coverage:** Recognising direct language that expresses a like.

### P02 — Naming a dislike politely

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read all options aloud.

**Question:** Sam does not like very loud music. Which sentence expresses this clearly and politely?

A. I would rather listen to quiet music.

B. Music is made of sounds.

C. Turn everything off forever!

D. Do you own a drum?

**Correct answer:** A — “I would rather” states a preference without being unkind.

**Coverage:** Identifying polite language for a dislike or alternative preference.

### P03 — A favourite choice

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Ask the question aloud and accept an oral response.

**Question:** Finish this sentence with your own idea: “My favourite fruit is ___.”

**Marking key:** Names one fruit to complete the preference statement. Award 1 mark. The choice itself is not judged.

**Coverage:** Completing a familiar sentence frame that states a favourite.

### P04 — Like or dislike?

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Read each sentence aloud. The child may point to **like** or **dislike** cards.

**Question:** Sort the sentences: (a) “I love splashing in puddles.” (b) “I do not enjoy scratchy jumpers.” (c) “Painting is fun for me.” (d) “I would prefer not to eat peas.”

**Marking key:** Like: (a), (c). Dislike: (b), (d). Award 1 mark per correct sort; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Classifying varied expressions of likes and dislikes.

### P05 — Choosing between two things

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the situation and options aloud.

**Question:** Layla likes both apples and pears, but she chooses pears first. What can she say?

A. Pears grow on trees.

B. I prefer pears today.

C. Are these pears ripe?

D. Put both fruits away.

**Correct answer:** B — “Prefer” shows which liked option she chooses.

**Coverage:** Recognising the word “prefer” as a way to compare choices.

### P06 — Listening for strength

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say both sentences with a neutral voice: “I like hopping” and “I love hopping.”

**Question:** Which sentence shows the stronger feeling?

**Marking key:** “I love hopping.” Award 1 mark. A child may repeat or identify the second sentence.

**Coverage:** Distinguishing different strengths of positive preference language.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P07 — Completing a preference sentence

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Offer the choices “blocks” and “puzzles”.

**Question:** Use the words “I would rather” to tell which activity you choose.

**Marking key:** Produces a complete choice such as “I would rather do a puzzle.” Award 1 mark for using the frame and 1 mark for naming one option; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Producing a preference with “would rather”.

### P08 — Making a harsh comment respectful

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read the statement aloud: “Yuck! That soup is horrible.”

**Question:** Change the statement so the speaker still tells the truth about their taste but uses respectful words.

**Marking key:** A suitable response such as “I do not like this soup” or “This soup is not my favourite.” Award 1 mark for an accurate dislike and 1 mark for respectful wording; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Rephrasing an unkind reaction as a respectful personal preference.

### P09 — Asking another person

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the prompt and options aloud.

**Question:** Which question invites a friend to share a preference about stories?

A. Stories have pages.

B. Read this story now.

C. Which story do you like best?

D. I left my story at home.

**Correct answer:** C — It asks the friend to name a preferred story.

**Coverage:** Recognising a question that elicits another person’s preference.

### P10 — Adding a simple reason

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Ask the child to choose “sandpit” or “climbing frame”.

**Question:** Tell your choice using this frame: “I like ___ because ___.”

**Marking key:** Gives a choice and a connected reason, such as “I like the sandpit because I can build.” Award 1 mark for the preference and 1 mark for a relevant reason; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Extending a like with a simple personal reason.

### P11 — Different phrases, same meaning

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Read the four sentences and two meaning labels aloud.

**Question:** Match each sentence to **chooses trains** or **chooses cars**: (a) “Trains are my favourite.” (b) “I would rather play with cars.” (c) “I like trains best.” (d) “I prefer cars.”

**Marking key:** Chooses trains: (a), (c). Chooses cars: (b), (d). Award 1 mark per correct match; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Recognising equivalent preference expressions.

### P12 — Agreeing without copying

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say, “A friend says, ‘I like the blue kite best.’ You like the blue kite too.”

**Question:** What could you say to show that you agree?

**Marking key:** A clear agreement such as “I like the blue one too” or “So do I.” Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Expressing a shared preference in response to another speaker.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P13 — Choosing a class song

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the scenario and options aloud.

**Question:** The class may sing “Rainy Day” or “Busy Bees”. Asha wants “Busy Bees”. Which response both states her choice and respects other choices?

A. Busy Bees is the only good song.

B. Everyone must choose my song.

C. I refuse to sing anything.

D. I would prefer Busy Bees, but we can listen to both ideas.

**Correct answer:** D — It states a preference without dismissing classmates.

**Coverage:** Expressing a group choice respectfully.

### P14 — Understanding an indirect preference

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read the sentence aloud: “The swings are fun, but I would choose the slide first.”

**Question:** Which playground activity does the speaker prefer? What words helped you know?

**Marking key:** The slide (1 mark); identifies “would choose … first” as the clue (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Inferring preference from comparative language.

### P15 — Responding to a different opinion

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say, “You like rainy days. Your partner says, ‘I like sunny days best.’”

**Question:** Reply in a way that shares your different preference and stays friendly.

**Marking key:** A respectful difference such as “I like rainy days best, but it is okay that we like different weather.” Award 1 mark for stating the child’s preference and 1 mark for a friendly response; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Expressing disagreement about preferences without criticising another person.

### P16 — Ordering strength of feeling

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Read each sentence with the same tone.

**Question:** Put these statements from weakest dislike to strongest dislike: (a) “It is not my favourite.” (b) “I really do not like it.” (c) “I would rather choose something else.”

**Correct answer:** (a), (c), (b) is the intended order. “Not my favourite” is mild; “would rather” expresses a clearer alternative; “really do not like” is strongest.

**Coverage:** Comparing the strength of dislike expressions.

### P17 — Making a shared choice

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read the scenario aloud and scribe if required.

**Question:** Two children may choose one game. Max likes hide-and-seek. Imani prefers statues. Give each child a sentence to share their preference, then give one question they could ask to help decide fairly.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for Max’s clear preference, 1 mark for Imani’s clear preference, and 1 mark for a fair decision question such as “Could we play one game now and the other later?” or “Which game did we play last time?”; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Combining individual preference language with collaborative decision-making.

### P18 — Choosing words at snack time

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read all options aloud.

**Question:** The snack choices are plain yoghurt and strawberry yoghurt. Which sentence clearly says that the speaker accepts either but slightly favours strawberry?

A. I like both, but I prefer strawberry.

B. Strawberry is the only choice anyone should make.

C. I will not eat yoghurt.

D. Is yoghurt kept cold?

**Correct answer:** A — It communicates liking both and favouring one.

**Coverage:** Expressing a qualified preference between two acceptable choices.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P19 — Finding two clues

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read the statement aloud: “I enjoy painting, but making things with clay is my favourite.”

**Question:** What does the speaker like, and what does the speaker like best?

**Marking key:** Likes painting (1 mark); likes clay best (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Separating a general like from a strongest preference.

### P20 — Repairing a disagreement

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read the dialogue aloud: Kim says, “Cats are best.” Rui replies, “No! Cats are boring. Dogs are better.”

**Question:** Change Rui’s reply so it states Rui’s preference without putting down Kim’s choice. Explain why your version is better.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for a respectful statement such as “I prefer dogs”; 1 mark for acknowledging different preferences, such as “We like different pets”; 1 mark for explaining that the revision avoids insulting Kim’s choice; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Revising preference language to protect another person’s feelings.

### P21 — Working out an unstated choice

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the situation and options aloud.

**Question:** At the craft table, Bella puts aside the markers, reaches for the watercolours and says, “These make softer colours.” What can we reasonably infer?

A. Bella cannot see the markers.

B. Bella prefers using watercolours for this picture.

C. Bella dislikes every kind of art.

D. Bella thinks watercolours are food.

**Correct answer:** B — Her action and comment show the choice she favours for the task.

**Coverage:** Inferring a preference from words and actions together.

### P22 — Choosing language for an uncertain preference

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say, “You have never tried either mango or kiwi. Someone asks which one you like best.”

**Question:** What honest answer could you give before tasting them?

**Marking key:** States uncertainty rather than inventing a preference; for example, “I do not know yet because I have not tried them.” Award 1 mark for uncertainty and 1 mark for the reason; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Distinguishing a genuine preference from an unknown choice.

### P23 — Comparing three expressions

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: (1) “I like trains.” (2) “Trains are my favourite.” (3) “I do not mind trains.”

**Question:** Which speaker shows the strongest positive preference? Which shows the weakest? Explain using the words in the sentences.

**Marking key:** Sentence 2 is strongest (1 mark) because “favourite” identifies the top choice (1 mark). Sentence 3 is weakest (1 mark) because “do not mind” shows acceptance rather than strong liking (1 mark); 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Analysing degrees of positive preference.

### P24 — Planning a class survey response

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “The class is choosing a read-aloud book. You like the animal story and the space book equally.”

**Question:** Give a truthful response to the survey and suggest one way to make the final choice.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for language showing equal preference, such as “I like both equally”; 1 mark for a workable choice method, such as taking a class vote, alternating books or drawing one title fairly; 1 mark for explaining that no false favourite was claimed; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Communicating equal preferences and contributing to a fair decision.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Finding a preference word

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the sentence and options aloud.

**Question:** In the sentence “I prefer the green cup,” which word tells us that the speaker is choosing one cup over another?

A. I

B. green

C. prefer

D. cup

**Correct answer:** C — “Prefer” signals the favoured choice.

**Coverage:** Identifying explicit preference vocabulary.

### E02 — Like, dislike or question?

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Read the labels and statements aloud.

**Question:** Match each statement to **like**, **dislike** or **asks for a preference**: (a) “I enjoy listening to rain.” (b) “Which season do you like best?” (c) “I would rather not wear wool.”

**Marking key:** (a) like, (b) asks for a preference, (c) dislike. Award 1 mark each; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Distinguishing three communicative purposes related to preference.

### E03 — Stating a favourite

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Show or name the choices: triangle, circle and rectangle.

**Question:** Use a complete sentence to name your favourite shape.

**Marking key:** A complete preference statement such as “The circle is my favourite shape.” Award 1 mark for naming one offered shape and 1 mark for clear preference language; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Independently producing a complete favourite statement.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Making words considerate

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “This book is stupid. I hate it.”

**Question:** Rewrite the comment so the reader expresses a dislike without insulting a book that someone else may enjoy.

**Marking key:** A personal, respectful formulation such as “This book is not for me” or “I did not enjoy this story.” Award 1 mark for retaining the dislike and 1 mark for respectful personal wording; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Applying respectful language when expressing a negative preference.

### E05 — Explaining a mixed preference

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “I like cycling and swimming. If I can choose only one today, I would rather swim.”

**Question:** Does the speaker dislike cycling? What is the speaker’s choice today? Use evidence from the sentence.

**Marking key:** No, the speaker likes cycling (1 mark). The choice today is swimming (1 mark), supported by “would rather swim” (1 mark); 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Interpreting a preference without turning the unchosen option into a dislike.

### E06 — Responding to two classmates

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say, “One classmate likes building tall towers. Another likes building wide bridges. You enjoy both.”

**Question:** Respond in a way that clearly tells both classmates your preference.

**Marking key:** Expresses equal or dual liking, such as “I enjoy both towers and bridges” (1 mark), and responds inclusively to both classmates, such as suggesting trying each (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Communicating a non-exclusive preference in a social situation.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Analysing four choices

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read the four comments: Ana: “I love the drums.” Bo: “The drums are all right.” Cora: “I would rather hear the flute.” Dev: “I do not like the drums.”

**Question:** Who has the strongest positive feeling about the drums? Who expresses a dislike? Who prefers something else? Explain one answer using the exact clue words.

**Marking key:** Ana has the strongest positive feeling (1 mark); Dev dislikes the drums (1 mark); Cora prefers something else (1 mark); explains one answer using “love”, “do not like” or “would rather” correctly (1 mark); 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Analysing strength and direction of preference across several speakers.

### E08 — Solving a fair-choice problem

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read the scenario aloud and scribe if needed.

**Question:** Four children are choosing the class’s next movement break. Two prefer dancing, one likes stretching best, and one says, “I like either.” Give words each kind of speaker could use to state the preference clearly. Then suggest a fair way for the group to decide.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for a clear dancing preference; 1 mark for a clear stretching preference; 1 mark for wording that genuinely accepts either option; 1 mark for a fair method such as a vote while recognising the flexible response; 1 mark for explaining how the method uses the stated preferences; 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Synthesising varied preference language and using it in a group decision.
