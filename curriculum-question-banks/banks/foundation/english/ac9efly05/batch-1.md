# AC9EFLY05 — Pass 1 question bank

All short texts in this bank are newly authored for SkillrHub.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Finding a stated detail

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read: “Mira packed a red towel and a yellow bucket before walking to the beach.”

**Question:** What colour was the bucket?

A. blue

B. red

C. yellow

D. green

**Correct answer:** C — The text states “a yellow bucket”.

**Coverage:** Retrieving literal information from a listened-to text.

### P02 — Recognising a prediction

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read: “Dark clouds gather. The wind lifts dry leaves. Luis reaches for his raincoat.”

**Question:** Which thought is a sensible prediction?

A. The leaves are made of metal.

B. It snowed last year.

C. Luis owns three shoes.

D. It may rain soon.

**Correct answer:** D — The clouds, wind and raincoat are clues to likely rain.

**Coverage:** Making a simple prediction from textual clues.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Visualising a description

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Read slowly: “A tiny green frog sits on a wide lily pad. Two pink flowers float nearby.”

**Question:** Draw or describe the picture you made in your mind. Include three details from the sentence.

**Marking key:** 1 mark each for frog/green or tiny detail, wide lily pad, and two pink nearby flowers; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Visualising explicit descriptive details.

### P04 — Making a connection

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Read: “On her first day, Nia holds Dad’s hand tightly at the classroom door. A smiling child invites her to the puzzle table.”

**Question:** Tell a connection to something you have experienced, seen or read that helps you understand Nia’s feelings.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for a relevant connection involving new places, nervousness, welcome or friendship and 1 mark for explaining how it helps interpret Nia; 2 marks total. A child may refer to another text rather than personal experience.

**Coverage:** Using text-to-self/world/text connection to support understanding.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Summarising the main event

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “A gust blows Ravi’s hat into a pond. A duck nudges it towards the bank. Ravi reaches down and lifts the dripping hat out.”

**Question:** Summarise what happened in one sentence without every small detail.

**Marking key:** A concise account including lost/blown hat and recovery/help, such as “A duck helped Ravi get his hat back from the pond.” Award 1 mark for main problem and 1 mark for resolution; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Condensing a short sequence into its central idea.

### P06 — Asking a useful question

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Read: “The class finds three different footprints beside the vegetable garden.”

**Question:** Ask one question that would help the class investigate or understand the footprints.

**Marking key:** A relevant open or factual question, such as “Which animals made them?” or “Are the tracks new?” Award 1 mark for relevance and 1 mark for information-seeking purpose; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Generating a comprehension question from an information gap.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Inferring an unstated cause

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “When Sora enters the kitchen, flour covers the bench and tiny pawprints cross the floor. The puppy hides behind the chair.”

**Question:** What probably happened before Sora entered? Give three clues that support your inference.

**Marking key:** Infers the puppy got into/spilled/walked through the flour (1 mark); 1 mark each for up to three relevant clues—flour mess, tiny pawprints, hiding puppy; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Inferring a prior event from converging textual clues.

### P08 — Revising a prediction

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read Part 1: “Tess hears scratching inside a box marked ‘Garden Supplies’.” Pause for a prediction. Then read Part 2: “A dry branch pokes through a torn corner whenever the wind moves the box.”

**Question:** Give your first prediction, then explain how Part 2 confirms or changes it.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for a plausible initial prediction; 1 mark for identifying branch/wind explanation; 1 mark for clearly revising or confirming the prediction; 1 mark for referring to evidence from both parts; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Monitoring understanding by updating a prediction with new evidence.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Literal location

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read: “The spare key hangs on a hook behind the blue door.”

**Question:** Where is the key?

A. On a hook behind the blue door

B. Beneath a red table

C. Inside a garden box

D. Beside the front gate

**Correct answer:** A — This location is directly stated.

**Coverage:** Retrieving a precise literal detail.

### E02 — Naming the main idea

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “Some seeds travel on the wind. Light seeds may float far from the parent plant before landing.”

**Question:** What is this text mainly about?

**Marking key:** How some/light seeds travel or spread by wind. Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Identifying main idea in a brief informative text.

### E03 — Choosing a useful question

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read: “A story ends with a sealed letter left on the doorstep.”

**Question:** Which question most directly addresses missing story information?

A. How many letters are in the word door?

B. Who sent the letter and what does it say?

C. Are all doorsteps made of stone?

D. What colour is your own pencil?

**Correct answer:** B — It targets the unresolved object and sender.

**Coverage:** Selecting a question that deepens text understanding.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Predicting from behaviour

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “Before the race, Elio checks both shoelaces, takes three slow breaths and stands behind the start line.”

**Question:** What is Elio likely to do next? Give two clues.

**Marking key:** Likely begin/run the race (1 mark); 1 mark each for two supporting clues such as shoelaces, breathing/preparation or start line; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Predicting a next action using character behaviour and setting.

### E05 — Visualising position words

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Read: “A striped ball rests beneath the bench. A sunhat lies on the bench, and a bottle stands beside its right leg.”

**Question:** Draw the arrangement or place three objects to match it.

**Marking key:** 1 mark each for ball beneath bench, hat on bench and bottle beside the bench’s right leg; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Constructing a mental/spatial model from language.

### E06 — Connecting to explain a choice

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “The power goes off, so Jo places a torch on the table pointing at the ceiling rather than holding it close to one book.”

**Question:** Use what you know about light to explain Jo’s choice.

**Marking key:** Explains that reflected/spread light from the ceiling can light a wider area for more people, or otherwise accurately connects experience of torches/light spread to the choice. Award 1 mark for relevant connection and 1 mark for explanation; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Applying background knowledge to interpret an action.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Combining comprehension strategies

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “A young magpie repeatedly carries twigs to the same fork in a tree. By afternoon, a small round shape has formed between the branches.”

**Question:** State the literal actions, infer what the magpie is making, describe what you visualise and ask one useful next question.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for literal twig-carrying/location; 1 mark for inferring a nest; 1 mark for a text-consistent visual description; 1 mark for a relevant question; 1 mark for citing a clue that supports the inference; 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Integrating retrieval, inference, visualisation and questioning.

### E08 — Summarising and tracking change

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “At first, the pond surface is smooth. A breeze begins, making tiny ripples. Strong wind follows, and waves push leaves to one side. Later, the wind stops and the water slowly settles.”

**Question:** Summarise the whole text in two sentences and explain how your mental picture changes from beginning to middle to end.

**Marking key:** Up to 2 marks for a concise summary including wind’s effect and settling; 1 mark each for accurate beginning, middle and ending visual states; 1 mark for explaining the causal sequence; 6 marks total.

**Coverage:** Summarising and visualising change across an informational sequence.
