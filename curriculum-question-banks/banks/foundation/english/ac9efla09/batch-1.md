# AC9EFLA09 — Pass 1 question bank

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Letter or punctuation mark

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Show the symbols **m**, **?**, **T** and **.** in large print. Name them if visual access requires it.

**Question:** Sort the symbols into **letters** and **punctuation marks**.

**Marking key:** Letters: m, T. Punctuation marks: ?, full stop. Award 1 mark per correct sort; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Distinguishing alphabetic characters from punctuation.

### P02 — A capital for a name

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the sentence choices and emphasise that the child is named Mia.

**Question:** Which version writes the child’s name correctly?

A. I sat beside mIA.

B. I sat beside mia.

C. I sat beside Mia.

D. I sat beside .mia

**Correct answer:** C — A person’s name begins with a capital letter.

**Coverage:** Recognising capitalisation of a familiar proper name.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Marking a sentence boundary

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Show or read the unedited sentence: “the rabbit is hiding”

**Question:** What two changes would show where the sentence begins and ends?

**Marking key:** Capital T at the beginning (1 mark) and a full stop at the end (1 mark). Correct version: “The rabbit is hiding.”

**Coverage:** Applying initial capital and terminal full stop conventions.

### P04 — Ending a question

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read “Where is my hat” with questioning intonation, then the options.

**Question:** Which punctuation mark belongs at the end?

A. :

B. .

C. ,

D. ?

**Correct answer:** D — A question mark signals that the sentence asks a question.

**Coverage:** Selecting a question mark as terminal punctuation.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Editing names and sentence starts

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Show or read: “on monday, leo visits ana.”

**Question:** Find the four letters that need to be capitals and say the corrected sentence.

**Marking key:** “On Monday, Leo visits Ana.” Award 1 mark for sentence-initial O and 1 mark for correctly capitalising all three names Monday, Leo and Ana; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Applying capitals to a sentence beginning and familiar names.

### P06 — Reading emotion from an end mark

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Show “We won.” and “We won!”

**Question:** Read or repeat each sentence in a way that suits its end mark. What extra feeling can the exclamation mark signal?

**Marking key:** Differentiates neutral statement from excited/emphatic delivery (1 mark) and identifies excitement, strong feeling or emphasis (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Connecting terminal punctuation with expression and meaning.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Finding hidden sentences

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Show or read: “sam opened the gate the goats ran out”

**Question:** Rewrite or say the message as two clear sentences. Explain how capitals and end marks help the reader.

**Marking key:** “Sam opened the gate. The goats ran out.” Award 1 mark for the name Sam capitalised, 1 mark for both sentence starts/end boundaries, and 1 mark for explaining that the marks separate the two events or show where to start/stop; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Restoring multiple sentence boundaries and a name capital.

### P08 — Choosing between two end marks

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “A child writes ‘Watch out’ because a ball is rolling towards someone.”

**Question:** Should the child end the warning with a full stop or an exclamation mark? Choose one, write or say the complete warning, and explain the effect.

**Marking key:** Prefers “Watch out!” (1 mark), uses a capital W and exclamation mark correctly (1 mark), and explains that it signals an urgent/strong warning (1 mark); 3 marks total. Accept a full stop only with a coherent explanation of calm delivery, but the urgency mark is required for full credit.

**Coverage:** Selecting punctuation to match communicative force.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Recognising punctuation

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the options by name as well as showing them.

**Question:** Which option is a punctuation mark rather than a letter?

A. !

B. b

C. R

D. s

**Correct answer:** A — The exclamation mark is punctuation; the others are letters.

**Coverage:** Independently categorising a written symbol.

### E02 — Correctly writing a place name

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Tell the child that Perth is the name of a city.

**Question:** Which version uses the capital letter correctly?

A. We went to perth.

B. We went to Perth.

C. we went to Perth.

D. We Went To perth.

**Correct answer:** B — The sentence and the city name begin with capitals in the required places.

**Coverage:** Recognising capitals for both sentence start and proper name.

### E03 — Signalling an ending

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Ask the prompt aloud.

**Question:** Name one punctuation mark that can show the end of a sentence.

**Marking key:** Full stop, question mark or exclamation mark. Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Recalling a terminal punctuation mark.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Correcting a simple statement

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Show: “my socks are wet”

**Question:** Write or tell the corrected sentence and name the punctuation mark you added.

**Marking key:** “My socks are wet.” (1 mark for capital, 1 mark for full stop) and names “full stop” (1 mark); 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Editing and naming boundary features in a statement.

### E05 — Correcting a direct question

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Show: “can ellie come too.”

**Question:** Make all changes needed so the writing is a correctly marked question.

**Marking key:** “Can Ellie come too?” Award 1 mark for capital C, 1 mark for capital E in Ellie and 1 mark for replacing the full stop with a question mark; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Coordinating capitalisation and interrogative punctuation.

### E06 — Explaining a changed meaning

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Show and read “Really.” and “Really?”

**Question:** Explain how the different end marks guide the reader to hear or understand the word differently.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for identifying the first as a statement/confirmation; 1 mark for the second as a question/surprise or uncertainty; 1 mark for explaining that punctuation changes the expected voice/meaning; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Analysing the semantic and prosodic effect of terminal punctuation.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Editing a three-sentence note

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Show or read: “dear ben i found your cap is it blue”

**Question:** Edit the note into three sentences: a greeting, a statement and a question. Use all necessary capitals and end marks.

**Marking key:** A correct version is “Dear Ben. I found your cap. Is it blue?” Award 1 mark for capitalising Dear, Ben, I and Is as required; 1 mark for greeting boundary; 1 mark for statement boundary; 1 mark for question mark; 1 mark for preserving clear meaning; 5 marks total. Accept a comma after “Dear Ben” if taught, provided three sentence units remain clear.

**Coverage:** Applying boundary and name conventions across a short connected text.

### E08 — Diagnosing reader confusion

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Show: “look maya the bus is here are you ready”

**Question:** Explain two reasons this is hard to read, then edit it into clear sentences with suitable expression.

**Marking key:** Up to 2 marks for identifying missing capitals/name capital and missing sentence/end punctuation; 1 mark for “Look, Maya!” or another correctly bounded call; 1 mark for “The bus is here.”; 1 mark for “Are you ready?”; 5 marks total. Do not require the comma for Foundation full credit if oral grouping is clear.

**Coverage:** Diagnosing and repairing capitals and varied terminal punctuation in connected speech-like writing.
