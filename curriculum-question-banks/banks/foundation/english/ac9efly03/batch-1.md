# AC9EFLY03 — Pass 1 question bank

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Imagined or factual?

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the options aloud.

**Question:** Which sentence is clearly imaginative?

A. At midnight, the teacup grew wings and flew away.

B. A teacup is used for holding a drink.

C. Ceramic can break if it falls.

D. Some cups have handles.

**Correct answer:** A — It presents an invented event that does not normally happen.

**Coverage:** Recognising an imaginative event against factual statements.

### P02 — Features of two text kinds

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Read the features and labels **imaginative** and **informative**.

**Question:** Sort: (a) made-up characters, (b) labelled diagram, (c) facts about a topic, (d) invented problem and ending.

**Marking key:** Imaginative: (a), (d). Informative: (b), (c). Award 1 mark each; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Classifying characteristic features of imaginative and informative texts.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Using excerpt clues

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “The moon snail polished every star before dawn, but one star kept wriggling away.”

**Question:** Is this imaginative or informative? Give one clue.

**Marking key:** Imaginative (1 mark); clue such as fictional moon snail, polishing stars, or wriggling star (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Justifying text classification with internal evidence.

### P04 — Comparing language choices

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read both: Text A: “Octopuses have eight arms.” Text B: “Ollie Octopus knitted eight scarves at once.”

**Question:** Which statement best compares them?

A. Both are instructions for knitting.

B. A gives a fact; B uses an imagined character and action.

C. A is imaginary because numbers are never factual.

D. B is a labelled science diagram.

**Correct answer:** B — The texts use the same animal in factual and imaginative ways.

**Coverage:** Comparing factual and fictional treatment of one subject.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Turning a fact into fiction

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Give the fact “A caterpillar changes into a butterfly.”

**Question:** Add one clearly imaginative detail to turn the fact into the start of a story, while keeping the caterpillar and change.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for retaining the real change and 1 mark for an unmistakably fictional addition such as speech, magical goal or invented event; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Transforming factual content through imaginative elaboration.

### P06 — Making a story detail informative

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “The story says, ‘The angry volcano shouted red words into the sky.’”

**Question:** Rewrite the idea as a simple informative sentence about what a volcano can do.

**Marking key:** A factual sentence such as “A volcano can erupt hot lava, ash and gas.” Award 1 mark for removing personification and 1 mark for plausible factual information; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Converting figurative imaginative language into factual explanation.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Preventing a false fact

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “An animal fact page includes: ‘At night, wombats hold secret tea parties underground.’ It is presented beside true facts without a story label.”

**Question:** Explain the problem and suggest two ways to repair the page.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for identifying the invented claim as misleading in an informative text; up to 2 marks for repairs such as remove/replace it with a verified fact, label it as an imaginative caption, or move it into a story box; 1 mark for explaining how a repair helps readers distinguish fact from fiction; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Evaluating inappropriate mixing of fictional claims with factual information.

### P08 — Analysing a hybrid page

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “The top half tells a story about a raindrop named Ria travelling from a cloud. A box below says, ‘Water falls from clouds as rain’ and shows a labelled diagram.”

**Question:** Identify the imaginative and informative parts. Give two clues and explain why the page contains both.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for identifying Ria/story as imaginative; 1 mark for a fictional clue; 1 mark for identifying fact/diagram as informative; 1 mark for factual/diagram clue; 1 mark for explaining combined engagement and learning; 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Distinguishing modes within a mixed-purpose text.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Recognising informative content

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the options aloud.

**Question:** Which excerpt is most clearly informative?

A. “Captain Magnet sailed beyond the Moon.”

B. “The magnet became lonely and hugged every spoon.”

C. “A magnet can attract some objects made of iron or steel.”

D. “Once, a magnet whispered to a key.”

**Correct answer:** C — It states testable factual information without invented events.

**Coverage:** Identifying factual explanatory language.

### E02 — Naming one difference

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Ask the prompt aloud.

**Question:** Tell one difference between an imaginative text and an informative text.

**Marking key:** Any accurate contrast, such as invented characters/events versus facts/explanations, or story structure versus headings/labels/diagrams. Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Articulating a basic cross-text distinction.

### E03 — Sorting titles by likely type

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Read the titles and labels aloud.

**Question:** Match each title to **imaginative** or **informative**: (a) “Why Spiders Make Webs”, (b) “The Spider Who Sewed the Sky”, (c) “Parts of a Spider”, (d) “Sally Spider’s Surprise Party”.

**Marking key:** Informative: (a), (c). Imaginative: (b), (d). Award 1 mark each; 4 marks total. Accept discussion that title (a) could be literary only if the child explains ambiguity; accompanying factual features would settle it.

**Coverage:** Predicting text kind from conventional title cues while recognising ambiguity.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Identifying evidence in paired texts

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read Text A: “Platypuses lay eggs.” Text B: “Pippa Platypus delivered a moon-shaped egg to the king.”

**Question:** Which text is informative and which is imaginative? Give one clue for each.

**Marking key:** A informative + factual claim clue (1 mark); B imaginative + invented character/event clue (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Classifying paired excerpts with explicit evidence.

### E05 — Choosing suitable features

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Say, “A writer is making two pages about shadows: one story and one fact page.”

**Question:** Match each feature to the best page: talking shadow, labelled light-source diagram, character problem, observation fact.

**Marking key:** Story: talking shadow, character problem. Fact page: labelled light-source diagram, observation fact. Award 1 mark each; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Assigning content features according to text kind.

### E06 — Repairing the wrong style

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “A fact card begins, ‘Long ago, the cheeky Sun hid behind a mountain because it wanted a holiday.’”

**Question:** Rewrite it as one informative sentence about sunset.

**Marking key:** A simple accurate account such as “The Sun appears to set when Earth turns and our location faces away from it.” For Foundation, accept “At sunset, the Sun appears to move below the horizon” (1 mark for factual style, 1 mark for removing personification); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Recasting personified explanation into informative style.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Comparing complete text plans

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “Plan A has a main character, a problem with a missing shell and a happy ending. Plan B has a heading, labelled shell diagram and three true facts.”

**Question:** Identify each text type and explain how at least two features in each support that classification.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for Plan A imaginative and 1 mark each for two supporting features; 1 mark for Plan B informative and 1 mark each for two supporting features; 6 marks total.

**Coverage:** Analysing clusters of structural features across text kinds.

### E08 — Creating a clear pair on one topic

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Give the shared topic “the ocean at night”.

**Question:** Create one sentence for an imaginative story and one for an informative text. Then name two features you would add to each full text so readers could easily tell them apart.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for clearly imaginative sentence; 1 mark for accurate informative sentence; up to 2 marks for appropriate story features; up to 2 marks for appropriate informative features; 1 mark for explaining the contrast; 7 marks total.

**Coverage:** Producing and differentiating two text kinds from common subject matter.
