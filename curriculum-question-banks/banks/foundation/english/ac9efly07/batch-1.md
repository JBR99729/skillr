# AC9EFLY07 — Pass 1 question bank

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — A clear spoken report

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the options as if speaking to a class.

**Question:** Which opening clearly tells peers what the report is about?

A. Today I will tell you how our class made fruit salad.

B. Um, there was stuff and things.

C. Guess what I might say.

D. My report has ended.

**Correct answer:** A — It introduces the topic and event directly.

**Coverage:** Recognising a purposeful oral-text opening.

### P02 — Volume for peers

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Stand a typical small-class distance away.

**Question:** Say, “We found a worm in the garden,” at a volume suitable for the whole class—not a whisper and not a shout.

**Marking key:** Award 1 mark if the message is clearly audible at group distance and controlled rather than shouted.

**Coverage:** Producing appropriate presentation volume.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Ordering an event report

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Read: (a) “Finally, we hung the picture to dry.” (b) “First, we mixed blue and yellow paint.” (c) “Next, we painted a green tree.”

**Question:** Put the spoken report in order and say it smoothly.

**Correct answer:** (b), (c), (a). Award 1 mark for sequence and 1 mark for an audible connected delivery; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Structuring a brief chronological oral report.

### P04 — Adding useful topic words

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say, “You are reporting how a seed was planted. Use at least two of these words: **seed**, **soil**, **water**, **pot**.”

**Question:** Give two connected sentences for your report.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for two relevant connected sentences and 1 mark for accurate use of at least two supplied topic words; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Incorporating learned vocabulary into spoken reporting.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Planning from three prompts

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Give prompts: **where — what happened — result**. Situation: “You built a bridge from blocks and tested it with toy cars.”

**Question:** Plan and deliver a short report that answers all three prompts.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for place/context, 1 mark for building/testing event, 1 mark for a result, and 1 mark for logical, audible delivery; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Using an oral scaffold to organise event information.

### P06 — Changing delivery for distance

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Ask the child to say “The shell has a striped pattern” first to one nearby peer, then to a group several metres away.

**Question:** Deliver the sentence twice and explain one voice change you made.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for suitable partner volume, 1 mark for increased clarity/volume to the distant group, and 1 mark for explaining the adjustment; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Deliberately adapting volume to audience distance.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Repairing a confusing presentation

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “A speaker begins, ‘Then it broke. We put it there. That was before the other thing.’ The class does not know the topic.”

**Question:** Identify two problems and replace the opening with two clear sentences for a report about testing a paper tower.

**Marking key:** Up to 2 marks for problems such as no topic, unclear pronouns, wrong sequence or vague words; 1 mark for introducing the paper-tower test; 1 mark for a clear first event/setup; 1 mark for logical wording; 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Diagnosing and revising coherence in a spoken report.

### P08 — Delivering a complete mini-report

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Situation: “Your group sorted classroom objects by the materials they were made from.” Allow brief planning with picture or keyword cues.

**Question:** Give a 3–4 sentence report to peers with an opening, two details and a closing result. Use a suitable class volume.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for topic opening, up to 2 marks for accurate sorting/material details, 1 mark for result/conclusion, 1 mark for logical order and 1 mark for audible controlled volume; 6 marks total.

**Coverage:** Creating and delivering a complete short oral report.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Selecting a relevant detail

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say, “The topic is a class walk to observe autumn leaves.” Read the options.

**Question:** Which detail belongs in the spoken report?

A. My favourite breakfast is toast.

B. We saw red, orange and brown leaves under the trees.

C. A spaceship can travel far away.

D. The classroom door has a handle.

**Correct answer:** B — It directly reports an observation from the walk.

**Coverage:** Selecting topic-relevant oral-report content.

### E02 — Stating why volume matters

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Ask the prompt aloud.

**Question:** Why should a speaker use enough volume when reporting to peers?

**Marking key:** So the audience can hear and understand the message. Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Explaining a functional purpose of voice volume.

### E03 — Choosing a logical closing

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read: “We placed the toy boat in the tub. It stayed on top of the water.” Then read options.

**Question:** Which closing best reports the result?

A. Boats can be many colours.

B. Next week has five school days.

C. Our test showed that the toy boat floated.

D. Let me start the report again.

**Correct answer:** C — It summarises the observed test outcome.

**Coverage:** Recognising an effective oral-report conclusion.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Reporting from notes

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Give notes: **windy day / kite rose / string tangled / adult helped**.

**Question:** Turn the notes into a clear 3-sentence report for peers.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for weather/setup, 1 mark for rise/tangle events in order, 1 mark for help/resolution, and 1 mark for connected audible delivery; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Expanding concise notes into an organised oral text.

### E05 — Correcting overly loud delivery

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say, “During a report to six nearby classmates, a child shouts every sentence.”

**Question:** What should the child change, and what should stay the same?

**Marking key:** Reduce volume to a clear small-group level (1 mark) while keeping words distinct/audible and the message content intact (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Refining vocal delivery without losing clarity.

### E06 — Creating a topic-specific opening

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Topic: “How the class compared which container held more water.”

**Question:** Give an opening that names the investigation and prepares peers for the report.

**Marking key:** A clear opening naming comparison of container capacity/water amount, such as “We tested two containers to find which held more water.” Award 1 mark for topic and 1 mark for purpose; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Formulating an informative oral introduction.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Evaluating two presentations

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “Speaker A gives events in order but whispers and uses ‘thing’ for every object. Speaker B is audible and names the ramp, car and box, but gives the result before explaining the test.”

**Question:** Identify one strength and two improvements for each speaker. Then choose which single improvement is most urgent and justify it.

**Marking key:** 1 mark each for accurate analysis of A’s order, volume and vague vocabulary (3 marks); 1 mark each for B’s volume/vocabulary and sequence issue (3 marks); 1 mark for a reasoned priority tied to audience understanding; 7 marks total.

**Coverage:** Analysing content organisation, vocabulary and voice across oral texts.

### E08 — Presenting an observation with audience checks

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Situation: “A snail moved across a tray, leaving a shiny trail. It took about two minutes to reach a lettuce leaf.”

**Question:** Deliver a brief report with an opening, ordered observations, time detail and result. Afterward, explain how you checked that peers could hear and follow it.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for topic opening, 1 mark for movement/trail observation, 1 mark for time, 1 mark for reaching lettuce/result, 1 mark for order/cohesion, 1 mark for suitable volume, and 1 mark for a valid audience check such as facing peers, watching/listening for response or asking if heard; 7 marks total.

**Coverage:** Delivering and reflecting on a complete audience-aware spoken report.
