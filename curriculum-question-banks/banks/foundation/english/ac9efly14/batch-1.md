# AC9EFLY14 — Pass 1 question bank

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Reading the

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Show the word **the** by itself for 3 seconds.

**Question:** Read this word without sounding it out letter by letter.

**Correct answer:** **the**.

**Coverage:** Automatically recognising a common high-frequency word.

### P02 — Writing my from memory

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say: “Write the word **my**, as in ‘my hat’.” Do not display the word.

**Question:** Write the whole word from memory.

**Correct answer:** **my**.

**Coverage:** Recalling the spelling of a high-frequency word.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Finding and in a sentence

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Show: “I packed an apple **and** a pear.” Read the sentence aloud once.

**Question:** Which word joins the two things that were packed?

A. packed

B. apple

C. and

D. pear

**Correct answer:** C — **and** joins “an apple” with “a pear”.

**Coverage:** Reading a high-frequency word in a meaningful sentence.

### P04 — Matching is and go to meanings

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Show the words **is** and **go**, then read: (a) “We ___ now.” (b) “The cup ___ full.”

**Question:** Place each word in the sentence where it makes sense, then read both completed sentences.

**Marking key:** (a) **go**; (b) **is** (1 mark each); reads both sentences accurately (1 mark). 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Recognising familiar high-frequency words through syntax and meaning.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Completing a classroom message

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Show: “___ can go to the mat.” Say that the speaker is talking about themself.

**Question:** Which high-frequency word completes the message?

A. Is

B. It

C. In

D. I

**Correct answer:** D — The capital word **I** names the speaker and completes “I can go to the mat.”

**Coverage:** Selecting a high-frequency word using sentence meaning and grammar.

### P06 — Reading familiar names on labels

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Show three labels: the child’s first name, **Mum**, and the familiar class-character name **Sam**. Substitute another locally familiar family word if needed.

**Question:** Read each label. Tell one clue that helps you recognise each word quickly.

**Marking key:** Reads all 3 familiar words (1 mark each); gives one valid clue for at least 2 words, such as first letter, word shape, known letter pattern or repeated classroom exposure (1 mark). 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Reading personally and instructionally familiar words.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Repairing mixed-up high-frequency words

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Show: “Teh dog si on the rug.” Explain that two common words have their letters mixed up.

**Question:** Find and correct both words, rewrite the sentence and read it aloud to check that it makes sense.

**Marking key:** Corrects **Teh** to **The** (1 mark); corrects **si** to **is** (1 mark); rewrites “The dog is on the rug.” with sentence capital and full stop preserved (1 mark); reads it accurately and confirms meaning (1 mark). 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Proofreading high-frequency words in connected text.

### P08 — Creating with known words

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Provide the word cards **I, can, see, the, dog** and a full-stop card.

**Question:** Arrange at least four word cards to make a complete sentence, copy it and read it back. Explain how you checked the order.

**Marking key:** Constructs a meaningful sentence such as “I can see the dog.” (2 marks); copies the selected words accurately (1 mark); reads the sentence (1 mark); gives a valid order/meaning check (1 mark). 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Reading, ordering and writing high-frequency and familiar words in a sentence.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Reading you in a note

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Show the note: “Thank **you**.” Point to the bold word.

**Question:** Read the bold word, then read the whole note.

**Correct answer:** **you**; “Thank you.”

**Coverage:** Recognising a high-frequency word inside a familiar message.

### E02 — Writing go independently

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say: “Write **go**, as in ‘We go home’.” Do not provide letter cards.

**Question:** Write the word from memory.

**Correct answer:** **go**.

**Coverage:** Independently recalling a high-frequency spelling.

### E03 — Choosing the correct familiar word

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Show a picture of a school bag and read the options as words only if needed.

**Question:** Which word is the correct label for the picture?

A. bag

B. big

C. bed

D. bug

**Correct answer:** A — **bag** is the familiar word that names the pictured object.

**Coverage:** Reading a familiar decodable word and checking it against meaning.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Filling two gaps with high-frequency words

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Show the words **we** and **are** beside: “___ ___ ready.”

**Question:** Put both words in the correct order and read the completed sentence.

**Marking key:** Builds “**We are ready.**” in that order (2 marks) and reads it accurately (1 mark). 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Sequencing high-frequency words within a sentence frame.

### E05 — Distinguishing look-alike words

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read: “This belongs to me. It is ___ hat.” Show the options without naming them.

**Question:** Which word completes the sentence?

A. me

B. my

C. may

D. am

**Correct answer:** B — “It is **my** hat” shows ownership and is grammatically complete.

**Coverage:** Discriminating a target high-frequency word from visually similar words.

### E06 — Writing a familiar person’s name

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Ask the child to choose the first name of a familiar person. If they prefer, supply the class name **Mila** orally.

**Question:** Write the name, beginning with a capital letter, then read it aloud.

**Marking key:** Writes the chosen familiar name recognisably (1 mark); uses an initial capital and lower-case letters where appropriate (1 mark); reads the same name (1 mark). 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Accurately reading and writing a personally familiar proper name.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Editing a short message

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Show: “i can see teh red hen”.

**Question:** Correct the two high-frequency-word errors, add the missing sentence punctuation, rewrite the message and read your final version.

**Marking key:** Changes **i** to **I** (1 mark); changes **teh** to **the** (1 mark); adds a full stop and maintains the initial capital (1 mark); rewrites the remaining words accurately (1 mark); reads “I can see the red hen.” correctly (1 mark). 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Applying stored word knowledge during sentence-level editing.

### E08 — Using a small known-word bank

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say: “Write a message about a pet.” Display only the required high-frequency words **this, is, my**; do not display the child’s chosen pet word.

**Question:** Use all three displayed words and one familiar animal word to write a complete message. Read it aloud and point to each required word.

**Marking key:** Uses **This is my** in a logical sentence (2 marks); writes a suitable familiar animal word, such as **cat**, **dog** or **fish** (1 mark); includes an initial capital and end punctuation (1 mark); reads the message while accurately locating the required words (1 mark). 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Integrating high-frequency and familiar words in independent text creation.
