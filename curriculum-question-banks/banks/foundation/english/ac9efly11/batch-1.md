# AC9EFLY11 — Pass 1 question bank

Letter sounds are assessed using their most common sound in familiar words and
the learner’s Australian English pronunciation. Avoid adding a schwa to isolated
consonant sounds.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Naming mixed-case letters

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Show one at a time in random order: **a, D, b, C, A, c, d, B**.

**Question:** Name each letter, regardless of case.

**Marking key:** a/A, d/D, b/B, c/C, a/A, c/C, d/D, b/B. Award 1 mark per correct name; 8 marks total.

**Coverage:** Naming upper- and lower-case forms for a–d.

### P02 — Matching case pairs

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Show upper-case **E, F, G, H** and shuffled lower-case **h, e, g, f**.

**Question:** Match each upper-case letter to its lower-case partner.

**Marking key:** E–e, F–f, G–g, H–h. Award 1 mark each; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Recognising case pairs for e–h.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Common sounds for i–l

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Show **i, J, k, L** in random order.

**Question:** Name each letter and say its most common sound in **insect, jam, kite, leg**.

**Marking key:** i /i/, j /j/, k /k/, l /l/. Award 1 mark for each name and 1 mark for each sound; 8 marks total.

**Coverage:** Linking letter names and common phonemes for i–l.

### P04 — Choosing words for m–p

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Show **M, n, O, p** and say **moon, nest, octopus, pig** in shuffled order.

**Question:** Match each letter to the word beginning with its common sound, then say the sound.

**Marking key:** M–moon /m/; n–nest /n/; O–octopus /o/; p–pig /p/. Award 1 mark per correct match and 1 per sound; 8 marks total.

**Coverage:** Applying common initial sounds for m–p.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Sorting q–t by initial sound

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Show **q, R, s, T** and picture/name choices **queen, rabbit, sun, tent**.

**Question:** Match, name each letter and give its sound at the start of the word.

**Marking key:** q–queen /kw/ in the common **qu** spelling, R–rabbit /r/, s–sun /s/, T–tent /t/. Award 1 mark per match/name pair and 1 per sound; 8 marks total. Explain that q commonly works with u.

**Coverage:** Connecting q–t letter forms with common word-initial sounds.

### P06 — Recognising u–x in two cases

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Show **U u, V v, W w, X x**.

**Question:** Name each pair and give a word that demonstrates its common sound. For x, use a word where /ks/ is heard clearly.

**Marking key:** 1 mark each for names u, v, w, x; 1 mark each for valid sound examples such as umbrella /u/, van /v/, web /w/, box /ks/; 8 marks total.

**Coverage:** Recognising case pairs and applying common sounds for u–x.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Distinguishing y and z

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Show **Y, y, Z, z** plus the words **yellow, zip, yes, zoo**.

**Question:** Sort the words by beginning sound, name both letters in each case and explain how you knew.

**Marking key:** Y/y: yellow, yes, /y/ (3 marks); Z/z: zip, zoo, /z/ (3 marks); 1 mark for explaining comparison of first phoneme with letter; 7 marks total.

**Coverage:** Discriminating and explaining the final two alphabetic letter–sound links.

### P08 — Alphabet case-and-sound audit

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Present all 26 lower-case letters in a random 5×6 grid with four blank cells. Ask the child to choose six, including at least one from a–f, g–m, n–s and t–z.

**Question:** For each chosen letter, name it, write or point to its upper-case partner and give its common sound in a word.

**Marking key:** For each of six letters: 1 mark for name, 1 for correct case partner and 1 for a valid common-sound example; 18 marks total. Record unselected letters for later diagnostic checking rather than assuming mastery.

**Coverage:** Integrating recognition, case matching and sound knowledge across the alphabet.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Letter names from lower case

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Show **r, b, y, h, n, e** in random order.

**Question:** Name each lower-case letter shown.

**Marking key:** r, b, y, h, n, e. Award 1 mark each; 6 marks total.

**Coverage:** Rapid lower-case letter-name recognition across the alphabet.

### E02 — Letter names from upper case

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Show **Q, L, A, X, M, G** in random order.

**Question:** Name each upper-case letter shown.

**Marking key:** q, l, a, x, m, g. Award 1 mark each; 6 marks total.

**Coverage:** Rapid upper-case letter-name recognition across varied forms.

### E03 — Common consonant sounds

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Show **f, S, d, V, c, j**.

**Question:** Give the most common sound for each in **fish, sun, dog, van, cat, jam**.

**Marking key:** /f/, /s/, /d/, /v/, /k/, /j/. Award 1 mark each; 6 marks total.

**Coverage:** Retrieving common consonant phonemes from graphemes.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Completing case pairs

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Show **B–b, T–__, __–k, P–p, __–w, I–__**.

**Question:** Fill or say the four missing letter forms.

**Marking key:** T–t, K–k, W–w, I–i. Award 1 mark each; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Retrieving case partners in both directions.

### E05 — Selecting by sound, not letter name

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say **goat** and show G, J, D, K.

**Question:** Which letter represents the first sound in **goat**?

A. K

B. J

C. D

D. G

**Correct answer:** D — G commonly represents /g/ at the start of goat.

**Coverage:** Mapping an initial phoneme to the corresponding grapheme.

### E06 — Finding a final x sound

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say **fox, fan, fog, fin**.

**Question:** Which word demonstrates the common /ks/ sound represented by x, and where is the sound?

**Marking key:** Fox (1 mark), at the end (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Recognising a letter’s common sound in final position.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correcting confused letter–sound links

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “A learner says: c always sounds /s/ in **cat**; g sounds /j/ in **goat**; and q works alone for /kw/ in **queen**.”

**Question:** Correct each statement using the target words and explain the usual letter pattern for queen.

**Marking key:** c in cat = /k/ (1 mark); g in goat = /g/ (1 mark); queen begins with qu representing /kw/ (2 marks); identifies that q commonly appears with u in familiar English words (1 mark); clear oral comparison (1 mark); 6 marks total.

**Coverage:** Analysing and repairing common letter–sound misconceptions.

### E08 — Comprehensive diagnostic sample

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Present eight random letters selected after, not before, the practice session: four upper-case and four lower-case, spanning the alphabet. Include at least one visually dissimilar case pair (such as G/g) and one vowel.

**Question:** For each, name it, provide the other case form and say its common sound in a word.

**Marking key:** Per letter: 1 mark name, 1 mark case partner, 1 mark valid common-sound word; 24 marks total. Record exact errors by letter and response type to guide further teaching.

**Coverage:** Sampling integrated alphabet knowledge under independent conditions.
