# AC9EFLY10 — Pass 1 question bank

These are oral phonological-awareness tasks. Do not show written target words
unless an approved adjustment requires print.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Counting words in a sentence

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Say naturally, “The dog ran home.” Provide counters.

**Question:** Move one counter for each word you hear, then tell the number of words.

**Marking key:** The / dog / ran / home = four words. Award 1 mark for one-to-one segmentation and 1 mark for count; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Segmenting a spoken sentence into word units.

### P02 — Blending three phonemes

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say /m/ /a/ /t/ with a short even pause between phonemes.

**Question:** Blend the sounds to make one word.

**Marking key:** “mat”. Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Orally blending a three-phoneme single-syllable word.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Segmenting a CVC word

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say **fish** naturally; clarify that the task is sounds, not letters.

**Question:** Stretch **fish** and say each separate sound.

**Marking key:** /f/ /i/ /sh/ — three phonemes. Award 1 mark for correct sequence and 1 mark for treating /sh/ as one sound; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Segmenting a single-syllable word containing a digraph phoneme.

### P04 — Isolating a medial vowel

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say **sun**.

**Question:** What sound is in the middle of **sun**?

**Marking key:** /u/ (the short vowel in Australian English). Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Isolating a medial phoneme.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Adding an initial phoneme

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say, “Start with **at**. Add /s/ to the beginning.”

**Question:** What new word do you make?

**Marking key:** “sat”. Award 1 mark for the new word and 1 mark for explaining/performing that /s/ was placed first; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Manipulating a word through initial-phoneme addition.

### P06 — Deleting a final phoneme

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say, “Say **farm**. Now say it again without the final /m/ sound.”

**Question:** Say the word left after removing the final sound from **seat**.

**Marking key:** “far” in non-rhotic Australian English. Award 1 mark. Ensure the task is administered in the learner’s dialect; accept the locally appropriate phonological result.

**Coverage:** Deleting a final phoneme from a single-syllable spoken word.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Substituting one phoneme

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “Change the first sound in **map** from /m/ to /t/.”

**Question:** Say the new word, then segment both the old and new words to show what stayed and what changed.

**Marking key:** “tap” (1 mark); map = /m/ /a/ /p/ (1 mark); tap = /t/ /a/ /p/ (1 mark); explains medial/final sounds stayed while initial changed (1 mark); 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Performing and analysing initial-phoneme substitution.

### P08 — Following a phoneme-change chain

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Give one direction at a time: “Say **pin**. Change /p/ to /t/. Now change the final /n/ to /p/.”

**Question:** Say each word in the chain and explain the two changes.

**Marking key:** pin → tin → tip. Award 1 mark for each word in sequence (3 marks) and 1 mark for accurately explaining initial then final substitution; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Maintaining and manipulating phonemes across a two-step word chain.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Sentence word boundaries

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Say, “My red kite fell.”

**Question:** Tap once for each word and give the count.

**Marking key:** My / red / kite / fell = four. Award 1 mark for segmentation and 1 mark for count; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Independently segmenting a four-word spoken sentence.

### E02 — Oral phoneme blending

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say /r/ /u/ /g/ with even pauses.

**Question:** Blend the sounds.

**Marking key:** “rug”. Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Blending a regular CVC word.

### E03 — Initial-phoneme isolation

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say **chip**.

**Question:** What is the first sound?

**Marking key:** /ch/ as one phoneme. Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Isolating an initial affricate phoneme represented by a digraph in print.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Segmenting four phonemes

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say **stop**.

**Question:** Say every sound in order and give the number of sounds.

**Marking key:** /s/ /t/ /o/ /p/, four phonemes. Award 1 mark for ordered segmentation and 1 mark for count; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Segmenting a single-syllable word with an initial consonant cluster.

### E05 — Deleting an initial phoneme

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say, “Say **smile**. Now say it without the first /s/ sound.”

**Question:** What word remains?

**Marking key:** “mile”. Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Removing an initial phoneme from a consonant cluster.

### E06 — Adding a final phoneme

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say, “Start with **row**. Add /d/ to the end.”

**Question:** What new word is formed?

**Marking key:** “road” in Australian English. Award 1 mark for the new word and 1 mark for locating /d/ at the end; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Final-phoneme addition to form a new spoken word.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Comparing two segmentations

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say **ship** and **slip**. A learner segments both as three sounds.

**Question:** Segment each word correctly and explain why one has three phonemes and the other has four.

**Marking key:** ship = /sh/ /i/ /p/, three (2 marks); slip = /s/ /l/ /i/ /p/, four (2 marks); explains /sh/ is one phoneme while /s/ and /l/ remain separate in the cluster (1 mark); 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Analysing phoneme count across digraph and cluster structures.

### E08 — Solving and explaining a word ladder

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Give the starting word **cat** and goal **dog**. Say, “Change one sound at a time. Every step must be a real word.” Offer the number of steps: cat → ___ → ___ → dog.

**Question:** Complete one valid chain and state which sound changes at each step.

**Marking key:** One valid chain is cat → cot → dot → dog. Award 1 mark for each correct intermediate word (2 marks), 1 mark for goal word, and 1 mark each for explaining /a/→/o/, /k/→/d/, /t/→/g/ substitutions; 6 marks total. Accept any valid one-phoneme-at-a-time chain in the learner’s dialect.

**Coverage:** Applying phoneme substitution strategically across a multi-step chain.
