# AC9EFLY01 — Pass 1 question bank

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Story or information?

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the description and options aloud.

**Question:** A book tells how a made-up rabbit travels to the moon and talks to stars. What kind of text is it mainly?

A. A set of safety instructions

B. A factual weather report

C. A story

D. A timetable

**Correct answer:** C — It uses imagined characters and events to tell a story.

**Coverage:** Identifying a familiar imaginative text.

### P02 — Purpose of an information page

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say, “A page titled ‘How Bees Make Honey’ has labelled pictures and facts.”

**Question:** What is the page’s main purpose?

**Marking key:** To inform/teach/explain how bees make honey. Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Naming the purpose of a simple informative text.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Matching text and purpose

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Read each text and purpose aloud.

**Question:** Match: (a) a picture story about a runaway sock, (b) a poster showing handwashing steps, (c) a card saying “Happy Birthday”. Purposes: entertain with a story, instruct how to do something, send a greeting.

**Marking key:** (a) entertain with a story; (b) instruct; (c) send a greeting. Award 1 mark each; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Connecting familiar text types to communicative purposes.

### P04 — Using clues to identify purpose

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read: “The page has the heading ‘Koalas’, a labelled body picture and three fact boxes.” Then read the options.

**Question:** What is this text most likely trying to do?

A. List the times buses leave

B. Invite a koala to a party

C. Tell an imaginary adventure with no facts

D. Give information about koalas

**Correct answer:** D — The heading, labels and fact boxes signal an informative purpose.

**Coverage:** Inferring purpose from text features.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Same topic, different purpose

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Describe Text A: “A story in which a penguin learns to dance.” Describe Text B: “A fact sheet showing where penguins live and what they eat.”

**Question:** How are the purposes different?

**Marking key:** Text A mainly entertains/tells imagined events (1 mark); Text B informs/teaches facts (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Comparing literary and informative purposes on one topic.

### P06 — Choosing a useful text

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the situation and options aloud.

**Question:** A child wants to learn the correct steps for planting a seed. Which text would best meet that purpose?

A. Numbered instructions with pictures of each step

B. A fantasy tale about a singing flower

C. A birthday invitation

D. A rhyme about windy weather

**Correct answer:** A — Step-by-step instructions are designed to guide an action.

**Coverage:** Selecting a text according to an information need.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — When a title misleads

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “A book is titled ‘All About Trains’, but every page tells a made-up adventure about one talking train. It has no facts or labels.”

**Question:** Is the book mainly a story or an informative text? Explain two clues and suggest a clearer title.

**Marking key:** Identifies it as a story (1 mark); gives up to 2 clues such as talking character, imagined adventure or absence of facts; gives a suitable story title (1 mark); 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Resolving conflict between title and internal purpose clues.

### P08 — Planning for a purpose

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “Your class wants younger children to recognise three playground birds.”

**Question:** Plan a text for this purpose. Name its type, two features it should include and why those features help.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for an informative form such as chart, booklet or fact page; up to 2 marks for useful features such as clear photos/drawings, names, labels or simple identifying facts; 1 mark for linking features to bird recognition; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Designing a familiar informative text around audience and purpose.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Purpose of a narrative

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read: “A book follows a nervous cloud through an imaginary sky race.” Then read the options.

**Question:** What is its most likely main purpose?

A. To show tomorrow’s exact weather

B. To entertain by telling a story

C. To instruct readers to build a kite

D. To record class attendance

**Correct answer:** B — Imagined character and events signal narrative entertainment.

**Coverage:** Identifying literary purpose from a synopsis.

### E02 — Identifying an instructional text

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the options aloud.

**Question:** Which text is mainly designed to tell someone how to do something?

A. “Dear Grandma, thank you for visiting.”

B. “The dragon sneezed blue sparks.”

C. “First fold the paper; next press the crease.”

D. “The concert starts at six.”

**Correct answer:** C — It uses ordered directions to guide an action.

**Coverage:** Recognising instructional purpose from wording.

### E03 — Naming a purpose

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Describe “a school notice listing the date, time and place of a picnic”.

**Question:** Why was the notice made?

**Marking key:** To inform families/students about when and where the picnic happens. Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Expressing the purpose of a functional notice.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Checking whether a text meets its purpose

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say, “A page promises to explain how to care for a guinea pig, but it only shows one decorative picture and no information.”

**Question:** Does the page meet its purpose? Name one addition it needs.

**Marking key:** No (1 mark); suggests relevant information such as food, water, shelter, cleaning or handling guidance (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Evaluating whether content fulfils an informative purpose.

### E05 — Choosing between two sources

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “Text A is a funny story about a fish wearing shoes. Text B is a labelled diagram of fish fins and gills.”

**Question:** Which would you use to learn about fish body parts? Explain why Text B is more useful and what Text A is useful for.

**Marking key:** Chooses Text B (1 mark); connects labels/diagram to learning body parts (1 mark); identifies Text A’s storytelling/entertainment purpose (1 mark); 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Selecting and contrasting texts according to purpose.

### E06 — Inferring audience and purpose

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “Please return your library books by Friday” on a notice beside the classroom door.

**Question:** Who is the likely audience, and what should they do?

**Marking key:** Students/families/class members who borrowed books (1 mark); return the books by Friday (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Inferring audience and intended response from a functional text.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Analysing a mixed-purpose book

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “A book tells a made-up story about a seed called Sam. After each story page, a small box gives a true fact about seed growth.”

**Question:** Explain the book’s two purposes. Give one clue for each and describe how a reader might use the two parts differently.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for story/entertainment purpose; 1 mark for the imagined character/event clue; 1 mark for informing/teaching purpose; 1 mark for true fact-box clue; 1 mark for explaining different use, such as following the adventure versus learning facts; 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Analysing multiple purposes within a hybrid text.

### E08 — Creating two texts for one event

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “The class has watched caterpillars become butterflies.”

**Question:** Plan one story text and one informative text about the experience. Give each a title, state its purpose and name two content features that would make the purposes clearly different.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for a suitable story title/purpose; 1 mark for imaginative story feature; 1 mark for suitable informative title/purpose; 1 mark for factual/organising feature; 1 mark for a clear contrast between how the texts treat the experience; 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Designing contrasting literary and informative texts from shared subject matter.
