# AC9EFLY09 — Pass 1 question bank

These are spoken-language tasks. Do not show written words unless an adjustment
requires it, because print may change what is being assessed.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Hearing a rhyme pair

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say the options; do not show them.

**Question:** Which word rhymes with **cake**?

A. fish

B. cup

C. lake

D. moon

**Correct answer:** C — “Cake” and “lake” share the /ake/ rime.

**Coverage:** Recognising rhyme in spoken words.

### P02 — Counting syllables

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Say **banana** naturally.

**Question:** Clap once for each syllable and tell how many parts you heard.

**Marking key:** ba-na-na, three claps/syllables. Award 1 mark for segmentation and 1 mark for count; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Identifying syllable units in a multisyllabic spoken word.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Recognising alliteration

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say each phrase clearly; do not show print.

**Question:** Which phrase repeats the same beginning sound most strongly?

A. jump and clap

B. big red kite

C. moon over water

D. six sleepy seals

**Correct answer:** D — The three content words begin with /s/.

**Coverage:** Recognising an alliterative initial-phoneme pattern.

### P04 — Isolating the first phoneme

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say **fish**, stretching only if needed.

**Question:** What is the first sound in **fish**?

**Marking key:** /f/. Award 1 mark. Accept the sound, not the letter name.

**Coverage:** Isolating an initial phoneme.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Generating a rhyme

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say, “Tell me a real or pretend word that rhymes with **log**.”

**Question:** Give the rhyming word and repeat the pair.

**Marking key:** A valid rhyme such as dog, fog, frog (allow onset cluster) or nonsense word **zog**. Award 1 mark for matching rime and 1 mark for accurately repeating the pair; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Generating a spoken rhyme independently.

### P06 — Segmenting and recombining syllables

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say **sunflower**.

**Question:** Break the word into spoken syllables, then blend the parts back into the whole word.

**Marking key:** sun-flow-er (regional realisation may compress the final sequence), followed by “sunflower”. Award 1 mark for a plausible spoken syllable segmentation and 1 mark for recombination; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Manipulating syllable units in a compound word.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Finding two different odd words

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say **sun, sock, moon, sand**.

**Question:** Which word does not begin like the others? Then listen for ending sounds: do any two words rhyme? Explain both answers.

**Marking key:** **moon** is the initial-sound odd word because the others begin /s/ (2 marks); **sun** and **moon** do not rhyme, and no pair in the set forms a full end rhyme (1 mark for accurate rhyme judgement and 1 mark for an ending-sound explanation); 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Comparing the same word set by initial phoneme and rhyme criteria.

### P08 — Creating a sound-pattern line

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “Make a short silly line with three words beginning with /m/, then add a final word that rhymes with **day**.”

**Question:** Perform your line and identify the /m/ words and rhyme word.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for three meaningful or playful /m/-initial words, 1 mark for a valid rhyme with day, 1 mark for a coherent/performance-ready line, and 1 mark for correctly identifying the sound features; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Generating combined alliteration and rhyme constraints in speech.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Rhyme recognition

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say the target **star** and options orally.

**Question:** Which word rhymes with **star**?

A. car

B. sit

C. tree

D. boat

**Correct answer:** A — “Star” and “car” share the ending /ar/ sound in Australian English.

**Coverage:** Independently matching spoken rhymes.

### E02 — Syllable count

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say **computer** naturally.

**Question:** Tap its syllables and give the number.

**Marking key:** com-pu-ter, three syllables in standard Australian pronunciation. Award 1 mark for segmentation and 1 mark for count; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Segmenting a three-syllable spoken word.

### E03 — Final phoneme

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say **map**.

**Question:** What sound do you hear at the end?

**Marking key:** /p/. Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Isolating a final phoneme.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Completing alliteration

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say “Daring dogs dig ___” and options orally.

**Question:** Which word best continues both the /d/ sound pattern and the sentence meaning?

A. softly

B. deep

C. moon

D. apples

**Correct answer:** B — “Deep” begins /d/ and sensibly describes digging.

**Coverage:** Selecting for phonological pattern and semantic fit.

### E05 — Generating two rhymes

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say **light**.

**Question:** Give two different words that rhyme with **light**.

**Marking key:** Any two valid rhymes such as night, bright, kite, right, sight. Award 1 mark each; 2 marks total. Nonsense rhymes are accepted if phonologically correct.

**Coverage:** Producing multiple members of a rhyme family.

### E06 — Blending syllables

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say the parts with a clear pause: **rain — bow**.

**Question:** Blend the parts into one word, then tell how many syllables the whole word has.

**Marking key:** “Rainbow”, two syllables. Award 1 mark for blending and 1 mark for count; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Blending and quantifying syllables in a compound.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Sorting by two sound rules

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say **cat, cap, dog, log, cup, frog**. Repeat once.

**Question:** Make one group of words beginning /k/ and a different group containing words that rhyme with **dog**. Explain why **cat** and **cap** belong together in one rule but do not rhyme.

**Marking key:** /k/ group: cat, cap, cup (1 mark); dog-rhyme group: dog, log, frog (1 mark); identifies shared initial /k/ for cat/cap (1 mark); explains their final/rime sounds differ /at/ and /ap/ (1 mark); recognises sorting rules examine different word positions (1 mark); 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Classifying and explaining onset versus rhyme relationships.

### E08 — Designing and explaining a sound game

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “Create a four-word spoken sound game: two words must rhyme, and the other two must begin with the same sound but not rhyme.”

**Question:** Say your four words, place them into the two groups and explain each rule.

**Marking key:** 1 mark each for two valid rhyming words; 1 mark each for two words sharing an initial phoneme but with different rimes; 1 mark for correct grouping; 1 mark for accurate explanation of both rules; 6 marks total.

**Coverage:** Generating and metalinguistically explaining contrasting sound patterns.
