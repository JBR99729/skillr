# AC9EFLE04 — Pass 1 question bank

All verse stimuli in this bank are newly authored for SkillrHub.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Hearing rhyme

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read each pair clearly without showing print first.

**Question:** Which pair of words rhymes?

A. moon / map

B. moon / spoon

C. spoon / sun

D. map / moon

**Correct answer:** B — “Moon” and “spoon” share the same ending sound.

**Coverage:** Recognising an end-rhyme sound pattern.

### P02 — Finding repetition

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Chant: “Tap on the table, tap on the tin, tap when the drumming is ready to begin.”

**Question:** Which word is repeated to make a sound pattern?

**Marking key:** “Tap”. Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Identifying lexical repetition in a chant.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Completing a rhyme

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read: “A frog on a log / waved hello to a ___.” Then read the options.

**Question:** Which word completes the strongest rhyme?

A. fish

B. duck

C. dog

D. snail

**Correct answer:** C — “Dog” rhymes with “frog” and “log” and fits the sentence.

**Coverage:** Selecting a meaningfully and phonologically suitable rhyming word.

### P04 — Copying a beat pattern

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Speak and clap the bold beats: “**STAMP** like a horse, **TIP** like a toe.” Repeat once.

**Question:** Copy the words and beat pattern, then replace **horse** with another animal while keeping the two strong beats.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for accurately copying the two-beat pattern and 1 mark for a coherent substitution delivered with the pattern, such as “STAMP like a bear, TIP like a toe”; 2 marks total. Rhyme is not required.

**Coverage:** Replicating rhythmic stress while adapting one element.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Extending an alliterative line

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say, “Busy beetles bounce.”

**Question:** Add one word beginning with the same /b/ sound to extend the playful sound pattern.

**Marking key:** Adds a meaningful /b/ word, such as “below”, “bravely” or “backwards”, and repeats the complete line. Award 1 mark for initial-sound match and 1 mark for a sensible line; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Replicating an initial-sound pattern in original language.

### P06 — Choosing a line with matching rhythm

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Clap a steady four-beat pattern while reading “Rain on the roof, drum-drum-drum-drum.” Then read each choice at a natural pace.

**Question:** Which second line best echoes the short, repeated beat pattern?

A. The weather report is on the noticeboard.

B. Yesterday we discovered an enormous umbrella.

C. A butterfly rested silently near us.

D. Feet on the floor, stomp-stomp-stomp-stomp.

**Correct answer:** D — Its structure and four repeated sound words mirror the first line.

**Coverage:** Comparing rhythmic structure across lines.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Repairing a broken rhyme pattern

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “Bright little star / seen from afar / glow through the night / over my shoe.”

**Question:** The first two lines rhyme. Change the final words of one or both last lines so they also form a rhyme and still make sense.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for a true end-rhyme pair, 1 mark for two coherent revised lines, and 1 mark for identifying the matching ending sounds. Example: “glow through the **night** / shimmer so **bright**”; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Diagnosing and restoring an end-rhyme pattern.

### P08 — Creating a patterned chant

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “Make a two-line chant about packing up. Use a repeated phrase or beat and one pair of rhyming words.”

**Question:** Perform your chant, then point out the repeated part and the rhyme.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for two connected lines, 1 mark for a deliberate repeated phrase/beat, 1 mark for a valid rhyme, and 1 mark for correctly identifying both pattern features; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Composing and explaining combined rhythm and rhyme patterns.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Identifying the rhyming line

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read: “A crab in the sand / held a shell in its hand.” Then read the options.

**Question:** Which words create the rhyme?

A. sand / hand

B. crab / shell

C. the / its

D. held / in

**Correct answer:** A — They share the same final sound.

**Coverage:** Locating end-rhyme words in verse.

### E02 — Counting repeated sounds

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say slowly, “Flip, flap, flutter, the flags fly free.”

**Question:** What beginning sound is repeated across many words?

**Marking key:** /f/. Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Identifying an alliterative phoneme.

### E03 — Recognising a refrain

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Chant: “Sweep to the left—clean it up! Sweep to the right—clean it up! Gather the leaves—clean it up!”

**Question:** Which group of words acts as the repeated refrain?

**Marking key:** “Clean it up!” Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Identifying a recurring phrase in a chant.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Supplying a sensible rhyme

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “Soft is the light / that glows in the ___.”

**Question:** Add a word that rhymes with **light** and makes sense.

**Marking key:** “night” is the strongest expected answer. Accept “bright” only if the child revises the grammar, such as “that glows warm and bright.” Award 1 mark for rhyme and 1 mark for sentence meaning; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Generating a constrained rhyme that preserves meaning.

### E05 — Replicating a line structure

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Read: “Slow goes the snail, low goes the trail.”

**Question:** Create a new two-part line that copies the pattern “___ goes the ___, ___ goes the ___.”

**Marking key:** 1 mark for preserving the repeated syntactic/rhythmic frame, 1 mark for two coherent clauses, and 1 mark for a sound link such as rhyme or repeated initial sound; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Replicating combined grammatical and sound structure.

### E06 — Comparing two performances

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Perform “Tiptoe, tiptoe, quiet and slow” first with even soft beats, then with random loud pauses.

**Question:** Which performance better matches the words and rhythm? Explain one reason.

**Marking key:** Chooses the even soft performance (1 mark) because steady/soft delivery matches tiptoeing, quietness or slow movement (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Evaluating how performance realises verbal rhythm and meaning.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Analysing a four-line pattern

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “Wake, little seed, / wake in the ground. / Stretch, little seed, / without any sound.”

**Question:** Identify two sound or rhythm patterns and explain how each helps join the lines.

**Marking key:** Up to 2 marks for identifying features such as repeated “little seed”, repeated imperative structure, similar beat, or ground/sound rhyme; up to 2 marks for explaining how each creates expectation/cohesion/musicality; 1 mark for referring accurately to words from the verse; 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Analysing multiple patterning devices in original verse.

### E08 — Adapting without losing the pattern

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “Clap when the red kite rises high; / tap when the blue kite crosses the sky.”

**Question:** Adapt both lines so they are about two boats, while keeping the action cue, similar rhythm and end rhyme. Explain what you preserved.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for two boat-focused lines, 1 mark for distinct action cues, 1 mark for a valid end rhyme, 1 mark for broadly parallel rhythm/structure, and 1 mark for explaining at least two preserved pattern features; 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Transforming content while retaining literary sound structure.
