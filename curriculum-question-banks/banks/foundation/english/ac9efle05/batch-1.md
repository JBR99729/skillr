# AC9EFLE05 — Pass 1 question bank

All story stimuli in this bank are newly authored for SkillrHub.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Retelling in event order

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Read: “A beetle loses its shiny button, follows a trail through the grass and finds the button in a magpie’s nest.” Then read the event cards in mixed order.

**Question:** Put these retelling parts in order: (a) finds the button, (b) loses the button, (c) follows the trail.

**Correct answer:** (b), (c), (a). Award 1 mark for the complete order.

**Coverage:** Preserving event sequence in a simple retell.

### P02 — Keeping the important event

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read: “A lonely lighthouse flashes a pattern that guides a lost boat safely to shore.” Then read the options.

**Question:** Which event must be included in a short retell?

A. Boats can carry ropes.

B. The lighthouse is painted every summer.

C. The lighthouse guides the lost boat with its flashing pattern.

D. The shore has many kinds of sand.

**Correct answer:** C — It is the central action and resolution of the story.

**Coverage:** Selecting essential rather than unrelated information for a retell.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Oral beginning, middle and end

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Read twice: “Tavi plants a mystery seed. Overnight it grows into a vine. Tavi climbs up and finds a tiny cloud garden.”

**Question:** Retell the story using the words **first**, **next** and **finally**.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for each accurately sequenced event and 1 mark for using the three sequence words appropriately; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Producing an organised oral retell from a short literary text.

### P04 — Choosing performance details

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “In a story, a tired moon yawns, dims its light and asks the stars to sing.”

**Question:** If you acted as the moon, name one movement and one voice choice that would show the character clearly.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for a relevant movement such as stretching/yawning/drooping and 1 mark for a suitable voice such as slow, sleepy or soft; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Translating character description into performance choices.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Retelling through three images

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Read: “A crab discovers a bell under the sand, rings it, and calls a school of fish to a seaside concert.”

**Question:** Plan or draw three pictures that retell the beginning, middle and ending. Explain what happens in each.

**Marking key:** 1 mark each for images representing discovery, ringing/calling and concert ending; 1 mark for an oral explanation that links the images in sequence; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Representing narrative sequence visually.

### P06 — Adapting the setting

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “Original: A rabbit crosses a forest to deliver a warm scarf to a friend.”

**Question:** Adapt the story so it happens in a snowy town. Change two details but keep the main purpose of the journey.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for retaining delivery/helping-friend purpose, up to 2 marks for coherent setting changes such as transport, obstacles, clothing or locations, and 1 mark for a connected adapted account; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Changing setting details while preserving a story’s central action.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Deciding what may change

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “A small robot cannot reach a high shelf. A tall robot helps, and together they share the toy they retrieve.”

**Question:** You want to adapt the characters into animals. Name two details you can change and two story ideas you should keep so the adaptation remains recognisable.

**Marking key:** Up to 2 marks for changeable details such as animal species, setting, object or dialogue; up to 2 marks for retained ideas such as reach problem, help/cooperation and sharing resolution; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Distinguishing adaptable surface details from core narrative structure.

### P08 — Planning a group performance

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “A wind-up bird stops singing. A mouse turns its key, and the bird sings a thank-you tune.”

**Question:** Plan a short performance with roles, three actions and one line of dialogue. Explain how your plan retells all key events.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for allocating bird and mouse roles; up to 2 marks for three actions covering problem, help and outcome; 1 mark for relevant dialogue; 1 mark for explaining coverage/order of key events; 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Converting a complete narrative into a performable sequence.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Recognising an accurate retell

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read: “A snail rides on a leaf across a puddle, rescues a stranded ant and returns before the leaf sinks.” Then read the options.

**Question:** Which retell preserves the important events?

A. A leaf explains how water evaporates.

B. A snail sleeps under a rock while an ant flies away.

C. An ant builds a bridge before any puddle appears.

D. A snail crosses a puddle on a leaf, helps an ant and gets back safely.

**Correct answer:** D — It keeps the central journey, rescue and safe return in order.

**Coverage:** Evaluating fidelity in a concise retell.

### E02 — Naming an adaptation

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say, “A class changes a story’s castle into a space station but keeps the same characters and problem.”

**Question:** Is the class copying exactly or adapting? Explain one clue.

**Marking key:** Adapting (1 mark) because the setting/details changed while some story elements stayed (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Distinguishing adaptation from exact reproduction.

### E03 — Selecting a useful retell image

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read: “The key moment is when the fox returns a baby bird to its nest.” Then read the options.

**Question:** Which image best communicates that event?

A. The fox lifting the bird gently towards the nest

B. An empty sky with no characters

C. The fox asleep before the bird appears

D. A labelled diagram of fox teeth

**Correct answer:** A — It directly represents the key action and characters.

**Coverage:** Selecting visual representation for a narrative event.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Retelling from a character’s view

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Read: “A child opens a shed and surprises a family of tiny dragons hiding inside.”

**Question:** Retell the event as if you are one of the dragons. Include what you saw, felt and did.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for first-person dragon viewpoint, 1 mark for accurate encounter details, 1 mark for a plausible feeling and 1 mark for a resulting action; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Retelling a familiar event through an altered narrative viewpoint.

### E05 — Adapting a problem for play

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “In a story, a paper plane is stuck in a tree.”

**Question:** Change the problem so it can be acted safely inside a classroom, while keeping the idea of retrieving something out of reach.

**Marking key:** A safe workable adaptation such as a scarf placed on a high pretend shelf and characters solving how to retrieve it. Award 1 mark for out-of-reach continuity and 1 mark for safe indoor performability; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Adapting an event to performance constraints while retaining narrative function.

### E06 — Checking a missing resolution

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “A retell shows a penguin losing a map and searching three places. It then stops.”

**Question:** What should be added, and why?

**Marking key:** Adds a resolution/ending such as finding the map or choosing another solution (1 mark) because the search problem otherwise remains unresolved (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Diagnosing and repairing incompleteness in a retell.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Comparing a text and performance retell

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “Original: A shy star flashes three times to warn a night bird about a storm. Performance: An actor playing the star shouts continuously while the bird dances and no storm is mentioned.”

**Question:** Identify what the performance keeps, changes and accidentally loses. Suggest two revisions that would make it a clearer adaptation.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for a retained element (star/bird), 1 mark for a change (shouting/dancing), 1 mark for the lost warning/storm/three flashes, and 1 mark each for two targeted revisions restoring key meaning while allowing performance choices; 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Analysing fidelity and purposeful change in adaptation.

### E08 — Designing two retell modes

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “A sleepy giant misplaces a tiny bell. A child follows its sound, finds it in the giant’s boot and returns it.”

**Question:** Plan both a three-image retell and a short role-play. Explain one strength of each mode and show how both include the same central events.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for three images covering loss/search-or-clue/return; 1 mark for roles and performance sequence; 1 mark for explaining how images show ordered visual moments; 1 mark for explaining how role-play shows voice/action; 1 mark for confirming the same central events appear in both; 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Coordinating and evaluating visual and dramatic retell modes.
