# AC9EFLE02 — Pass 1 question bank

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Naming a feeling about an event

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the original mini-story: “Omar’s paper boat sank. His friend helped him make a stronger one.” Then read the options.

**Question:** Which response clearly shares a feeling about what happened?

A. Omar is a name.

B. The story has two sentences.

C. Paper can be folded.

D. I felt glad when the friend helped Omar.

**Correct answer:** D — It names a feeling and connects it to a story event.

**Coverage:** Recognising a personal emotional response to an event.

### P02 — A thought about a character

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “Suki returned a lost purse to the office without opening it.”

**Question:** What do you think about Suki’s choice?

**Marking key:** Gives a relevant evaluative thought, such as “It was honest/helpful/responsible.” Award 1 mark. A different view earns the mark if connected clearly to the choice.

**Coverage:** Stating a simple judgement about a character’s action.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Feeling plus reason

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Read: “A tiny mouse practised every day and finally rang the enormous bell.”

**Question:** Complete: “I felt ___ when the mouse rang the bell because ___.”

**Marking key:** 1 mark for a plausible feeling and 1 mark for a reason linked to practice, effort, success or the difficult task; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Supporting an emotional story response with an event-based reason.

### P04 — Different readers, different responses

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say, “In a story, a character climbs a very high ladder. Jo says the part is exciting. Lee says it is worrying.”

**Question:** Can both responses make sense? Explain briefly.

**Marking key:** Yes (1 mark); explains that readers can have different feelings/perspectives, or that height/action can feel exciting to one person and risky to another (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Recognising that readers may respond differently to the same event.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Responding as events change

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “At first, a joey cannot find its mother. Later, it hears her call and follows the sound home.”

**Question:** Share one feeling about the first event and a different feeling about the ending. Give a reason for each.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for a plausible first feeling and linked reason; 1 mark for a contrasting ending feeling and linked reason; 1 mark for clearly connecting each response to the correct event; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Tracking and explaining changing emotional responses across a plot.

### P06 — Using character evidence

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “When thunder begins, Ben closes the curtains, pulls the blanket over his head and calls for Dad.”

**Question:** How do you think Ben feels? Name one action that supports your answer.

**Marking key:** A plausible feeling such as afraid, worried or startled (1 mark) plus a relevant action from the text (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Supporting a thought about a character with behavioural evidence.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — More than one feeling

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “Mei wins the class race, but her best friend falls just before the finish.”

**Question:** Explain two feelings Mei might have at the same time and what in the event could cause each feeling.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for a feeling linked to winning, 1 mark for a different feeling linked to the friend’s fall, and 1 mark for explaining that both can occur together; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Reasoning about mixed character emotions caused by one complex event.

### P08 — Disagreeing about a character respectfully

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “A character secretly eats the last biscuit, then admits it and apologises. One reader says, ‘The character is bad.’”

**Question:** Give a more careful response to the character. Include a thought about both the first choice and the later choice.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for evaluating the secret eating as unfair/wrong; 1 mark for recognising honesty/apology/repair later; 1 mark for avoiding a fixed label and describing actions instead; 1 mark for a coherent overall response; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Forming a nuanced response to changing character behaviour.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — A response connected to the story

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read: “The old dog waits by the window until its family returns.” Then read the options.

**Question:** Which comment is a relevant personal response?

A. I felt relieved when the family came back because the dog had been waiting.

B. Windows are made in factories.

C. The word dog has three letters.

D. Families can own cars.

**Correct answer:** A — It expresses a feeling and reason tied to the story.

**Coverage:** Selecting a text-connected response over unrelated observations.

### E02 — Identifying a character thought

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “Even though the path was muddy, Niko carried the injured bird to a wildlife helper.”

**Question:** Give one word or phrase that describes what you think of Niko’s action.

**Marking key:** A relevant response such as kind, caring, determined or responsible. Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Expressing a concise evaluative response to character conduct.

### E03 — Event and emotion

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Read the events and likely reader feelings.

**Question:** Match each event to a plausible response: (a) a lost kitten is found, (b) a tower collapses just before judging, (c) friends share an umbrella. Feelings: relief, disappointment, warmth/happiness.

**Marking key:** (a) relief, (b) disappointment, (c) warmth/happiness. Award 1 mark each; 3 marks total. Accept another logical match only with a convincing oral explanation.

**Coverage:** Connecting story events with plausible emotional responses.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Explaining a favourite moment

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “A crab tries three unsuitable shells before finding one that fits.”

**Question:** Choose one moment that could be your favourite and explain why.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for naming a specific moment from the stimulus and 1 mark for a coherent personal reason; 2 marks total. No single moment is required.

**Coverage:** Selecting and justifying a preferred story event.

### E05 — A character changes a plan

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “Lena plans to keep every flower she picks. When she sees that the park has very few flowers left, she puts them back and decides to draw them instead.”

**Question:** What do you think of Lena at the beginning and at the end? Use an event detail for each thought.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for a relevant beginning response tied to taking all flowers; 1 mark for an ending response tied to replacing/drawing them; 1 mark for recognising learning or change; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Revising a character response as evidence changes.

### E06 — Comparing reader reactions

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say, “A story ends before revealing what is inside a locked suitcase.”

**Question:** One reader feels annoyed and another feels curious. Give a reason that makes each response understandable.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for linking annoyance to the missing answer/unfinished feeling and 1 mark for linking curiosity to wanting to imagine or discover the contents; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Explaining contrasting reactions to an unresolved ending.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Responding to a difficult choice

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “During a game, Arin notices the referee has wrongly awarded Arin’s team a point. Telling the truth may make the team lose, but Arin speaks up.”

**Question:** Share your thoughts and feelings about Arin’s decision. Explain what may have made the choice difficult and use two details from the event.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for a clear thought/judgement; 1 mark for a relevant feeling; 1 mark for explaining the conflict between honesty and winning; up to 2 marks for accurate use of event details; 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Forming an evidence-based response to an ethical character decision.

### E08 — Following a character across three events

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “First, Peta laughs when a classmate’s model breaks. Next, Peta notices the classmate crying. Finally, Peta apologises and helps rebuild it.”

**Question:** Explain how your response to Peta changes from the first event to the last. Include a feeling or thought at each stage and say which action matters most to your final response.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for a response to the laughter; 1 mark for a response to noticing the hurt; 1 mark for a response to apology/help; 1 mark for explaining a change in the reader’s judgement; 1 mark for selecting and justifying the most influential action; 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Analysing evolving reader response across character development.
