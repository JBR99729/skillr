# AC9EFLE01 — Pass 1 question bank

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Finding a connection

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Read: “Mina walks to the park with her grandfather. They feed the ducks and talk on the way home.”

**Question:** Share one part of Mina’s outing that is similar to or different from an outing you have had.

**Marking key:** Identifies a specific story detail (1 mark) and states a relevant similarity or difference from their experience (1 mark). Accept a connection to an observed or imagined family outing if the child prefers not to share personal information. 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Making a simple text-to-self connection to an event.

### P02 — Noticing everyday life in an image

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Describe an illustration: “A family sits around a low table, sharing bowls of noodles with chopsticks.”

**Question:** What does this image show about the family’s everyday life?

**Marking key:** States one visible detail, such as the food, utensils, shared meal or table (1 mark), and links it to a family routine or way of eating together (1 mark). Do not assess whether the routine matches the child’s own family. 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Identifying a daily-life detail represented in a literary image.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Comparing homes in a story

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Read: “Arlo lives high in an apartment. He takes a lift downstairs and plays in a shared courtyard.”

**Question:** Name one detail about Arlo’s home. Then say how that detail is similar to or different from a home you know.

**Marking key:** Gives one accurate story detail (1 mark) and a clear similarity or difference connected to that detail (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Comparing a depicted setting with a familiar setting.

### P04 — Choosing a thoughtful response

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the story reminder and options aloud: “In the story, Tia feels worried when she starts at a new school.”

**Question:** Which response best shares an idea about Tia’s experience?

A. The book has twelve pages.

B. Tia is a word with four letters.

C. Tia is worried; I felt that way when something was new for me.

D. Schools have doors and windows.

**Correct answer:** C — It connects Tia’s feeling and event with a relevant experience.

**Coverage:** Recognising a relevant personal response to a character’s experience.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Responding to a poem’s feeling

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Read this original verse: “Boots splash deep / in silver rain. / I race the drops / down every drain.”

**Question:** How does the speaker feel about the rain? Give one clue from the poem and say whether rain has ever made you feel the same or different.

**Marking key:** Identifies an supported feeling such as excited, playful or happy (1 mark); cites a clue such as “splash”, “race” or the lively actions (1 mark); makes a relevant same/different connection (1 mark). 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Sharing a response by combining textual evidence with experience.

### P06 — Life in two Australian settings

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “In one story, Jo walks from a flat to a busy beach. In another, Max crosses a large dry paddock to feed hens.”

**Question:** How do the settings make Jo’s and Max’s everyday activities different?

**Marking key:** Connects Jo’s coastal/urban setting to walking to the beach (1 mark) and Max’s rural setting to crossing a paddock or caring for hens (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Relating contrasting Australian settings to characters’ activities.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Comparing a family routine

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “Every Friday evening, Ivo’s family cooks together. One person washes vegetables, one stirs the pot and another sets the table. They eat when everyone is ready.”

**Question:** Explain what this passage shows about Ivo’s family routine. Compare two details with a meal routine you know.

**Marking key:** Explains that the family shares preparation/eats together (1 mark); identifies 2 accurate passage details (2 marks); gives a relevant comparison for each detail, stating same or different (2 marks). 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Sustaining a detailed comparison between a literary event and experience.

### P08 — Combining words and images

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Describe a story page: “The words say, ‘Niko hurried before the tram bell rang.’ The picture shows tall buildings, a tram stop and Niko holding an adult’s hand.”

**Question:** Share one idea about Niko’s morning. Use one clue from the words and one from the picture, then make a connection to a journey you know.

**Marking key:** Gives a sensible idea, such as Niko is hurrying to catch transport (1 mark); uses a word clue (1 mark); uses an image clue (1 mark); makes a relevant same/different connection (1 mark). 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Forming and supporting a personal response to a multimodal literary text.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Recognising a text-to-self connection

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read: “Luca practises tying his shoelaces. At first the loops fall apart, but he keeps trying.” Then read the options.

**Question:** Which sentence makes a clear connection to Luca’s experience?

A. The story uses three sentences.

B. Luca’s name begins with a capital letter.

C. Shoelaces can be many colours.

D. I also had to practise before I could do something by myself.

**Correct answer:** D — It compares the character’s experience of practising with the reader’s experience.

**Coverage:** Identifying a relevant connection to a literary event.

### E02 — Reading a cultural detail respectfully

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “At the doorway, Hana places her outdoor shoes on a rack before joining her family inside.”

**Question:** What family practice does the story show?

**Marking key:** States that Hana removes/leaves outdoor shoes at the entrance before going inside. Award 1 mark. Do not require a reason not supplied by the text.

**Coverage:** Identifying a practice revealed through a character’s action.

### E03 — Sharing a feeling connection

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Read: “Ben waits behind the curtain before his class concert. His hands shake, but he smiles when he hears his family clap.”

**Question:** How might Ben feel before and after he hears the clapping? Connect one of those feelings to an experience you have had or can imagine.

**Marking key:** Gives a supported before-feeling such as nervous/excited (1 mark); a supported after-feeling such as encouraged/happy/relieved (1 mark); and one relevant connection (1 mark). 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Responding to changing character feelings and relating them to experience.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Setting changes an experience

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “Priya’s story is set on a cattle station far from town. School lessons arrive online, and a long drive is needed to visit the library.”

**Question:** Choose one event and explain how the setting makes it different from the same event in a suburb near a school or library.

**Marking key:** Selects online schooling or the long library journey (1 mark) and gives a logical comparison linked to distance/access in the two settings (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Explaining how a setting shapes a character’s everyday experience.

### E05 — Responding without guessing

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Describe an illustration: “A child wears a bright embroidered jacket while dancing with relatives at a community celebration.”

**Question:** Which comment is best supported and respectful?

A. The clothing and dancing may be important parts of this celebration.

B. Everyone in the child’s country dresses this way every day.

C. The child must dislike ordinary clothes.

D. All community celebrations are exactly the same.

**Correct answer:** A — It notices visible details without making an unsupported claim about a whole group.

**Coverage:** Sharing an evidence-based idea about cultural features in an image.

### E06 — Comparing an unfamiliar experience

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Read this original verse: “Snow at the window, / soup on the stove, / three woollen blankets / tucked round my toes.”

**Question:** What kind of evening is shown? Say one way it may be similar to or different from evenings where you live.

**Marking key:** Identifies a cold/winter/snowy evening using a verse clue (1 mark); states one relevant similarity or difference (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Comparing an experience in a poem with the reader’s context.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — What a story reveals about family life

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “On Sunday morning, Sami’s cousins arrive with bread and fruit. Everyone helps move chairs into the garden. The children serve water while the adults finish cooking, and the whole family eats outside.”

**Question:** Explain three things this story shows about Sami’s family life. Then compare one of them with a family or community gathering you know.

**Marking key:** Identifies 3 supported details, such as relatives gathering, sharing food, helping with tasks or eating outdoors (3 marks); gives one clear same/different comparison (1 mark); supports it with a detail from the stimulus and the chosen experience (1 mark). 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Interpreting multiple social details and making a supported connection.

### E08 — Building a response from two story images

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Describe two illustrations: “In the first, Mei hides a broken toy behind her back while her friend searches for it. In the second, Mei returns the toy, tells the truth and helps repair it.”

**Question:** Share your idea about Mei’s choice. Use evidence from both images, explain how her feelings may have changed and connect the event to a time when telling the truth mattered in real life or in another story.

**Marking key:** States a relevant evaluation of Mei’s choice (1 mark); uses evidence from image 1 (1 mark) and image 2 (1 mark); explains a plausible change in feeling (1 mark); gives a relevant real-life or text connection (1 mark). 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Developing an evidence-based response and connection across a visual sequence.
