# AC9EFLA05 — Pass 1 question bank

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — A complete spoken idea

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read each option with natural intonation.

**Question:** Which group of words expresses a complete idea?

A. Two red.

B. Under the chair.

C. The puppy sleeps.

D. After lunch.

**Correct answer:** C — It tells a complete idea about what the puppy does.

**Coverage:** Distinguishing a simple sentence from incomplete word groups.

### P02 — Counting ideas as sentences

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say clearly, with a pause: “The rain stopped. We went outside.”

**Question:** How many sentences did you hear? Tell one idea from each sentence.

**Marking key:** Two sentences (1 mark); identifies that the rain stopped and that the people went outside (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Recognising separate sentences as separate complete ideas in speech.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Completing an unfinished idea

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say, “The yellow bird …” and pause.

**Question:** Add words so the group becomes a sentence with a complete idea.

**Marking key:** Adds a sensible action/state, such as “The yellow bird is singing.” Award 1 mark for completion and 1 mark for a coherent idea; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Expanding a sentence beginning into a complete message.

### P04 — Separating two ideas

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Say without a pause, “I found my hat it was under the bench.” Then repeat slowly if needed.

**Question:** Clap where one idea ends and the next begins, then say the two sentences.

**Marking key:** Places the boundary after “hat” (1 mark) and produces “I found my hat. It was under the bench.” as two complete ideas (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Segmenting a stream of words into sentence-sized ideas.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Choosing a sentence for a picture

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Describe a picture of “a child pouring water into a plant pot”, then read the options.

**Question:** Which sentence clearly expresses the main idea in the picture?

A. A little watering can.

B. Green and brown.

C. Beside the window.

D. The child waters the plant.

**Correct answer:** D — It communicates who is acting and what is happening.

**Coverage:** Matching a complete sentence to a represented event.

### P06 — Finding what is missing

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “The tall tree.”

**Question:** This group names something but does not tell a complete idea. What could you add to make it a sentence?

**Marking key:** Adds what the tree does or is, such as “The tall tree sways” or “The tall tree is old.” Award 1 mark for an appropriate completion and 1 mark for explaining or demonstrating that the completed version tells more; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Identifying and supplying the idea-completing part of a sentence.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Repairing an unclear message

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “A child points towards the playground and says only, ‘The blue ball.’ The teacher does not know what has happened.”

**Question:** Turn the child’s words into a sentence that gives the teacher a useful complete idea. Explain what information you added.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for a complete useful sentence, such as “The blue ball rolled through the gate”; 1 mark for identifying added action/state information; 1 mark for explaining how it makes the message clearer; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Revising an incomplete utterance for communicative clarity.

### P08 — One picture, two sentence ideas

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Describe: “A koala sits in a fork of a tree while rain falls.”

**Question:** Say two different sentences about the scene. Each sentence must express its own complete idea.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for a complete sentence about the koala, 1 mark for a distinct complete sentence about the rain/tree/scene, and 1 mark for keeping the two ideas meaningfully different; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Generating distinct sentence units from one shared stimulus.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Sentence or fragment

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read each option aloud.

**Question:** Which option is a sentence that communicates a complete event?

A. Our bus arrived.

B. At the gate.

C. The noisy bus.

D. Before nine.

**Correct answer:** A — It tells what happened to the bus.

**Coverage:** Independently identifying a complete declarative sentence.

### E02 — Hearing a sentence boundary

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say, “Dad closes the window. The room becomes quiet.”

**Question:** Repeat only the second sentence.

**Marking key:** “The room becomes quiet.” Award 1 mark. Minor grammatical variation is acceptable if the second idea remains complete.

**Coverage:** Isolating one sentence from connected spoken discourse.

### E03 — What a sentence does

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Ask the prompt aloud.

**Question:** Why do speakers and writers use sentences?

**Marking key:** Explains that a sentence communicates/expresses a complete idea or message. Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Articulating the communicative purpose of a sentence.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Completing a report

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say, “A class observation begins, ‘The ice cube …’”

**Question:** Finish the sentence to report one clear observation.

**Marking key:** A complete observable idea such as “The ice cube is melting” or “The ice cube feels cold.” Award 1 mark for sentence completion and 1 mark for a relevant observation; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Constructing a complete sentence for an informative purpose.

### E05 — Fixing a broken instruction

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “Your shoes beside the door.”

**Question:** Change the words into a complete instruction.

**Marking key:** A clear instruction such as “Put your shoes beside the door.” Award 1 mark for adding an appropriate action and 1 mark for preserving the intended place; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Converting a fragment into an imperative sentence.

### E06 — Dividing a message

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “The gate opened the children walked into the garden.”

**Question:** Say this as two sentences, then explain why two sentences are useful here.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for “The gate opened”; 1 mark for “The children walked into the garden”; 1 mark for explaining that each sentence carries one complete event/idea or makes the sequence clearer; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Segmenting linked events into sentence units and justifying the boundary.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Improving a picture caption

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Describe a picture of three ducks crossing a path. The existing caption says only “Three ducks.”

**Question:** Explain why the caption does not report the whole event, then write or say a stronger sentence caption.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for noting that “Three ducks” only names and does not tell what happens; 1 mark for a complete caption such as “Three ducks cross the path”; 1 mark for including the action; 1 mark for explaining that the new sentence expresses the picture’s event; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Analysing and revising a nominal fragment into an event sentence.

### E08 — Building a clear two-sentence account

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “You planted a bean seed yesterday. Today a tiny shoot is visible.”

**Question:** Give a two-sentence account. Use one sentence for yesterday and one for today, and explain how the sentences organise the ideas.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for a complete planting sentence; 1 mark for a complete shoot-observation sentence; 1 mark for correct time connection; 1 mark for explaining that each sentence holds one event/time idea; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Creating and explaining a sequence of sentence-sized ideas.
