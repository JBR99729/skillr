# AC9EFLE03 — Pass 1 question bank

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Recognising a literary story

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the descriptions aloud.

**Question:** Which text is most clearly a made-up literary story?

A. A tale about a moon moth that learns to sing

B. A label naming the parts of a plant

C. A list of bus departure times

D. A sign that says “Wash your hands”

**Correct answer:** A — It presents an imaginative character and event.

**Coverage:** Distinguishing an imaginative literary text from functional/informative texts.

### P02 — Character, event or ending

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Read the examples and feature labels aloud.

**Question:** Match: (a) “Kip the kangaroo”, (b) “Kip loses his red scarf”, (c) “At last, Kip finds it beside the creek” to **character**, **event**, or **ending**.

**Marking key:** (a) character, (b) event, (c) ending. Award 1 mark each; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Identifying core narrative features in a short stimulus.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Finding the beginning

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read all options aloud.

**Question:** Which sentence would work best as the beginning of a story?

A. That is how the garden gate was opened at last.

B. One windy morning, Rosa found a silver key on her doorstep.

C. The end.

D. Rosa had already told everyone what happened next.

**Correct answer:** B — It introduces a time, character and event that can begin the story.

**Coverage:** Recognising an opening that establishes a narrative.

### P04 — Identifying a literary text type

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Read the text descriptions and labels **story**, **poem** and **play**.

**Question:** Match: (a) lines with repeated rhythm about rain, (b) characters’ names followed by words for actors to say, (c) a narrator tells how a lost fox gets home.

**Marking key:** (a) poem, (b) play, (c) story. Award 1 mark each; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Recognising three literary text forms from structural clues.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — What is missing?

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “Beginning: A robot wakes in a toy shop. Event: It searches every shelf for its missing wheel.”

**Question:** Which story feature is missing, and give one possible version of it.

**Marking key:** Identifies an ending (1 mark) and supplies a coherent resolution, such as finding the wheel or solving the problem another way (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Recognising and supplying a missing narrative feature.

### P06 — Separating character from setting

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “In a snowy valley, a young wombat named Willa builds a warm shelter.”

**Question:** Who is the character, and where does the story take place?

**Marking key:** Character: Willa/the young wombat (1 mark). Place/setting: a snowy valley (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Identifying character and setting within one sentence.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Restoring a mixed-up narrative

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Read: (a) “Finally, the kite drops safely into the paddock.” (b) “A gust pulls Noor’s kite from her hands.” (c) “Noor and Dad run after it.”

**Question:** Put the events in story order and explain which part acts as the ending.

**Correct answer:** (b), (c), (a). Award 1 mark for correct order, 1 mark for identifying (a) as the ending and 1 mark for explaining that it resolves the runaway-kite event; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Sequencing events and justifying narrative closure.

### P08 — Planning a complete mini-story

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Provide this character: “a shy cloud”.

**Question:** Plan a beginning, one main event and an ending for a story about the shy cloud. Keep the same character connected through all three parts.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for an opening that introduces the cloud/situation, 1 mark for a central event or problem, 1 mark for a coherent ending, and 1 mark for continuity across the parts; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Constructing a simple narrative framework with linked features.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Recognising a poem

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the options aloud, including the line breaks.

**Question:** Which example is most likely from a poem?

A. “Library opens at 9 am.”

B. “Ingredients: flour, milk, egg.”

C. “Tap, tap, rain on the track; / drip from the roof and trickle back.”

D. “A triangle has three sides.”

**Correct answer:** C — It uses compact lines, rhythm and sound patterning typical of a poem.

**Coverage:** Identifying a literary text type from language features.

### E02 — Naming the main character

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “Mara and her cat follow a trail of feathers, but Mara makes every decision in the adventure.”

**Question:** Who is the main character?

**Marking key:** Mara. Award 1 mark. The cat is a character but not the main decision-maker described.

**Coverage:** Distinguishing the central character from another participant.

### E03 — Recognising an event

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the options aloud.

**Question:** Which option describes an event rather than a character or place?

A. A short adventure story

B. A clever blue penguin

C. Beside a quiet harbour

D. The bridge cracks during the storm.

**Correct answer:** D — It tells something that happens.

**Coverage:** Categorising a narrative event.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Choosing a useful ending

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read: “A young dragon accidentally freezes the village fountain and tries several ways to thaw it.” Then read the options.

**Question:** Which ending best resolves this story problem?

A. The dragon warms the fountain with a lantern and water flows again.

B. A fountain is made from stone.

C. Long ago, there was a different village.

D. The dragon’s wings are silver.

**Correct answer:** A — It concludes the established effort and solves the frozen-fountain problem.

**Coverage:** Selecting a coherent resolution for a narrative setup.

### E05 — Comparing story and play features

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say, “A story may have a narrator. A play page may list LEO: before the words an actor says.”

**Question:** Give one feature that helps a reader tell the play page from the story page.

**Marking key:** Names character labels before speech, dialogue arranged for actors, or stage/action directions. Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Comparing organisational features of two literary forms.

### E06 — Adding a character action

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “Beginning: Eda finds a door painted on a tree. Ending: Eda returns home with a glowing seed.”

**Question:** Add one event in the middle that logically connects the beginning and ending.

**Marking key:** A coherent event involving entering/opening/investigating the door and obtaining the seed. Award 1 mark for connection to the door and 1 mark for connection to the seed; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Supplying a bridging event between narrative endpoints.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Analysing a complete narrative

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “At dawn, a young seal finds a fishing net tangled around a rock. She calls other seals, and together they pull it free. At sunset, the group rests on the clear shore.”

**Question:** Identify the character, beginning situation, main event and ending. Explain how the ending connects to the earlier problem.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for young seal (main character), 1 mark for net tangled at dawn (beginning/situation), 1 mark for calling/pulling together (event), 1 mark for resting on clear shore (ending), and 1 mark for explaining that the net problem has been removed/resolved; 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Identifying and connecting multiple narrative features in one text.

### E08 — Transforming a story into a play

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “Story: A baker asks a neighbour for help when every loaf begins floating towards the ceiling.”

**Question:** Turn this moment into a tiny play excerpt. Name at least two characters, give each something to say, and include one action for an actor to perform.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for two appropriate character labels, 1 mark for baker dialogue, 1 mark for neighbour dialogue, 1 mark for a relevant stage/action direction, and 1 mark for retaining the floating-loaf event; 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Applying distinctive play features while preserving story content.
