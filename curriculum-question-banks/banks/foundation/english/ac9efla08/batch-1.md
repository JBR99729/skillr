# AC9EFLA08 — Pass 1 question bank

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — A word from an everyday routine

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the situation and options aloud.

**Question:** Which word names the container a child might pack with food for school?

A. telescope

B. lunchbox

C. seedling

D. rectangle

**Correct answer:** B — “Lunchbox” belongs to the familiar routine of taking lunch.

**Coverage:** Recognising vocabulary from an everyday experience.

### P02 — A word used in a maths topic

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read all options aloud.

**Question:** The class is learning about shapes. Which word belongs most directly to that topic?

A. breakfast

B. whisper

C. triangle

D. puppy

**Correct answer:** C — “Triangle” is topic vocabulary used to name a shape.

**Coverage:** Identifying school-topic vocabulary.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Matching words to familiar contexts

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Read the context labels and words aloud.

**Question:** Match each word to where it is most likely used: (a) **helmet**, (b) **ingredient**, (c) **goalkeeper**, (d) **cocoon**. Contexts: riding a bike, cooking, soccer, learning about butterfly growth.

**Marking key:** helmet–riding a bike; ingredient–cooking; goalkeeper–soccer; cocoon–butterfly growth. Award 1 mark each; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Connecting vocabulary to everyday, interest and school contexts.

### P04 — Choosing a precise action word

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say, “A snail moves very slowly across a leaf,” then read the options.

**Question:** Which word most precisely completes “The snail ___ across the leaf”?

A. bakes

B. shouts

C. flashes

D. crawls

**Correct answer:** D — “Crawls” names the slow movement accurately.

**Coverage:** Selecting context-appropriate vocabulary for an observed action.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Working out a topic word

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “A habitat is the place where a plant or animal lives. The pond is a frog’s habitat.”

**Question:** In this explanation, what does **habitat** mean? Give another possible habitat.

**Marking key:** States that it is a place/home where a living thing lives (1 mark) and gives a plausible example such as forest, ocean, nest area or desert (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Learning a new school-topic word from definition and example.

### P06 — Building an interest word bank

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Ask the child to choose one familiar interest, such as drawing, dancing, gardening, vehicles or a sport.

**Question:** Name three words that would help you talk clearly about that interest. Explain one word if your listener may not know it.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for each of three relevant words and 1 mark for a clear explanation/example of one word; 4 marks total. Do not judge the chosen interest.

**Coverage:** Generating and explaining vocabulary from a personal-interest context.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Fixing an overgeneral word

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “During a science observation, a child says, ‘The thing on the plant opened.’ The child means the flower.”

**Question:** Replace **thing** with a clearer word and explain why the new sentence is more useful.

**Marking key:** Replaces it with “flower” or a more precise suitable plant-part word (1 mark), gives a complete revised sentence (1 mark), and explains that the precise word identifies exactly what opened (1 mark); 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Revising vague vocabulary for precision in a school context.

### P08 — One word in two contexts

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “In art, a class talks about a **pattern** of red and blue shapes. In music, the class claps a **pattern** of loud and soft beats.”

**Question:** What is similar about the meaning of **pattern** in both contexts, and what is different?

**Marking key:** 1 mark for identifying an order/design that repeats or follows a rule in both; 1 mark for shapes/colours in art; 1 mark for sounds/beats in music; 1 mark for clearly contrasting the materials/modes; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Comparing how a shared topic word operates across learning contexts.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Vocabulary for weather

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read all options aloud.

**Question:** Which word specifically describes a short period of rain?

A. shower

B. blanket

C. chapter

D. whisper

**Correct answer:** A — In a weather context, a shower is a brief fall of rain.

**Coverage:** Recognising context-specific weather vocabulary.

### E02 — Naming a classroom tool

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Describe “a tool with two blades and handles, used to cut paper safely”.

**Question:** What is the tool called?

**Marking key:** Scissors. Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Retrieving familiar classroom vocabulary from a functional description.

### E03 — Sorting topic words

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Read the words **heavy**, **rhyme**, **root**, **cube** and contexts **measurement**, **poetry**, **plants**, **3D shapes**.

**Question:** Match each word to its school-learning context.

**Marking key:** heavy–measurement; rhyme–poetry; root–plants; cube–3D shapes. Award 1 mark each; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Classifying vocabulary across four learning topics.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Learning a word from contrast

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “Glass is transparent, so light passes through and we can see through it. Cardboard is not transparent.”

**Question:** What does **transparent** mean? Name one other transparent object.

**Marking key:** Means light passes through/can be seen through (1 mark); gives a plausible example such as clear window glass or clean water in a clear container (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Deriving meaning from definition and contrast in a science context.

### E05 — Selecting the useful detail word

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read: “A child is giving directions to a toy hidden below a chair.” Then read the options.

**Question:** Which sentence uses the most useful position vocabulary?

A. The toy is somewhere.

B. The toy is under the chair.

C. The toy is nice.

D. There is a chair.

**Correct answer:** B — “Under” precisely describes the toy’s location.

**Coverage:** Applying spatial vocabulary to communicate location.

### E06 — Explaining a sport word

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say, “A friend has never heard the word **dribble** in basketball.”

**Question:** Explain the word so the friend could recognise or try the action.

**Marking key:** Explains that dribbling involves repeatedly bouncing/controling the ball with a hand while moving or standing (1 mark for bouncing repeatedly, 1 mark for control/hand or movement detail); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Giving a functional explanation of interest-specific vocabulary.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Choosing words for two audiences

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “You found a caterpillar on a leaf. You will tell a younger child, then report the observation to your class.”

**Question:** Give one sentence for each audience. Use at least one useful science word in the class report, then explain the vocabulary difference.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for a clear child-friendly sentence; 1 mark for a class-report sentence; 1 mark for accurate science vocabulary such as caterpillar, leaf, insect or observation; 1 mark for explaining why the class report is more precise or topic-focused; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Adapting vocabulary precision to audience and purpose.

### E08 — Creating a mini glossary

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “The class is making an information page about growing beans.”

**Question:** Choose three useful topic words. Explain each in Foundation-friendly language and use one in a sentence about bean growth.

**Marking key:** Up to 3 marks for relevant words such as seed, root, shoot, stem, soil or sprout; up to 3 marks for accurate child-friendly explanations; 1 mark for an accurate sentence using one chosen word; 7 marks total.

**Coverage:** Selecting, defining and applying a coherent set of topic vocabulary.
