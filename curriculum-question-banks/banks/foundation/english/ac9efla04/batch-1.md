# AC9EFLA04 — Pass 1 question bank

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Finding the front of a book

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Place a familiar picture book in front of the child, closed, and read the options aloud.

**Question:** Which feature most clearly helps a reader identify the front cover?

A. A page number in the middle

B. The title and main cover picture

C. The final sentence of the story

D. The blank inside of the back cover

**Correct answer:** B — A title and cover image commonly identify the book at its front.

**Coverage:** Recognising front-cover conventions.

### P02 — Moving through paper pages

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Give the child a closed book placed upside down and back-to-front.

**Question:** Make the book ready to read, open it at the beginning and show how you would move to the next page.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for orienting the book/front correctly, 1 mark for locating the beginning, and 1 mark for turning one page in the conventional sequence; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Demonstrating the physical organisation and sequence of a book.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Starting a screen text

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Describe a story screen with a title, a large **Start** button, a speaker symbol and a battery symbol. Read the options aloud.

**Question:** Which control would a reader most likely choose to begin the story?

A. Battery

B. Speaker

C. Start

D. The device clock

**Correct answer:** C — The labelled Start control signals where the text begins.

**Coverage:** Using a labelled navigation control in a simple digital text.

### P04 — Tracking one line of print

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Write the sentence “The small dog runs home.” on one line in large print.

**Question:** Point to where reading begins, move your finger in the reading direction, and show where the sentence ends.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for starting at the left-most word “The”, 1 mark for tracking left to right in order, and 1 mark for ending after “home.”; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Applying left-to-right print direction within a line.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Returning to a digital menu

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Describe a screen showing a short animal video, a house-shaped **Home** button and a right arrow.

**Question:** The reader wants to leave the video and choose a different animal from the main menu. Which control should the reader try, and why?

**Marking key:** Chooses the Home/house button (1 mark) and explains that it usually returns to the first or main menu screen (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Interpreting a familiar digital navigation convention.

### P06 — Print and screen organisation

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Read the features and labels **paper book** and **screen story** aloud.

**Question:** Match each action to its most likely text form: (a) turn a page by lifting its corner, (b) tap a right arrow, (c) use the cover to find the title, (d) swipe to reveal the next screen.

**Marking key:** Paper book: (a), (c). Screen story: (b), (d). Award 1 mark each; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Comparing navigation conventions across print and digital texts.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Repairing mixed-up story pages

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Read these page descriptions in mixed order: (a) “At last, the seedling has two green leaves.” (b) Cover: “A Seed Wakes” with an author’s name. (c) “First, a seed is placed in damp soil.” (d) “Next, a tiny shoot pushes upward.”

**Question:** Put the parts in the order a reader should meet them and explain one clue you used.

**Correct answer:** (b), (c), (d), (a). Award 1 mark for the cover first, 1 mark for the three events in sequence, and 1 mark for a valid clue such as “first”, “next” or “at last”; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Reconstructing book organisation using cover and sequence conventions.

### P08 — Planning a two-screen information text

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “Make a simple digital text about caring for a class plant. It will have a first screen and a second screen.” The child may draw or answer orally.

**Question:** What should appear on each screen, and what control would help the reader move between them?

**Marking key:** 1 mark for an organised first screen such as a title/introduction, 1 mark for relevant second-screen information such as watering or sunlight, 1 mark for a clear navigation control such as a labelled arrow or Next button, and 1 mark for explaining its direction/function; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Designing a logically ordered simple digital text with navigation.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Locating a book title

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the options aloud.

**Question:** Where would a reader usually look first to learn the name of a picture book?

A. Inside a pencil case

B. Under the reading table

C. On another book’s last page

D. On the front cover

**Correct answer:** D — The front cover conventionally displays the title.

**Coverage:** Identifying the usual location of a book title.

### E02 — What a page number does

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Show or describe the numerals 4, 5 and 6 at the bottoms of three consecutive pages.

**Question:** What do these numerals help a reader know?

**Marking key:** They identify the pages and/or show their order. Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Explaining the organising function of page numbers.

### E03 — Choosing the next-screen control

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Describe four screen symbols, then read the options.

**Question:** A reader has finished one screen and wants the following part. Which control most clearly signals “next”?

A. A right-pointing arrow

B. A crossed-out speaker

C. A rubbish-bin symbol

D. A question mark labelled Help

**Correct answer:** A — A right arrow commonly moves a reader forward.

**Coverage:** Recognising forward-navigation symbolism.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Beginning in the wrong place

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say, “A child opens a story at the final page and is confused.”

**Question:** What should the child do, and how will that help?

**Marking key:** Return to the front/beginning (1 mark) so the story events can be read in their intended order and make sense (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Correcting an error in book navigation.

### E05 — A button that does not match

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say, “On the last screen of a digital story, a button says Start even though it returns to the menu.”

**Question:** Suggest a clearer label or symbol and explain why it is better.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for a suitable replacement such as Home, Menu or a house symbol; 1 mark for explaining that it tells the reader where the control actually goes; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Evaluating clarity of digital navigation labels.

### E06 — Comparing two beginnings

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Describe: “Book A begins with a cover, title and first story page. Screen Text B opens on a menu with a title and a Begin button.”

**Question:** Give one similarity and one difference in how the two texts help a reader begin.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for a valid similarity such as both display a title or signal a beginning; 1 mark for a valid difference such as opening/turning a page versus tapping a button; 1 mark for linking the difference to print or screen form; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Comparing entry conventions across formats.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Organising a class photo book

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say, “Four parts of a class photo book are ready: a front cover and title, photos of planting seeds, photos of watering, and a final photo of flowers.”

**Question:** Put the parts in a useful order and explain two organising features that would help a reader.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for cover/title first; 1 mark for planting before watering before flowers; up to 2 marks for useful features such as page numbers, captions, headings or clear page sequence; 1 mark for explaining how one feature supports navigation or meaning; 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Organising a complete print text for chronological reading.

### E08 — Diagnosing a confusing screen story

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “A screen story has no title. Every screen shows three identical unlabelled circles. One goes forward, one goes back and one closes the story.”

**Question:** Explain why a new reader may be confused and propose three changes that would make the text easier to use.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for explaining that the purpose of the circles is unclear; 1 mark for adding a title/starting cue; 1 mark each for two distinct navigation improvements such as arrows, labels, familiar home/close symbols or consistent placement; 1 mark for explaining how one improvement guides the reader; 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Analysing and redesigning organisation in a simple digital text.
