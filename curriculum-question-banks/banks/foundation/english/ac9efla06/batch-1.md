# AC9EFLA06 — Pass 1 question bank

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Words working in order

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the options exactly as written.

**Question:** Which word order makes a clear sentence?

A. The jumps frog.

B. Jumps frog the.

C. Frog the jumps.

D. The frog jumps.

**Correct answer:** D — The words are grouped and ordered to tell who and what happens.

**Coverage:** Recognising meaningful word order in a simple sentence.

### P02 — Who and what happens

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Read the two sentence beginnings and endings aloud.

**Question:** Match each “who” group to the ending that makes sense: (a) “The baby” (b) “Three ducks” with (1) “paddle across the pond” (2) “sleeps in the cot”.

**Marking key:** (a)–(2), (b)–(1). Award 1 mark for each correct match; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Combining compatible participant and action groups.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Rebuilding a mixed sentence

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Place word cards or read: **the / chases / kitten / string / the**.

**Question:** Put the words in an order that clearly tells what happens.

**Correct answer:** “The kitten chases the string.” Award 1 mark for correct grouping/order and 1 mark for retaining all five words; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Arranging word groups into conventional sentence structure.

### P04 — Changing one group

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say, “The brown horse runs beside the fence.”

**Question:** Keep “runs beside the fence” but change the group that tells who runs. Say the new sentence.

**Marking key:** Replaces “The brown horse” with another logical participant group, such as “My little dog”, while retaining the required ending. Award 1 mark for the replacement group and 1 mark for a meaningful complete sentence; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Substituting one functional word group while preserving sentence meaning.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Adding useful detail

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say, “Birds sing.”

**Question:** Add a group of words that tells **where** the birds sing.

**Marking key:** A coherent expansion such as “Birds sing in the tall tree.” Award 1 mark for a location group and 1 mark for a meaningful whole sentence; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Expanding a sentence with a location group.

### P06 — Choosing groups for a scene

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Describe a picture of “two children carrying a basket towards the garden”, then read the options.

**Question:** Which combination of word groups best matches the whole scene?

A. Two children / carry a basket / to the garden.

B. A basket / carries two children / under the sea.

C. The garden / carries / two baskets.

D. Two gardens / sleep / in a basket.

**Correct answer:** A — Each group contributes accurate information about who, what and where.

**Coverage:** Selecting coordinated word groups to represent an event.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Explaining a meaning change

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: (1) “The dog follows the boy.” (2) “The boy follows the dog.”

**Question:** Do the sentences mean the same thing? Explain what changed.

**Marking key:** States that the meanings differ (1 mark), identifies who follows in each sentence (1 mark), and explains that changing the order of the “dog” and “boy” groups changes who does the action (1 mark); 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Analysing how group order changes participant roles and meaning.

### P08 — Repairing an impossible grouping

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “The picnic eats the family under a shady tree.”

**Question:** Rearrange or replace a word group to make the sentence sensible, then tell why your version works.

**Marking key:** A logical repair such as “The family eats the picnic under a shady tree” (1 mark for who/action relationship, 1 mark for a coherent remaining group) and an explanation that people eat the picnic rather than the picnic eating people (1 mark); 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Diagnosing and repairing incompatible word-group roles.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Meaningful sentence structure

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read each option aloud.

**Question:** Which arrangement tells a clear idea about a possum?

A. Climbs the a branch possum.

B. A possum climbs the branch.

C. The climbs possum branch a.

D. Branch a possum the climbs.

**Correct answer:** B — Its word groups occur in an order that communicates who acts and what is affected.

**Coverage:** Independently recognising conventional group order.

### E02 — Finding the action group

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say, “The tiny boat floats on the lake.”

**Question:** Repeat the group of words that tells what the boat does.

**Marking key:** “floats on the lake” (accept “floats” for 1 mark; full group earns 2 marks). Maximum 2 marks.

**Coverage:** Isolating the predicate/action group in a spoken sentence.

### E03 — Finding the participant group

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say, “After lunch, our class visits the library.”

**Question:** Which group tells who visits?

**Marking key:** “our class”. Award 1 mark.

**Coverage:** Identifying the group that names the participant.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Joining compatible groups

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Read beginnings: (a) “The bright moon” (b) “My wet socks”. Read endings: (1) “hang near the heater” (2) “shines above the houses”.

**Question:** Match the groups to make two sensible sentences.

**Marking key:** (a)–(2), (b)–(1). Award 1 mark each; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Selecting semantically compatible subject and predicate groups.

### E05 — Moving a time group

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say, “We feed the chickens every morning.”

**Question:** Move “every morning” to the beginning and say the sentence. Does the main event stay the same?

**Marking key:** “Every morning, we feed the chickens” (1 mark); yes, the feeding event remains, while the time group moved (1 mark); 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Manipulating a time group while preserving core meaning.

### E06 — Expanding two parts

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Give the sentence “The fish swims.”

**Question:** Add words to tell what the fish is like and where it swims. Explain which added group gives each detail.

**Marking key:** A coherent sentence such as “The striped fish swims between the rocks” (1 mark for participant detail, 1 mark for location detail, 1 mark for correctly identifying/explaining each addition); 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Expanding coordinated word groups for descriptive precision.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Two sentences, different focus

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “The wind moves the leaves.” and “The leaves move in the wind.”

**Question:** Explain what each sentence places first and how the sentences describe the scene differently.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for identifying “the wind” as first/focus in sentence 1; 1 mark for “the leaves” in sentence 2; 1 mark for explaining the wind acts on leaves in the first; 1 mark for explaining the second focuses on leaves moving and gives wind as surrounding/cause information; 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Comparing meaning created by different group structures.

### E08 — Constructing and testing a sentence

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Provide these groups: **near the creek**, **builds a nest**, **a small bird**, **in spring**.

**Question:** Use every group to make one clear sentence. Then explain how a listener can work out who, what happens, where and when.

**Marking key:** 1 mark for a grammatical coherent order, such as “In spring, a small bird builds a nest near the creek”; 1 mark each for correctly identifying who (“a small bird”), action (“builds a nest”), place (“near the creek”) and time (“in spring”); 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Synthesising and analysing four functional word groups.
