# AC9SFI05 — Pass 1 question bank

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Sharing an observation clearly

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say: “A child observed a leaf through a magnifier.”

**Question:** Which sentence shares a specific observation?

A. I saw tiny branching lines across the leaf.

B. Leaves are the best things ever.

C. You already know what I saw.

D. Something happened somewhere.

**Correct answer:** A — It tells others the visible detail that was observed.

**Coverage:** Recognising a precise observation statement for an audience.

### P02 — Telling a question from a prediction

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Read: (a) “Which ball will bounce highest?” (b) “I think the rubber ball will bounce highest.”

**Question:** Label one statement **question** and the other **prediction**, then explain the difference.

**Marking key:** (a) question; (b) prediction (1 mark each); explains that the question asks what will be found, while the prediction gives the expected result (1 mark). 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Labelling and communicating different parts of an inquiry.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Reporting movement to a partner

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Roll an empty cardboard tube on its curved side, or show a short teacher-made video.

**Question:** Tell a partner what moved, how it moved and one feature that may have helped. Then listen to one partner question.

**Marking key:** Names the tube (1 mark); describes rolling/path (1 mark); links curved side/shape to movement (1 mark); listens and responds relevantly to a question (1 mark). 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Orally communicating an observation, idea and response.

### P04 — Choosing a format for animal features

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say: “The class wants families to compare the beaks, feet and feathers of three birds.”

**Question:** Which format would make the comparison clearest?

A. An unlabelled page covered by one large colour

B. A labelled poster with one section for each bird

C. A recording that says only “birds are nice”

D. A list of unrelated lunch foods

**Correct answer:** B — Labels and separate sections let viewers find and compare the observed features.

**Coverage:** Selecting a communication format suited to comparative visual evidence.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Recounting a garden observation

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read the notes: “Question: What visits the lavender? Prediction: butterflies. Observation: 2 bees and 1 butterfly in five minutes.”

**Question:** Use the notes to give a three-sentence report that keeps the question, prediction and observation distinct.

**Marking key:** Accurately states the question (1 mark), prediction (1 mark) and timed numerical observation (1 mark) in a logical report (1 mark). 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Organising inquiry information into a clear oral or written sequence.

### P06 — Showing how a tool is used

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide a magnifying glass and a safe shell sample away from direct sunlight.

**Question:** Role-play explaining to a younger student how the tool helps, how to use it safely and one detail it reveals.

**Marking key:** Explains that the tool enlarges visible details (1 mark); demonstrates safe adjustment/handling away from Sun and eyes (1 mark); names one genuine shell detail (1 mark); uses clear, audience-appropriate words (1 mark). 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Communicating scientific equipment use through explanation and demonstration.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Improving an unclear science poster

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Describe a poster titled “Stuff”. It shows 3 dots and a ball picture but has no labels, question, prediction or explanation.

**Question:** Redesign the poster to communicate a three-bounce observation. Add a useful title, key or labels, the original question, a prediction and an evidence-based result.

**Marking key:** Adds a specific title/question about bouncing (1 mark); states a prediction as an expectation (1 mark); labels 3 dots/arcs as 3 observed bounces (1 mark); writes a result that distinguishes evidence from prediction (1 mark); uses a clear key/layout (1 mark). 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Repairing a multimodal communication so an inquiry can be understood independently.

### P08 — Answering audience questions with evidence

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “A group says, ‘The smooth ramp made our car travel farther.’ An audience member asks, ‘How do you know it was the ramp and not a stronger push?’”

**Question:** Give an honest response using the evidence available. State what the group can claim, what remains uncertain and one improvement for the next test.

**Marking key:** Reports the observed farther travel on the smooth ramp without overstating cause (1 mark); acknowledges push strength may have differed (1 mark); says the cause is uncertain from this test (1 mark); proposes the same release method/push, same car and repeated trials (2 marks). 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Responding to critical questions while separating evidence from uncertain explanation.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Best way to share a sound

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say: “A student wants others to hear the rhythm of a frog call observed from a safe distance.”

**Question:** Which record communicates that feature most directly?

A. A colour-only chart with no labels

B. A sealed blank envelope

C. A short audio recording

D. A model that makes no sound

**Correct answer:** C — Audio preserves the sound pattern for others to hear.

**Coverage:** Matching a communication medium to auditory evidence.

### E02 — Sharing a prediction precisely

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Provide the idea: “Maybe the ball.” State that 2 balls will be dropped from the same height.

**Question:** Turn the idea into a complete prediction another group could understand.

**Marking key:** Names a specific ball and expected movement outcome, such as “I predict the rubber ball will bounce higher than the foam ball” (1 mark), and may add a relevant reason for a second mark. 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Converting fragmentary thinking into a communicable prediction.

### E03 — Listening and responding

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say: “A partner reports, ‘I counted five ants near the crumbs.’”

**Question:** Which response shows active scientific listening?

A. I was not listening, but I agree.

B. I will talk about something else now.

C. Your observation cannot matter.

D. Did you count for a set amount of time?

**Correct answer:** D — It asks a relevant clarifying question about how the evidence was collected.

**Coverage:** Selecting a constructive response to another person’s observation.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Turning notes into a result statement

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Show: “Prediction: cube rolls. Observation: cube slid 40 cm and did not roll.”

**Question:** Give one clear result sentence to share with the class.

**Marking key:** States that the cube slid 40 cm/did not roll and therefore the observation did not match the rolling prediction. Award 1 mark for accurate observation and 1 for comparison; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Synthesising recorded prediction and observation in a concise report.

### E05 — Choosing labels for a model

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Show a clay model of a flower with visible petals, stem, leaves and roots.

**Question:** Which addition would best help viewers understand the represented external features?

A. Arrows and accurate part labels

B. A secret code with no key

C. Glitter covering every feature

D. A title about toy cars

**Correct answer:** A — Accurate labels connect model parts to the observed plant features.

**Coverage:** Improving a three-dimensional scientific representation for an audience.

### E06 — Selecting evidence for a spoken claim

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “Claim: We observed more birds in the morning than at lunch. Records: morning tally 8; lunch tally 3.”

**Question:** Explain how a speaker should use the records to support the claim.

**Marking key:** States both counts accurately (1 mark) and compares them explicitly, such as 8 is 5 more than 3/more were counted in the morning (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Citing numerical observations when communicating a comparison.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Planning a complete mini-presentation

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Provide these records: “Question: Which surface lets a toy car travel farthest? Prediction: tile. Observations: tile 120 cm, carpet 45 cm, grass 30 cm.”

**Question:** Plan a one-minute presentation with an opening question, prediction, visual display, result and audience invitation. State exactly what the evidence supports.

**Marking key:** Includes the inquiry question (1 mark); prediction (1 mark); proposes an accurate labelled chart/line display of all 3 distances (2 marks); states tile travelled farthest in this test and compares with prediction (1 mark); avoids claiming tile always wins (1 mark); invites/answers questions appropriately (1 mark). 7 marks total.

**Coverage:** Structuring a multimodal scientific communication from complete investigation records.

### E08 — Evaluating two reports for trustworthiness

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read Report A: “The seed grew heaps. It was amazing.” Report B: “The shoot was 2 cm on Monday and 5 cm on Friday; the dated drawings show the change.”

**Question:** Decide which report is more useful to others. Compare its language and evidence, improve the weaker report and ask one question that would clarify the investigation.

**Marking key:** Selects B (1 mark); identifies specific measurements, dates and records as strengths (2 marks); explains A relies on vague/opinion words (1 mark); rewrites A with an observable measure/date or labelled comparison (1 mark); asks a relevant question such as how length was measured or what conditions were used (1 mark). 6 marks total.

**Coverage:** Evaluating and revising scientific communication for precision and transparency.
