# AC9SFU01 — Pass 1 question bank

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — An observable animal feature

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Show or describe a duck standing beside a pond.

**Question:** Which feature can be seen on the outside of the duck?

A. Feathers

B. A heartbeat

C. A stomach

D. Thoughts

**Correct answer:** A — Feathers are an external feature that can be observed without looking inside the animal.

**Coverage:** Distinguishing an observable external animal feature from internal or unobservable features.

### P02 — Plant parts we eat

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Show pictures of a carrot, lettuce and an apple on their plants.

**Question:** Match each food to the plant part it is: root, leaf or fruit.

**Marking key:** Carrot → root; lettuce → leaf; apple → fruit. Award 1 mark each; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Identifying familiar foods as external parts of plants.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Grouping by body covering

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide pictures or models of a cat, lizard, parrot and dog.

**Question:** Make a group with fur and a group without fur. Name the animals in each group and state the feature you used.

**Marking key:** Fur: cat, dog; without fur: lizard, parrot (2 marks); states body covering/fur as the grouping feature (1 mark). 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Sorting animals by one visible body-covering feature.

### P04 — Humans and other animals

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Show a standing child and a standing dog.

**Question:** Name one external feature both have and one external feature that is different.

**Marking key:** Gives one observable similarity, such as eyes, mouth, legs or skin covering the body (1 mark), and one observable difference, such as 2 legs versus 4, hands versus paws, or body hair/fur pattern (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Comparing human external features with those of another animal.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Looking closely at a flower

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Describe two views of the same flower: an ordinary view and a magnified view showing tiny lines on the petals and pollen grains.

**Question:** What extra details can be observed with the magnifying glass? Why are these still external features?

**Marking key:** Names a magnified detail such as petal lines or pollen (1 mark); explains it is on the outside/surface and can be seen without opening the plant (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Using magnification to identify finer external plant features.

### P06 — Two valid animal groupings

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Provide models of a butterfly, magpie, ant and kangaroo.

**Question:** Sort the animals in one way using a visible feature. Then sort the same animals in a different way and explain both rules.

**Marking key:** Gives one complete valid grouping and rule, such as wings/no wings (2 marks); gives a different valid grouping and rule, such as 2 legs/more than 2 legs or antennae/no antennae (2 marks). 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Recognising that one collection can be classified by different external features.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Where should the platypus go?

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say: “A class has a ‘has fur’ group and a ‘has a bill’ group. A platypus has both fur and a bill.”

**Question:** Where could the platypus be placed? Explain two fair choices and suggest a way to show that it belongs to both groups.

**Marking key:** Places it in the fur group with evidence (1 mark); places it in the bill group with evidence (1 mark); recognises both choices follow the stated rules (1 mark); suggests overlapping circles, a copied picture in both groups or a note/link (1 mark). 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Reasoning about an animal that meets more than one classification rule.

### P08 — Designing a plant sorting key

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Show four plants: a fern with no flowers, a daisy with flowers, a small citrus tree with flowers and fruit, and grass with narrow leaves.

**Question:** Make a two-step sorting plan using only visible features so another child can sort all four plants. Explain what to check at each step.

**Marking key:** First uses a visible feature that divides the set, such as flowers present/not present (1 mark); uses a second visible feature, such as fruit, leaf shape or stem type, to separate items further (1 mark); assigns all 4 plants consistently (2 marks); describes observable checks rather than guesses about unseen parts (1 mark). 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Constructing a simple hierarchical classification from external plant features.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Feature of a fish

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Show a clear side-view image of a fish.

**Question:** Which pair contains only external features visible in the image?

A. Lungs and bones

B. Fins and scales

C. Hunger and memory

D. Heart and blood

**Correct answer:** B — Fins and scales can be observed on the outside of the fish.

**Coverage:** Identifying two external animal features.

### E02 — Naming a plant feature

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Show a seedling with roots visible through a clear container.

**Question:** Name two plant parts you can observe and describe one visible feature of each.

**Marking key:** Names 2 visible parts, such as roots, stem or leaves (1 mark each), and describes a relevant visible feature for each, such as root length/branching, stem colour or leaf shape (1 mark each). 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Naming and describing observable features of a young plant.

### E03 — Choosing a grouping rule

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Show pictures of a snail, spider, beetle and worm.

**Question:** Which rule would place the spider and beetle together but the snail and worm together in the other group?

A. Is alive / is not alive

B. Is small / is large

C. Has visible legs / has no visible legs

D. Lives outside / lives inside

**Correct answer:** C — The spider and beetle have visible legs; the snail and worm do not.

**Coverage:** Selecting a consistent external-feature rule for a given grouping.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Checking an inaccurate sort

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say: “A child labels a group ‘animals with wings’ and puts a bat, eagle and mouse in it.”

**Question:** Which animal does not follow the rule? Move it to a suitable group and explain using an external feature.

**Marking key:** Identifies mouse (1 mark); moves it to a group such as animals without wings/animals with fur (1 mark); explains that a mouse has no wings, while the bat and eagle do (1 mark). 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Evaluating and repairing a classification using visible evidence.

### E05 — Comparing two leaves

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Describe Leaf A as long with smooth edges and Leaf B as round with jagged edges.

**Question:** State one similarity and two differences that could be used to describe or group the leaves.

**Marking key:** Gives a visible similarity, such as both are leaves/green/have a central vein if described (1 mark), plus differences in shape and edge (1 mark each). 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Comparing multiple external plant features precisely.

### E06 — Choosing evidence from a close-up

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Show a magnified photograph of a moth wing with tiny overlapping scales.

**Question:** Which observation is supported by the close-up?

A. The wing is heavier than the moth’s body.

B. The moth likes warm nights best.

C. The moth can remember every flower.

D. The wing surface has many tiny overlapping pieces.

**Correct answer:** D — It reports a visible external detail; the other claims are not shown.

**Coverage:** Separating a magnified observation from unsupported claims.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Classifying a mixed collection

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Provide images of a rose, pine seedling, chicken, turtle, strawberry plant and rabbit.

**Question:** First separate plants from animals using external evidence. Then divide each set into 2 smaller groups using a different visible feature. Label every group and justify each rule.

**Marking key:** Correctly separates 3 plants and 3 animals (2 marks); makes 2 valid plant subgroups with a stated visible rule (2 marks); makes 2 valid animal subgroups with a stated visible rule (2 marks); places all items consistently and uses observable evidence (1 mark). 7 marks total.

**Coverage:** Applying successive external-feature rules across plants and animals.

### E08 — Comparing classification systems

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say: “Ravi groups a duck, penguin and kookaburra together because they have feathers. Ava groups the duck and penguin together because they swim, leaving the kookaburra separate.”

**Question:** Are both systems useful? Explain what each system shows, identify which rule is based directly on an external feature and suggest one question to decide which system to use.

**Marking key:** Recognises both as useful for different purposes (1 mark); explains Ravi’s feather rule (1 mark); explains Ava’s movement/behaviour rule (1 mark); identifies feathers as the directly observable external feature (1 mark); gives a relevant purpose question such as “Are we comparing body coverings or how they move?” (1 mark). 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Evaluating classifications according to feature type and purpose.
