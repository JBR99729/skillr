# AC9SFH01 — Pass 1 question bank

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Observation or question?

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read the options aloud while showing a garden snail.

**Question:** Which sentence is an observation rather than a question?

A. What will the snail eat?

B. Where does the snail sleep?

C. Why does the snail move slowly?

D. The snail has two long feelers.

**Correct answer:** D — It reports a feature that can be seen; the other sentences ask for information.

**Coverage:** Distinguishing a direct observation from a scientific question.

### P02 — A tool for a closer view

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say: “A person wants to see the tiny lines on a leaf without damaging it.”

**Question:** Which tool would best extend their sight?

A. Magnifying glass

B. Paintbrush

C. Ruler used as a drumstick

D. Closed lunchbox

**Correct answer:** A — A magnifying glass makes small visible details easier to observe.

**Coverage:** Identifying equipment that helps people make detailed observations.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Using more than one sense

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Play a safe recording of a frog call while showing a photograph of the frog. State that students will not touch or taste the animal.

**Question:** Give one observation from sight and one from hearing. How does using both help you learn more?

**Marking key:** Gives an accurate visual observation from the image (1 mark); gives an accurate sound observation from the recording (1 mark); explains that the senses provide different/complementary information (1 mark). 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Combining sensory evidence to extend knowledge of an animal.

### P04 — Recording what was noticed

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Read three observations: (a) bird feather colours, (b) a bird call, (c) number of birds at a feeder. Provide recording methods: colour drawing, sound recording, tally marks.

**Question:** Match each observation to the recording method that would preserve it best.

**Marking key:** (a) colour drawing; (b) sound recording; (c) tally marks. Award 1 mark each; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Selecting recording methods suited to different kinds of observations.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Turning noticing into a question

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say: “A child notices that one garden patch has many worms and another has few.”

**Question:** Ask a question that could help the child learn more from this observation.

**Marking key:** Gives a relevant open question linked to the observation, such as “Why are there more worms in one patch?” or “Is the soil wetter where more worms are found?” Award 2 marks for a clear, investigable link; 1 mark for a relevant but broad question.

**Coverage:** Generating a scientific question from an observed pattern.

### P06 — How a wildlife scientist learns

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Describe: “A wildlife scientist sits quietly at a distance, takes timed photographs of chimpanzees and writes what each animal does.”

**Question:** Name two ways the scientist makes or records observations and explain why keeping a record is useful.

**Marking key:** Identifies any 2 of quiet watching, distance viewing, timed photographs or written notes (1 mark each); explains that records can be checked, compared or shared later (1 mark). 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Recognising observation and recording practices used by scientists.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Observation, inference and question

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Show a photograph of wet ground with several bird footprints but no bird.

**Question:** State one observation that the image proves, one idea that might explain it and one question that could test or explore your idea. Label each part.

**Marking key:** Observation reports visible wet ground/footprints without claiming an unseen event (1 mark); idea/inference is plausible, such as a bird walked there (1 mark); question is relevant, such as which bird made the tracks or when they appeared (1 mark); labels the three kinds of statement correctly (1 mark). 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Separating observed evidence, tentative explanation and further question.

### P08 — Planning to observe a tree over time

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say: “A class wants to learn how one tree changes across four weeks.”

**Question:** Plan what they should observe, which tools or senses they could use, how they should record each visit and one question the records may help answer.

**Marking key:** Names relevant changing features such as leaf number/colour, buds, flowers, animals or height of new growth (1 mark); selects safe senses/tools such as sight, camera, magnifier or ruler with adult guidance (1 mark); proposes a consistent dated drawing/photo/table (1 mark); asks a question answerable by the records (1 mark); includes observing the same tree/feature at regular times for comparison (1 mark). 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Designing repeated observations and records to answer a natural-world question.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — A useful scientific question

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Show two seedlings, one beside a sunny window and one farther away.

**Question:** Which sentence asks something that observations could help answer?

A. Seedlings are always perfect.

B. Which seedling grows more over the next week?

C. Green is everyone’s favourite colour.

D. Plants are nicer than rocks.

**Correct answer:** B — Growth can be observed and recorded over time.

**Coverage:** Recognising an observation-based question about the natural world.

### E02 — Choosing an observation record

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say: “A naturalist wants to remember the exact pattern of spots on a beetle.”

**Question:** Choose a suitable way to record the observation and explain why it fits.

**Marking key:** Chooses a close photograph or detailed labelled/colour drawing (1 mark) and explains that it preserves the visible spot pattern for later checking/sharing (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Matching a visual observation to an enduring record.

### E03 — Information from hearing

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Play or describe two different bird-call recordings with no image.

**Question:** What can be directly observed from the recordings?

A. One bird has a longer beak.

B. Both birds have blue wings.

C. The calls differ in pitch, rhythm or length.

D. The birds built nests in the same tree.

**Correct answer:** C — Sound qualities can be heard; the visual and location claims are not available.

**Coverage:** Identifying what auditory evidence can and cannot show.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Improving a vague report

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “I looked at the rock. It was interesting.”

**Question:** Explain why this is not a detailed observation and replace it with a sentence containing two observable rock features.

**Marking key:** Notes that “interesting” gives an opinion but no specific evidence (1 mark); supplies 2 visible or safely felt features, such as grey, speckled, rough, smooth, layered or rounded (1 mark each). 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Replacing subjective commentary with specific observations.

### E05 — Adding equipment to human senses

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say: “A person can see the Moon but wants to observe more surface detail.”

**Question:** Which action is most useful and safe?

A. Stare directly at the Sun instead.

B. Taste a picture of the Moon.

C. Shout at the Moon and wait for an answer.

D. View it through a suitable telescope with adult guidance.

**Correct answer:** D — A telescope extends sight; the other actions provide no useful evidence or are unsafe.

**Coverage:** Explaining how observation equipment extends a sense safely.

### E06 — Linking records to a new question

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “Photographs taken each morning show a flower opening wider from Monday to Wednesday and closing again by Friday.”

**Question:** State the pattern in the observations and ask one new question prompted by it.

**Marking key:** Accurately states the opening-then-closing pattern over the days (1 mark); asks a relevant question, such as whether light, time, water or flower age affects opening (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Using recorded observations to recognise change and generate further inquiry.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Selecting methods for a pond study

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say: “A class will observe a pond from behind a safety barrier. They want to learn which animals visit and what sounds are heard.”

**Question:** Plan a safe observation method using at least two tools or senses. Include records for animal visits and sounds, and write two questions the evidence could help answer.

**Marking key:** Maintains the barrier/follows adult safety directions (1 mark); uses suitable methods such as sight/binoculars/camera and hearing/audio recorder (2 marks); proposes a tally/drawing/photo record for visits and a separate sound record (2 marks); asks 2 answerable questions linked to the evidence (2 marks). 7 marks total.

**Coverage:** Integrating safe multimodal observations, records and questions.

### E08 — Evaluating two observers’ claims

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “Observer A writes, ‘The ant carried a crumb for 20 seconds before dropping it.’ Observer B writes, ‘The ant dropped it because it was lazy.’ Both watched the same event.”

**Question:** Decide which statement is a direct observation. Explain the problem with the other statement, rewrite it as an observation and add a useful question.

**Marking key:** Identifies A as direct observation (1 mark); explains that “lazy” guesses an unseen reason/judges the ant (1 mark); rewrites B using visible action only, such as “The ant dropped the crumb” (1 mark); asks a relevant question such as whether crumb size affects carrying time (1 mark); distinguishes evidence from explanation clearly (1 mark). 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Critiquing claims and converting inference into evidence and inquiry.
