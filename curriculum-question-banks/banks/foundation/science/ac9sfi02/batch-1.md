# AC9SFI02 — Pass 1 question bank

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — A safe investigation choice

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say: “The class is carrying magnifying glasses outside.”

**Question:** Which action is safest?

A. Run while looking through the lens.

B. Walk, hold the tool carefully and follow the teacher’s directions.

C. Point the lens towards the Sun.

D. Leave the school area without an adult.

**Correct answer:** B — It controls the equipment and follows supervision; the other actions create clear risks.

**Coverage:** Selecting safe conduct during an outdoor investigation.

### P02 — Why science does not use taste here

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Show sealed samples of an unknown soil, leaf and powder. State that they are for observation only.

**Question:** Which sense must not be used on these samples, and why?

**Correct answer:** Do not taste them because unknown materials may be harmful, dirty or unsafe to eat.

**Coverage:** Explaining why taste is excluded from material observations.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Matching senses to observations

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Provide safe examples: viewing shell stripes, listening to a rain recording and gently feeling a teacher-approved smooth stone.

**Question:** Match each observation to the main sense used: sight, hearing or touch.

**Marking key:** Shell stripes → sight; rain recording → hearing; smooth stone → touch. Award 1 mark each; 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Identifying the senses used for different safe observations.

### P04 — Recording without touching wildlife

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say: “A butterfly lands on a flower inside the marked garden boundary.”

**Question:** What is the best way to observe and record it safely?

A. Taste the flower it landed on.

B. Grab its wings for a closer look.

C. Watch from the path and take a photograph or drawing.

D. Chase it through the garden beds.

**Correct answer:** C — It gathers visual evidence while protecting the observer, animal and garden.

**Coverage:** Observing living things safely and non-intrusively.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Using a magnifier correctly

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide a magnifying glass and a fallen leaf on a tray. Ensure the activity is away from direct sunlight.

**Question:** Demonstrate how to obtain a clear view, then state one new detail you observe.

**Marking key:** Keeps the tool away from eyes/Sun and adjusts distance rather than running/moving unsafely (1 mark); handles the sample as directed (1 mark); reports a specific magnified detail such as veins, edge or surface marks (1 mark). 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Safely operating observation equipment and reporting evidence.

### P06 — Planning a sound observation

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say: “The class will listen for birds in the schoolyard for two minutes.”

**Question:** Give two safety or fairness rules and one way to record what is heard.

**Marking key:** Gives 2 suitable rules, such as remain in marked area, stay with adult/group, stand still, listen quietly or use the same time (1 mark each); proposes tally marks, sound words, voice recording or teacher-provided table (1 mark). 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Combining safe behaviour, consistent observation and recording.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Repairing an unsafe plan

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “To investigate a mystery liquid, a child plans to taste it, smell it closely, splash it on their skin and run to fetch a cup.”

**Question:** Identify four unsafe actions and replace them with a safe teacher-directed observation plan.

**Marking key:** Identifies tasting, close smelling, skin contact/splashing and running as unsafe (up to 4 marks); proposes leaving it sealed or following teacher instructions, observing visually from a safe distance and recording visible features without contact (up to 2 marks). 6 marks total.

**Coverage:** Critiquing multiple hazards and redesigning an investigation safely.

### P08 — Choosing senses and tools for a tree

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say: “Students will study a tree without climbing, breaking branches or leaving the path.”

**Question:** Plan three safe observations using different senses or tools. State what each could reveal and one boundary that protects people or the tree.

**Marking key:** Gives 3 appropriate observations, such as sight/binoculars for birds, hearing for calls, gentle touch of a reachable fallen bark sample, camera for records or magnifier for a fallen leaf (3 marks); states what each reveals (up to 2 marks); includes a clear boundary such as stay on path, no climbing/breaking/tasting and adult supervision (1 mark). 6 marks total.

**Coverage:** Designing a varied, ethical and safe natural-world observation.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Sun-safe fieldwork

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say: “The class will observe plants outside on a sunny day.”

**Question:** Which preparation is appropriate?

A. Wander beyond the marked area to find rare plants.

B. Look directly at the Sun to judge its brightness.

C. Remove protective clothing to feel more sunlight.

D. Follow the school’s sun-safety plan, bring water and stay with the group.

**Correct answer:** D — It follows established protection and supervision procedures.

**Coverage:** Recognising sun-safe and supervised investigation practice.

### E02 — Observation from touch

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Provide a sealed texture board with a smooth section and a rough section approved for touch.

**Question:** Make one comparison using touch and describe how you used the board safely.

**Marking key:** States which section feels smoother/rougher (1 mark); describes gentle touch and following the teacher’s direction/using only the approved board (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Reporting tactile evidence within a safe procedure.

### E03 — Tool for distant viewing

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say: “A bird is resting high in a tree. Students must remain on the ground.”

**Question:** Which tool helps them observe safely?

A. Binoculars

B. A climbing rope used without an adult

C. A tasting spoon

D. A sharp probe

**Correct answer:** A — Binoculars extend sight while students remain at a safe distance.

**Coverage:** Selecting equipment that reduces risk while extending observation.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Why close smelling is unsafe

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Show a sealed container labelled “Unknown material — teacher use only”.

**Question:** Explain why a student should not open it or place their nose over it. What may they safely record?

**Marking key:** Explains the unknown material/fumes could be harmful and teacher directions must be followed (1 mark); states they may record safe visible features of the sealed container/sample, such as colour, level or particles if visible (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Applying sensory safety limits to an unknown substance.

### E05 — Safe digital recording

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say: “A tablet will be used to photograph a line of ants near the path.”

**Question:** Which method is best?

A. Place the tablet on the ants for a close-up.

B. Stand on the path, hold the tablet securely and zoom without blocking or touching the ants.

C. Walk backwards without looking while filming.

D. Follow the ants into an out-of-bounds area.

**Correct answer:** B — It protects the student, equipment and animals while collecting a visual record.

**Coverage:** Using digital observation equipment safely around living things.

### E06 — Selecting a complete record

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say: “During a two-minute listening task, a child hears a low croak 4 times and a high chirp 7 times.”

**Question:** Show how the child could record both the type and number of sounds without relying on memory.

**Marking key:** Creates a labelled tally/table/dot record with croak = 4 and chirp = 7 (2 marks); includes the 2-minute observation period or another identifying label (1 mark). 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Recording sensory observations accurately in a simple representation.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Risk-checking a mini investigation

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “Students want to compare wet and dry soil. The samples are on trays, but one student plans to taste them; another plans to rub soil near their eyes; a third wants to use a magnifier at the sunny window.”

**Question:** Create a safe procedure that still allows useful sight and touch observations. Address each risk, include safe tool use and name what evidence will be recorded.

**Marking key:** Prohibits tasting and contact near eyes (2 marks); uses only teacher-approved gentle touch with handwashing or tools as directed (1 mark); moves magnifier away from direct Sun/uses it safely (1 mark); keeps samples on trays and follows supervision (1 mark); records relevant moisture appearance, clumping, texture or particle observations (2 marks). 7 marks total.

**Coverage:** Integrating hazard control with purposeful sensory observation.

### E08 — Explaining limits of the senses

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say: “A sealed jar contains a clear liquid. One child says, ‘It looks like water, so it is safe.’”

**Question:** Explain why sight alone cannot prove identity or safety. State what a Foundation student may observe, what they must not do and who should decide any further testing.

**Marking key:** Explains that different liquids can look alike and appearance does not prove safety (2 marks); gives safe visual observations such as clear/colourless, bubbles or level without claiming identity (1 mark); states no opening, tasting, touching or close smelling (1 mark); assigns further decisions/testing to the teacher or qualified adult using approved procedures (1 mark). 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Reasoning about evidence limits and adult-controlled safety decisions.
