# AC9SFI04 — Pass 1 question bank

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Same or different?

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read: “Prediction: The marble will roll to the end of the ramp. Observation: The marble rolled to the end.”

**Question:** How do the observation and prediction compare?

A. The prediction changed the marble.

B. They are opposite.

C. No observation was made.

D. They match.

**Correct answer:** D — Both state that the marble reaches the end.

**Coverage:** Identifying a simple match between prediction and observation.

### P02 — Prediction comes first

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Provide the cards: **make a prediction**, **observe the test**, **compare the two**.

**Question:** Put the three actions in a sensible investigation order.

**Correct answer:** Make a prediction → observe the test → compare the two.

**Coverage:** Sequencing prediction, evidence collection and comparison.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — A bounce that did not match

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “Prediction: The cloth ball will bounce 3 times. Observation: It did not bounce.”

**Question:** State one clear difference between the prediction and observation.

**Correct answer:** The prediction expected 3 bounces, but the observation recorded 0/no bounces.

**Coverage:** Expressing a numerical mismatch between expectation and result.

### P04 — Completing a comparison table

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Show two rows: (a) predicted 2 birds, observed 2 birds; (b) predicted a flower, observed only leaves. Provide labels **matched** and **did not match**.

**Question:** Place the correct comparison label beside each row.

**Marking key:** (a) matched; (b) did not match. Award 1 mark each; 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Classifying prediction–observation pairs in a provided template.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Partly matching a prediction

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “Prediction: We will see ants and butterflies near the flowers. Observation: We saw ants and bees, but no butterflies.”

**Question:** Which part matched and which parts differed?

**Marking key:** Ants matched (1 mark); butterflies were predicted but not observed (1 mark); bees were observed but not predicted (1 mark). 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Recognising partial agreement and unexpected observations.

### P06 — Choosing evidence over memory

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say: “A child remembers predicting that the cube would slide, but their dated prediction card says ‘roll’. The cube was observed sliding.”

**Question:** Which comparison should the class record?

A. The written prediction did not match the sliding observation.

B. It matched because the child now remembers “slide”.

C. The observation should be changed to “roll”.

D. The prediction card should be hidden.

**Correct answer:** A — The recorded prediction is the evidence to compare with the recorded observation.

**Coverage:** Using contemporaneous records to make an honest comparison.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Learning from an unexpected result

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “Prediction: The large rubber ball will bounce higher than the small rubber ball. Observation: The small ball bounced higher in all three trials.”

**Question:** Compare the result with the prediction, explain why the prediction should not be rewritten and suggest one next question.

**Marking key:** States that the result did not match and specifies the reversed outcome (2 marks); explains the original prediction must remain as an honest before-test record (1 mark); asks a relevant next question about size, air pressure, drop consistency or another factor (1 mark); treats the result as information rather than failure (1 mark). 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Responding scientifically to repeatable evidence that contradicts a prediction.

### P08 — Comparing a detailed field prediction

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “Prediction: Under the logs we will find more than 5 small animals, mostly slaters. Observation: We found 7 small animals—4 slaters, 2 worms and 1 beetle.”

**Question:** Break the prediction into its parts and decide which parts matched, partly matched or did not match. Use the numbers and animal names as evidence.

**Marking key:** Quantity prediction matched because 7 is more than 5 (1 mark); slaters were observed as predicted (1 mark); explains “mostly slaters” is supported because 4 of 7 is more than each other type and over half, with guidance (2 marks); notes worms and beetle were additional observations not named (1 mark). 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Comparing multi-part predictions with categorical and numerical observations.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Selecting the observation

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read: “Before the test: ‘I think the metal spoon will feel colder.’ After touching teacher-approved room-temperature samples: ‘The metal spoon felt colder than the wooden spoon.’”

**Question:** Which statement is the observation?

A. I think the metal spoon will feel colder.

B. The metal spoon felt colder than the wooden spoon.

C. Spoons are useful.

D. Wood is always warm.

**Correct answer:** B — It reports what was noticed during the safe comparison.

**Coverage:** Distinguishing a post-test observation from a prediction.

### E02 — Comparing colours found

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “Prediction: We will find red and yellow leaves. Observation: We found red, yellow and brown leaves.”

**Question:** State what matched and what was unexpected.

**Marking key:** Red and yellow matched the prediction (1 mark); brown was observed but not predicted (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Identifying matched and additional categories in an observation.

### E03 — Honest conclusion language

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read: “Predicted: the paper boat would float for 1 minute. Observed: it sank after 20 seconds.”

**Question:** Which sentence compares the records accurately?

A. The record should say 1 minute to avoid a mistake.

B. The prediction was correct because boats always float.

C. The observation did not match; the boat sank sooner than predicted.

D. No comparison can be made.

**Correct answer:** C — It retains and directly compares both recorded times.

**Coverage:** Selecting accurate language for a non-matching result.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Comparing shape predictions

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “Prediction: the cone will roll straight. Observation: it rolled in a curved path.”

**Question:** Use the words **same** and **different** to compare the movement and path.

**Marking key:** States that both prediction and observation involved rolling (same, 1 mark), but straight versus curved path differed (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Separating a matching movement type from a mismatching path detail.

### E05 — Reading a guided table

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Show: Plant A predicted 2 new leaves, observed 2; Plant B predicted 3, observed 1; Plant C predicted 0, observed 0.

**Question:** Which plants had observations that exactly matched their predictions?

A. Plant B only

B. Plants A and B

C. Plants B and C

D. Plants A and C

**Correct answer:** D — A has 2 = 2 and C has 0 = 0; B has 1 rather than 3.

**Coverage:** Comparing several prediction–observation pairs in a table.

### E06 — When the observation record is incomplete

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say: “Prediction: The toy car will travel farther on tile than carpet. Observation note: ‘The car moved.’ No distances or comparison were recorded.”

**Question:** Can the class decide whether the prediction matched? Explain what observation was missing.

**Marking key:** Says no/not yet (1 mark); explains that the tile and carpet travel distances or a clear farther/shorter comparison were not recorded (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Recognising when evidence is insufficient for comparison.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Comparing four-part weather predictions

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “Prediction for the morning: cloudy, no rain, strong wind, 12°C. Observation: cloudy, light rain, gentle wind, 12°C.”

**Question:** Create a four-row comparison showing which parts matched and which differed. Write one precise summary sentence and one new question.

**Marking key:** Cloud matched (1 mark); temperature matched (1 mark); rain differed (1 mark); wind strength differed (1 mark); summary accurately notes 2 matches and 2 differences (1 mark); asks a relevant follow-up question (1 mark). 6 marks total.

**Coverage:** Systematically comparing a multi-variable prediction with observations.

### E08 — Resolving two students’ comparisons

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “Prediction: 5 snails will be found. Observation 1: first search found 3. Observation 2: second equal-length search found 2 more different snails. Sam says the prediction did not match because 3 were found first. Lee says it matched because 5 were found altogether.”

**Question:** Decide which comparison fits the stated investigation period. Explain the role of both records and how the class should label the total to avoid confusion.

**Marking key:** Chooses Lee if both searches were part of the defined investigation (1 mark); combines 3 + 2 = 5 without double-counting (1 mark); explains Sam used only the first partial record (1 mark); labels the total as 5 snails across 2 equal-length searches/the full investigation (1 mark); notes the conclusion would differ if the prediction was specifically for one search (1 mark). 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Resolving a prediction comparison by defining the observation period and combining records correctly.
