# AC9SFI01 — Pass 1 question bank

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Finding a question

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read each sentence with its natural intonation.

**Question:** Which sentence asks for something to be found out?

A. Which ball will bounce highest?

B. The red ball is round.

C. I bounced a ball yesterday.

D. This ball feels soft.

**Correct answer:** A — It is a question that an investigation could explore.

**Coverage:** Recognising a scientific question among statements and observations.

### P02 — A prediction before looking

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Say: “You are about to look under a damp garden pot. Think about what you have noticed in similar places.”

**Question:** Predict one living thing you might observe and give a reason from experience.

**Marking key:** Gives a plausible organism, such as worm, slug, ant or small plant (1 mark), and a relevant reason based on damp, dark or sheltered places previously observed (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Making a reasoned prediction from a familiar experience.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Asking about a vegetable part

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Show broccoli with stem, leaves and flowering head.

**Question:** Ask one question that would help you learn which plant part people eat.

**Marking key:** Gives a relevant question, such as “Is the part we eat a flower, leaf or stem?” Award 2 marks for a clear question about the plant part; 1 mark for a related but broad question.

**Coverage:** Posing a focused question from an everyday plant experience.

### P04 — Predicting movement down a ramp

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Show a ball and a flat block at the top of one ramp.

**Question:** Predict how each object will move after the same gentle push. Use their shapes to explain your predictions.

**Marking key:** Predicts ball rolls and block slides/tumbles or moves less smoothly (2 marks); links ball to curved surface and block to flat faces (2 marks). 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Making comparative movement predictions from prior shape knowledge.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Improving a question about materials

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say: “A child asks, ‘What about these cups?’ The cups are paper, metal and plastic.”

**Question:** Which revision is a clearer question for an investigation?

A. Are cups good?

B. Which cup material keeps water from leaking for ten minutes?

C. Why is everything made?

D. What is my favourite cup?

**Correct answer:** B — It identifies the materials, an observable outcome and a time for comparison.

**Coverage:** Refining a vague prompt into a focused, observable question.

### P06 — Predicting a schoolyard observation

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say: “The class will count animals seen in a sunny grass area and under shaded bushes.”

**Question:** Predict which place may have more small animals. Give a reason based on something you have seen or learnt.

**Marking key:** Makes either plausible place prediction (1 mark) and gives a relevant experience-based reason, such as shelter/moisture under bushes or insects visiting sunny flowers in grass (1 mark). Prediction is not marked by whether later observations match. 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Supporting a fieldwork prediction with prior experience.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Question, prediction and reason

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Show two toy cars with different-sized wheels and one ramp.

**Question:** Pose a question about the wheels and movement. Make a prediction that answers your question before testing, and explain the experience or observation behind it.

**Marking key:** Poses a clear comparative question (1 mark); gives a prediction that directly answers it (1 mark); provides a relevant reason from prior rolling/toy experience (1 mark); keeps the statement testable through movement observation (1 mark). 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Building a coherent inquiry sequence from question to reasoned prediction.

### P08 — Predictions can differ

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say: “Kai predicts an egg-shaped object will roll straight because it is curved. Noor predicts it will wobble because it is not equally round on every side.”

**Question:** Explain why both are predictions rather than observations. Decide which reason is more detailed, then state what the class must do to find out.

**Marking key:** Explains that both statements are made before the test (1 mark); recognises Noor’s reason considers the uneven shape, while accepting Kai used a real curved feature (1 mark); states the object must be rolled under agreed conditions and its path observed/recorded (1 mark); notes that evidence, not confidence, decides (1 mark). 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Evaluating competing predictions and identifying the need for evidence.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Prediction or observation?

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say: “Before opening a seed pod, Ari says, ‘I think there will be six seeds inside.’”

**Question:** What kind of statement is Ari making?

A. A measurement result

B. A completed observation

C. A prediction

D. A safety rule

**Correct answer:** C — Ari states what they expect before looking inside.

**Coverage:** Identifying a prediction by its timing and purpose.

### E02 — Asking about a ball

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Show a foam ball and a rubber ball of the same size.

**Question:** Pose one question about how their materials may affect movement.

**Marking key:** Gives a comparative, observable question such as “Which ball will bounce higher when dropped from the same height?” Award 2 marks for including the material comparison and movement outcome; 1 mark if relevant but incomplete.

**Coverage:** Posing an investigable question about material and movement.

### E03 — Experience supporting a prediction

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Read: “I predict the ice cube will make the metal tray feel colder.”

**Question:** Which reason best shows the prediction is based on experience?

A. Every prediction must be correct.

B. Metal is my favourite colour.

C. I have not looked at the tray.

D. Last time ice touched a spoon, the spoon became cold.

**Correct answer:** D — It links a similar past observation to the new expectation.

**Coverage:** Selecting relevant experiential evidence for a prediction.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Turning an observation into a question

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “After rain, many mushrooms appeared near one tree but none near the path.”

**Question:** Ask one focused question prompted by this difference.

**Marking key:** Gives a relevant question about conditions or location, such as “Is the soil wetter near the tree than beside the path?” or “Do mushrooms grow more in shade?” Award 2 marks for a focused comparison; 1 mark for a relevant broad question.

**Coverage:** Generating inquiry from a contrasting natural-world observation.

### E05 — Matching a prediction to a question

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** State the question: “Which surface lets the toy car travel farthest: tile, carpet or grass?”

**Question:** Which prediction answers that exact question?

A. The car will travel farthest on tile because it is smooth.

B. The car is blue and has four wheels.

C. Carpet feels soft under my hand.

D. How fast can a bicycle travel?

**Correct answer:** A — It names one expected result and gives a relevant surface-based reason.

**Coverage:** Checking that a prediction directly responds to an inquiry question.

### E06 — Making a prediction measurable

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read: “I think the plant by the window will do better.”

**Question:** Rewrite the prediction so the class knows exactly what to observe during one week.

**Marking key:** Gives a measurable/observable outcome and time, such as “After one week, the window plant will have more new leaves than the cupboard plant.” Award 2 marks for outcome and comparison/time; 1 mark for a clearer but incomplete revision.

**Coverage:** Refining an imprecise prediction into an observable expectation.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Planning inquiry from playground experience

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say: “Children have noticed that puddles disappear at different times in sunny and shady places.”

**Question:** Write a comparative question, predict which puddle will disappear first, give an experience-based reason and name two observations needed to check the prediction.

**Marking key:** Gives a focused sunny/shady question (1 mark); makes a clear prediction (1 mark); provides a relevant heat/sunlight or prior-puddle reason (1 mark); identifies 2 useful observations such as equal starting size and time until each disappears or repeated size records (2 marks). 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Developing a complete question–prediction–evidence plan from experience.

### E08 — Analysing reasons behind two predictions

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “For a seed test, Mia predicts the watered pot will sprout first because plants she has cared for needed water. Leo predicts the unwatered pot will sprout first because he likes its blue label.”

**Question:** Compare the two reasons, decide which prediction is better grounded in experience and explain how a fair observation could still surprise both students.

**Marking key:** Selects Mia’s reason as relevant to seed/plant needs (1 mark); explains that label preference does not support sprouting (1 mark); describes observing equivalent pots that differ only in planned watering under safe conditions (2 marks); explains predictions are not guaranteed and observations may differ (1 mark). 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Evaluating relevance of experience-based reasons without treating predictions as facts.
