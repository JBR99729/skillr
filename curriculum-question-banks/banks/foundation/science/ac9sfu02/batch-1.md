# AC9SFU02 — Pass 1 question bank

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Naming a movement

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Show a ball travelling across the floor while turning over and over.

**Question:** Which word best describes the ball’s movement?

A. Melting

B. Rolling

C. Stretching

D. Floating

**Correct answer:** B — An object rolls when it travels while turning over its curved surface.

**Coverage:** Using an accurate word to describe observable movement.

### P02 — Shape on a slope

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Show a wooden sphere and a wooden cube at the top of the same gentle ramp.

**Question:** Predict which object will roll more easily. Name the shape feature that supports your answer.

**Marking key:** Predicts the sphere (1 mark) and links the prediction to its curved/round surface rather than its colour (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Relating a curved shape to likely rolling movement.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Sorting toys by movement

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide or describe a spinning top, toy car, sliding block and bouncing ball.

**Question:** Group the toys by their main movement: spins, rolls, slides or bounces. Demonstrate or describe each movement.

**Marking key:** Top → spins; car → rolls; block → slides; ball → bounces (1 mark each). Accept that an object can move in another way if the child demonstrates it and labels the chosen movement accurately. 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Grouping familiar objects according to observed movement.

### P04 — Comparing similar shapes of different sizes

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say: “A small rubber ball and a large rubber ball are released together down the same ramp.”

**Question:** What should you observe to compare how their sizes influence movement? Name two movement details to record.

**Marking key:** Names 2 observable measures/descriptions, such as which reaches the bottom first, distance travelled, path, rolling speed or number/height of bounces after the ramp (1 mark each). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Identifying observations needed to compare the movement of different-sized objects.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Material affects bouncing

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Describe 2 same-sized balls: one solid rubber and one soft cloth. Both are dropped from the same height.

**Question:** Which prediction is most reasonable, and why?

A. Both must bounce exactly the same because they are round.

B. The cloth ball will turn into rubber when it lands.

C. The rubber ball will probably bounce higher because its material springs back more readily.

D. Neither can move because their colours are unknown.

**Correct answer:** C — Keeping size and drop height alike lets the material be considered, and rubber commonly rebounds more than soft cloth.

**Coverage:** Predicting how material may affect rebound while other factors are controlled.

### P06 — An egg-shaped object on a ramp

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Show an egg-shaped plastic object rolling down a slope.

**Question:** Describe its likely path and compare it with the path of a round marble.

**Marking key:** Describes the egg shape as wobbling, curving or rolling unevenly (1 mark); describes the marble as rolling more smoothly/straight (1 mark); links the difference to shape (1 mark). 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Comparing movement paths produced by regular and uneven curved shapes.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Planning a fair material test

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say: “You want to find out whether rubber, foam or cloth balls bounce differently.”

**Question:** Plan a fair test. State what must stay the same, what will change and what observation you will record.

**Marking key:** Changes ball material (1 mark); keeps relevant factors the same, including ball size/shape, drop height and surface (up to 3 marks); records bounce height or another consistent movement observation (1 mark); repeats or compares in the same way (1 mark). 6 marks total.

**Coverage:** Designing a controlled comparison of material and movement.

### P08 — Explaining a surprising race

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Say: “A large smooth ball reaches the bottom of a ramp before a small crumpled paper ball. A child says, ‘Large things always move faster.’”

**Question:** Explain why that conclusion is not yet fair. Name two factors besides size that could have influenced the race and suggest a better comparison.

**Marking key:** Rejects the “always” claim because more than size differs (1 mark); identifies shape and material/surface interaction as possible factors (2 marks); suggests comparing objects of similar shape and material but different size, or changing one factor at a time (2 marks). 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Identifying confounded movement factors and improving a comparison.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — How a toy car moves

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Show a toy car pushed across a flat floor.

**Question:** Which part mainly allows the car to roll?

A. Its seat colour

B. Its painted windows

C. Its sticker

D. Its wheels

**Correct answer:** D — The round wheels rotate against the floor, allowing rolling movement.

**Coverage:** Linking a visible shape feature to an object’s movement.

### E02 — Describing a tube

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Roll a cardboard tube first on its curved side and then place it upright on a flat end.

**Question:** Describe how its movement changes when a different surface touches the floor.

**Marking key:** States that it rolls on the curved side (1 mark) but rests, tips or slides rather than rolling freely on the flat end (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Describing how orientation changes the movement of one shape.

### E03 — Factor that has changed

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say: “Two wooden balls have the same shape. One is small and one is large.”

**Question:** Which factor is different?

A. Size

B. Shape

C. Material

D. Ramp

**Correct answer:** A — Size is the stated difference; both are wooden balls of the same shape.

**Coverage:** Identifying the movement factor varied in a comparison.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Reading bounce observations

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Read the results: “Rubber ball: high bounce. Foam ball: low bounce. Both were the same size and dropped from the same height.”

**Question:** What difference was observed, and which object feature may explain it?

**Marking key:** States rubber bounced higher than foam (1 mark) and identifies material as the relevant differing feature (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Linking recorded movement differences to material.

### E05 — Predicting a block’s movement

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Show a rectangular wooden block placed flat at the top of a ramp.

**Question:** What is it most likely to do after a gentle push?

A. Bounce repeatedly like a rubber ball

B. Slide down on a flat face

C. Roll smoothly like a marble

D. Float upward from the ramp

**Correct answer:** B — A flat-faced block is likely to slide rather than roll smoothly.

**Coverage:** Predicting movement from flat surfaces and rigid shape.

### E06 — Improving a ball-size comparison

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say: “A child rolls a small glass marble on tiles and a large foam ball on carpet to test size.”

**Question:** Why can this test not show the effect of size alone? Give one change that would make it fairer.

**Marking key:** Explains that material and surface also differ (1 mark); suggests keeping material, shape and surface the same while changing size (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Recognising and correcting multiple changed variables in a movement test.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Classifying movement with evidence

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Provide a coin, toy wheel, eraser and soft beanbag plus a short ramp.

**Question:** Predict and then observe how each object moves. Create useful groups, name each group and explain how shape or material supports every placement.

**Marking key:** Records a movement for all 4 objects (2 marks); makes at least 2 consistent groups such as rolls/slides/tumbles or moves easily/does not (2 marks); links placements to curved/flat shape or soft/rigid material with evidence (up to 2 marks); distinguishes prediction from observation (1 mark). 7 marks total.

**Coverage:** Integrating prediction, observation and feature-based movement classification.

### E08 — Deciding which factor matters

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Read: “Ball A is small, rubber and round; it bounces high. Ball B is large, cloth and round; it does not bounce. Ball C is large, rubber and round; it bounces high.”

**Question:** Use the three observations to decide whether size or material is the better explanation for high bouncing. Compare the most useful pair and explain what further test would strengthen the conclusion.

**Marking key:** Selects material as the better-supported factor (1 mark); compares B and C because they share size/shape but differ in material and bounce (2 marks); may also note A and C both rubber and bounce despite different sizes (1 mark); proposes repeating drops or testing same-sized balls of additional materials under equal conditions (1 mark). 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Reasoning from controlled comparisons to identify an influential movement factor.
