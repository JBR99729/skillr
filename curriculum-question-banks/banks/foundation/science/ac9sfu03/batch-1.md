# AC9SFU03 — Pass 1 question bank

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Material or object?

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Show a metal spoon.

**Question:** Which word names the material rather than the object?

A. Handle

B. Spoon

C. Metal

D. Kitchen

**Correct answer:** C — Metal is the substance; spoon names the object.

**Coverage:** Distinguishing a material name from an object name.

### P02 — Describing fabric

**Type:** Oral response

**Delivery:** Provide a safe square of soft cotton fabric to observe and handle.

**Question:** Describe two properties you can observe without naming its colour.

**Marking key:** Gives 2 suitable properties, such as soft, flexible, light, woven, thin or textured (1 mark each). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Using property vocabulary to describe a familiar material.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — One object, several materials

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Show a pencil with a wooden body, graphite core, painted surface and metal-and-rubber eraser end.

**Question:** Name two materials in the pencil and identify the part made from each.

**Marking key:** Gives any 2 correct material–part pairs, such as wood/body, graphite/core, metal/eraser holder or rubber/eraser (1 mark each). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Recognising that one everyday object can contain multiple materials.

### P04 — Sorting by flexibility

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide a rubber band, wooden craft stick, fabric strip and metal spoon. Students should not bend objects beyond the teacher’s safe limit.

**Question:** Sort the materials into flexible and not flexible in this test. Describe the observation that supports each placement.

**Marking key:** Flexible: rubber band and fabric strip; not flexible under gentle force: craft stick and spoon (2 marks); supports placements with observed bending/folding or resistance (1 mark). 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Grouping materials by an observed mechanical property.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Magnifying a paper surface

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Describe ordinary and magnified views of torn paper; the close-up shows many tiny fibres.

**Question:** What extra property or detail does the magnifier reveal? How is that different from guessing what the paper contains?

**Marking key:** Identifies visible fibres/rough torn edge or texture (1 mark); explains the claim is based on a close observation rather than an unsupported guess (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Using magnification to extend evidence about material properties.

### P06 — Choosing material for a rain cover

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say: “A cover must bend around a bag and keep rain off.”

**Question:** Which tested material is the best choice?

A. Dissolving paper tissue

B. Stiff absorbent cardboard

C. Heavy rough brick

D. Flexible waterproof plastic sheet

**Correct answer:** D — Its observed flexibility and water resistance suit both needs.

**Coverage:** Selecting a material by matching observable properties to purpose.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Why a saucepan uses different materials

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Show a saucepan with a metal bowl and a thick plastic handle. State that an adult uses it for cooking and it may become hot.

**Question:** Explain why the two parts are made from different materials. Use a property and purpose for each part.

**Marking key:** Links the metal bowl to being hard/strong and suitable for heating food (2 marks); links the plastic handle to being rigid/easy to hold and a poorer heat conductor than metal, helping an adult hold it more safely (2 marks); recognises that different parts need different properties (1 mark). 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Relating contrasting material properties to functions within one object.

### P08 — Designing a materials display

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Provide samples or images of wood, glass, metal, rubber, fabric and plastic.

**Question:** Plan a display that groups the materials by two observable properties. Give the group labels, place every material and explain how a visitor could check your choices safely.

**Marking key:** Uses 2 observable properties, such as transparent/not, flexible/not, smooth/rough or hard/soft (2 marks); places all samples consistently based on the provided examples (2 marks); gives safe checks using sight or gentle touch/bending as directed (1 mark). Accept classifications that acknowledge a property can vary between samples. 5 marks total.

**Coverage:** Constructing and communicating an evidence-based materials classification.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Identifying glass

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Show a clear drinking tumbler identified as being made of glass; do not allow handling.

**Question:** Which pair best describes observable properties of this glass sample?

A. Transparent and hard

B. Soft and stretchy

C. Furry and absorbent

D. Flexible and woven

**Correct answer:** A — The sample can be seen through and has a hard surface.

**Coverage:** Selecting accurate observable properties for a named material.

### E02 — Parts of a shoe

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Show a shoe with fabric upper, rubber sole and plastic eyelets.

**Question:** Name two different materials and the shoe part made from each.

**Marking key:** Gives any 2 correct pairs from fabric/upper, rubber/sole and plastic/eyelets (1 mark each). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Identifying multiple materials in a composite object.

### E03 — A property of rubber

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Show a rubber band being stretched safely by an adult.

**Question:** Which observation supports describing this rubber as stretchy?

A. It makes every object waterproof.

B. It becomes longer when gently pulled and returns towards its old shape.

C. It changes into metal when released.

D. It always has the same colour.

**Correct answer:** B — It describes the visible response of the sample to a gentle force.

**Coverage:** Linking a property word to direct observational evidence.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Correcting an evidence-free label

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Say: “A display labels a sealed wooden block ‘soft inside’ even though nobody has opened or tested it.”

**Question:** Why is that label not supported? Replace it with one property that could be observed safely from the outside.

**Marking key:** Explains that the inside was not observed/tested (1 mark); supplies a supported external property for the shown sample, such as hard, solid, smooth/rough or opaque (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Distinguishing observation-based descriptions from unsupported claims.

### E05 — Selecting a window material

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Say: “A model house needs a window that lets light through and keeps its shape.”

**Question:** Which sample best meets both needs?

A. Soft modelling clay

B. Thick black fabric

C. Clear rigid plastic

D. Opaque wooden board

**Correct answer:** C — It is transparent and rigid, matching the two stated requirements.

**Coverage:** Applying two material properties to an object-design choice.

### E06 — Comparing foil and fabric

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Provide teacher-approved pieces of aluminium foil and cotton fabric.

**Question:** Give one property they share and two properties that differ, based on safe observations.

**Marking key:** Gives one supported similarity, such as both flexible/thin (1 mark); gives 2 supported contrasts, such as shiny/dull, smooth/woven, crumples/returns differently or absorbency if safely tested (1 mark each). 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Comparing materials across several observable properties.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Investigating an unknown object

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Present a sealed classroom object made from a rigid plastic body, soft foam pad and metal clip.

**Question:** Plan safe observations to identify its materials and properties. Record evidence for each part and explain why one test may not suit every material.

**Marking key:** Identifies/records all 3 parts separately (1 mark); proposes safe sight, gentle touch or magnifier observations (2 marks); links evidence to plausible materials/properties (up to 3 marks); explains a limitation, such as not bending the metal clip or not using taste/unsafe force (1 mark). 7 marks total.

**Coverage:** Planning differentiated observations for a multi-material object.

### E08 — Evaluating two lunchbox designs

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Describe Box A as thin cardboard with a paper hinge and Box B as rigid plastic with a flexible plastic hinge. Both must be reused and protect food from spills.

**Question:** Choose the more suitable design. Compare at least three relevant properties, name one possible limitation of your choice and support every claim with the description or a safe test.

**Marking key:** Chooses Box B with a logical reason (1 mark); compares 3 relevant properties such as rigidity, water resistance, durability or hinge flexibility (3 marks); states a reasonable limitation, such as greater weight or eventual plastic damage (1 mark); identifies description evidence or a safe test such as gentle flex/water-drop test on samples (1 mark). 6 marks total.

**Coverage:** Evaluating a material choice against multiple functional requirements and evidence.
