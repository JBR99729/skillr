# AC9E3LA01 - Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Key idea:** Good speakers share turns. They listen, wait, respond to the previous idea and invite others to contribute.
- **Match the situation:** Friendly conversations can be informal. A class presentation, interview or school meeting usually needs more formal language.
- **Useful phrases:** “I agree because…”, “Could you explain…?”, “What do you think?” and “Excuse me, may I add…?”
- **Common trap:** Taking a turn does not mean changing the subject or speaking over someone.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 - Knowledge and terms

### P01 - Choose the cooperative next turn

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Visual:**

| Speaker | Speech bubble |
|---|---|
| Ava | “I think the class garden needs more shade.” |
| Noah | ? |

**Question:** Which reply best continues Ava’s idea: “Why?”; “I agree because lettuce wilts in strong sun.”; or “My shoes are new.”?

**Marking key:** “I agree because lettuce wilts in strong sun.” It responds to Ava’s idea and adds a reason.

**Coverage:** Recognises a relevant turn that helps speakers cooperate.

### P02 - Formal or informal greeting

**Type:** Sorting

**Delivery:** Online, support drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students write F for formal or I for informal.

**Question:** Sort these greetings by where they fit best: “Good morning, Ms Tran”; “Hey, Sam!”; “Hello, members of the council”; “Hi, mate!”

**Marking key:** Formal: “Good morning, Ms Tran”; “Hello, members of the council”. Informal: “Hey, Sam!”; “Hi, mate!”

**Coverage:** Distinguishes social language by degree of formality.

## Tier 2 - Core skill practice

### P03 - Repair an interruption

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Visual:**

> **Mia:** “The best part of the excursion was—”  
> **Leo:** “I KNOW! The bus!”  
> **Mia:** “I hadn’t finished.”

**Question:** What should Leo say next: “Too slow”; “Sorry for interrupting—please finish”; or “Anyway, the bus was loud”?

**Marking key:** “Sorry for interrupting—please finish.”

**Coverage:** Applies a turn-taking phrase to repair an uncooperative exchange.

### P04 - Invite a quiet group member

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Three students have spoken during a group task, but Priya has not. Write one respectful sentence that invites Priya to contribute.

**Marking key:** A suitable invitation such as “Priya, what do you think?” or “Would you like to add an idea, Priya?”

**Coverage:** Uses inclusive language to share speaking turns.

## Tier 3 - Guided application

### P05 - Put the discussion moves in order

**Type:** Sequencing

**Visual:**

| Card A | Card B | Card C | Card D |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ask a follow-up question | Listen to the answer | Wait for the speaker to finish | Respond to their idea |

**Question:** Arrange the cards into a cooperative sequence, beginning before the other speaker has finished.

**Marking key:** Wait for the speaker to finish; listen to the answer; respond to their idea; ask a follow-up question.

**Coverage:** Sequences listening and turn-taking behaviours.

### P06 - Change chat language for an assembly

**Type:** Editing

**Question:** Kai tells a friend, “Hey, chuck us that sports trophy.” Rewrite the request as if Kai were speaking to the principal during assembly.

**Marking key:** A polite formal version such as “Excuse me, could you please pass me the sports trophy?”

**Coverage:** Adjusts language to suit a formal setting and audience.

## Tier 4 - Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 - Diagnose the failed group talk

**Type:** Extended response

**Visual:**

| Student | What happened |
|---|---|
| Ben | spoke 6 times |
| Aisha | spoke 1 time |
| Ruby | was interrupted twice |
| Tom | asked no questions |

**Question:** Identify two cooperation problems shown in the table and suggest one practical improvement for each.

**Marking key:** Any two evidence-based pairs, such as Ben dominated—invite others; Ruby was interrupted—wait until she finishes; Aisha had little opportunity—ask for her view; no follow-up questions—ask speakers to explain.

**Coverage:** Interprets visible discussion evidence and proposes fair turn-taking strategies.

### P08 - Disagree without ending the conversation

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Your partner says, “We should present our project as a poster,” but you prefer a video. Write a respectful two-sentence reply that disagrees, gives a reason and keeps the discussion open.

**Marking key:** Includes respectful disagreement, a relevant reason and an invitation or compromise; for example, “I see why a poster would be clear, but a video could show the experiment happening. Could we use a short video with a summary poster?”

**Coverage:** Applies cooperative language while expressing a different opinion.

## Exam questions

## Tier 1 - Knowledge and terms

### E01 - Match the phrase to its job

**Type:** Matching

**Visual:**

| Phrase | Job choices |
|---|---|
| “Could you say more about that?” | invite / clarify / disagree / repair |
| “Sorry, I spoke over you.” | invite / clarify / disagree / repair |
| “Luca, what is your view?” | invite / clarify / disagree / repair |
| “I understand, but my evidence differs.” | invite / clarify / disagree / repair |

**Question:** Match each phrase to the job it performs in a conversation.

**Marking key:** Could you say more—clarify; sorry—repair; Luca—invite; I understand but—disagree.

**Coverage:** Identifies the function of useful interaction language.

### E02 - Select the interview question

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Question:** A student is interviewing a firefighter for the school news. Which opening is most suitable: “Tell me stuff”; “Good afternoon. Could you explain what happens when an emergency call arrives?”; or “Oi, is your job scary?”

**Marking key:** “Good afternoon. Could you explain what happens when an emergency call arrives?”

**Coverage:** Selects appropriately formal and precise interaction language.

## Tier 2 - Core skill practice

### E03 - Find the turn that does not connect

**Type:** Hotspot / selection

**Visual:**

> **1.** “Our survey shows most students walk to school.”  
> **2.** “That surprises me. How many students answered?”  
> **3.** “Twenty-four students answered.”  
> **4.** “My dog can catch a tennis ball.”

**Question:** Select the speech turn that breaks the topic of the discussion.

**Marking key:** Turn 4.

**Coverage:** Recognises whether a contribution connects to preceding turns.

### E04 - Complete the chairperson's sentence

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** At a class meeting, two students begin speaking together. Complete the chairperson’s sentence respectfully: “Let’s hear from Sofia first, then ____.”

**Marking key:** A fair continuation naming or inviting the second student, such as “we’ll hear Marcus’s idea.”

**Coverage:** Uses language that manages turns fairly.

## Tier 3 - Guided application

### E05 - Compare two ways of asking

**Type:** Extended response

**Visual:**

| At lunch with a friend | At a community presentation |
|---|---|
| “What d’you reckon?” | “Would you please share your view?” |

**Question:** Explain why both questions can be appropriate even though their language is different.

**Marking key:** The informal wording suits a familiar friend; the more formal, polite wording suits a public event or less familiar audience. Both invite another person’s contribution.

**Coverage:** Explains how formality changes with social context.

### E06 - Turn commands into cooperation

**Type:** Editing

**Question:** Rewrite “Stop talking. Use my idea.” as one or two sentences that help a group cooperate.

**Marking key:** A respectful version that allows turns and discussion, such as “Could I explain my idea when you finish? Then we can compare both ideas.”

**Coverage:** Replaces controlling language with cooperative interaction.

## Tier 4 - Problem solving and reasoning

### E07 - Plan fair speaking turns

**Type:** Extended response

**Visual:**

| Group task | Time available | Students |
|---|---:|---:|
| Choose a book-week theme | 8 minutes | 4 |

**Question:** Give the group a simple speaking plan that lets everyone contribute and includes time to respond to ideas.

**Marking key:** A workable plan with an initial turn for each student and shared response/decision time; for example, one minute each to propose an idea, three minutes to compare, and one minute to agree.

**Coverage:** Designs an equitable interaction structure from given constraints.

### E08 - Resolve a disagreement formally

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** During a school committee meeting, one student says, “That idea is rubbish.” Write what the chairperson could say to restore respectful discussion and invite evidence.

**Marking key:** A formal response that rejects disrespect without attacking the student and requests reasons; for example, “Please respond to the idea respectfully. Could you explain your concern and give a reason?”

**Coverage:** Applies formal social conventions to a realistic conflict.
