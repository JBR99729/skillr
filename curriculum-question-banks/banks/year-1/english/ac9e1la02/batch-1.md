# AC9E1LA02 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Rule:** A preference says which option someone likes or chooses. A strong response adds a relevant reason, often with because.
- **Worked example:** ‘I prefer the shaded park because it is cooler for our picnic’ gives a choice and a reason connected to it.
- **Useful language:** Use I like, I dislike or I prefer, followed by because. Respectful disagreement can begin ‘I prefer a different option because…’.
- **Visual interaction:** Use choice cards and reason cards with spoken, drag/tap, keyboard and paper sentence-building options.
- **Common trap:** ‘Because it is better’ repeats the preference but does not explain what makes it better.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Find the reason

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** In ‘I like mango because it is sweet’, which words give the reason?

**Marking key:** ‘because it is sweet’ (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Identifying a reason in a preference statement.

### P02 — Match preferences and reasons

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match ‘I prefer boots’, ‘I like the library’ and ‘I dislike loud music’ to ‘they keep my feet dry’, ‘I can find books’ and ‘it hurts my ears’.

**Marking key:** Each preference matches its relevant reason (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting preferences with logical reasons.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Give an oral preference

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Choose drawing or building and state a complete preference sentence with because.

**Marking key:** States one choice and a relevant reason in a complete sentence (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Expressing a preference with supporting language.

### P04 — Improve a weak reason

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Improve ‘I prefer apples because apples are better’ by adding a specific reason.

**Marking key:** Example: ‘I prefer apples because they are crisp and easy to pack’ (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Replacing circular reasoning with relevant detail.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order a reasoned response

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: name the choice; add because; give the reason; compare options if helpful.

**Marking key:** Name choice → add because → give reason → optional comparison (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing a preference-and-reason structure.

### P06 — Draw choice and evidence

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw a choice between a shady bench and a sunny bench for a hot day. Add one preference sentence.

**Marking key:** Picture shows both options and sentence chooses shade for a relevant heat/sun reason (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Combining a visual choice with a reasoned preference.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Separate fact and preference

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** ‘The red bag has two pockets’ and ‘I like the red bag best’ are different. Label the fact and the preference.

**Marking key:** Two pockets is a checkable fact; liking it best is a personal preference (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Distinguishing factual information from preference.

### P08 — Give respectful disagreement

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A partner prefers indoor games. State a different preference with a respectful reason.

**Marking key:** Uses respectful wording, names an alternative and gives a connected reason (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Expressing a differing preference respectfully.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Complete a reason

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Finish: ‘I would choose the larger water bottle because…’ with a useful reason.

**Marking key:** Example: ‘because it holds more water for the long walk’ (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Completing a reasoned choice.

### E02 — Match reasons to audiences

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match a quiet book corner, sturdy lunchbox and short route to reading without distraction, protecting food and arriving sooner.

**Marking key:** Each option matches its relevant practical reason (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Selecting reasons that support choices.

### E03 — Sequence two viewpoints

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: Ana states her preference; Ben listens; Ben states a different preference with because; Ana acknowledges it.

**Marking key:** Ana preference → Ben listens → Ben reasoned preference → Ana acknowledges (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Organising a respectful exchange of preferences.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Compare two reasons

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Which is more informative: ‘I like this story because it is good’ or ‘because the surprise ending made me laugh’? Explain.

**Marking key:** Second; it names a specific feature and response rather than using vague praise (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating the quality of supporting reasons.

### E05 — Demonstrate changing preference

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Explain how new information could change a choice between walking and taking a bus on a rainy day.

**Marking key:** States an initial preference, relevant weather information and a revised or maintained choice with reason (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Adapting a preference when circumstances change.

### E06 — Draw two audience choices

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw a poster choice for a young child and for an adult. Add a reason for using different words or images.

**Marking key:** Shows two audience-aware choices and explains one relevant difference (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Justifying preferences for different audiences.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Repair an unrelated reason

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** ‘I prefer the blue umbrella because my sandwich is cheese’ does not work. Explain why and write a connected reason.

**Marking key:** Sandwich detail does not support umbrella choice; supplies relevant reason such as colour visibility or rain protection (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Checking relevance between preference and reason.

### E08 — Build a balanced choice

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Choose between two class excursion options. Give two relevant reasons for your preference and acknowledge one advantage of the other option.

**Marking key:** Clear preference, two connected reasons and one accurate positive point about the alternative (5 marks).

**Coverage:** Supporting and qualifying a preference.
