# AC9E1LE05 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Rule:** A retell keeps the important characters and main plot in order. An adaptation deliberately changes one element while keeping the story understandable.
- **Worked example:** Original: a mouse finds a key and opens a garden gate. Adaptation: a robot finds a code and opens a space station, while the find-and-open plot remains.
- **Retelling plan:** Name characters and setting, tell beginning/problem, important attempts and ending, and use clear sequence words and topic vocabulary.
- **Visual interaction:** Use original class-story cards, storyboard frames and role cards with speech, drawing, keyboard and paper alternatives.
- **Common trap:** A retell is not every tiny detail, and an adaptation should not change so much that the plot loses its connection.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Name essential content

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** For a retell, which matters more: the main problem or the colour of an unimportant cup? Explain.

**Marking key:** Main problem; it drives the plot, while incidental cup colour can be omitted (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Selecting important story information.

### P02 — Match retell parts

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match beginning, middle and ending to introduce, face/solve problem and show resolution.

**Marking key:** Beginning ↔ introduce; middle ↔ problem/attempts; ending ↔ resolution (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting story structure with retell stages.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Retell three events

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Using cards about a bird losing, searching for and finding a ribbon, orally retell the events in order.

**Marking key:** Includes character and all three events in logical order with sequence words (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Orally retelling a simple plot.

### P04 — Choose an adaptation

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Change a forest setting to an underwater setting. Name one character or object detail that must also change logically.

**Marking key:** Example bird becomes fish/diver, path becomes reef route, or fire becomes safe underwater obstacle (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Adapting details consistently with setting.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order a storyboard

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: problem appears; character introduced; solution tried; ending shows result.

**Marking key:** Character introduced → problem → attempt/solution → ending/result (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing a retell structure.

### P06 — Draw four retell frames

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw beginning, problem, attempt and resolution for an original story about a missing lunchbox.

**Marking key:** Four coherent frames preserve character and causal plot order (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing a retell through storyboard images.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Use precise story words

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Replace went in ‘The rabbit went through the grass’ with a verb showing how it moves.

**Marking key:** Suitable verb such as hopped, bounded or crept, preserving meaning (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Selecting vocabulary for vivid retelling.

### P08 — Explain what to keep

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** When adapting a character from child to alien, name two plot relationships or events that could stay the same.

**Marking key:** Any two important goals, problems, helpers, attempts or resolution links remain recognisable (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Maintaining plot coherence in adaptation.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Summarise an opening

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Original opening: ‘At dawn, Suri packed water, checked the map and left to find the missing goat.’ Retell the essential opening in one sentence.

**Marking key:** Suri leaves at dawn prepared to search for a missing goat, retaining character, setting/time and goal (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Condensing an opening without losing main information.

### E02 — Match delivery modes

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match role-play, drawing and digital slides to act dialogue, show visual sequence and combine images/text/audio.

**Marking key:** Each mode matches its useful retelling affordance (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Selecting modes for retell and adaptation.

### E03 — Sequence linking words

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Choose and order four linking words suitable for a retell: first, next, then, finally.

**Marking key:** First → next → then → finally (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Using temporal cohesion in oral retelling.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Adapt the problem

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A character's bridge breaks before they cross a stream. Change the story to a city setting with an equivalent problem.

**Marking key:** Plausible equivalent such as bus cancellation/closed crossing blocks journey, while goal and problem role remain (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Transferring plot function to a new setting.

### E05 — Demonstrate character voice

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Retell one event twice: once as a worried character and once as a confident character.

**Marking key:** Vocabulary, voice or gesture clearly distinguishes both while event stays recognisable (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Adapting character voice in role-play.

### E06 — Draw original and adapted plots

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw two four-frame strips with the same plot shape but different characters and settings. Label the shared problem and resolution pattern.

**Marking key:** Both strips have recognisably parallel problem/attempt/resolution despite changed details (5 marks).

**Coverage:** Comparing original and adapted story structures.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Repair a confusing retell

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A retell gives the ending first, omits the problem and introduces a new character without explanation. Describe three repairs.

**Marking key:** Restore logical order, include main problem, introduce/connect or remove new character; any three clear fixes (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating coherence and completeness of a retell.

### E08 — Deliver a complete adaptation

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Adapt this original plot: ‘A child follows clues to return a lost parcel.’ Give a new character and setting, then orally outline beginning, problem, two events and resolution using sequence language.

**Marking key:** Distinct but connected adaptation includes all six requested elements, logical causality and clear sequence vocabulary (7 marks).

**Coverage:** Creating and structuring an extended oral adaptation.
