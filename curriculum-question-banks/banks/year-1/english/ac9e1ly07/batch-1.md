# AC9E1LY07 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Rule:** A short presentation needs an opening that names the topic, a middle with ordered key ideas and a conclusion that finishes the message. Voice and gesture should help the audience understand.
- **Worked example:** Opening: ‘Today I will explain frog needs.’ Middle: water, food and shelter. Conclusion: ‘These resources help frogs survive in their habitat.’
- **Delivery check:** Use audible volume, steady pace, clear words, purposeful gestures and visuals large enough to understand.
- **Visual interaction:** Use three-part planning cards, cue cards and accessible slide/storyboard frames with oral or recorded alternatives.
- **Common trap:** Speaking loudly or adding many gestures does not improve meaning unless delivery suits the room, topic and audience.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Identify an opening

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Which sentence best opens a talk: ‘Today I will show how seeds grow’ or ‘That is all’?

**Marking key:** ‘Today I will show how seeds grow’ (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Recognising a topic-setting opening.

### P02 — Match presentation parts

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match opening, middle and conclusion to introduce topic, explain key ideas and finish/summarise.

**Marking key:** Each part matched correctly (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting presentation structure with function.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Deliver a three-part talk

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Give a 20-second talk about a favourite safe activity with an opening, one middle detail and conclusion.

**Marking key:** All three parts are present and spoken clearly at suitable volume/pace (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Delivering a simple structured personal presentation.

### P04 — Choose topic vocabulary

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** For a talk about measuring length, name two useful topic words and use one in a sentence.

**Marking key:** Any two such as length, unit, equal, end-to-end; sentence uses one accurately (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Selecting vocabulary for a learnt topic.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order presentation cards

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: key idea two; opening; conclusion; key idea one.

**Marking key:** Opening → key idea one → key idea two → conclusion (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing a short presentation.

### P06 — Draw one useful slide

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw a slide for a talk about animal habitats with a short heading, labelled image and no paragraph of tiny text.

**Marking key:** Readable heading, relevant labelled visual and concise layout (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Designing a supportive presentation visual.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Adjust pace

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Why should a speaker pause briefly after an important idea instead of rushing?

**Marking key:** Pause gives audience time to process and highlights the idea (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Explaining how pace supports understanding.

### P08 — Plan purposeful gestures

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** For a talk comparing a small and large object, describe two gestures that support meaning without distracting.

**Marking key:** Uses controlled size/comparison gestures aligned with spoken points (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Selecting gestures connected to content.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Write a conclusion

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Finish a talk about keeping water clean with one concluding statement.

**Marking key:** A clear summary or call linked to topic, such as ‘Clean water helps living things stay healthy’ (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Creating a relevant conclusion.

### E02 — Match delivery problems and fixes

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match whispering, racing and looking only at screen to increase volume, slow/pause and orient to audience/use cue cards.

**Marking key:** Each problem matched to appropriate fix (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Improving oral delivery.

### E03 — Sequence rehearsal

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: revise unclear section; practise; ask for listener feedback; present.

**Marking key:** Practise → seek feedback → revise/rehearse → present (2 marks; revision and second rehearsal understood).

**Coverage:** Using rehearsal and feedback to prepare.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Explain visual purpose

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A slide shows a labelled force arrow while the speaker explains direction. How does the visual help?

**Marking key:** Makes direction visible and supports the topic word/ spoken explanation (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Discussing multimodal support for oral content.

### E05 — Demonstrate volume choices

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Say one sentence for a partner nearby and for a class group. Adjust volume without shouting and explain.

**Marking key:** Both are audible for audience size; explanation links volume to distance/room (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Adapting volume to audience.

### E06 — Draw cue cards

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Create three cue cards for opening, two key ideas and conclusion about a learnt topic. Use keywords, not a full script.

**Marking key:** Cards cover all sections, sequence logically and use concise cues/topic words (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Planning oral structure with prompts.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correct an overloaded presentation

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A 30-second talk has 12 slides, tiny writing and no conclusion. Suggest three improvements.

**Marking key:** Reduce slides/ideas, enlarge/simplify visuals/text and add clear conclusion; any three relevant fixes (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating presentation design and structure.

### E08 — Plan and deliver a learnt-topic talk

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Prepare a one-minute presentation about pushes and pulls or another learnt topic. Include opening, three ordered points, conclusion, two topic words, one useful visual and planned delivery choices.

**Marking key:** All requested content/structure elements present; vocabulary accurate; visual relevant; volume, pace and gesture support audience understanding (8 marks).

**Coverage:** Integrating structure, vocabulary, multimodality and delivery.
