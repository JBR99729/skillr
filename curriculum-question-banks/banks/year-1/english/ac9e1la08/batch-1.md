# AC9E1LA08 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Rule:** Images add meaning differently according to text type: story pictures show character, setting and mood; information images show facts, parts or processes; persuasive images attract attention and support a viewpoint.
- **Worked example:** A labelled frog diagram identifies body parts, while a story picture of a frog hiding under a leaf can show fear and setting.
- **Comparison strategy:** Ask what the image shows that the words do not, what details it highlights and why that suits the text's purpose.
- **Visual interaction:** Use original paired mock images with captions and full text descriptions for matching, tap, keyboard and paper tasks.
- **Common trap:** An attractive image may set mood without proving a factual claim.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Name an image contribution

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A story picture shows dark clouds behind a character. What meaning might it add?

**Marking key:** It may establish stormy setting, danger or worried mood, supported by visible details (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Inferring story meaning from visual setting.

### P02 — Match image types and jobs

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match labelled diagram, story scene and advertisement photo to identify parts, show events/mood and attract/support choice.

**Marking key:** Each image type matches its purpose-related contribution (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting images with functions across text types.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Compare two frog images

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Describe how a labelled frog diagram and a colourful story illustration would be used differently.

**Marking key:** Diagram gives factual parts/features; illustration develops character, setting or event (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Comparing informational and literary image functions.

### P04 — Use a caption clue

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A photo caption says ‘Seedling after 7 days’. What information does the caption add?

**Marking key:** It identifies time and stage, helping readers interpret the image as evidence of change (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Explaining how captions guide image meaning.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order image reading

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: notice details; read title/caption; connect image to words; decide its contribution.

**Marking key:** Read title/caption and notice details → connect to text → decide contribution (2 marks; first two may swap).

**Coverage:** Sequencing multimodal interpretation.

### P06 — Draw for two purposes

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw one simple image of a bicycle for a safety guide and one for an adventure story. Label one difference.

**Marking key:** Safety image highlights labelled equipment/action; story image shows character/event/mood, with difference stated (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Adapting visual information to text purpose.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Find unsupported meaning

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** An image shows a smiling child holding a book. Can it prove every child likes the book? Explain.

**Marking key:** No; it shows one pictured response and may be staged; it cannot prove a universal claim (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating the limits of image evidence.

### P08 — Choose an informative image

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** For instructions on planting a seed, choose a decorative flower border or step-by-step pictures. Justify.

**Marking key:** Step pictures because they show action/order needed to complete the task (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Selecting visuals that support procedural meaning.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Infer a character feeling

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A character's shoulders droop and eyes look down. What feeling might the image suggest, and which details support it?

**Marking key:** A plausible feeling such as sad/disappointed with both visual details cited (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Inferring emotion from visual cues.

### E02 — Match fresh visuals

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match map, close-up photo and speech bubble to location, detail and character words.

**Marking key:** Map ↔ location; close-up ↔ detail; speech bubble ↔ character words (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Recognising specialised visual-text features.

### E03 — Sequence a comic event

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order three described panels: ball breaks window; child kicks ball; adult and child inspect damage.

**Marking key:** Child kicks → window breaks → inspection (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using images to reconstruct event order.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Compare image emphasis

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A food poster shows a large bright apple and tiny background text. What does the size make readers notice first?

**Marking key:** The apple/product; large size creates visual emphasis (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Interpreting salience in a persuasive image.

### E05 — Demonstrate caption change

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Give two captions for the same rain photo: one factual and one story-like. Explain the difference.

**Marking key:** Factual caption records condition/place/time; story caption builds event/mood, with clear contrast (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Changing image meaning through caption style.

### E06 — Draw labelled versus unlabelled

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw a simple plant twice, adding labels only to the information-text version. State what labels contribute.

**Marking key:** Information version identifies parts; explanation notes labels make factual reference precise (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing how labels add information.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correct a mismatched image

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A page titled ‘How bees collect nectar’ shows only a beach. Explain the problem and propose a useful replacement.

**Marking key:** Beach does not support topic; replace with bee at flower, sequence or labelled diagram showing nectar collection (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating relevance between image and text.

### E08 — Analyse three text types

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** For one image of a bush track, explain how a narrative, map guide and safety poster could each use or change it to create different meaning.

**Marking key:** Narrative adds event/mood; guide adds route/landmarks; safety poster highlights hazards/actions, with purpose links (6 marks).

**Coverage:** Comparing image contribution across literary, informative and persuasive texts.
