# AC9E1LY04 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Rule:** Blend known letter-sound patterns to read words, group words into meaningful phrases, notice punctuation and check that the sentence sounds right and makes sense.
- **Worked example:** In ‘The quick fox / jumps over the log’, blend unfamiliar words, pause by phrase, then reread if a word makes the meaning strange.
- **Self-check:** Ask: Did it sound like a sentence? Did it make sense with the picture and nearby words? Do the letters support the word I said?
- **Visual interaction:** Use original short texts with optional phrase marks, audio-after-attempt and keyboard-accessible word highlighting.
- **Common trap:** Do not guess from the first letter or picture alone; use all the letter-sound information and meaning together.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Blend a short word

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Blend the sounds /m/ /a/ /p/ and say the word.

**Marking key:** Map (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Blending phonemes into a decodable word.

### P02 — Match punctuation and reading

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match comma, full stop and question mark to short pause, stop and questioning voice.

**Marking key:** Comma ↔ short pause; full stop ↔ stop; question mark ↔ questioning intonation (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Using punctuation to guide phrasing.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Read in phrases

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Read ‘After lunch, / the small dog / slept under the bench.’ using the phrase marks, then explain one pause.

**Marking key:** Groups words meaningfully and links pause to comma/phrase meaning (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Applying phrasing to a short authentic sentence.

### P04 — Monitor a misread

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A reader says ‘The fish sat in the tree’ for text that says ‘The fin sat in the tray’. Name two checks needed.

**Marking key:** Check all letters/sounds and whether sentence meaning/grammar fits; reread exact text rather than guess (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Monitoring decoding with print and meaning.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order decoding steps

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: blend sounds; look through whole word; check sentence meaning; reread smoothly.

**Marking key:** Look through word → blend sounds → check meaning → reread smoothly (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing a word-solving routine.

### P06 — Draw phrase groups

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Copy ‘The red crab hid / beneath a flat rock’ and draw brackets around the two meaning groups.

**Marking key:** Correct two phrase groups marked without splitting determiners from nouns (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing meaningful phrasing.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Use grammar to check

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Which sounds grammatical: ‘The birds fly’ or ‘The birds flies’? Explain the check.

**Marking key:** ‘The birds fly’; plural birds agrees with fly in standard sentence pattern (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using grammatical knowledge to monitor reading.

### P08 — Reread for fluency

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Describe how a choppy first reading of a four-sentence text can be improved without racing.

**Marking key:** Solve words, mark phrases/punctuation, reread accurately at steady pace with expression while preserving meaning (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Planning fluency improvement through rereading.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Decode a consonant blend

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Blend /f/ /r/ /o/ /g/ and state the word.

**Marking key:** Frog (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Blending a word with an initial consonant cluster.

### E02 — Match word-solving clues

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match letters/sounds, grammar and context to print pattern, sentence structure and surrounding meaning.

**Marking key:** All three sources matched correctly (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Recognising complementary reading cues.

### E03 — Sequence a fluent question

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order the reading actions: notice question mark; read words accurately; group phrase; lift voice appropriately; check meaning.

**Marking key:** Read/solve words → group phrase → use question intonation → check meaning; noticing mark occurs before delivery (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Coordinating decoding, phrasing and punctuation.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Correct a picture guess

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** The picture shows a cat, but the printed word is ‘kitten’. Explain why saying cat is not accurate reading.

**Marking key:** Meaning is related but letters encode kitten; reader must attend to full word and say kitten (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Prioritising print evidence over picture guessing.

### E05 — Demonstrate two readings

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Read ‘No, wait for me!’ once word-by-word and once with meaningful phrasing and expression. Explain which communicates better.

**Marking key:** Fluent reading groups words, observes comma/exclamation and conveys plea; explanation identifies improvement (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Comparing choppy and fluent reading.

### E06 — Draw a self-monitor path

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Create four boxes labelled decode, phrase, meaning check and reread. Add one action to each.

**Marking key:** Four accurate actions in a useful sequence (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing a complete reading-monitoring cycle.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Repair meaning after error

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A reader decodes ‘The goat floats past the boat’ as ‘The goat flies past the boat’. Explain how print and meaning reveal the error.

**Marking key:** Flies does not match the letters in floats and changes the water context; reader reblends floats and rereads (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Using multiple cues to self-correct.

### E08 — Read and explain strategy

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Read the original text ‘At dusk, the small moth flaps near the lamp. It stops on the wall.’ Describe one decoding, one phrasing and one meaning-monitoring action used.

**Marking key:** Accurate reading plus specific evidence for sound blending, pause/grouping and sense/grammar check (6 marks).

**Coverage:** Integrating decoding, fluency and comprehension monitoring.
