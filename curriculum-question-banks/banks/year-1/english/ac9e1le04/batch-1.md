# AC9E1LE04 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Rule:** Rhyme matches ending sounds; alliteration repeats starting sounds in nearby words. Poets also use beat, repetition and sound words for effect.
- **Worked example:** ‘Busy bees buzz by’ repeats /b/ for alliteration. ‘Bee/tree’ rhyme because their final sounds match.
- **Listening strategy:** Listen for a repeated sound, name it, quote or repeat the relevant words, then describe whether it feels playful, quick, soft or strong.
- **Visual interaction:** Use original audio/read-aloud lines plus sound cards and mouth/sound cues with paper alternatives.
- **Common trap:** Alliteration uses sound, not just a repeated written letter: city and cat start with different sounds.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Find alliteration

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Which pair uses alliteration: silver snake or silver moon?

**Marking key:** Silver snake; both begin with /s/ (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Recognising repeated initial sounds.

### P02 — Match sound patterns

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match goat/boat, wild wind and tick-tock to rhyme, alliteration and sound word/repetition.

**Marking key:** Goat/boat ↔ rhyme; wild wind ↔ alliteration; tick-tock ↔ sound word/pattern (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Classifying literary sound devices.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Perform a line

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Say ‘Softly, slowly, snowflakes spin’ and emphasise the repeated starting sound.

**Marking key:** Performs clearly and identifies /s/ repetition (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Demonstrating alliteration in oral literature.

### P04 — Invent a rhyme

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Add a second line to ‘I see a bright red kite’ ending in a word that rhymes with kite.

**Marking key:** A meaningful line ending in night, light, flight or another true rhyme (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Creating a rhyming line.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order a chant

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order these phrases into two repeating sound units: clap-clap-stomp, clap-clap-stomp.

**Marking key:** Two identical clap-clap-stomp units (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Sequencing an imitated chant pattern.

### P06 — Draw sound images

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw two quick images for ‘pitter-patter’ rain and ‘boom’ thunder. Label how each word sounds.

**Marking key:** Images match soft repeated rain and sudden loud thunder; sound qualities labelled (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Connecting sound words with imagery.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Discuss effect

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** What feeling or pace might ‘Quick quails quiver and quake’ create? Use one sound clue.

**Marking key:** Plausible quick/nervous/playful effect supported by repeated /qu/ and short words (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Discussing the effect of alliteration.

### P08 — Invent a sound pattern

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Create a four-word phrase with at least three words beginning with the same sound.

**Marking key:** At least three initial sounds match and phrase remains understandable (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Inventing alliteration.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Hear end sounds

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Do moon and spoon rhyme? State the shared ending sound.

**Marking key:** Yes; both end with /oon/ (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Explaining rhyme through phonological evidence.

### E02 — Match lines and mood

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match ‘hush, hush, sleepy night’, ‘bang-clang-crash’ and ‘bouncy bears bound’ to calm, noisy and lively.

**Marking key:** Each line matches a supported sound-created mood (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting sound pattern with mood.

### E03 — Sequence a rhyme scheme

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order cat, moon, hat, spoon into an A-B-A-B ending pattern.

**Marking key:** Cat, moon, hat, spoon (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Arranging alternating rhymes.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Complete alliteration

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Replace big in ‘big fox’ to create alliteration with fox while keeping meaning sensible.

**Marking key:** Example: fast fox, furry fox or friendly fox (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Selecting words for repeated initial sounds.

### E05 — Demonstrate imitation

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Listen to or read ‘tap-tap-pause’ and reproduce it twice, then invent a one-step change.

**Marking key:** Accurately imitates twice and creates a clearly stated variation (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Imitating and adapting an oral sound pattern.

### E06 — Draw rhyme pairs

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw and label two original rhyme pairs, avoiding pairs that only look alike in spelling.

**Marking key:** Two pairs have matching spoken end sounds and labels (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Generating rhymes with visual support.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correct false alliteration

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A child says ‘giant goat’ alliterates because both start with g. Explain using sounds.

**Marking key:** Giant starts /j/ while goat starts /g/, so initial sounds differ (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Distinguishing letter repetition from sound repetition.

### E08 — Create and discuss a verse

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Write or perform two short lines containing alliteration, one rhyme pair and a repeated beat or phrase. Explain each feature.

**Marking key:** All three features are present, accurately named and contribute to a coherent mini-verse (6 marks).

**Coverage:** Inventing and discussing combined sound patterns.
