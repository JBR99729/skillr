# AC9E1LA04 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Rule:** Repetition brings words or lines back, rhyme repeats ending sounds, and rhythm creates a beat. Together they help a poem, chant or song feel connected.
- **Worked example:** ‘Tap your toes, touch your nose’ rhymes toes/nose; repeating ‘Tap, tap, tap’ strengthens the beat.
- **Sound clue:** Rhyme depends on sound, not identical spelling: blue and shoe rhyme, but cough and though do not.
- **Visual interaction:** Use beat marks, sound cards and repeated-line strips with audio, tap, keyboard and paper options.
- **Common trap:** Words that start alike use alliteration; rhyme usually matches sounds at the end.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Find the rhyme

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Which word rhymes with light: lamp, night or little?

**Marking key:** Night (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Recognising matching final sounds.

### P02 — Match sound features

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match ‘go, go, go’, ‘cat/hat’ and a steady clap pattern to repetition, rhyme and rhythm.

**Marking key:** Go phrase ↔ repetition; cat/hat ↔ rhyme; claps ↔ rhythm (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Distinguishing repetition, rhyme and rhythm.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Perform a beat

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Say ‘Rain on the roof, rain on the ground’ with a steady four-beat clap. Repeat the line.

**Marking key:** Maintains a steady beat and repeats the full line accurately (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Demonstrating rhythm and repetition.

### P04 — Complete a rhyming line

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Complete ‘The frog sat on a __’ with a word that rhymes with log.

**Marking key:** Log or another contextually suitable rhyme such as bog (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Supplying a rhyme that fits meaning.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order a chant pattern

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order the cards to make two repeats: stomp, clap, clap, stomp, clap, clap.

**Marking key:** Stomp-clap-clap repeated twice (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Sequencing a repeated rhythmic unit.

### P06 — Draw beat marks

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Write or draw four beat marks under ‘Little bird, flap your wings’ and show which word or syllable lands on each beat.

**Marking key:** Shows a plausible steady four-beat grouping aligned to the spoken line (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing rhythm visually.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Explain cohesion

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Why does repeating ‘Home again’ at the end of each verse help a song feel connected?

**Marking key:** It creates a repeated anchor/refrain that links verses and helps listeners remember the idea (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Explaining how repetition creates cohesion.

### P08 — Invent a two-line chant

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Write or say two short lines with one repeated phrase and one rhyming pair.

**Marking key:** Contains clear repetition, a defensible end rhyme and connected meaning (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Creating cohesion through two sound devices.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Hear a fresh rhyme

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Do play and day rhyme? Explain using the final sound.

**Marking key:** Yes; both end with the /ay/ sound (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Explaining rhyme by sound.

### E02 — Match lines to devices

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match ‘Swish, swish goes the broom’, ‘star/far’ and ‘clap-rest-clap-rest’ to repetition, rhyme and rhythm pattern.

**Marking key:** Each example matches its sound device (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Classifying sound patterns in short texts.

### E03 — Sequence a sound unit

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order ding, dong, dong, ding, dong, dong into two repeats of its smallest unit.

**Marking key:** Ding-dong-dong / ding-dong-dong (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Identifying and reconstructing a repeated sound unit.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Repair a rhyme

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** The lines end ‘mouse’ and ‘moon’. Replace moon with a word that keeps the second line meaningful and rhymes with mouse.

**Marking key:** A suitable word such as house, with a meaningful revised line (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Revising a line to create rhyme.

### E05 — Demonstrate rhythm change

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Say one line first with a steady beat and then with random pauses. Explain which version is easier to join as a chant.

**Marking key:** Steady version is easier because listeners can predict and follow the rhythm (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Comparing rhythmic and irregular delivery.

### E06 — Draw a refrain pattern

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw three verse boxes and place the same refrain card after each. Label the repeated words.

**Marking key:** Same refrain follows every verse and repeated text is clearly labelled (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing repetition across a text.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correct spelling-only reasoning

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A child says move and love rhyme because both end in ‘ove’. Explain the mistake.

**Marking key:** Rhyme depends on spoken ending sound; move and love have different vowel sounds (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Distinguishing sound rhyme from spelling pattern.

### E08 — Analyse a short chant

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** In ‘Turn around, touch the ground; turn around, make no sound’, identify repetition, two rhyming words and the likely rhythmic effect.

**Marking key:** Repetition: turn around; rhyme: ground/sound; repeated structure supports a predictable beat and cohesion (5 marks).

**Coverage:** Analysing multiple cohesive sound features.
