# AC9E1LY12 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Rule:** A letter can represent different sounds in different words. Every spoken syllable contains one vowel sound, even when the spelling uses y for that vowel sound.
- **Worked example:** c is /k/ in cat and /s/ in city. The two syllables in robot are ro/bot, and each has a vowel sound.
- **Word strategy:** Use the whole word and known pattern to choose a letter's sound; clap or tap the spoken beats to find syllables and listen for a vowel sound in each.
- **Visual interaction:** Use sound-choice cards and syllable arcs with audio, tap, keyboard and paper marking alternatives.
- **Common trap:** Count spoken syllables, not separate vowel letters; boat has two vowel letters but one vowel sound and one syllable.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Compare c sounds

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** What sounds does c represent in cat and city?

**Marking key:** Cat begins /k/; city begins /s/ (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Recognising two sounds represented by one letter.

### P02 — Match words and syllables

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match cat, sunset and banana to 1, 2 and 3 syllables.

**Marking key:** Cat ↔ 1; sunset ↔ 2; banana ↔ 3 (1 mark each, allowing standard Australian pronunciation).

**Coverage:** Counting syllables in spoken words.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Tap vowel beats

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Say robot, tap its syllables and identify the vowel sound in each spoken beat.

**Marking key:** Ro/bot, 2 syllables; long /o/ in ro and short /o/ in bot (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Locating vowel sounds in syllables.

### P04 — Choose the s sound

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** In sat and rose, does s represent the same final/initial sound? State both.

**Marking key:** Sat has /s/; the s in rose commonly represents /z/ (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Recognising differing sounds for letter s.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order syllable checking

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: say word; tap beats; listen for vowel sound in each; check whole word.

**Marking key:** Say → tap → identify vowel sound per beat → check (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing a syllable-analysis routine.

### P06 — Draw syllable arcs

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Write picnic and draw one arc under each syllable. Circle one vowel letter in each syllable.

**Marking key:** Pic/nic with two arcs; i in each syllable circled (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing syllables and vowel letters.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Explain boat

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Why is boat one syllable even though it contains o and a?

**Marking key:** Oa works together to represent one vowel sound in one spoken beat (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Separating letter count from vowel-sound/syllable count.

### P08 — Compare letter a

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Say the vowel sound represented by a in cat and apron. Explain that the surrounding word pattern helps.

**Marking key:** Cat has short /a/; apron begins long /ā/ in standard pronunciation; whole-word pattern/context selects sound (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Recognising multiple sounds for letter a.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Find the vowel sound

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** How many syllables are in fish, and what vowel sound must the syllable contain?

**Marking key:** One syllable containing short /i/ (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Identifying the vowel nucleus of a syllable.

### E02 — Match letter sounds

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match g in goat, g in giant and y in happy to /g/, /j/ and final /ee/.

**Marking key:** Goat g ↔ /g/; giant g ↔ /j/; happy y ↔ /ee/ (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting letters with context-dependent sounds.

### E03 — Sequence spoken syllables

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order the syllables ket, bas to make basket, then state the vowel sound in each.

**Marking key:** Bas/ket; short /a/ then short /e/ (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Ordering syllables and identifying their vowel sounds.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Check a vowelless claim

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A student says the spoken word stop has no vowel sound because it begins and ends with consonants. Correct them.

**Marking key:** Stop has one syllable with short /o/ between the consonants (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Recognising the required vowel sound inside a cluster word.

### E05 — Demonstrate letter u sounds

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Say cut, put and use, listening to the sound represented by u in each.

**Marking key:** Identifies different common u sounds in the three words without requiring technical symbols (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Exploring multiple sounds for one vowel letter.

### E06 — Draw sound and syllable maps

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Map music and cup. Mark syllable arcs and highlight the letter u in both, then describe the sound difference.

**Marking key:** Music has two syllables and initial long /yoo/ or /oo/ by accent; cup one syllable short /u/; difference stated (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Comparing letter sound across word contexts.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correct vowel-letter counting

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A child counts three syllables in rain because r-a-i-n has several letters. Explain and give the correct count.

**Marking key:** Rain is one spoken beat/syllable; ai represents one vowel sound, so letter count does not equal syllable count (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Correcting a letter-based syllable misconception.

### E08 — Analyse a varied set

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** For city, cake and happy, identify one letter that represents a less basic sound, count syllables and state a vowel sound in each syllable.

**Marking key:** City: c=/s/, 2 syllables; cake: a_e long /ā/, 1; happy: y=/ee/, 2; vowel sounds identified appropriately (7 marks).

**Coverage:** Integrating flexible letter-sound and syllable knowledge.
