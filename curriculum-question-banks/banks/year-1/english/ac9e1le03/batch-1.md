# AC9E1LE03 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Rule:** Characters are who the story is about; setting is where and when it happens; plot is the connected sequence of events, often including a problem and resolution.
- **Worked example:** In a story about Bea searching a night market for her lost scarf, Bea is the character, the night market is the setting, and the search-and-find events form the plot.
- **Discussion strategy:** Name the feature, cite a word or event, and explain how it matters to the story.
- **Visual interaction:** Use original character, setting and plot-event cards with spoken, drag/tap, keyboard and paper alternatives.
- **Common trap:** A topic is not the plot; ‘friendship’ is an idea, while the plot tells what happens.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Identify the setting

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** ‘At sunrise, Kian waits beside the river.’ State the setting.

**Marking key:** Beside the river at sunrise (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Identifying place and time in setting.

### P02 — Match features

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match character, setting and plot to who, where/when and connected events.

**Marking key:** Character ↔ who; setting ↔ where/when; plot ↔ events (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Recognising core story features.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Act a character goal

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** A character wants to reach a high shelf safely. Demonstrate or explain one action that could move the plot toward the goal.

**Marking key:** Gives a safe, character-led action such as asking an adult, linked to goal (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Connecting character goal with plot action.

### P04 — Find the problem

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Mara brings a picnic, but wind blows away the map. What is the story problem?

**Marking key:** The map is blown away/lost, making navigation difficult (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Identifying a plot problem.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order a mini-plot

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: key is found under a pot; gate is locked; friends search; they enter the garden.

**Marking key:** Gate locked → search → key found → enter garden (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing problem, attempts and resolution.

### P06 — Draw a setting clue

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw a setting for a story at night in a train station. Label two details showing where or when.

**Marking key:** Two clear location/time clues such as platform, tracks, clock, darkness or lights (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing setting through visual detail.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Explain character effect

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** How might a very patient character act differently from an impatient character when a bus is late?

**Marking key:** Patient character waits/checks calmly; impatient character may complain/rush, affecting subsequent events (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Discussing how traits influence plot.

### P08 — Connect all three features

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Create a one-sentence story idea naming a character, setting and a problem that starts the plot.

**Marking key:** All three features are explicit and logically connected (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Generating a coherent story premise.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Name the resolution

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A puppy cannot cross a puddle; the character places a safe board bridge and the puppy crosses. What is the resolution?

**Marking key:** The safe board bridge lets the puppy cross (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Identifying how a plot problem is resolved.

### E02 — Match characters to goals

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match lost cyclist, hungry magpie and curious child to find path, find food and discover a sound's source.

**Marking key:** Each character matches the logical goal (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting characters with plot motivations.

### E03 — Sequence setting change

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: storm begins; family enters cabin; morning sun appears; they continue walking.

**Marking key:** Storm → cabin shelter → morning sun → continue walk (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing plot through a changing setting.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Compare two settings

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** How would a chase plot change if it happened in an open field rather than a crowded market?

**Marking key:** Gives one relevant difference in obstacles, visibility, movement or tension (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Explaining how setting shapes events.

### E05 — Demonstrate character response

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Show or describe how a brave but careful character responds to a strange noise behind a door.

**Marking key:** Response combines courage with checking/seeking help rather than unsafe action (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Expressing character traits through plot behaviour.

### E06 — Draw plot mountain

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw four boxes for opening, problem, attempt and resolution for a story about a broken toy wheel.

**Marking key:** Four logical, connected stages with problem addressed in resolution (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing basic plot structure.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correct a setting mix-up

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A story says it is midday at the beach, but every detail shows stars and snow-covered streets. Explain the mismatch and revise two details.

**Marking key:** Identifies inconsistent time/place and changes at least two details to create one coherent setting (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating consistency of setting clues.

### E08 — Analyse an original story

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** ‘In a quiet library, Omar hears a kitten behind a shelf. He asks the librarian, they move a box safely, and the kitten is reunited with its owner.’ Identify character, setting, problem, two events and resolution.

**Marking key:** Omar/librarian/kitten as characters; library setting; trapped/hidden kitten problem; two accurate actions; reunion resolution (6 marks).

**Coverage:** Analysing plot, character and setting together.
