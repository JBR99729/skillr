# AC9E1LA09 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Rule:** Topic vocabulary names ideas precisely in subjects such as Mathematics, Science, Health and the Arts. Its meaning is learned through definitions, examples, diagrams and context.
- **Worked example:** In Mathematics, ‘difference’ can mean how much two amounts differ, as in 9 − 6 = 3, not merely that objects look unlike.
- **Word-learning strategy:** Notice the topic, read nearby clues, check a glossary or label, then use the word in a sentence that shows its meaning.
- **Visual interaction:** Use subject-labelled word, definition and example cards with audio, tap, keyboard and paper matching.
- **Common trap:** Some words have everyday and topic meanings, so the sentence and subject determine which meaning fits.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Identify a science word

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Which word is topic vocabulary for where an animal lives: habitat, happy or quickly?

**Marking key:** Habitat (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Recognising a science term.

### P02 — Match terms and subjects

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match add, habitat and rhythm to Mathematics, Science and English/Arts.

**Marking key:** Add ↔ Mathematics; habitat ↔ Science; rhythm ↔ English/Arts (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Linking topic vocabulary with learning areas.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Use a glossary

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Demonstrate how to find the word ‘capacity’ in a short alphabetical glossary and state its measurement meaning.

**Marking key:** Locates C entry and explains capacity as how much a container can hold (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using a glossary to learn topic vocabulary.

### P04 — Infer from context

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** ‘The magnet attracts the clip, pulling it closer.’ What does attracts mean here?

**Marking key:** Pulls/draws closer (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Using sentence context to infer a science term.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order word learning

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: use word in a new sentence; notice unfamiliar word; read definition/example; check meaning fits topic.

**Marking key:** Notice → read clues/definition → check topic fit → use in sentence (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing a vocabulary-learning strategy.

### P06 — Draw a vocabulary example

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw and label an example of equal groups for Mathematics. Include the words equal and group.

**Marking key:** Shows groups with the same count and labels both terms meaningfully (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing mathematical vocabulary visually.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Compare everyday and maths meaning

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Explain how ‘table’ differs in ‘set the table’ and ‘record results in a table’.

**Marking key:** First is furniture; second is organised rows/columns of information (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Recognising a word with topic-specific meaning.

### P08 — Choose precise wording

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Replace ‘the thing moved’ with a sentence using the science words object, push and direction.

**Marking key:** Example: ‘The push moved the object to the left direction/left’ with terms used accurately (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Applying topic vocabulary in context.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Recognise a measurement term

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** What does length tell us about an object?

**Marking key:** How long it is from one end to the other (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Understanding a Mathematics measurement word.

### E02 — Match terms and examples

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match prediction, character and tally to expected result, person/animal in story and counting marks.

**Marking key:** Each term matches its topic example (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting cross-curricular vocabulary with meanings.

### E03 — Sequence a diagram lookup

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: find label line; locate unfamiliar label; look at pictured part; read nearby definition.

**Marking key:** Locate label → follow line to part → inspect image → read definition/context (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using a labelled diagram to understand vocabulary.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Select a topic meaning

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** In ‘The mass of the bag is greater’, does mass mean a church event or amount of matter/heaviness comparison?

**Marking key:** The measurement meaning related to how heavy the bag is at this level (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Selecting meaning from learning-area context.

### E05 — Demonstrate word teaching

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Teach a partner the word sequence using a definition, an example and a non-example.

**Marking key:** Defines ordered events/terms, gives valid sequence and an unordered/non-sequence example (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Explaining topic vocabulary with contrasts.

### E06 — Draw a mini word wall

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Create entries for force, noun and capacity with a subject label and small example for each.

**Marking key:** All three are correctly defined/illustrated and assigned to suitable subjects (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Organising learning-area vocabulary.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correct vague subject words

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A report says ‘The stuff did something.’ Replace it with at least three suitable topic words for a water-absorbency test.

**Marking key:** Example uses material, water/drop, absorbed/repelled, observation/result accurately (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Improving precision with scientific vocabulary.

### E08 — Explain one word across topics

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Choose one word such as pattern, change or volume. Give two subject meanings or uses and a sentence for each.

**Marking key:** Two accurate topic-linked uses and two context sentences showing distinct or connected meanings (5 marks).

**Coverage:** Comparing vocabulary use across learning areas.
