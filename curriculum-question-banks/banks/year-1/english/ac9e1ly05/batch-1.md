# AC9E1LY05 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Rule:** Comprehension strategies help build meaning: visualise details, predict from clues, connect relevant knowledge, summarise main events and ask questions. Literal meaning is stated; inferred meaning is worked out from clues.
- **Worked example:** ‘Pia grabbed her umbrella as dark clouds gathered.’ Literally she has an umbrella; we can infer she expects rain because of the cloud clue.
- **Strategy check:** Name the strategy, cite the word/image clue, and explain how it improves understanding.
- **Visual interaction:** Use original micro-texts, picture descriptions and strategy cards with spoken, tap, keyboard and paper responses.
- **Common trap:** A prediction is not any possible event; it should follow from text clues and can change when new evidence appears.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Find literal meaning

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Text: ‘The blue cup is beside the sink.’ Where is the cup?

**Marking key:** Beside the sink (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Retrieving explicitly stated information.

### P02 — Match strategies and actions

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match visualise, predict and summarise to make a mind picture, say what may happen and state the main idea/events.

**Marking key:** Each strategy matched correctly (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Recognising core comprehension strategies.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Visualise from detail

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Listen to ‘A tiny boat rocks on silver water under a round moon.’ Describe or show the mind picture using three details.

**Marking key:** Includes boat size, water description and moon/night detail (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Building a visual image from language.

### P04 — Make a supported prediction

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A character packs a torch and enters a dark shed. Predict what they may do next and cite one clue.

**Marking key:** Plausible next action involving light/search, supported by torch or darkness (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Predicting from textual clues.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order a summary

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order the important events: seed sprouts; child plants seed; child waters soil; plant grows leaves.

**Marking key:** Plant seed → water → sprout → leaves grow (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing events for summary.

### P06 — Draw literal and inferred

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw what is stated—wet footprints by a door—and one possible inference. Label the clue and idea separately.

**Marking key:** Stated clue accurate; plausible inference such as someone entered from rain, clearly labelled as inference (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Separating evidence from inferred meaning.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Ask a useful question

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** After reading that a bridge is closed, write a question that would help understand what the character may do.

**Marking key:** Relevant question about route, goal, safety or next action (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Questioning to extend comprehension.

### P08 — Connect without leaving text

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A character feels nervous before performing. Give a relevant connection and explain how it helps understand the character.

**Marking key:** Connection concerns a new/performance situation and explains nerves, courage or preparation (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Using connection to deepen interpretation.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Infer a feeling

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** ‘Max stared at the broken model and his shoulders dropped.’ What might he feel, and which clue supports it?

**Marking key:** Sad/disappointed/worried, supported by broken model and dropped shoulders (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Inferring emotion from action and context.

### E02 — Match clues to inferences

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match empty bowl, muddy shoes and yawning character to hungry pet, outdoor activity and tiredness, using cautious language.

**Marking key:** Each clue matched to plausible inference with may/might understood (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting evidence with inferred meaning.

### E03 — Sequence prediction revision

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: make prediction; read new clue; compare clue; keep or change prediction.

**Marking key:** Predict → read clue → compare → revise/confirm (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Updating predictions as evidence changes.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Summarise a micro-text

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** ‘A gust lifts Noor's hat. She chases it across the oval and catches it at the fence.’ Summarise in one sentence.

**Marking key:** Noor chases and catches her windblown hat; retains main character/problem/result (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Summarising key events concisely.

### E05 — Demonstrate two strategies

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** For a short teacher-read text, think aloud one prediction and one question, naming the clues used.

**Marking key:** Both strategies are relevant and each is linked to a text clue (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Applying multiple comprehension strategies orally.

### E06 — Draw a strategy map

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw five branches labelled visualise, predict, connect, summarise and question. Add one action to each.

**Marking key:** Five accurate actions organised under correct strategy labels (5 marks).

**Coverage:** Organising comprehension strategy knowledge.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correct an unsupported inference

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A picture shows one child holding a red ball. A reader says every child loves red. Explain the problem.

**Marking key:** One image does not show every child's preference; only the visible child/ball can be stated, and feeling needs more evidence (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating inference strength.

### E08 — Integrate strategies

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Text: ‘The path ahead was flooded. Lee unfolded a map and pointed uphill.’ Give a literal fact, inference, prediction, useful question and one-sentence summary, citing clues.

**Marking key:** Five accurate components: flood/map/uphill facts; route-change inference/prediction; connected question; concise summary, each evidence-linked (7 marks).

**Coverage:** Combining literal and inferred comprehension strategies.
