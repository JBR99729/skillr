# AC9E1LY15 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Rule:** A morpheme is a meaningful word part. A base such as play can combine with -s, -ed or -ing to show who, time or ongoing action and build a word family.
- **Worked example:** play, plays, played and playing share the base play. -s can mark one person/thing acting, -ed past action and -ing ongoing action.
- **Word strategy:** Find the base, cover or separate the added part, read both meanings, then choose the form that fits the sentence.
- **Visual interaction:** Use base-and-ending tiles, family trees and sentence slots with tap, keyboard and paper alternatives.
- **Common trap:** Words that merely start alike are not always one grammatical family; the shared base and added part must carry related meaning.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Find the base

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** What base word is inside played?

**Marking key:** Play (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Recognising a base within an inflected word.

### P02 — Match endings and meanings

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match -s, -ed and -ing in plays, played and playing to one actor/present form, past and ongoing action.

**Marking key:** -s ↔ plays/present one actor; -ed ↔ past; -ing ↔ ongoing (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting grammatical morphemes with meanings.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Build a word family

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Use tiles to make jump, jumps, jumped and jumping. Read each word.

**Marking key:** All four forms constructed/read accurately with base jump retained (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Creating an inflectional word family.

### P04 — Choose the time form

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Complete ‘Yesterday we __ in puddles’ using jump, jumped or jumping.

**Marking key:** Jumped (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Selecting past-tense morphology from context.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order a family

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order these from base to added endings: looking, look, looked, looks.

**Marking key:** Look first; looks/looked/looking follow as derived forms, with any logical ending order accepted after base (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Recognising base and family members.

### P06 — Draw a family tree

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw read at the trunk and add reads, reading and reader as branches. Label the added parts.

**Marking key:** Base read and related branches -s, -ing, -er accurately shown; reader accepted as related agent noun (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing derivational and inflectional family links.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Explain shared meaning

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** How are walk and walked related, and what new meaning does -ed add?

**Marking key:** Both name walking action; -ed places it in the past/completed time (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Explaining morpheme contribution.

### P08 — Create sentence contrasts

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Use plays and played in two sentences that make their time meanings clear.

**Marking key:** Two grammatical sentences showing present/habitual versus past action (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Applying family forms in context.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Recognise an ongoing form

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Which word fits ‘The dog is __ now’: bark, barked or barking?

**Marking key:** Barking (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Using -ing with an auxiliary for ongoing action.

### E02 — Match bases and families

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match help, paint and kick to helper/helped/helping; painter/painted/painting; kicker/kicked/kicking.

**Marking key:** Each family matches its base (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Grouping words by meaningful base.

### E03 — Sequence word building

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: read whole word; find base; choose -ing; join play + ing.

**Marking key:** Find/read base play → choose -ing → join playing → read whole word (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing morphological word construction.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Choose subject agreement

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Complete ‘The bird __’ and ‘The birds __’ using sings and sing.

**Marking key:** The bird sings; the birds sing (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using -s according to singular subject.

### E05 — Demonstrate morpheme removal

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Say playing, remove the -ing part and state the base and meaning change.

**Marking key:** Playing → play; ongoing form returns to base action (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Using morpheme removal to decode meaning.

### E06 — Draw base and compound

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Show how play and ground join to make playground. Explain how the two meaningful parts help.

**Marking key:** Play + ground = playground; combined meaning is a ground/place for play (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Recognising meaningful parts in a compound family.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correct a false family

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A student groups play, plate and played because they begin pla. Explain which belong and why.

**Marking key:** Play and played share base/meaning; plate only shares letters and is unrelated (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Distinguishing morphemic family from visual similarity.

### E08 — Analyse a family in text

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Sentence: ‘The painter painted while her helper was helping.’ Identify the bases, added morphemes and meaning each form contributes.

**Marking key:** Paint base + -er person, paint + -ed past; help + -er person, help + -ing ongoing with was; relationships explained (7 marks).

**Coverage:** Analysing grammatical and derivational morphemes in context.
