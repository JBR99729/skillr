# AC9E1LY14 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Rule:** High-frequency words appear often, so readers learn to recognise and write them quickly. Some are fully decodable; others include a less expected part that must be remembered.
- **Worked example:** In have, h and v are regular, while the final e does not make a long-a sound. Say, spell, cover, write and check.
- **Practice strategy:** Read the word in a sentence, notice the tricky part, write it from memory, then compare and correct.
- **Visual interaction:** Use uncluttered word cards, audio, sentence contexts and keyboard/paper recall rather than speed-only competition.
- **Common trap:** Fast guessing is not mastery; the word must be read accurately and spelled correctly in meaningful text.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Read a common word

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Read the word one in ‘I have one bag.’

**Marking key:** One, read accurately (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Recognising a high-frequency word in context.

### P02 — Match words and sentences

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match have, said and pretty to ‘I __ a hat’, ‘Mia __ hello’ and ‘a __ shell’.

**Marking key:** Have ↔ possess hat; said ↔ spoke hello; pretty ↔ describes shell (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Reading high-frequency words through sentence context.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Write from memory

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Look at have, cover it, write it and check each letter.

**Marking key:** h-a-v-e in correct order, with self-check (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Encoding a high-frequency word from memory.

### P04 — Find the tricky part

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** In one, which spelling part does not match the most expected sounds for a beginning reader?

**Marking key:** The whole pronunciation is less predictable from o-n-e; response may identify initial o sounding /w/+/u/ or the unusual pattern (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Noticing an irregular high-frequency spelling.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order word practice

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: read in sentence; notice tricky part; cover and write; check/correct.

**Marking key:** Read → notice → cover/write → check/correct (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing an effective high-frequency-word routine.

### P06 — Draw a word memory card

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Create a card for said with the word, one sentence and a mark around the less expected ai spelling.

**Marking key:** Said spelled correctly, sentence meaningful and ai highlighted as remembered spelling (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Building orthographic memory for a common word.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Choose the correct word

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Complete ‘They __ coming with us’ using are or our.

**Marking key:** Are (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Selecting a high-frequency word by grammar and meaning.

### P08 — Edit a common-word sentence

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Correct: ‘I hav one littel bag.’

**Marking key:** ‘I have one little bag.’ (3 corrections/marks).

**Coverage:** Applying remembered high-frequency spellings in context.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Read without picture guessing

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** The sentence says ‘Come here, please.’ Read the two high-frequency location/action words.

**Marking key:** Come and here (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Recognising high-frequency words in connected text.

### E02 — Match commonly confused words

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match where, were and we're to a place question, past form of are and we are.

**Marking key:** Where ↔ place question; were ↔ past are; we're ↔ we are (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Distinguishing visually similar frequent words.

### E03 — Sequence a sentence

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: some / We / have / apples.

**Marking key:** We have some apples. (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Reading and arranging high-frequency words in syntax.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Recall a taught set

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Write one, have and pretty from memory, then circle any corrected letter.

**Marking key:** All three final spellings accurate; correction process shown if needed (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Writing an increasing bank of high-frequency words.

### E05 — Demonstrate rapid accurate reading

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Read a mixed row—said, the, have, come, one—twice, aiming for smooth accuracy rather than racing.

**Marking key:** All five read accurately with improved or steady smoothness (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Developing automatic recognition.

### E06 — Draw meaning contrasts

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Create small context pictures or labels for there, their and they're and write one sentence for each.

**Marking key:** Three meanings distinguished and spellings used correctly in age-supported sentences (5 marks).

**Coverage:** Connecting frequent homophones with meaning and spelling.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correct visual similarity error

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A reader says have for here because both begin with h. Explain a better checking method.

**Marking key:** Look through all letters, blend/recognise the whole word and check sentence meaning; first letter alone is insufficient (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Monitoring high-frequency word recognition.

### E08 — Use a high-frequency word bank

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Write a four-sentence mini-text using at least eight taught high-frequency words, then underline them and check spelling.

**Marking key:** Four coherent sentences; eight appropriate frequent words underlined and accurately spelled after checking (8 marks).

**Coverage:** Applying automatic words in connected writing.
