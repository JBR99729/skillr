# AC9E1LY06 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Rule:** Plan for purpose, write complete simple sentences, use precise topic words, then reread for meaning, sentence boundaries, capitals, punctuation and known spelling patterns.
- **Worked example:** Draft: ‘frogs live near water they eat insects’. Edit: ‘Frogs live near water. They eat insects.’ The report now has two clear sentence boundaries.
- **Purpose patterns:** A report groups facts, an opinion states a view and reasons, and a recount orders events with time words.
- **Visual interaction:** Use purpose planners, movable sentence cards and multimodal caption frames with keyboard and paper options.
- **Common trap:** Editing is more than neat copying; reread to find and fix meaning, grammar, punctuation and spelling.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Add a sentence boundary

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Edit ‘The bird landed it drank water’ into two simple sentences.

**Marking key:** ‘The bird landed. It drank water.’ with capital and full stops (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Editing run-together sentences.

### P02 — Match purposes and openings

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match report, opinion and recount to ‘Koalas are…’, ‘I think… because…’ and ‘Yesterday, first…’.

**Marking key:** Each purpose matched correctly (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Selecting language features for writing purpose.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Reread aloud

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Read the draft ‘My dog fast.’ aloud and improve it into a grammatical simple sentence.

**Marking key:** Example: ‘My dog runs fast.’ adds a suitable verb and complete idea (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using rereading to repair grammar.

### P04 — Choose topic vocabulary

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Replace thing in ‘The thing pushed the car’ with precise words for a forces report.

**Marking key:** Example: ‘The hand applied a push that moved the toy car.’ (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using topic-specific vocabulary in a report.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order the writing cycle

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: draft sentences; choose purpose/topic; reread and edit; publish/share.

**Marking key:** Choose purpose/topic → draft → reread/edit → publish/share (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing creation and editing.

### P06 — Draw a multimodal recount

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Create three panels for first, next and finally in an imagined lost-key event. Add one sentence per panel.

**Marking key:** Three logical events, complete sentences and appropriate time words/punctuation (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Creating a sequenced multimodal recount.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Edit an opinion reason

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Improve ‘I like the park. It is good.’ by linking a specific reason.

**Marking key:** Example: ‘I like the park because its shady trees keep the play area cool.’ (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Strengthening an opinion with specific support.

### P08 — Check a report paragraph

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Draft: ‘A habitat is a place living things live. it gives water food or shelter’ Edit capitals, punctuation and any grammar needed.

**Marking key:** ‘A habitat is a place where living things live. It can provide water, food or shelter.’ (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Editing a short information report.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Spell a two-syllable word

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Write the word picnic in the sentence ‘We packed a ___.’ Then reread the sentence.

**Marking key:** Picnic spelled correctly in a complete punctuated sentence (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Applying known spelling in context.

### E02 — Match editing checks

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match capital, full stop and spelling check to sentence start/name, sentence end and known letter pattern.

**Marking key:** All checks matched correctly (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Recognising editing targets.

### E03 — Sequence a recount

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order and combine: finally we arrived; first we packed; next we walked to the bus.

**Marking key:** First packed → next walked → finally arrived, in complete sentences or a coherent short recount (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Organising chronological writing.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Add a useful caption

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A report image shows roots below soil. Write a caption using the topic words roots and absorb.

**Marking key:** Example: ‘Roots absorb water from the soil.’ with accurate meaning and punctuation (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Creating topic-specific multimodal text.

### E05 — Demonstrate a meaning edit

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Read ‘The tiny elephant fit in my pocket’ in a factual report. Explain whether to revise fact, purpose or text type.

**Marking key:** For factual report, revise inaccurate claim; alternatively identify it as imaginative, clearly explaining purpose (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Editing content to match text purpose.

### E06 — Draw an editing checklist

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Create a five-box checklist for complete sentence, capital, ending mark, topic word and spelling. Apply it to one original sentence.

**Marking key:** Five accurate checks and a sentence demonstrably reviewed/corrected (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Using a visual self-editing routine.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correct purpose mixing

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A frog report begins with facts, switches to ‘You must buy a frog’, then tells an unrelated dream. Explain two problems and revise the plan.

**Marking key:** Identifies persuasive/unrelated intrusions; plans grouped factual sections/captions suited to report (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Maintaining purpose and cohesion during editing.

### E08 — Create and edit three forms

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** On the topic ‘rain’, write one report sentence, one opinion with reason and a three-sentence recount. Reread and mark one edit you made.

**Marking key:** Forms suit all three purposes; simple sentences, topic words and boundaries are accurate; one genuine edit identified (8 marks).

**Coverage:** Creating and editing short texts for different purposes.
