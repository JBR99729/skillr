# AC9E1LA03 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Rule:** A text's purpose shapes its organisation. A recount orders real events; a narrative builds characters and a problem; an opinion states a view and reasons; an information text groups facts; an explanation shows how or why.
- **Worked example:** ‘First we planted the seed. Next we watered it. Finally we labelled the pot’ is organised as a recount because events follow time order.
- **Text clues:** Look for time words in recounts, problem and resolution in stories, reasons in opinions, headings in information texts, and ordered causes or steps in explanations.
- **Visual interaction:** Use purpose, section and text-card sorts with tap, keyboard and paper alternatives.
- **Common trap:** A text can contain facts but still have another main purpose; decide what the whole text is trying to do.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Name a recount clue

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Which organising clue is common in a recount: time order, a price list or random facts?

**Marking key:** Time order (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Recognising chronological organisation in recounts.

### P02 — Match purposes and structures

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match narrative, opinion and information report to problem-resolution, view-reasons and grouped facts.

**Marking key:** Narrative ↔ problem-resolution; opinion ↔ view-reasons; report ↔ grouped facts (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting text purposes with organisation.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Retell an event structure

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Orally organise a playground event using first, next and finally.

**Marking key:** Gives three logically ordered events with the time words used accurately (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Using chronological organisation for a recount.

### P04 — Identify an explanation

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A text has the heading ‘How rain forms’ and gives ordered causes. What is its purpose and why?

**Marking key:** To explain; it tells how a process happens using ordered causes (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Identifying purpose from organisational features.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order story sections

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: problem; opening with character and setting; ending/resolution; events trying to solve the problem.

**Marking key:** Opening → problem → attempts/events → resolution (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing a simple narrative structure.

### P06 — Draw an information layout

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw a page about frogs with a title, two fact headings and one labelled image.

**Marking key:** Includes all named features and groups relevant information beneath headings (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing the organisation of an informative text.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Compare report and opinion

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Both texts are about school gardens. Explain one organisational difference between a report and an opinion text.

**Marking key:** Report groups facts under headings; opinion begins with a view and supports it with reasons (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Comparing organisation for informative and persuasive purposes.

### P08 — Choose sections for a purpose

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Plan a short text explaining how to wash hands. Name three sections or ordered parts and why order matters.

**Marking key:** Includes start/materials if needed, ordered actions and result; order supports successful procedure/explanation (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Planning organisation for an explanatory purpose.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Recognise an opinion opening

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** ‘Our class should have more reading time’ most likely begins which kind of text? Give the next useful section.

**Marking key:** Opinion text; next gives one or more reasons/evidence (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using an opening statement to infer text purpose.

### E02 — Match headings to text types

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match ‘What happened on Monday’, ‘Why shadows change’ and ‘Koala facts’ to recount, explanation and information report.

**Marking key:** Monday ↔ recount; shadows ↔ explanation; koala facts ↔ report (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Interpreting titles and headings as purpose clues.

### E03 — Sequence an opinion

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: concluding reminder; state preference; give reason one; give reason two.

**Marking key:** State preference → reason one → reason two → concluding reminder (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing a simple opinion text.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Find misplaced information

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A frog report has headings ‘Body’, ‘Habitat’ and ‘My favourite game’. Which heading does not fit the report topic?

**Marking key:** ‘My favourite game’; it is unrelated personal information (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Checking organisational relevance in an information text.

### E05 — Demonstrate two openings

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Give one opening sentence for a narrative about a lost kite and one for a factual report about kites.

**Marking key:** Narrative introduces character/setting/event; report introduces factual topic (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Adapting openings to different purposes.

### E06 — Draw a compare-plan

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw simple organisers for a recount and an explanation. Label the order used in each.

**Marking key:** Recount shows chronological events; explanation shows ordered steps/causes leading to an outcome (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing differences between text structures.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correct a mixed structure

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A narrative begins with the ending, lists unrelated facts and never shows a problem. Explain two changes that would make its organisation clearer.

**Marking key:** Adds/clarifies opening and problem, orders events toward resolution, removes or integrates unrelated facts; any two explained (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Revising organisation to suit narrative purpose.

### E08 — Plan three purposes

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** For the topic ‘a class pet’, outline a recount, an information report and an opinion text. Give the organising parts for each.

**Marking key:** Recount: timed events; report: grouped facts/headings; opinion: view, reasons, conclusion, all topic-relevant (6 marks).

**Coverage:** Applying distinct organisational structures to one topic.
