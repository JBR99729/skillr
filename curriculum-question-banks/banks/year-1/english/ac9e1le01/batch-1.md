# AC9E1LE01 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Rule:** Authors use naming words, actions, dialogue and description; illustrators use colour, line, size, position and detail. Together these choices build characters, settings and events.
- **Worked example:** ‘Niko crept past the rattling gate’ suggests caution through crept, while a close-up of wide eyes can strengthen the feeling.
- **Discussion clue:** Name the exact word or visible detail, then explain what it suggests rather than only saying the text is interesting.
- **Visual interaction:** Use original story snippets and fully described images with spoken, tap, keyboard and paper response choices.
- **Common trap:** An image may add information not stated in the words, but readers should point to evidence before inferring.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Find a character clue

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** In ‘Tara tucked the injured bird gently into a box’, which word best shows how Tara acts?

**Marking key:** Gently; it suggests care (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using verb or adverb choice to discuss character.

### P02 — Match choices and effects

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match jagged lines, tiny figure and warm golden light to danger/tension, feeling alone and comfort/warmth.

**Marking key:** Each visual choice matches its likely literary effect (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Interpreting illustration techniques.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Perform dialogue clues

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Say ‘I can do it’ once confidently and once nervously. Explain how voice creates two versions of the character.

**Marking key:** Uses clearly different voice and explains confidence versus uncertainty (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Exploring how spoken delivery develops character.

### P04 — Describe a setting

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** The words say ‘Wind whistled between the empty sheds.’ What setting feeling do they create, and which words help?

**Marking key:** A plausible lonely/eerie/windy feeling, supported by whistled and empty (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Discussing language used to create setting.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order event clues

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: muddy footprints appear; Jo follows them; the missing puppy is found; rain begins.

**Marking key:** A logical narrative order beginning with rain/footprints and ending with puppy found; explanation accepts rain first then prints (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing events from causal clues.

### P06 — Draw an added detail

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw a simple illustration for ‘The boat entered the bay’ that makes the event feel dangerous. Label two visual choices.

**Marking key:** Uses two relevant choices such as dark waves, tilted boat or looming rocks and labels their effect (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Creating visual mood for an event.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Compare word and image

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Words call a room ‘quiet’, but the picture shows open cupboards and a broken vase. What extra idea might the image add?

**Marking key:** Something may have happened before the quiet moment; cites disorder as evidence and keeps inference tentative (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Combining verbal and visual evidence.

### P08 — Discuss two character portrayals

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** One character is described as ‘bounding ahead’; another ‘waited and checked the map’. Compare what these choices suggest.

**Marking key:** First seems energetic/impulsive; second careful/thoughtful, supported by exact actions (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Comparing characterisation through actions.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Interpret image position

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** An illustration places a small child at the bottom of a very tall staircase. What might scale and position suggest?

**Marking key:** The staircase/challenge feels large and the child may feel small, uncertain or overwhelmed (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Inferring meaning from visual scale and placement.

### E02 — Match fresh language clues

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match whispered, burst through and curled beside the fire to secrecy/caution, sudden energetic event and comfort/rest.

**Marking key:** All three language choices matched to supported effects (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Interpreting verbs and phrases in literature.

### E03 — Sequence a visual event

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order the described panels: empty nest; bird carrying twig; half-built nest; completed nest with eggs.

**Marking key:** Empty/start → twig carried → half-built → completed with eggs (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using images to reconstruct event development.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Explain a colour change

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A story moves from grey pictures during a search to bright pictures when a friend is found. What does the change contribute?

**Marking key:** Signals a shift from worry/sadness to relief/happiness and highlights the event's emotional change (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Discussing visual colour across events.

### E05 — Demonstrate character movement

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Act or describe how an illustrator might show a rushed character and a calm character without words.

**Marking key:** Gives distinct pose/line/movement details for both and connects them to pace/feeling (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing character through visual action.

### E06 — Draw paired settings

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw the same park as welcoming and as mysterious. Label two changed details.

**Marking key:** Two recognisable versions with at least two purposeful changes such as light, viewpoint, shadows or activity (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Comparing how visual choices create setting mood.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correct an unsupported claim

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A reader says a character is cruel only because their coat is black. Explain why stronger evidence is needed.

**Marking key:** Clothing colour alone does not prove personality; use actions, words, expressions and events as evidence (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating character inferences.

### E08 — Analyse a multimodal moment

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Snippet: ‘Ari lifted the lid slowly.’ Image description: Ari leans back, one hand covers their mouth, and pale light escapes the box. Explain how language and image work together to build character, setting and event.

**Marking key:** Slowly builds suspense; posture/hand suggest caution or surprise; light makes box/event mysterious; connects evidence across all three elements (6 marks).

**Coverage:** Integrating language and image evidence in literary discussion.
