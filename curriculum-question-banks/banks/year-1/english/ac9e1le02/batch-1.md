# AC9E1LE02 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Rule:** A text-to-self connection links a character, event, place or feeling with an experience or knowledge from your life. Explain both the similarity and any difference.
- **Worked example:** ‘The character felt nervous on the first day. I also felt nervous at a new club, but a friend helped me sooner.’
- **Discussion language:** Use This reminds me of…, Both…, Unlike…, and I understand the character because…. Listen respectfully because experiences differ.
- **Visual interaction:** Use original story-event cards and optional experience prompt cards; students may speak, draw or write without revealing private details.
- **Common trap:** A connection must help discuss the text; sharing an unrelated memory does not explain character or event.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Name a connection

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A story character learns to ride a scooter. Give a relevant type of personal connection a reader might make.

**Marking key:** Learning a new skill, practising, falling safely or receiving help; connection relates to the event (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Recognising a relevant text-to-self connection.

### P02 — Match events and connections

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match losing a library book, welcoming a new student and caring for a seedling to losing something, meeting someone new and caring for a living thing.

**Marking key:** Each story event matches its relevant experience category (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting literary events with experience.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Share a safe oral response

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Choose an invented character feeling—excited, worried or proud—and give a non-private experience or imagined example that helps explain it.

**Marking key:** Names feeling, gives appropriate connection and explains the link (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Using experience to discuss character feeling.

### P04 — Complete a connection sentence

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Finish: ‘The rainy-day story reminds me of __ because __.’

**Marking key:** A coherent event and reason connected to rain/indoor activity or feeling (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Expressing a connection with explanation.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order a thoughtful response

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: name text event; state own experience; explain similarity/difference; return to what this shows about character.

**Marking key:** Text event → experience → compare → insight about character (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing an evidence-connected literary response.

### P06 — Draw text and self panels

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw one panel of a character helping a lost animal and one safe imagined/personal helping event. Label a similarity.

**Marking key:** Both panels are clear and one meaningful similarity is labelled (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing a text-to-self connection visually.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Check relevance

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A story is about sharing lunch. A reader talks only about a new video game. What would make the response more relevant?

**Marking key:** Connect to sharing, hunger, friendship or a comparable choice and explain how it helps understand the story (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating relevance of a personal connection.

### P08 — Respect a different response

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Two readers connect differently to a character moving house. Explain how both responses can be useful.

**Marking key:** Experiences and feelings differ; each response is useful if connected to text evidence and shared respectfully (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Recognising diversity in literary responses.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Infer through experience

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A character practises a song many times before performing. How could experience with practice help a reader understand the character?

**Marking key:** It can explain effort, nerves, improvement or pride, linked to repeated practice (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using experience to deepen character understanding.

### E02 — Match response starters

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match ‘Both…’, ‘Unlike…’ and ‘This helps me understand…’ to similarity, difference and insight.

**Marking key:** Both ↔ similarity; Unlike ↔ difference; helps understand ↔ insight (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Using comparative language in literary discussion.

### E03 — Sequence an empathetic reply

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: listen; refer to the speaker's text connection; acknowledge difference; add own connected idea.

**Marking key:** Listen → refer/acknowledge → note difference respectfully → add idea (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Participating respectfully in literary discussion.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Explain changed understanding

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A reader once felt left out of a game. How might that connection change their understanding of a lonely character?

**Marking key:** May notice the character's feelings/need for inclusion and interpret actions with empathy; stays linked to story (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Explaining how experience shapes response.

### E05 — Demonstrate two connections

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Give one connection to a setting and one to a character feeling from an original class story.

**Marking key:** Two distinct, relevant connections with brief explanations (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Connecting with different literary elements.

### E06 — Draw similarity and difference

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw a story picnic and a different meal experience. Label one shared feature and one difference.

**Marking key:** Meaningful shared feature and contrast are clearly tied to the two events (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Comparing a literary event with experience.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Repair an overclaim

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A reader says, ‘The character must feel exactly as I did.’ Explain why this is too certain.

**Marking key:** Connection suggests possibilities, but character evidence and different circumstances matter; use may/might and cite text (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Limiting inference when using personal experience.

### E08 — Build a complete response

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** For a story where a child keeps trying to fix a kite, write or say a response naming the event, a safe connection, one similarity, one difference and what the connection reveals.

**Marking key:** All five elements are present and relevant; insight concerns persistence, frustration, help or success supported by event (5 marks).

**Coverage:** Developing an extended text-to-self literary response.
