# AC9E1LA06 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Rule:** A simple sentence expresses one complete event or idea. It usually names who or what and tells what happens or is true.
- **Worked example:** ‘The tiny frog jumps.’ is complete: the frog is who, and jumps tells what happens.
- **Sentence check:** Read it alone: does it make sense without another part? Check a capital letter and sentence-ending punctuation when writing.
- **Visual interaction:** Use who/what and action/state cards to build clauses with tap, keyboard and paper alternatives.
- **Common trap:** ‘Under the old bridge’ gives detail but is not a complete sentence because it does not state what happens.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Identify a complete sentence

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Is ‘Birds sing.’ a complete simple sentence? Explain briefly.

**Marking key:** Yes; Birds names who/what and sing tells what they do (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Recognising a complete independent clause.

### P02 — Match sentence parts

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match The dog, My shoes and Rain to barks, are wet and falls.

**Marking key:** Dog ↔ barks; shoes ↔ are wet; rain ↔ falls (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Combining who/what with a complete predicate.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Build one sentence

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Use the cards ‘The silver fish’ and ‘swims quickly’ to make and say a complete sentence.

**Marking key:** ‘The silver fish swims quickly.’ with complete meaning (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Constructing a simple clause from parts.

### P04 — Repair a fragment

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Change ‘Beside the tall tree’ into a complete simple sentence.

**Marking key:** Example: ‘A rabbit waits beside the tall tree.’ (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Adding a subject and event to a fragment.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order a sentence

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: softly, sings, The bird.

**Marking key:** The bird sings softly. (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Sequencing words into a grammatical simple sentence.

### P06 — Draw sentence meaning

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw the event in ‘The puppy carries a red sock’ and label who/what and what happens.

**Marking key:** Drawing matches one event; puppy labelled who/what and carries sock labelled action (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Connecting clause structure with represented meaning.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Find two events

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** ‘The bell rang and the children ran’ contains two events. Name them.

**Marking key:** Bell rang; children ran (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Distinguishing a single event from two clauses/events.

### P08 — Create from a picture

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** For a picture of a child watering a plant, write one simple sentence containing one complete event.

**Marking key:** Example: ‘Mina waters the plant.’ with subject and verb, capital and full stop (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Creating a simple sentence from visual stimulus.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Check an incomplete clause

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Why is ‘Because the rain stopped’ incomplete when it stands alone?

**Marking key:** Because signals that another idea is needed; the clause does not complete the relationship by itself (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Recognising a dependent fragment at an accessible level.

### E02 — Match beginnings and endings

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match The moon, Our class and A bee to glows, reads together and lands on the flower.

**Marking key:** Each subject matches a grammatical meaningful predicate (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Forming complete simple sentences.

### E03 — Sequence with details

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: after lunch, rests, The sleepy cat.

**Marking key:** The sleepy cat rests after lunch. (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Arranging a simple clause with added detail.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Remove an extra event

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Rewrite ‘The train stopped and people got off’ as one simple sentence about the train only.

**Marking key:** ‘The train stopped.’ or another one-event train clause (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Editing to a single event or idea.

### E05 — Demonstrate complete and incomplete

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Say one complete sentence about rain and one incomplete group of words. Explain the difference.

**Marking key:** Complete example expresses who/what plus event/state; fragment lacks a necessary part (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Contrasting a sentence with a fragment.

### E06 — Draw two clause parts

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw two boxes for ‘The bright kite’ and ‘flies above the park’. Join them and write the sentence.

**Marking key:** Correct two parts joined as ‘The bright kite flies above the park.’ (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing subject and predicate structure.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correct a run-together sentence

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** ‘I packed my bag I walked to school’ has two complete ideas joined without punctuation. Fix it.

**Marking key:** Example: ‘I packed my bag. I walked to school.’ (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Identifying and repairing run-together clauses.

### E08 — Analyse sentence additions

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Start with ‘The fox rests.’ Add an adjective, a place detail and a time detail while keeping one main event. Explain why it remains a simple sentence.

**Marking key:** Valid expanded sentence retains one clause/event; identifies rests as single main action despite added details (5 marks).

**Coverage:** Expanding a simple sentence without adding another clause.
