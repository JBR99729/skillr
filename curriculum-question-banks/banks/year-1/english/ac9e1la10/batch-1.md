# AC9E1LA10 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Rule:** Start a sentence and familiar proper names with capital letters. Use a full stop for a statement, question mark for a direct question and exclamation mark for strong feeling or urgent words.
- **Worked example:** ‘Mia lives in Canberra.’ names a person and place with capitals. ‘Where is Mia?’ asks a question.
- **Editing check:** Read the sentence's purpose, choose one ending mark, then check the first word and names of people, places, days and months.
- **Visual interaction:** Use movable capital-letter and punctuation cards with spoken, tap, keyboard and paper editing alternatives.
- **Common trap:** Do not add an exclamation mark to every exciting topic; choose it only when the sentence itself exclaims or warns.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Choose an ending mark

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Add the correct punctuation: ‘Where is my hat__’

**Marking key:** Question mark: Where is my hat? (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Punctuating a direct question.

### P02 — Match sentence purposes

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match ‘The bus is here’, ‘Is the bus here’ and ‘Stop’ to full stop, question mark and exclamation mark.

**Marking key:** Statement ↔ full stop; question ↔ question mark; urgent Stop ↔ exclamation mark (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting sentence purpose with ending punctuation.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Say three sentence types

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Say one statement, one question and one exclamation about a dog, using matching voice.

**Marking key:** Three distinct purposes with suitable intonation and complete wording (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Demonstrating punctuation meaning through speech.

### P04 — Capitalise names

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Correct: ‘on monday, leo visits perth.’

**Marking key:** ‘On Monday, Leo visits Perth.’ (4 corrections/marks).

**Coverage:** Using capitals for sentence start and familiar proper nouns.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order an editing check

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: find names; read sentence purpose; add ending mark; check first letter.

**Marking key:** Read purpose → check first letter/names → add or verify ending mark (2 marks; middle checks may swap).

**Coverage:** Sequencing a basic punctuation edit.

### P06 — Draw punctuation speech bubbles

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw one question and one urgent warning in speech bubbles. End each correctly.

**Marking key:** One complete direct question with ? and one appropriate warning/exclamation with ! (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing punctuation choices in dialogue.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Explain a capital

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Why does ‘Friday’ need a capital in ‘We swim on Friday’?

**Marking key:** Friday is the proper name of a day (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Explaining capitalization of familiar proper nouns.

### P08 — Edit a short message

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Edit ‘dear sam can you come on tuesday’ with capitals and punctuation.

**Marking key:** ‘Dear Sam, can you come on Tuesday?’; accepts age-appropriate line break/comma handling (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Editing a message for names, day and question punctuation.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Punctuate a statement

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Write correctly: ‘the library closes at three’.

**Marking key:** ‘The library closes at three.’ (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Applying sentence-start capital and full stop.

### E02 — Match names and common nouns

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match river/the Murrumbidgee, city/Canberra and girl/Asha to common noun/proper name pairs.

**Marking key:** All three common/proper pairs correctly matched and proper names capitalised (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Distinguishing common nouns from familiar proper nouns.

### E03 — Sequence a mixed sentence

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order and punctuate: tomorrow / Will / rain / it.

**Marking key:** Will it rain tomorrow? (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Constructing and punctuating a question.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Choose between marks

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** ‘What a huge wave__’ could take which mark, and what feeling does it show?

**Marking key:** Exclamation mark; surprise/excitement/alarm depending on reading (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using exclamation punctuation for strong feeling.

### E05 — Demonstrate mark meaning

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Read ‘You found it.’ and ‘You found it!’ aloud. Explain the difference.

**Marking key:** Full stop is neutral statement; exclamation adds strong excitement/surprise/emphasis (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Interpreting punctuation through prosody.

### E06 — Draw an editing code

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Create three simple symbols or labels reminding writers to check capital, name and ending mark. Apply them to ‘sara ran home’.

**Marking key:** Three clear checks and corrected ‘Sara ran home.’ (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Using a visual editing routine.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correct over-punctuation

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A student writes ‘I ate lunch!!!’ in a calm factual report. Explain and revise.

**Marking key:** Report statement normally needs one full stop: ‘I ate lunch.’; multiple exclamation marks are unnecessary (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating punctuation for purpose and tone.

### E08 — Edit a mini-paragraph

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Edit: ‘on july 4 maya went to darwin did she see the museum yes she did’.

**Marking key:** ‘On July 4, Maya went to Darwin. Did she see the museum? Yes, she did.’ with capitals and three correct endings (7 marks; commas may be teacher-supported).

**Coverage:** Applying capitals and varied sentence punctuation in connected text.
