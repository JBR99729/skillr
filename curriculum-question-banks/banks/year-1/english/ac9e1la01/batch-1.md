# AC9E1LA01 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Rule:** Words, voice, facial expression and gesture work together. A question asks, an answer provides information, an offer gives help, a request asks politely, and a command directs action.
- **Worked example:** ‘Could you pass the blue pencil, please?’ with an open hand is a polite request; ‘Pass the pencil now’ in a firm voice sounds like a command.
- **Key clue:** Match expression and gesture to meaning: a puzzled face can signal a question, while pointing may show which object is meant.
- **Visual interaction:** Use labelled speech, face and gesture cards with drag, tap, keyboard and paper matching options.
- **Common trap:** The same words can sound friendly, surprised or cross when voice and expression change.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Identify a request

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** What is the purpose of ‘Please may I borrow the ruler?’

**Marking key:** It is a polite request asking to borrow the ruler (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Recognising language used to request.

### P02 — Match words and purposes

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match ‘Would you like help?’, ‘Where is the library?’ and ‘Stand behind the line’ to offer, question and command.

**Marking key:** Help ↔ offer; library ↔ question; stand ↔ command (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting sentence forms with interaction purposes.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Show two meanings

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Say ‘That is the one’ once with a helpful pointing gesture and once with an unsure face. Explain how the meaning changes.

**Marking key:** Demonstrates clear certainty/help versus uncertainty and explains the effect of expression/gesture (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Using non-verbal cues to change interaction meaning.

### P04 — Turn a command into a request

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Rewrite ‘Give me the scissors’ as a polite request.

**Marking key:** Example: ‘Could you please pass me the scissors?’ (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Choosing polite language for a request.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order an information exchange

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: answer ‘It starts at ten’; ask ‘When does the game start?’; acknowledge ‘Thank you’.

**Marking key:** Ask question → provide answer → acknowledge (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing a question-and-answer interaction.

### P06 — Draw matching expressions

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw one face for ‘What a wonderful surprise!’ and one for ‘I do not understand yet.’ Add a matching gesture or label.

**Marking key:** Shows delighted/surprised and puzzled expressions with appropriate gesture/label (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing how facial expression supports spoken meaning.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Explain a mixed message

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A speaker says ‘I am happy to help’ while frowning and turning away. Why might the listener be confused?

**Marking key:** Words signal willingness, but expression/body language signal reluctance or unhappiness (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Interpreting agreement or conflict between verbal and non-verbal cues.

### P08 — Choose language for a situation

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A classmate drops their books. Give an offer, a helpful gesture and a suitable facial expression.

**Marking key:** Example offer ‘Would you like help?’, gesture to assist/pick up, and friendly concerned expression (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Combining language and non-verbal cues appropriately.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Classify an exclamation

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** ‘Watch out!’ is spoken loudly as a ball approaches. What is its interaction purpose?

**Marking key:** It is an exclamation/warning intended to get urgent attention (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Recognising an exclamation used as a warning.

### E02 — Match fresh cues

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match raised eyebrows with ‘Really?’, open palm with ‘Here you are’, and finger to lips with ‘Please be quiet’.

**Marking key:** Each gesture/expression matches the stated spoken meaning (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Interpreting coordinated verbal and non-verbal cues.

### E03 — Sequence a polite offer

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: listen to the answer; notice someone needs help; ask ‘May I help?’; act if they agree.

**Marking key:** Notice → offer → listen → act with agreement (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing a respectful offer and response.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Provide clear information

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A visitor asks where the office is. Write one clear answer using a direction and landmark.

**Marking key:** Gives an understandable direction and landmark, such as ‘Walk straight to the blue door beside the library’ (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using language to provide information.

### E05 — Demonstrate question and command

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Use the same action, closing a door, to demonstrate a polite question and an urgent command. Keep both situations appropriate.

**Marking key:** Uses question form/polite tone versus concise urgent command with matching expression/gesture (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Adapting language and delivery to interaction purpose.

### E06 — Draw a three-panel interaction

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw someone asking for a missing hat, another person pointing to it, and the first person thanking them. Add speech bubbles.

**Marking key:** Panels show question, information with gesture, and acknowledgement in logical order (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing a complete multimodal exchange.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Repair an impolite exchange

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A student shouts ‘Move!’ when they need to pass. Rewrite the words and describe a respectful gesture.

**Marking key:** Example ‘Excuse me, could I please get past?’ with calm voice and non-threatening gesture (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Revising language and gesture for respectful interaction.

### E08 — Compare two deliveries

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Explain how ‘Can you come here?’ might mean a friendly request or an angry command. Describe voice, face and gesture for each.

**Marking key:** Contrasts at least two delivery cues for both meanings and connects them to listener interpretation (5 marks).

**Coverage:** Analysing how multimodal delivery changes interaction meaning.
