# AC9E1LY02 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Rule:** Good group interaction shares speaking time, uses a clear voice, listens without interrupting, responds to what others said and adds relevant ideas or questions.
- **Worked example:** ‘I heard Ava say the ramp should be lower. I agree because…, and I wonder…’ shows listening, response and a new question.
- **Listening clues:** Face or orient toward the speaker as appropriate, pause your own task, remember a key idea and ask or answer a connected question.
- **Visual interaction:** Use turn cards, listening checklists and discussion-stem cards with spoken, tap, keyboard and paper options.
- **Common trap:** Waiting silently is not enough if you ignore the speaker's idea; a useful response connects to what was said.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Identify turn-taking

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** What should you do when another person is speaking in a group?

**Marking key:** Listen without interrupting and wait for an appropriate turn (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Recognising a basic turn-taking behaviour.

### P02 — Match skills and examples

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match clear speaking, active listening and response to audible words, remembering the idea and ‘I agree because…’.

**Marking key:** Each skill matched to its example (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting interaction skills with behaviours.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Practise a connected reply

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Listen to ‘I think we should sort by size’ and give a reply that refers to the idea.

**Marking key:** Reply explicitly mentions sorting/size and agrees, questions or extends respectfully (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Responding to another contribution.

### P04 — Turn a comment into a question

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** After someone says ‘The plant near the window grew taller’, ask a relevant follow-up question.

**Marking key:** Example: ‘Did both plants receive the same water?’ or ‘How much taller?’ (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Contributing a connected inquiry question.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order a discussion turn

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: listen; signal or wait; speak clearly; invite/allow next speaker.

**Marking key:** Listen → wait/signal → speak → yield turn (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing respectful participation.

### P06 — Draw listening behaviours

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw a three-panel group discussion showing speaker, active listener and next turn. Add one label in each panel.

**Marking key:** Panels show clear role changes and behaviours such as listen, respond, wait (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing turn-taking and listening visually.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Repair an unrelated response

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A speaker explains a book ending. The reply is ‘I had cereal.’ Rewrite the reply so it connects.

**Marking key:** A relevant response about the ending, character, feeling or question (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Maintaining topic relevance in interaction.

### P08 — Contribute without repeating

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A group already says a bridge must be strong. Add a different relevant design idea and explain it.

**Marking key:** Adds a new feature such as width, stability or material with connected reason (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Extending group ideas.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Recognise active listening evidence

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A listener says, ‘So you observed six birds, not five—is that right?’ Which skills are shown?

**Marking key:** Listening/remembering, checking understanding and responding to contribution (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Identifying active listening in paraphrase.

### E02 — Match response stems

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match ‘Can you explain…?’, ‘I agree because…’ and ‘Another idea is…’ to question, reasoned response and contribution.

**Marking key:** Each stem matched correctly (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Selecting language for interaction purposes.

### E03 — Sequence disagreement

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: listen fully; state different view calmly; refer to idea; give reason.

**Marking key:** Listen → refer to idea → state different view → give reason (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Organising respectful disagreement.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Adjust speaking clarity

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A partner cannot hear or understand a rushed answer. Name two changes the speaker should make.

**Marking key:** Suitable changes include volume, pace, articulation, facing/orientation or shorter wording (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Adapting speech for listener understanding.

### E05 — Demonstrate group roles

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** In a three-person discussion, demonstrate one speaker, one listener who paraphrases and one person who asks a question.

**Marking key:** All three roles performed distinctly and content remains connected (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Using multiple interaction roles.

### E06 — Draw a turn tracker

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Create a simple visual tracker that helps four group members take turns without ranking ideas.

**Marking key:** Shows four equal turn opportunities and a clear, non-competitive method (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Designing a support for equitable turn-taking.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correct interrupting behaviour

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A student speaks over every answer because they are excited. Explain two respectful strategies that keep their idea available.

**Marking key:** Wait/write idea/use agreed signal and acknowledge current speaker; two strategies explained (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Managing contributions without interruption.

### E08 — Evaluate a discussion

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A group has one loud speaker, two silent members and many repeated ideas. Suggest changes for turn-taking, clarity, listening, response and new contributions.

**Marking key:** One practical improvement for each of the five named skills, connected to the scenario (6 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating and improving group interaction.
