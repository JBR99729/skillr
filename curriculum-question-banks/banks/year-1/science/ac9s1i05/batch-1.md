# AC9S1I05 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Concept answer:** After a test, compare the result with the prediction and other records. A fair investigation changes one planned factor and keeps relevant conditions the same.
- **Key vocabulary:** Fair means the comparison is not confused by extra changes. A repeat is another trial; a further question follows from what the evidence reveals.
- **Observable example:** If two towels differ in size and material, absorbency cannot be linked to material alone; equal-sized pieces make a fairer comparison.
- **Visual model:** Use prediction/result cards, same/different checklists and side-by-side observation tables.
- **Common misconception:** A prediction that does not match is not a failed investigation; honest unexpected results are useful evidence.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Compare result and prediction

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Prediction: the red car will travel farther. Result: the blue car travels farther. Did they match?

**Marking key:** No; the observed result was the opposite of the prediction (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Comparing a prediction with an observation.

### P02 — Match fairness controls

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match car, surface and start line to use same car, same surface and same start when testing push strength.

**Marking key:** Each factor matches its keep-the-same control (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Identifying controlled conditions in a fair test.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Repeat an observation

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Perform or inspect three same-condition ramp trials and record whether the distances are similar or varied.

**Marking key:** Records all three trials and gives an accurate comparison rather than selecting only one (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Using repeat observations to assess consistency.

### P04 — Find the unfair change

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** One ice cube is large in shade and one is small in sun. Why can the test not show the effect of location alone?

**Marking key:** Both size and location changed, so size may also affect melting (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Recognising multiple changed factors.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order post-test thinking

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: record result; compare with prediction; compare classmates' observations; ask a new question.

**Marking key:** Record → compare with prediction → compare others → pose further question (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing evaluation after investigation.

### P06 — Draw a fair comparison

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw two identical toy cars at the same start line on two labelled surfaces. Mark the one factor being compared.

**Marking key:** Cars/start are same and only surface differs, clearly labelled (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing a fair surface investigation.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Explain different observations

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Two groups record 9 blocks and 11 blocks for similar trials. Give one sensible reason to check without saying either group lied.

**Marking key:** Check release, start, unit placement, surface or recording; difference may come from procedure/measurement variation (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Considering reasons for variation between observers.

### P08 — Suggest a further question

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A plant near the window grew taller than one farther away. Write a next question that investigates light more fairly.

**Marking key:** Example: With same plant type, water and pot, does distance from the same window affect growth? (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Developing a guided follow-up question.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — State an honest conclusion

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** The prediction said paper would absorb most, but cloth absorbed more in all three trials. Write the conclusion.

**Marking key:** Cloth absorbed more than paper in these trials; prediction was not supported (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Reporting evidence that contradicts a prediction.

### E02 — Match problems and improvements

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match different water amounts, one trial and unlabelled samples to equal amounts, repeat trials and add labels.

**Marking key:** Each problem matches its direct improvement (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Selecting improvements to reliability and fairness.

### E03 — Sequence a fair retest

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: choose equal pieces; add equal drops; label materials; compare absorption after same time.

**Marking key:** Choose/label equal pieces → add equal drops → wait same time → compare (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing controls in an absorbency retest.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Compare class records

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Group A observes 4 sprouts; Group B observes 5; Group C observes 4. State the shared pattern and variation.

**Marking key:** Most records are near 4–5; A/C agree at 4 and B is one higher (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Comparing others' observations quantitatively.

### E05 — Demonstrate a fairness check

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Use a checklist to inspect a paper-plane test for same paper, start, throw method and measuring unit.

**Marking key:** Checks all four, identifies any difference and proposes a specific fix (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Applying a fair-test checklist.

### E06 — Draw prediction versus result

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw two labelled panels: a predicted floating object and an observed sinking result. Add one comparison sentence.

**Marking key:** Panels clearly differ and sentence states prediction was not supported by the observation (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Communicating a prediction-result comparison.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Audit a one-trial claim

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** One seed sprouted first, so a child says its pot type always makes seeds sprout faster. Explain why the claim is too strong.

**Marking key:** One trial may vary; other factors must be controlled, more seeds/trials are needed, and conclusion should be limited to this observation (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating evidence strength and overgeneralisation.

### E08 — Evaluate and extend an investigation

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Two cars of different mass are pushed by different people on different floors. Explain why the comparison is unfair, redesign it and write one useful further question.

**Marking key:** Identifies multiple confounds; keeps car/push/start/surface same while changing one factor; proposes a connected testable question (5 marks).

**Coverage:** Redesigning a fair investigation and extending inquiry.
