# AC9S1I03 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Concept answer:** An observation records what is noticed or measured. Good records use specific words, units, labels, dates or images so another person can understand them.
- **Key vocabulary:** An observation is evidence gathered with senses or tools. An inference is an idea that might explain evidence; an informal measurement uses units such as cubes or hand spans.
- **Observable example:** ‘The shoot is 5 cubes tall on Tuesday’ is stronger evidence than ‘The plant looks good’.
- **Visual model:** Use labelled observation tables, photo sequences and identical informal units; offer drawing and written alternatives.
- **Common misconception:** A guess about cause is not the same as an observation of what happened.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Observation or idea

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Classify ‘The leaf has three brown spots’ as an observation or an explanation.

**Marking key:** Observation; it describes visible evidence without giving a cause (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Distinguishing observation from inference.

### P02 — Match records and tools

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match plant height, sound and colour change to cube measure, audio recording and labelled photo/drawing.

**Marking key:** Height ↔ cubes; sound ↔ audio; colour change ↔ photo/drawing (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Selecting appropriate observation records.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Measure informally

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Use equal cubes to measure the height of a model plant and record number, unit and date.

**Marking key:** Accurate end-to-end cube count with ‘cubes’ and date/time label (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Making and recording an informal measurement.

### P04 — Improve a vague record

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Rewrite ‘The rock is nice’ as a specific observation using at least two visible or touch-safe properties.

**Marking key:** Example: The teacher-approved rock is grey, rough and has a curved edge (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using precise observable language.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order photo recording

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: photograph from marked place; label date; place object at mark; compare images.

**Marking key:** Place object → photograph consistently → label date → compare images (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing consistent digital observation.

### P06 — Draw a change record

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw a seed before sprouting and after sprouting. Label two observed differences.

**Marking key:** Two time points and two valid visible changes such as root/shoot/size (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Recording change through labelled drawings.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Separate evidence and cause

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A puddle is smaller at 2 pm. State the observation, then one possible explanation using ‘might’.

**Marking key:** Observation: puddle smaller; inference: water might have evaporated/drained, clearly marked as possible (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Separating observation from tentative explanation.

### P08 — Plan repeatable records

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Describe how to record a classroom plant once a day so four records can be compared fairly.

**Marking key:** Same time/view/measurement unit, dated table or photos, and specific features measured/observed (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Planning consistent longitudinal observations.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Read an informal measure

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A worm model spans 7 identical paperclips placed end-to-end. Record the result correctly.

**Marking key:** 7 paperclips long (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Reporting an informal measurement with units.

### E02 — Match detail to usefulness

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match ‘warm’, ‘18 cubes long’ and ‘three loud taps’ to qualitative temperature, informal length and counted sound observation.

**Marking key:** Each record matches the relevant observation kind (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Recognising different forms of observation data.

### E03 — Sequence table entries

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order the entries for one observation: object name; date/time; measured or seen result; unit/label.

**Marking key:** A sensible record includes all four, with result and unit together (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Structuring a complete observation record.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Compare two digital images

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Image 1 shows 2 open flowers; Image 2 a week later shows 5. State the recorded change without claiming a cause.

**Marking key:** Open flowers increased from 2 to 5, a change of 3 (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Extracting evidence from dated digital records.

### E05 — Demonstrate sound recording

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Tap a teacher-approved object three times and record the sound with words or audio, including number of taps.

**Marking key:** Record identifies object, three taps and a sound quality such as high/low or loud/soft (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Making a repeatable sound observation.

### E06 — Draw a labelled table

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Create a three-row table for date, weather description and puddle width in equal blocks.

**Marking key:** Clear headings, three dated rows and values with block units (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Designing a table for mixed observations.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Audit changing units

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A plant is measured with large blocks on Monday and small blocks on Friday. Explain why the numbers cannot be compared directly.

**Marking key:** Different unit sizes change the count; remeasure/use identical units and consistent endpoints (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating consistency of informal measurements.

### E08 — Create a complete evidence record

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** For a toy car trial, record start mark, surface, informal distance, movement description and one photo label. Explain which parts are observations.

**Marking key:** All five details recorded; identifies distance/motion/photo as evidence and avoids unsupported cause claims (5 marks).

**Coverage:** Integrating measurement, description and digital evidence.
