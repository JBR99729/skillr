# AC9S1H01 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Concept answer:** People observe, measure, test and use patterns to make practical decisions about health, food, weather, materials, plants and machines.
- **Key vocabulary:** A pattern is something that repeats or changes in a regular way. A scientific prediction states what may happen and is supported by observations or experience.
- **Observable example:** If wet clothes dried faster on several warm breezy days, a person may predict similar conditions will help another load dry.
- **Visual model:** Use daily-life photo sequences, simple records and prediction/evidence cards with text alternatives.
- **Common misconception:** A prediction is not a promise or guess without a reason; new evidence can show it was wrong.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Find science in daily life

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Give one way a cook uses observation or measurement.

**Marking key:** Example such as measuring ingredients, timing heating or observing colour/texture change (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Identifying science practices in cooking.

### P02 — Match jobs and science use

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match gardener, nurse and builder to observing plant needs, measuring temperature and testing material strength.

**Marking key:** Gardener ↔ plant needs; nurse ↔ temperature; builder ↔ material strength (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting daily work with science practices.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Use a weather pattern

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** A three-day record shows dark clouds followed by rain each day. Make a cautious prediction for a fourth dark-cloud day.

**Marking key:** Rain may follow, with the repeated observation as reason; does not claim certainty (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Making a pattern-based prediction.

### P04 — Observation or prediction

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Classify: ‘The soup is steaming now’ and ‘It may cool if left on the table.’

**Marking key:** Steaming now is an observation; may cool is a prediction (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Distinguishing present evidence from a prediction.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order a practical science process

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: notice slippery floor; place warning/avoid area; identify water as possible cause; dry safely with adult procedure.

**Marking key:** Notice → identify possible cause → warn/avoid → follow safe drying procedure (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing science-informed safety action.

### P06 — Draw pattern evidence

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw a four-day record in which a plant bends toward the window each day. Add one supported prediction.

**Marking key:** Shows repeated bending toward light and predicts continued bending/growth toward the window cautiously (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing evidence for a daily-life prediction.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Explain a material choice

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Why might a raincoat maker choose a water-resistant material rather than absorbent paper?

**Marking key:** Water-resistant material limits water passing through; paper absorbs/weakens, based on material evidence (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using science knowledge in product choice.

### P08 — Compare evidence and guessing

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** One child predicts a puddle will dry because yesterday's puddle dried in sun. Another predicts it because 7 is lucky. Which is scientific and why?

**Marking key:** First prediction; it uses relevant past observation, while lucky number is not evidence about drying (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating reasons for predictions.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Identify a household use

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Describe how a fridge uses science knowledge to help keep food safe.

**Marking key:** Keeps food cooler, slowing spoilage; response connects temperature with storage (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Describing science used in food storage.

### E02 — Match patterns and decisions

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match repeated hot afternoons, soil drying quickly and a torch battery fading to carry water, check plant moisture and replace/recharge battery.

**Marking key:** Each pattern matches the sensible decision (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Using observed patterns for practical decisions.

### E03 — Sequence a prediction check

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: record what happened; notice a pattern; make a prediction; observe the next event.

**Marking key:** Notice pattern → make prediction → observe next event → record result (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Following a prediction-and-check cycle.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Use a cooking observation

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Bread is browning quickly before the timer ends. State one safe science-informed action an adult could take and the evidence used.

**Marking key:** Adult lowers heat/checks/removes as appropriate; evidence is rapid browning, not timer alone (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using observation to adjust a daily process safely.

### E05 — Demonstrate tool choice

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Choose a timer, thermometer or ruler for checking baking duration, body temperature and shelf length. Explain each choice.

**Marking key:** Timer ↔ duration; thermometer ↔ temperature; ruler ↔ length, with correct reasons (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Selecting measurement tools for daily questions.

### E06 — Draw a decision record

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw a simple table showing three days of pet-water checks and one action based on a repeated low-water pattern.

**Marking key:** Records three observations and a responsible action such as more frequent checks/refills with adult oversight (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Communicating pattern-based care decisions.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correct a certainty claim

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A forecast was correct twice, so a child says it can never be wrong. Explain why science predictions can change.

**Marking key:** Predictions use available patterns/evidence but conditions vary; new observations improve or revise them (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Understanding uncertainty in scientific predictions.

### E08 — Analyse a daily-life problem

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Wet towels stayed damp indoors for two days but dried outside on a warm breezy day. Propose a prediction, identify evidence, and name one factor to test fairly next time.

**Marking key:** Predicts warm/breezy outdoor conditions may speed drying; cites records; tests one factor such as airflow while keeping towel size/wetness/location consistent (5 marks).

**Coverage:** Applying patterns and fair comparison to a practical problem.
