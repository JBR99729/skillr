# AC9S1U02 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Concept answer:** Light, temperature, weather and living things can change through a day and across seasons. These patterns affect clothing, activities and care for plants or animals.
- **Key vocabulary:** Daily means within or from one day to another. Seasonal means connected with a season over months; weather is the condition at a particular time.
- **Observable example:** Morning may be cool and dim, midday brighter and warmer, and evening cooler again, so clothing or activities may change.
- **Visual model:** Use labelled day timelines, weather tables and seasonal photo sequences; never rely on colour alone.
- **Common misconception:** A season does not make every day identical. A cool day can occur in summer and weather varies by place.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Name a daily change

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Give one environmental change that can be observed from morning to night.

**Marking key:** One valid change such as daylight, temperature, shadow position or animal activity (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Identifying an observable daily environmental change.

### P02 — Match change and response

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match rain, strong sunshine and cold air to umbrella, shade/hat and warm layer.

**Marking key:** Rain ↔ umbrella; sunshine ↔ shade/hat; cold ↔ warm layer (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Relating conditions to everyday choices.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Build a day timeline

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Arrange sunrise, midday, sunset and night in order and describe the light at two points.

**Marking key:** Correct order and two accurate light observations (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing daily light changes.

### P04 — Weather or season

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A single rainy Tuesday occurs in summer. Does that one day prove the season changed? Explain.

**Marking key:** No; rain is daily weather, while a season is a longer pattern over months (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Distinguishing short-term weather from seasonal patterns.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order plant observations

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order these records through one day: long morning shadow, short midday shadow, long afternoon shadow.

**Marking key:** Morning long → midday short → afternoon long (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing a daily shadow pattern.

### P06 — Draw clothing choices

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw suitable clothing for a hot sunny day and a cool wet day. Label the condition each item responds to.

**Marking key:** Shows sensible sun/heat and rain/cool choices with accurate labels (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing how conditions affect everyday life.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Use a weekly pattern

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A week records 5 cool mornings and 2 warm mornings. What can be said about that week without claiming every morning was cool?

**Marking key:** Cool mornings were more frequent in the recorded week; two mornings were warm, so not all were cool (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Describing a pattern without overgeneralising.

### P08 — Explain an activity change

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Choose an outdoor activity and explain how heat, rain or daylight could change its time, clothing or location.

**Marking key:** Connects one environmental condition to a sensible change in at least two aspects (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Applying environmental changes to everyday planning.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Compare day and night

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** State one difference in light and one possible difference in animal activity between day and night.

**Marking key:** Day has sunlight and night is dark; gives a plausible activity contrast while allowing different animals have different patterns (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Comparing daily environmental conditions.

### E02 — Match seasonal evidence

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match flowering, leaf fall and repeated colder days to possible seasonal observations, noting that patterns vary by plant and place.

**Marking key:** Matches each observation plausibly and acknowledges local variation (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Interpreting seasonal indicators cautiously.

### E03 — Sequence a weather response

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: check the forecast/sky; notice rain is likely; choose rain protection; review conditions before leaving.

**Marking key:** Check conditions → notice rain likely → choose protection → review before leaving (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using observations or predictions in daily planning.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Read a temperature pattern

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Morning is 12°C, midday 21°C and evening 15°C. When is it warmest, and how does evening compare with morning?

**Marking key:** Midday warmest; evening is 3°C warmer than morning (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Interpreting a simple daily temperature record.

### E05 — Demonstrate shadow change

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Use a torch and object model to show how changing light direction changes a shadow. State what stayed the same.

**Marking key:** Changes light direction and observes shadow direction/length change while object remains the same (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Modelling a daily light-and-shadow relationship.

### E06 — Draw a seasonal care plan

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw two panels showing how care for a garden might differ during a hot dry period and a cool wet period.

**Marking key:** Shows evidence-based changes such as watering/shade versus reduced watering/drainage, with condition labels (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing seasonal effects on daily care.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correct a climate overclaim

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A child says, ‘Winter is always snowy everywhere.’ Explain why this is inaccurate.

**Marking key:** Winter conditions depend on location; many places have cool or wet winters without snow, and daily weather varies (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Recognising geographic and daily variation within seasons.

### E08 — Analyse a month record

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A four-week record shows daylight becoming slightly longer and afternoon temperatures usually rising, with several cool days. Describe the pattern and two possible everyday effects without claiming certainty.

**Marking key:** States the overall longer/warmer tendency, notes cool exceptions, and gives two plausible effects such as later outdoor time or lighter clothing (5 marks).

**Coverage:** Using repeated observations to discuss seasonal change and daily life.
