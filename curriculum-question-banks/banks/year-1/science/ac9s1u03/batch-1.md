# AC9S1U03 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Concept answer:** A push moves an object away from the source and a pull draws it closer. Force strength and direction can start, stop, speed up, slow, turn or change shape.
- **Key vocabulary:** Force means a push or pull. Strength describes how strong the force is; direction describes where it acts.
- **Observable example:** A gentle push moves a toy car a short distance; under the same conditions, a stronger push usually changes its speed or distance more.
- **Visual model:** Use arrows for direction and different arrow lengths or labels for strength, plus safe toy and playdough demonstrations.
- **Common misconception:** Moving objects do not always need a continuing push, and a stronger force does not guarantee the same result on different objects or surfaces.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Name the force

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A child draws a wagon toward their body. Is this a push or a pull?

**Marking key:** Pull (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Identifying a pull from movement relative to the source.

### P02 — Match force effects

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match push a ball, pull a drawer and squeeze playdough to move away, move closer/open and change shape.

**Marking key:** Ball push ↔ away; drawer pull ↔ closer/open; squeeze ↔ shape change (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting pushes and pulls with effects.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Compare safe pushes

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Using the same toy car and surface, give one gentle and one stronger push. Describe the observed motion without overclaiming.

**Marking key:** Records a relevant difference in speed/distance and notes the car/surface were kept the same (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Observing effects of force strength.

### P04 — Predict a direction

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A stationary ball is pushed to the left. Predict its first direction of movement and give the reason.

**Marking key:** Left; the applied push acts left (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Predicting motion from force direction.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order a stopping action

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: ball rolls; hand applies a safe opposite push; ball slows; ball stops.

**Marking key:** Ball rolls → opposite push → slows → stops (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing an effect of an opposing force.

### P06 — Draw force arrows

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw a box with one arrow showing a strong push right and one showing a gentle pull left. Label both.

**Marking key:** Correct opposite directions and clear strong/gentle labels or arrow convention (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing force direction and strength.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Explain a shape change

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Why can pressing soft clay change its shape while the same safe press may not visibly change a wooden block?

**Marking key:** Materials respond differently; soft clay deforms more easily while wood is more rigid under that force (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Relating a force effect to material properties.

### P08 — Plan a fair push test

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Plan how to compare a gentle and strong push on one toy car fairly. State what changes, what stays the same and what is observed.

**Marking key:** Changes push strength; keeps car, start and surface the same; observes distance/speed using a consistent method (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Designing a fair investigation of force strength.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Identify two force features

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A labelled arrow points down and is marked strong. State the force's direction and strength.

**Marking key:** Downward and strong (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Reading a force representation.

### E02 — Match actions and force types

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match close a cupboard door, open a zipped bag and stretch a spring gently to push, pull and pull.

**Marking key:** Door close ↔ push; zip open ↔ pull; stretch spring ↔ opposing pulls/pull (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Classifying familiar force actions.

### E03 — Sequence a direction change

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: rolling ball approaches; side push is applied; path bends; ball moves in a new direction.

**Marking key:** Approaches → side push → path bends → new direction (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing a force-caused direction change.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Compare surfaces carefully

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** The same car travels farther on tile than carpet after similar pushes. What observation is supported and what should be kept the same for a fair comparison?

**Marking key:** Supported: farther on tile in this test; same car, start and push method/strength should be controlled (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Describing a motion result and fair-test controls.

### E05 — Demonstrate push and pull

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Use a safe object to show one push and one pull in different directions. Describe each effect.

**Marking key:** Shows and labels both force types, directions and observed motion (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Demonstrating forces and their effects.

### E06 — Draw before and after

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw a sponge before and after a squeeze. Add arrows to show where the forces act.

**Marking key:** Shows a plausible compressed shape and inward force arrows (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing force-caused deformation.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correct a strength conclusion

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Two different balls receive pushes, and the lighter ball goes farther. A student says the push on it must have been stronger. Explain why the conclusion is not certain.

**Marking key:** Balls may differ in mass, material or rolling; push strength was not isolated, so a fair same-object comparison is needed (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating evidence about force strength.

### E08 — Predict a combined effect

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A toy car rolls east. A safe push acts west, then a second push acts north. Predict possible changes after each push and explain why actual results should be observed.

**Marking key:** West push may slow/stop/reverse; north push may turn the path; size/timing of forces and motion affect the result, so test evidence is needed (5 marks).

**Coverage:** Reasoning about sequential force direction effects.
