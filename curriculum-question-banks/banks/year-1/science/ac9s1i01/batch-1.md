# AC9S1I01 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Concept answer:** A useful science question can be explored by observing or testing. A prediction answers the question before the test and gives an evidence-based reason.
- **Key vocabulary:** A relationship is a way two things may be connected. A prediction is an expected result; evidence is an observation that supports a reason.
- **Observable example:** Question: Does a toy car travel farther on tile or carpet? Prediction: tile, because I observed less slowing there before.
- **Visual model:** Use question/prediction/evidence cards and picture comparisons with speech-to-text, tap and paper alternatives.
- **Common misconception:** ‘I like tile’ is a preference, not evidence that predicts motion.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Choose an explorable question

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Turn ‘The plant is taller’ into a question that could be explored with observations.

**Marking key:** Example: Does this plant grow taller each week? or Does more light affect its growth? (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Posing a question from an observation.

### P02 — Match questions and predictions

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match ‘Which ramp is faster?’, ‘Which towel absorbs more?’ and ‘Where does ice melt sooner?’ to relevant predicted outcomes.

**Marking key:** Each question is matched to an outcome addressing ramp, absorbency or melting place (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting predictions directly to questions.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Make a rolling prediction

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Before rolling the same ball on tile and carpet, state a prediction and one relevant experience-based reason.

**Marking key:** Predicts one surface and cites relevant prior rolling/friction observation (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Forming an evidence-based prediction.

### P04 — Question or statement

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Is ‘Do larger shadows form when the torch is closer?’ a question that can be explored? Explain.

**Marking key:** Yes; torch distance and shadow size can be changed/observed safely (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Recognising a testable relationship question.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order inquiry thinking

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: notice repeated wilting; ask whether watering time matters; predict based on experience; plan observations.

**Marking key:** Notice pattern → pose question → predict with reason → plan observations (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing question and prediction development.

### P06 — Draw question evidence

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw two paper planes with different wing shapes and add a question comparing their flight distance.

**Marking key:** Shows two wing forms and a clear comparative question about distance under same conditions (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing a relationship question visually.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Improve a vague question

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Change ‘What happens?’ into a clear question about how ramp height may affect toy-car distance.

**Marking key:** Example: Does the same car travel farther from a higher or lower ramp? (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Refining a question to name variables and observation.

### P08 — Compare two prediction reasons

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Prediction A uses results from three earlier trials. Prediction B uses a favourite colour. Explain which is better supported and why.

**Marking key:** A; repeated relevant observations are evidence, while colour preference is unrelated (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating evidence used for predictions.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Pose a sound question

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Two containers make different sounds when tapped safely. Write a question about one feature that might affect the sound.

**Marking key:** Valid example compares material, fill level or size while observing sound (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Posing a question about an observed relationship.

### E02 — Match observations to questions

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match fast melting near a window, taller shaded seedlings and longer throws with questions about warmth, light and force strength.

**Marking key:** Each observation matches its relevant relationship question (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Linking patterns with explorable questions.

### E03 — Sequence prediction parts

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: expected outcome; question; evidence-based reason; words such as ‘I predict’.

**Marking key:** Question → ‘I predict’/expected outcome → evidence-based reason (2 marks; wording order may combine final two).

**Coverage:** Structuring a prediction that answers a question.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Predict from a simple pattern

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A shadow was shortest near midday on three clear days. Predict its likely size near midday on another clear day and state the evidence.

**Marking key:** Likely short; cites the three repeated clear-day observations without claiming certainty (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using a repeated pattern to predict.

### E05 — Demonstrate question sorting

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Sort cards into explorable questions, preferences and statements. Explain one card in each group.

**Marking key:** Sort is defensible and explanations distinguish observable tests from likes and factual statements (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Classifying inquiry language.

### E06 — Draw a before-test prediction

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw the expected result of dropping equal water drops on paper and plastic, and label the reason for your prediction.

**Marking key:** Shows a plausible absorb/spread contrast and gives a relevant material-experience reason (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Communicating a prediction visually.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correct an after-test prediction

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A student watches the result and then writes, ‘I predict the result I just saw.’ Explain what must happen instead.

**Marking key:** Prediction must be recorded before observing the result; after the test, write an observation and comparison (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Distinguishing predictions from post-test observations.

### E08 — Develop an inquiry pair

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** From the pattern ‘larger pushes often moved the same car farther’, write a fair question and cautious prediction with a relevant reason.

**Marking key:** Question compares push strength for the same car/surface; prediction expects stronger push may move farther; reason cites observed pattern (5 marks).

**Coverage:** Creating a connected question, prediction and evidence chain.
