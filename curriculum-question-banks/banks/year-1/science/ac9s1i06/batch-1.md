# AC9S1I06 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Concept answer:** A science communication tells what was investigated, what was observed and what the evidence supports. Labels, tables, drawings and precise words help the audience understand.
- **Key vocabulary:** A finding is what the evidence shows. Scientific vocabulary names ideas precisely, such as habitat, force, absorb and observation.
- **Observable example:** ‘The cloth absorbed 8 drops, 3 more than paper’ is clearer than ‘Cloth was best’ because it includes evidence.
- **Visual model:** Use title-result-conclusion organisers, labelled diagrams and caption cards with oral, typed and paper options.
- **Common misconception:** A colourful poster is not automatically accurate; every claim must match the observations.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Choose a precise word

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Which scientific word means a push or pull?

**Marking key:** Force (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Using topic-specific science vocabulary.

### P02 — Match text parts

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match title, labelled diagram and finding to names topic, shows parts and states what evidence revealed.

**Marking key:** Title ↔ topic; diagram ↔ parts; finding ↔ evidence result (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Recognising features of a science communication.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Tell an observation

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Using a shown leaf, say or write one precise observation and label it observation.

**Marking key:** States a visible measurable feature without unsupported cause and labels it (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Communicating an observation precisely.

### P04 — Improve a finding

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Rewrite ‘The car went good’ using the evidence that it travelled 12 blocks on tile.

**Marking key:** Example: The car travelled 12 blocks on the tile in this trial (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Replacing vague language with measured evidence.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order a mini-report

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: method; title/question; finding; observations/results.

**Marking key:** Title/question → method → observations/results → finding (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing a simple science report.

### P06 — Draw and label a force

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw a person pushing a box. Add a direction arrow and labels ‘push’ and ‘force’.

**Marking key:** Accurate scene, direction arrow and both scientific labels (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Using a labelled diagram to communicate a force.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Separate observation and idea

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Write one observation about a melting ice cube and one idea that might explain it. Label each.

**Marking key:** Observation describes visible melting/size/time; idea is tentative and uses evidence-related reasoning (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Structuring evidence and interpretation separately.

### P08 — Select a format for data

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** For plant heights over four weeks, choose a table, labelled drawing or oral story as the main record. Justify your choice.

**Marking key:** Table is well justified for exact repeated values, or another format is justified with an accurate comparison method (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Choosing a communication form suited to evidence.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Write a supported sentence

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Data show paper 4 drops, cloth 9 and plastic 0. Write one finding using a number and comparison word.

**Marking key:** Example: Cloth absorbed 9 drops, 5 more than paper and 9 more than plastic (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Writing a quantitative evidence-based finding.

### E02 — Match everyday and scientific words

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match home/place to live, soaks up and careful looking to habitat, absorbs and observation.

**Marking key:** Home/place ↔ habitat; soaks up ↔ absorbs; careful looking ↔ observation (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting familiar language with scientific vocabulary.

### E03 — Sequence presentation parts

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: explain finding; introduce question; show labelled evidence; invite a question.

**Marking key:** Introduce question → show evidence → explain finding → invite question (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Organising a short oral science presentation.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Caption a digital image

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Write a caption for a dated photo showing a seedling with two leaves and a height of 6 cubes.

**Marking key:** Includes date/time context, two leaves and 6-cube height without unsupported cause (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Creating an informative digital-image caption.

### E05 — Demonstrate vocabulary use

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Give a 20-second explanation using the words prediction, observation and result correctly.

**Marking key:** Defines or uses prediction before test, observation as evidence and result as what happened (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Using linked inquiry vocabulary orally.

### E06 — Draw a result display

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Create a labelled three-column picture or block display for roll distances 3, 5 and 4 blocks.

**Marking key:** Accurate labelled values, common scale/unit and readable arrangement (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing findings visually.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correct an exaggerated conclusion

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A poster says, ‘Tile always makes every car fastest’ after one car travelled farther on tile once. Rewrite the conclusion.

**Marking key:** Limits claim to the tested car/trial and measured outcome; avoids ‘always/every’ (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Aligning conclusion strength with evidence.

### E08 — Create a complete science message

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Using results—sun: ice melted in 12 minutes; shade: 20 minutes—write a title/question, brief method, results, finding and one next question.

**Marking key:** All five parts present; same-size/same-container fairness noted; finding says ice melted sooner in sun in this test; next question is relevant (6 marks).

**Coverage:** Communicating a complete investigation accurately.
