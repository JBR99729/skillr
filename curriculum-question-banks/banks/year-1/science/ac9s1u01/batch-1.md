# AC9S1U01 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Concept answer:** Living things survive only when their basic needs are met. Animals need air, water, food and a suitable shelter; plants need air, water, light and a suitable place to grow.
- **Key vocabulary:** A habitat is the place where a living thing lives. A need is something required for survival, not just something preferred.
- **Observable example:** A pond gives a frog water, insects for food, air at the surface and plants or mud for shelter.
- **Visual model:** Use labelled habitat scenes and movable need cards with tap, keyboard and paper matching alternatives.
- **Common misconception:** A habitat is not merely a home shape; it includes the resources and conditions the living thing needs.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Name an animal need

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** State one basic need shared by a dog, a bird and a person.

**Marking key:** Any one of air, water or food (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Identifying a basic need shared by animals.

### P02 — Match needs and habitat features

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match water, food and shelter to pond water, insects and reeds in a frog habitat.

**Marking key:** Water ↔ pond water; food ↔ insects; shelter ↔ reeds (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting habitat features with animal needs.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Build a plant-care set

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** From picture cards showing water, sunlight, a sealed dark box and space, select what a healthy classroom plant needs.

**Marking key:** Selects water, light and suitable space; rejects the sealed dark box (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Recognising basic plant needs.

### P04 — Explain a bird nest

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A nest protects chicks, but it does not provide every need. Name two other things adult birds must find.

**Marking key:** Any two of food, water and air; food and water expected as resources outside the nest (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Distinguishing shelter from other animal needs.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order a pet-care check

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: refill clean water; notice the water bowl is empty; check the animal can reach it; tell the responsible adult.

**Marking key:** Notice empty bowl → tell responsible adult → refill as directed → check access (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing responsible care for a basic need.

### P06 — Draw a useful habitat

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw a habitat for a small fish and label water, food and a safe place to shelter.

**Marking key:** Labels all three relevant habitat features and keeps the fish in suitable water (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing how a habitat meets needs.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Compare two plant places

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** One seedling is beside a bright window and watered; another is sealed in a dark cupboard without water. Predict which is more likely to survive and explain.

**Marking key:** Window seedling; it has light, water and air, while the cupboard seedling lacks key needs (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Using needs to compare plant environments.

### P08 — Design a habitat checklist

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Create a four-part checklist for deciding whether a place can meet a rabbit's basic needs.

**Marking key:** Checks air, water, suitable food and safe shelter/space, with one relevant habitat feature for each (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Applying animal needs to habitat evaluation.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Identify need or want

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A cat has water, food and shelter but no blue toy. Is the blue toy a basic survival need? Explain.

**Marking key:** No; it may support play/enrichment, but colour-specific toy is not required for survival (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Distinguishing needs from preferences.

### E02 — Match living things to resources

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match a tree, bee and lizard to sunlight and water; nectar and air; insects and a warm shelter.

**Marking key:** Tree ↔ sunlight/water; bee ↔ nectar/air; lizard ↔ insects/warm shelter (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Relating varied living things to suitable resources.

### E03 — Sequence habitat use

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order a possum's evening actions: leave tree hollow; eat leaves or fruit; return to shelter; breathe air throughout.

**Marking key:** A sensible sequence begins at shelter, includes feeding, and returns to shelter; air is needed throughout (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Describing how an animal uses habitat resources.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Find the missing resource

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A garden enclosure has shade, air and water for guinea pigs but no food. State the missing need and one suitable action.

**Marking key:** Food; provide suitable guinea-pig food under responsible adult care (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Diagnosing an unmet animal need.

### E05 — Demonstrate a habitat sort

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Sort cards for water, light, seeds, insects, nest and soil into plant needs, animal needs or useful habitat features. Explain two placements.

**Marking key:** Sort recognises plant light/water and animal food/water/air; explanations connect resources to survival (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Classifying needs and habitat features.

### E06 — Draw a needs comparison

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw or diagram one need both a tomato plant and a chicken share, and one need that is met differently.

**Marking key:** Shared need such as water/air; difference such as plant light versus chicken food/shelter, accurately labelled (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Comparing plant and animal needs.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correct a single-need claim

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A student says, ‘A pond gives a duck water, so it meets every need.’ Explain why that conclusion is incomplete.

**Marking key:** The duck also needs air, food and safe shelter/space; one resource alone does not prove all needs are met (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating a habitat claim using multiple needs.

### E08 — Evaluate two habitats

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Compare a bare dry cage and a planted enclosure with clean water, suitable food, air and shelter for a small animal. Choose the more suitable habitat and support the choice with four needs.

**Marking key:** Chooses planted enclosure and links air, water, food and shelter/space to visible features; notes any feature still needing adult checking (5 marks).

**Coverage:** Using evidence to evaluate habitat suitability.
