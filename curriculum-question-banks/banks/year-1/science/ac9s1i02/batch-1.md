# AC9S1I02 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Concept answer:** A safe procedure lists actions in order, controls hazards and says when adult help or protective equipment is needed.
- **Key vocabulary:** A hazard is something that can cause harm. A procedure is an ordered set of steps; a fair test changes one planned factor.
- **Observable example:** For a ramp test, clear the floor, use a light toy, release rather than throw, keep hands away from the path and measure after it stops.
- **Visual model:** Use hazard/safe-action cards, step strips and teacher-approved equipment diagrams with text labels.
- **Common misconception:** Being careful is not a complete procedure; safe instructions must name what to do and avoid.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Identify a safe action

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Before testing toy cars on a ramp, state one action that keeps the path safe.

**Marking key:** Clear the path, keep people out of it or use a stable ramp under supervision (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Identifying a relevant safety control.

### P02 — Match hazards and controls

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match spilled water, unknown substance and moving toy to wipe/mark area with adult help, do not touch/taste, and keep clear of path.

**Marking key:** Each hazard matches its specific control (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting hazards with preventive actions.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Follow a safe water test

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Demonstrate transferring small amounts of clean water between stable plastic cups on a tray.

**Marking key:** Uses small amount, stable cups and tray, carries carefully and responds to spills safely (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Following a teacher-approved procedure.

### P04 — Explain no tasting

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Why must an unknown liquid never be tasted during an investigation?

**Marking key:** Appearance does not prove identity or safety; only approved observation under adult direction is allowed (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Explaining a fundamental investigation safety rule.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order ramp-test steps

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: record distance; clear path; release car from mark; wait until car stops.

**Marking key:** Clear path → release from mark → wait for stop → record distance (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing a safe investigation procedure.

### P06 — Draw a safe setup

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw a safe seed investigation with labelled cups, small water amount, stable tray and adult-approved location.

**Marking key:** Includes all named controls and no unsafe access or spills (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing a safe investigation setup.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Improve an unsafe plan

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A plan says, ‘Run while carrying a glass jar of hot water.’ Replace it with a safe Year 1 investigation approach.

**Marking key:** Do not use/carry hot water or run; use teacher-approved safe-temperature water, stable plastic container and adult handling as required (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Revising unsafe equipment and behaviour.

### P08 — Plan a fair safe test

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Plan how to compare two paper towels using equal water drops. Include two safety steps and two fair-test controls.

**Marking key:** Safety includes tray/wipe spills/no tasting; fairness includes equal towel size and water amount/application (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Combining safety and fairness in a procedure.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Choose protective behaviour

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** During a soil observation, what should a student do before and after handling teacher-approved samples?

**Marking key:** Follow teacher instructions, avoid face/mouth contact and wash hands afterwards (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Applying hygiene controls.

### E02 — Match procedure purposes

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match wear eye protection if directed, label cups and repeat the same release to protect eyes, avoid mix-ups and improve consistency.

**Marking key:** Each action matches the stated safety/data purpose (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Understanding why procedure steps matter.

### E03 — Sequence a spill response

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: move away; tell teacher; notice spill; follow teacher cleanup instruction.

**Marking key:** Notice spill → move/keep others away → tell teacher → follow cleanup instruction (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Responding safely to an incident.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Select suitable equipment

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** For measuring toy-car distance, choose between a paper strip/ruler and standing in front of the moving car. Explain.

**Marking key:** Choose paper strip/ruler from beside/after motion; standing in path is unsafe and unnecessary (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Selecting safe measurement methods.

### E05 — Demonstrate equipment check

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Inspect a simple ramp setup before use and point out stability, clear path and start mark.

**Marking key:** Correctly checks all three features and reports/fixes issues before release (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Conducting a pre-investigation safety check.

### E06 — Draw safe and unsafe panels

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw one unsafe and one corrected safe way to use scissors for cutting test strips, following class rules.

**Marking key:** Unsafe risk is clear; safe panel shows seated controlled cutting, correct grip/carrying and adult/class procedure (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Contrasting risky and controlled procedures.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Audit an incomplete procedure

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A procedure says only ‘Test both balls.’ Name three missing details needed for safety and reliable comparison.

**Marking key:** Examples: safe area, same release/start/surface, observation method, equipment check or adult instruction; any three explained (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating completeness of procedures.

### E08 — Write a complete mini-procedure

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Write ordered steps to test whether ice melts faster in shade or indirect sunlight without touching unknown liquids or leaving a spill hazard.

**Marking key:** Uses equal ice/containers, stable tray, teacher-approved locations, observation timing, safe cleanup and one changed location factor (5 marks).

**Coverage:** Designing a safe, fair and ordered investigation.
