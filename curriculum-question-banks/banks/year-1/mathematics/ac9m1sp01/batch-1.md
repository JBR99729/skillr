# AC9M1SP01 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Key idea:** Shapes are classified by features such as straight or curved edges, corners, flat faces and curved surfaces.
- **Method:** Look at the features, not colour, size or which way the shape is turned; use the same feature to compare.
- **Worked example:** A small tilted square and a large upright square are both squares because each has 4 equal sides and 4 corners.
- **Visual model:** Use sortable shape cards and solid objects with written labels, outlines and touch-friendly rotation.
- **Common trap:** Turning a shape does not change its name or features.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Name by features

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A flat shape has 3 straight sides and 3 corners. Name it.

**Marking key:** Triangle (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Recognising a triangle from defining features.

### P02 — Match objects and shapes

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match clock face, book cover and party hat to circle, rectangle and cone.

**Marking key:** Clock face ↔ circle; book cover ↔ rectangle; party hat ↔ cone (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Recognising shapes in familiar objects.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Sort shape cards

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Sort the provided circles, triangles, squares and rectangles by shape, ignoring colour and size.

**Marking key:** All cards are grouped by defining shape, including rotated examples (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Classifying familiar 2D shapes.

### P04 — Compare square and rectangle

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** State one similarity and one difference between a square and a non-square rectangle.

**Marking key:** Both have 4 straight sides/4 corners; a square has all sides equal while the shown rectangle does not (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Comparing features of quadrilaterals informally.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order a construction

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order the steps to make a triangle from sticks: join final ends; choose 3 sticks; place them so each touches two others; check 3 corners.

**Marking key:** Choose 3 sticks → arrange contacts → join final ends → check 3 corners (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing construction of a familiar shape.

### P06 — Draw rotated shapes

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw two triangles pointing in different directions and label the shared features.

**Marking key:** Both drawings have 3 straight sides and 3 corners, regardless of orientation (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Recognising shape invariance under rotation.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Find the odd object

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A ball, an orange and a box are grouped together as objects that roll easily. Which object does not fit best, and why?

**Marking key:** Box; its flat faces make it slide/stack rather than roll easily like the curved ball/orange (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Classifying objects by surface features.

### P08 — Create a two-rule sort

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Sort a set of familiar shapes first by curved/straight boundaries and then by number of corners. Explain where one shape belongs.

**Marking key:** Applies both stated rules accurately and justifies one placement using visible features (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Creating and explaining classifications.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Identify a solid

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** An object has two flat circular faces and one curved surface. Name the familiar solid.

**Marking key:** Cylinder (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Recognising a cylinder from surface features.

### E02 — Match solids and properties

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match cube, sphere and cylinder to only flat faces; only a curved surface; flat circular faces plus a curved surface.

**Marking key:** Cube ↔ only flat faces; sphere ↔ only curved surface; cylinder ↔ flat circles plus curved surface (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting familiar solids with properties.

### E03 — Sequence shape clues

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order these clues from least to most specific for a square: flat shape; has straight sides; has 4 sides; has 4 equal sides.

**Marking key:** Flat → straight sides → 4 sides → 4 equal sides (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using increasingly specific classification features.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Classify an environment object

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A window is tall and rectangular. Explain why its size does not affect its shape classification.

**Marking key:** Classification uses side/corner features; being tall or large does not change the rectangle's 4 straight sides and corners (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Separating defining from non-defining attributes.

### E05 — Build and compare solids

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Use modelling material to make a ball-like object and a box-like object. Demonstrate one movement difference.

**Marking key:** Makes curved and flat-faced forms; shows curved form rolls while flat-faced form can slide/stack (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Comparing solid objects through construction and movement.

### E06 — Draw a shape hunt

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw and label one circle, rectangle and cylinder found in a classroom or home.

**Marking key:** Three reasonable objects are drawn and correctly linked to the named shapes (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Recognising familiar shapes in the environment.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Audit a colour sort

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A student puts all red shapes together and calls the group ‘triangles’. Explain why the rule is unreliable and give a better rule.

**Marking key:** Colour does not prove shape; sort by 3 sides and 3 corners to identify triangles (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating a non-defining classification rule.

### E08 — Compare four objects

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Compare a can, ball, cereal box and coin. Group them in two useful ways and state the feature used each time.

**Marking key:** Two valid classifications, such as rolls/does not roll and circular face/no circular face, with every placement justified (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Applying multiple classification rules to familiar objects.
