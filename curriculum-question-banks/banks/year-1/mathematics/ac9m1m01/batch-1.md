# AC9M1M01 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Key idea:** Compare one matching attribute at a time: length, mass, capacity or duration.
- **Method:** Compare directly when objects can meet; use the same intermediary, such as one string or container, when they cannot.
- **Worked example:** A string matching Shelf A is longer than Shelf B. If the same string is used, Shelf A is longer.
- **Visual model:** Use aligned objects, balance scales, equal containers, timelines and comparison cards with non-colour labels.
- **Common trap:** Tall does not always mean heavy, and a wider container does not always hold more.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Choose the attribute

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Which attribute tells how much a bottle can hold: length, mass, capacity or duration?

**Marking key:** Capacity (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Naming the attribute used for container volume.

### P02 — Match tools and comparisons

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match balance scale, piece of string and sand timer to mass, length and duration.

**Marking key:** Balance ↔ mass; string ↔ length; timer ↔ duration (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Selecting an intermediary or tool for an attribute.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Compare two masses

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Place a book and pencil on a balance or use the shown balance image. State which is heavier and the evidence.

**Marking key:** Names the lower/heavier side and cites the balance position (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Making a direct mass comparison from evidence.

### P04 — Order three lengths

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Ribbon A is longer than B. Ribbon C is shorter than B. Order A, B and C from shortest to longest.

**Marking key:** C, B, A (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using comparison statements to order length.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Sequence events by duration

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order from shortest to longest: blink, eat lunch, school day.

**Marking key:** Blink → eat lunch → school day (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Ordering familiar event durations.

### P06 — Draw a capacity comparison

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw the same cup being used to compare two jugs. Label the jug needing more cupfuls.

**Marking key:** Shows equal cup units and labels the jug with more cupfuls as greater capacity (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing an indirect capacity comparison.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Use one intermediary

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Two desks are in different rooms. Explain how one piece of string can compare their lengths fairly.

**Marking key:** Match/mark the same string against each desk without stretching it, then compare the marks (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Explaining a fair indirect length comparison.

### P08 — Separate two attributes

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A large empty box and a small box of books are shown. Explain why size alone cannot decide which is heavier and name a fair test.

**Marking key:** Mass and outside size are different; use a balance or lift under safe supervision to compare mass (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Distinguishing mass from visible size.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Read a balance

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A balance shows a toy car side lower than a foam ball. Which has greater mass and how do you know?

**Marking key:** Toy car; its side is lower on the balance (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Interpreting a direct mass comparison.

### E02 — Match comparative language

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match longer, lighter and holds less to length, mass and capacity.

**Marking key:** Longer ↔ length; lighter ↔ mass; holds less ↔ capacity (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting comparison words to attributes.

### E03 — Order by capacity evidence

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Cup counts are Jug P: 4, Jug Q: 7, Jug R: 5 using the same cup. Order least to greatest capacity.

**Marking key:** P, R, Q (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Ordering capacity using a common informal unit.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Compare journey times

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** One walk takes 12 minutes and another takes 8 minutes. Which duration is longer and by how much?

**Marking key:** 12-minute walk; 4 minutes longer (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Comparing measured durations.

### E05 — Demonstrate direct length

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Show how to compare two pencils directly and state the alignment rule.

**Marking key:** Aligns the same starting ends on a straight baseline and compares finishing ends (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Demonstrating a fair direct length comparison.

### E06 — Draw an event timeline

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw and label breakfast, school and bedtime in daily order, then circle the event that usually lasts longest.

**Marking key:** Correct daily order; school circled as the longest of the named events (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing events and reasoning about duration.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correct an unfair capacity test

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** One child uses a small cup for Jug A and a large cup for Jug B, then compares cup counts. Explain why this is unfair and fix it.

**Marking key:** Different units make counts incomparable; use the same cup, filled the same way, for both jugs (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating fairness in indirect capacity comparison.

### E08 — Plan four comparisons

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** For two lunchboxes, describe a fair way to compare length, mass, capacity and time taken to open them. Keep each attribute separate.

**Marking key:** Length: align or same string; mass: balance; capacity: same filler/cup; duration: same timer and start/end rule (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Selecting appropriate methods across four attributes.
