# AC9M1N03 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Key idea:** Equal groups have the same number in every group. Skip counting counts those groups efficiently.
- **Method:** Choose a useful group size, make equal groups, skip count the full groups, then add any leftovers.
- **Worked example:** Six bags with 5 shells in each contain 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30 shells.
- **Visual model:** Use grouped counters, bundles, arrays or labelled hoops. Online grouping must also work by tap and keyboard.
- **Common trap:** Do not skip count unequal groups as if they were equal; count leftovers separately.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Count groups of two

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Four equal pairs of socks are shown. How many socks are there? Show the skip count.

**Marking key:** 2, 4, 6, 8; there are 8 socks (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Quantifying equal groups of 2.

### P02 — Match groups and totals

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match 3 groups of 5, 6 groups of 2 and 2 groups of 10 to 15, 12 and 20.

**Marking key:** 3 groups of 5 ↔ 15; 6 groups of 2 ↔ 12; 2 groups of 10 ↔ 20 (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting equal-group descriptions to totals.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Group twenty-five

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Arrange 25 counters into equal groups of 5 and skip count to find the total.

**Marking key:** Makes 5 groups of 5 and counts 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using materials and skip counting by 5.

### P04 — Tens and leftovers

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A jar holds 36 buttons. Explain how groups of 10 help count them.

**Marking key:** 3 full groups of 10 give 30, with 6 leftover; 30 + 6 = 36 (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Partitioning a collection into tens and ones.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order a five-count

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Put these number cards in the counting order used for 7 groups of 5: 25, 5, 35, 15, 30, 10, 20.

**Marking key:** 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35 (1 mark for full order).

**Coverage:** Sequencing skip counts by 5.

### P06 — Draw pairs to eighteen

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw 18 dots in equal groups of 2 and label the skip count.

**Marking key:** Shows 9 pairs and labels 2 through 18 by twos (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing a collection as equal pairs.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Count full groups and extras

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Twenty-two beads are placed in groups of 5. How many full groups and leftovers are there, and what is the total check?

**Marking key:** 4 full groups and 2 leftovers; 5, 10, 15, 20, then 21, 22 (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Combining skip counting with leftovers.

### P08 — Choose an efficient grouping

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Explain an efficient way to count 47 loose counters and why it is reliable.

**Marking key:** Makes 4 groups of 10 and 7 singles, counts 10, 20, 30, 40, then 7 more, and checks groups are complete (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Selecting and justifying an efficient grouping strategy.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Count six groups

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** There are 6 trays with 10 seedlings on each tray. State the total and the count used.

**Marking key:** 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60; 60 seedlings (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Skip counting groups of 10.

### E02 — Match fresh collections

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match 8 pairs, 4 groups of 5 and 7 groups of 10 to 16, 20 and 70.

**Marking key:** 8 pairs ↔ 16; 4 groups of 5 ↔ 20; 7 groups of 10 ↔ 70 (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Relating group structures to totals.

### E03 — Order the bundle count

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** A class bundles 53 straws. Order the counting steps: count 3 singles; make 5 bundles of 10; skip count 10 to 50.

**Marking key:** Make 5 bundles of 10 → skip count 10 to 50 → count 3 singles (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing a tens-and-ones count.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Find the hidden total

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Five equal bowls each contain 2 apples, and 3 apples remain on the table. How many apples are there?

**Marking key:** 5 groups of 2 = 10, then 10 + 3 = 13 apples (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Adding leftovers after equal-group counting.

### E05 — Demonstrate two group sizes

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Show 20 counters first in groups of 5 and then in groups of 2. State the number of groups each time.

**Marking key:** 4 groups of 5 and 10 groups of 2, both totalling 20 (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Repartitioning one collection into different equal groups.

### E06 — Draw a grouped collection

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw 42 marks using groups of 10 and leftovers. Label every group and the total.

**Marking key:** Shows 4 groups of 10 and 2 singles, labelled 42 (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Recording a large collection with grouped marks.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Audit an unequal count

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Sam counts bags containing 5, 5, 4 and 5 marbles as 5, 10, 15, 20. Explain the error and find the actual total.

**Marking key:** The third bag is not a full group of 5; actual total is 5 + 5 + 4 + 5 = 19 (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Detecting misuse of skip counting with unequal groups.

### E08 — Compare two counting plans

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** For 68 craft sticks, compare counting every stick with making groups of 10. Give a grouped count and explain which plan is easier to check.

**Marking key:** 6 groups of 10 and 8 singles make 68; grouping by 10 is easier to track/check because fewer count steps are needed (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating counting strategies for a large collection.
