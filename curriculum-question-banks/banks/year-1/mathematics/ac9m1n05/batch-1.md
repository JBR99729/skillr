# AC9M1N05 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Key idea:** A mathematical model turns a real situation into objects, a drawing or an equation that can be solved and checked.
- **Method:** Ask what is known and unknown, choose addition or subtraction, represent the story, calculate, then answer with a label.
- **Worked example:** A bookmark costs $3 and a pencil costs $4. A bar model shows 3 + 4, so the total is $7.
- **Visual model:** Use counters, Australian play coins, bar models and part-part-whole diagrams with accessible move-and-place controls.
- **Common trap:** Do not choose an operation from one word alone; decide whether the amount joins, leaves or is compared.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Join two collections

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** There are 7 red apples and 5 green apples. Draw or write a model and find the total.

**Marking key:** Model shows 7 + 5; total 12 apples (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Modelling an additive joining situation.

### P02 — Match stories and equations

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match these stories to 9 + 4, 13 − 4 and 13 − 9: 9 birds joined by 4; 4 of 13 birds fly away; compare 13 birds with 9 birds.

**Marking key:** Joined ↔ 9 + 4; flew away ↔ 13 − 4; comparison ↔ 13 − 9 (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Selecting operations from three additive structures.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Pay with play coins

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Show one way to pay exactly $8 using $1 and $2 play coins, then count the value.

**Marking key:** Any exact combination, such as four $2 coins, with correct count to $8 (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing a simple money amount.

### P04 — Find what remains

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A basket holds 16 oranges. Six are used. Represent and solve the situation.

**Marking key:** A removal model or 16 − 6 = 10; 10 oranges remain (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Modelling subtraction as removal.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order the modelling cycle

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Put these actions in order: answer with units; choose an operation and model; read what changes; calculate and check.

**Marking key:** Read what changes → choose an operation and model → calculate and check → answer with units (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing a mathematical-modelling process.

### P06 — Draw a money model

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** A card costs $6 and a ribbon costs $3. Draw coins or a bar model and show the total cost.

**Marking key:** Represents 6 and 3, combines them, and labels $9 (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing and solving a purchase total.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Work out the hidden start

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** After 5 shells are added, a collection has 14 shells. How many were there at first? Show an equation.

**Marking key:** 9 shells; 9 + 5 = 14 or 14 − 5 = 9 (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Modelling a change problem with an unknown start.

### P08 — Choose and justify a model

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A child has $15 and spends $7. Choose counters, coins, a bar model or a number line, solve the problem and explain why the model fits.

**Marking key:** $8 remains; representation shows 15 − 7 and explanation connects spending with a decrease (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Selecting, using and justifying a mathematical model.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Find a combined cost

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A fruit cup costs $5 and water costs $2. What is the total cost? Include a model or equation.

**Marking key:** $7; 5 + 2 = 7 with an appropriate representation (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Solving a simple transaction total.

### E02 — Match fresh models

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match 11 + 6, 17 − 6 and 17 − 11 to: joining 6 with 11; taking 6 from 17; finding how many more 17 is than 11.

**Marking key:** Joining ↔ 11 + 6; taking ↔ 17 − 6; comparing ↔ 17 − 11 (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Distinguishing additive situation types.

### E03 — Plan before calculating

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order the steps for a story about 18 balloons with 9 popping: state what is unknown; represent 18 − 9; note that balloons leave; solve and label.

**Marking key:** Note balloons leave → state remaining is unknown → represent 18 − 9 → solve 9 balloons and label (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Planning a subtraction model from context.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Compare pocket money

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Ravi has $12 and Mei has $8. How much more does Ravi have? Show the comparison.

**Marking key:** $4 more; 12 − 8 = 4 or a comparison bar model (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Modelling an additive comparison involving money.

### E05 — Act out a missing change

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Start with an unknown number of counters. Add 7 to finish with 19. Demonstrate how to find the start.

**Marking key:** Removes 7 from 19 or counts back to 12; starting amount 12 (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using materials for an unknown-start problem.

### E06 — Draw two-step evidence

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Mia buys a $4 notebook and a $5 pen and pays with $10. Draw a model showing the total and money left.

**Marking key:** Total $9, then $10 − $9 = $1 left; drawing clearly shows both steps (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing a two-step money situation.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Check an unreasonable answer

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** For 13 toy cars with 4 given away, a student answers 17. Explain why it is unreasonable and correct it.

**Marking key:** The amount must decrease; 13 − 4 = 9 cars, and 9 + 4 checks to 13 (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating reasonableness in a subtraction model.

### E08 — Model two possible stories

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Create one joining story and one comparison story that both use the numbers 8 and 6. Write and solve an equation for each.

**Marking key:** Valid joining story uses 8 + 6 = 14; valid comparison uses 8 − 6 = 2, with quantities and labels (5 marks).

**Coverage:** Creating and solving different additive models from the same numbers.
