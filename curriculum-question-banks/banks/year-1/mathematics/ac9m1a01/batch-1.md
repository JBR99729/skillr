# AC9M1A01 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Key idea:** A skip-counting pattern changes by the same amount each step, often +2, +5 or +10.
- **Method:** Compare neighbouring terms to find the rule, apply it to continue, and check every step follows the same rule.
- **Worked example:** 15, 20, 25, 30 follows +5. The next two terms are 35 and 40.
- **Visual model:** Use a number line, hundreds chart, bead string or repeating groups with movable number cards.
- **Common trap:** The starting number does not have to equal the step; 3, 5, 7 still counts by 2.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Name the rule

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** What is the rule for 6, 8, 10, 12? State the next number.

**Marking key:** Add 2; next is 14 (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Recognising a skip-counting rule by 2.

### P02 — Match patterns and steps

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match 5,10,15; 12,22,32; and 7,9,11 to +5, +10 and +2.

**Marking key:** 5,10,15 ↔ +5; 12,22,32 ↔ +10; 7,9,11 ↔ +2 (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting sequences with their constant steps.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Build a five-pattern

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Use counters in groups of 5 to show 10, 15, 20 and the next term.

**Marking key:** Shows groups representing 10, 15, 20, 25 and states 25 (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing a +5 sequence concretely.

### P04 — Fill two missing terms

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Complete 30, 40, __, __, 70 and explain the rule.

**Marking key:** 50 and 60; add 10 each time (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Continuing a skip-counting sequence by 10.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order a two-pattern

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order the cards 18, 12, 16, 10, 14 to make an increasing +2 pattern.

**Marking key:** 10, 12, 14, 16, 18 (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Sequencing numbers by twos.

### P06 — Draw shape groups

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw a pattern with 2 shapes in the first group, 4 in the second and 6 in the third. Add the next group.

**Marking key:** Shows groups of 2, 4, 6 and 8 shapes (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing a growing +2 pattern visually.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Find an incorrect term

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** The sequence is 20, 25, 30, 36, 40. Which term breaks the rule and what should it be?

**Marking key:** 36 breaks +5; it should be 35 (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Checking consistency of a number pattern.

### P08 — Create from an unusual start

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Create six terms of a +10 pattern starting at 7 and explain how the ones digit helps check it.

**Marking key:** 7, 17, 27, 37, 47, 57; the ones digit remains 7 when 10 is added (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Creating and explaining a +10 sequence.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Continue from twenty-three

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Write the next three terms in 23, 25, 27, __, __, __.

**Marking key:** 29, 31, 33 (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Continuing a +2 sequence from an odd start.

### E02 — Match representations

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match 8,13,18; 40,50,60; and 14,16,18 to groups growing by 5, 10 and 2.

**Marking key:** 8,13,18 ↔ +5; 40,50,60 ↔ +10; 14,16,18 ↔ +2 (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Interpreting varied skip-counting patterns.

### E03 — Rebuild a mixed-up pattern

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order 55, 35, 50, 40, 45 into a +5 sequence.

**Marking key:** 35, 40, 45, 50, 55 (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Ordering terms according to a +5 rule.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Use a chart clue

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** On a hundreds chart, a path goes 26, 36, 46, 56. State the movement and next two numbers.

**Marking key:** Move down one row/add 10; 66, 76 (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Connecting chart movement with skip counting by 10.

### E05 — Demonstrate equal jumps

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Show the pattern 4, 9, 14, 19 on a number line and state the jump size.

**Marking key:** Marks equal +5 jumps and states 5 (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Demonstrating a constant difference on a number line.

### E06 — Draw a symbol growth

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw four groups in which the number of stars grows 10, 20, 30, 40. Label the rule.

**Marking key:** Shows and labels four totals with rule +10 (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Creating a visual skip-counting pattern.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Compare two sequences

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Sequence A is 2,4,6,8. Sequence B is 2,7,12,17. Explain one similarity and one difference.

**Marking key:** Both increase by a constant amount; A adds 2 while B adds 5 (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Comparing constant-step sequences.

### E08 — Find the hidden start

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A +5 sequence ends 31, 36, 41. Write the three terms before 31 and explain your backward rule.

**Marking key:** 16, 21, 26; subtract 5 to work backwards, then +5 verifies all terms (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Extending a sequence backwards and justifying it.
