# AC9M1SP02 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Key idea:** Directions describe movement using a clear start, distance or steps, turn and destination.
- **Method:** Face the stated direction, follow one instruction at a time, and use landmarks to check position.
- **Worked example:** From the door, move 3 steps forward, turn right and move 2 steps to reach the bookshelf.
- **Visual model:** Use floor grids, arrows, maps and movable character tokens with spoken and keyboard alternatives.
- **Common trap:** Left and right depend on the mover's facing direction, so always state or show which way they face.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Follow one move

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A robot faces north. It moves 2 squares forward. Describe its movement and final relative position.

**Marking key:** Moves 2 squares north/forward from its start (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Following a simple directional instruction.

### P02 — Match direction words

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match forward, turn left and behind to straight ahead, rotate to the left side and at the back of.

**Marking key:** Each term matches its stated spatial meaning (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Understanding common direction and location language.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Walk a route

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** From a marked start, take 3 steps forward, turn left and take 1 step. State the landmark reached.

**Marking key:** Follows the route accurately and names the marked destination (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Demonstrating a multi-step route.

### P04 — Complete an instruction

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A toy is one square right of the start. Write one clear direction to reach it.

**Marking key:** Move 1 square right, with the starting facing direction clear if needed (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Giving a direct movement instruction.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order route cards

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order these cards: reach the tree; move 2 steps; face the tree; stop and check.

**Marking key:** Face tree → move 2 steps → reach tree → stop/check (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing route instructions.

### P06 — Draw arrows to a goal

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** On a 4-by-4 grid, draw a route from the bottom-left square to the square 2 up and 1 right.

**Marking key:** Route has two upward moves and one right move in any valid order and reaches the target (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing movement on a grid.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Explain a left-right change

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Kai faces the board, then turns around. Explain why the window that was on his left is now on his right.

**Marking key:** Turning changes Kai's facing direction, so his body-relative left/right reverse (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Reasoning about orientation and relative direction.

### P08 — Write an unambiguous route

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Write directions from the classroom door to a desk using a start, at least one turn, a step count and a landmark check.

**Marking key:** Includes all four features in a route that can be followed without guessing (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Creating precise multi-step directions.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Locate from clues

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** The lunchbox is under the table and to the left of the chair. Describe its position using both clues.

**Marking key:** Under the table and left of the chair (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Combining relative-location descriptions.

### E02 — Match arrows and words

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match ↑, → and ↶ to forward/up, right and turn left.

**Marking key:** ↑ ↔ forward/up on the map; → ↔ right; ↶ ↔ turn left (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting direction symbols and words.

### E03 — Sequence a map journey

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: turn right at the gate; start at the house; walk to the gate; finish at the pond.

**Marking key:** Start at house → walk to gate → turn right → finish at pond (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Ordering directions from a stated start.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Find a missing move

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A token moves 2 squares up from the start. The goal is the square immediately to its right. State the missing move.

**Marking key:** Move 1 square right (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Completing a route from relative-location information.

### E05 — Demonstrate opposite turns

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Face forward, turn left, return to the start direction, then turn right. Show the two final facing directions.

**Marking key:** Accurately demonstrates left and right as opposite quarter-turn directions (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Demonstrating turns from a common orientation.

### E06 — Draw two valid paths

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw two different routes from the top-left to bottom-right of a 3-by-3 grid without leaving the grid.

**Marking key:** Both routes begin/end correctly, remain inside the grid and use clear arrows (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Creating alternative routes to one destination.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Repair vague directions

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A note says ‘Go over there, then turn.’ Explain two details it needs before someone can follow it.

**Marking key:** Needs a clear start/facing direction and destination, distance/step count, turn direction or landmark; any two explained (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating clarity of movement instructions.

### E08 — Compare two routes

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Route A uses 3 steps right then 2 down. Route B uses 2 down then 3 right. Explain when they reach the same place and what could make one safer.

**Marking key:** On an open grid they have the same displacement/end; obstacles or crossings can make one route safer, with a relevant example (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Comparing routes using destination and constraints.
