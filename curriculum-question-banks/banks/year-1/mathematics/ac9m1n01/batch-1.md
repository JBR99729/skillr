# AC9M1N01 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Key idea:** The place of a digit tells its value. In 84, the 8 means 8 tens
  and the 4 means 4 ones.
- **Method:** To compare numbers, look at the hundreds first, then the tens, then
  the ones. On a number line, numbers increase as you move right. On a standard
  hundreds chart, right adds 1 and down adds 10.
- **Worked example:** Compare 68 and 86. The number 68 has 6 tens, while 86 has
  8 tens. Because 8 tens is greater than 6 tens, **86 > 68**.
- **Visual model:** Use bundles of 10 and single counters, an empty number line,
  and a chart extending to 120. Online ordering uses draggable number cards with
  tap-to-place and keyboard alternatives; on paper, students write the order.
- **Common trap:** Do not reverse look-alike numbers such as 16 and 61. Also
  remember that counting continues from 99 to 100.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Reading a two-digit numeral

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Question:** Which numeral shows seventy-two?

A. 27

B. 72

C. 70

D. 82

**Correct answer:** B — Seventy-two is 7 tens and 2 ones, so it is written as 72.

**Coverage:** Reading a spoken two-digit number as a numeral.

### P02 — Ordering number cards

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Show the cards 16, 60, 61 and 66. Online, students drag or tap each
card into a least-to-greatest row; arrow keys and an Enter/Space placement option
must be available. On paper, students write the ordered list.

**Question:** Put the number cards in order from smallest to largest.

**Marking key:** 16, 60, 61, 66 (1 mark for the complete correct order). Accept a
correct oral ordering when the task is teacher-read.

**Coverage:** Ordering easily confused two-digit numerals.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Missing number on a line

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Show a horizontal number line with equally spaced labels 44, 45,
blank and 47. Include a text alternative stating the labels in order.

**Question:** Write the missing number and explain how the number line helps.

**Marking key:** 46 (1 mark); explains that the labels increase by 1 from left to
right or that 46 is between 45 and 47 (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Locating a number in the counting sequence on a number line.

### P04 — Bundles of tens and ones

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Show 8 tied bundles of 10 sticks and 4 single sticks. Provide alt
text stating the same quantities.

**Question:** Which number is represented?

A. 48

B. 80

C. 84

D. 804

**Correct answer:** C — Eight tens are 80 and 4 ones make 84.

**Coverage:** Connecting a concrete tens-and-ones model to a numeral.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Moving down a hundreds chart

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Show a small standard hundreds-chart excerpt containing 24, 25,
34, 35, 44 and 45. Highlight 34 without highlighting the answer.

**Question:** Which number is directly below 34?

A. 35

B. 44

C. 24

D. 43

**Correct answer:** B — Moving down one row adds 10, so 34 becomes 44.

**Coverage:** Using the vertical pattern in a standard hundreds chart.

### P06 — Crossing from 99 to 100

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Complete the count: 97, 98, 99, ___, 101. Explain what comes after
99.

**Marking key:** 100 (1 mark); explains that counting on 1 from 99 gives 100 (1
mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Continuing the natural-number sequence across 99 and 100.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Explaining 16 and 61

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Show the numerals 16 and 61 beside optional tens-and-ones frames.

**Question:** Liam says, “16 and 61 are the same number because they use the same
digits.” Is Liam correct? Explain the value of each digit and state which number
is greater.

**Marking key:** States that Liam is not correct (1 mark); identifies 16 as 1 ten
and 6 ones (1 mark); identifies 61 as 6 tens and 1 one (1 mark); states 61 is
greater because it has more tens (1 mark). 4 marks total.

**Coverage:** Reasoning about place value in reversed two-digit numerals.

### P08 — Solving a number riddle

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Online, provide an accessible 100-to-120 number line on which the
student can tap, click or use arrow keys to select a point. On paper, show the
same labelled line and ask the student to circle the number.

**Question:** A mystery number is greater than 109, less than 120 and has 5 ones.
What is the number? Show or explain how you know.

**Marking key:** 115 (1 mark); explains that it lies from 110 to 119 and that 5 in
the ones place makes 115, or marks 115 correctly on the line (1 mark). 2 marks
total.

**Coverage:** Combining interval and place-value clues to identify a number.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Reading a three-digit number

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Question:** Which numeral shows one hundred and twelve?

A. 102

B. 112

C. 120

D. 12

**Correct answer:** B — One hundred and twelve is 1 hundred, 1 ten and 2 ones.

**Coverage:** Connecting a number name to a numeral beyond 100.

### E02 — Ordering across tens and hundreds

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Show the cards 39, 93, 103 and 109. Online, offer drag, tap-to-place
and keyboard ordering; on paper, provide four answer boxes.

**Question:** Arrange the cards from least to greatest.

**Marking key:** 39, 93, 103, 109 (1 mark for the complete correct order).

**Coverage:** Ordering two- and three-digit numbers to at least 120.

### E03 — Ten bundles and loose ones

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Show 10 bundles of 10 counters and 5 single counters, with matching
alt text.

**Question:** Which number does the model show?

A. 15

B. 100

C. 105

D. 150

**Correct answer:** C — Ten bundles of 10 make 100; 5 more ones make 105.

**Coverage:** Representing a number above 100 with grouped materials.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Completing an empty number line

**Type:** Short response

**Delivery:** Show equally spaced marks labelled 82, blank, 84, blank and 86.
Include the sequence as a text alternative.

**Question:** Fill both blank positions and state the counting step.

**Marking key:** Writes 83 and 85 in the correct positions (2 marks); states that
the line counts by ones (1 mark). 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Reading scale and relative position on an empty number line.

### E05 — Moving above on a chart

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Delivery:** Show a standard number-chart excerpt with 63 directly above 73.

**Question:** Which number is directly above 73?

A. 63

B. 72

C. 74

D. 83

**Correct answer:** A — Moving up one row subtracts 10: 73 − 10 = 63.

**Coverage:** Using the upward pattern in a standard number chart.

### E06 — Checking a comparison claim

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Mina says, “96 is greater than 106 because 9 is greater than 1.” Is
Mina correct? Explain using place value.

**Marking key:** States that Mina is not correct (1 mark); explains that 106 has
1 hundred while 96 has no hundreds, so 106 is greater (1 mark). 2 marks total.

**Coverage:** Comparing two- and three-digit numbers using the highest place.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Ordering and justifying

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Put 90, 99, 109 and 119 in order from smallest to largest. Explain
how the hundreds, tens and ones helped you decide.

**Marking key:** 90, 99, 109, 119 (2 marks); explains that the two-digit numbers
come before the three-digit numbers (1 mark); correctly compares tens or ones
within at least one pair, such as 90 before 99 or 109 before 119 (1 mark). 4
marks total.

**Coverage:** Ordering a mixed set and justifying the order with place value.

### E08 — Following a chart path

**Type:** Extended response

**Delivery:** Show a standard number chart extending past 100 with 87 selected.
Online, allow the student to follow arrow buttons with visible labels and a
keyboard alternative. On paper, show one down arrow followed by 3 right arrows.

**Question:** Start at 87. Move down one row, then move right 3 spaces. Which
number do you reach? Explain each change.

**Marking key:** Reaches 100 (1 mark); explains that down adds 10, giving 97 (1
mark); explains that 3 spaces right add 3, giving 100 (1 mark). 3 marks total.

**Coverage:** Applying horizontal and vertical number-chart patterns across 100.
