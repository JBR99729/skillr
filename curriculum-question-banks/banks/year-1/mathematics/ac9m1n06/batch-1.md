# AC9M1N06 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Key idea:** Equal sharing asks how many each person receives; grouping asks how many equal groups can be made.
- **Method:** Represent every object, make equal groups, count the result and check that no object was lost or counted twice.
- **Worked example:** Share 12 counters among 3 plates one at a time. Each plate gets 4, and 3 groups of 4 return to 12.
- **Visual model:** Use counters and hoops, plates, equal rows or grouping boxes with drag, tap and keyboard placement.
- **Common trap:** Equal means every full group has the same amount; leftovers must be reported, not hidden.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Share eight equally

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Eight strawberries are shared equally between 2 children. How many does each child receive?

**Marking key:** 4 each; a model shows 2 equal groups of 4 (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Modelling equal sharing between 2 recipients.

### P02 — Match sharing models

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match 12 shared among 3, 10 shared among 2 and 15 shared among 5 to 4 each, 5 each and 3 each.

**Marking key:** 12 among 3 ↔ 4 each; 10 among 2 ↔ 5 each; 15 among 5 ↔ 3 each (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting sharing situations to equal shares.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Make groups of three

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Use 15 counters to make groups of 3. State the number of groups.

**Marking key:** Makes 5 equal groups of 3 (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Modelling grouping with a fixed group size.

### P04 — Identify a leftover

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Fourteen pencils are placed into groups of 4. How many full groups and leftovers are there?

**Marking key:** 3 full groups of 4 and 2 pencils left over (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Reporting full groups and remainders informally.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order fair sharing

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order these steps for sharing 9 beads among 3 bowls: check all bowls are equal; place one bead in each bowl repeatedly; count 9 beads; state 3 in each.

**Marking key:** Count 9 → deal one to each bowl repeatedly → check equal → state 3 each (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing a fair-sharing method.

### P06 — Draw equal rows

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw 16 dots in 4 equal rows and label the number in each row.

**Marking key:** Shows 4 rows of 4 and labels 4 in each row (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing equal sharing as an array.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Decide share or group

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A coach has 18 markers and puts 3 in each bag. Is this sharing or grouping, and how many bags are filled?

**Marking key:** Grouping because the bag size is known; 6 bags of 3 (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Distinguishing grouping from sharing.

### P08 — Repair an unfair model

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A student shares 11 counters among 3 plates as 4, 4 and 3 and says it is equal. Explain what is wrong and describe the result accurately.

**Marking key:** The shares are not equal; 3 each can be shared equally with 2 left over, or the task needs a rule for leftovers (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating equality and reporting leftovers.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Share a new collection

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Twenty shells are shared equally among 5 students. Find each share and show a check.

**Marking key:** 4 each; 5 equal groups of 4 total 20 (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Solving an equal-sharing situation.

### E02 — Match groups to counts

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match 18 objects in groups of 2, 20 objects in groups of 5 and 12 objects in groups of 4 to 9, 4 and 3 groups.

**Marking key:** 18 in twos ↔ 9 groups; 20 in fives ↔ 4 groups; 12 in fours ↔ 3 groups (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Finding the number of equal groups.

### E03 — Sequence a grouping check

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order the steps for putting 24 blocks into groups of 6: form equal groups of 6; count all 24; count 4 groups; check 4 groups contain 24.

**Marking key:** Count 24 → form groups of 6 → count 4 groups → check the grouped total is 24 (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing grouping and verification.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Solve with one leftover

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Thirteen biscuits are placed 3 on each plate. How many full plates and biscuits left over?

**Marking key:** 4 full plates and 1 biscuit left over (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Solving a grouping problem with a leftover.

### E05 — Demonstrate two interpretations

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Use 12 counters to show ‘shared among 4’ and then ‘groups of 4’. State both answers.

**Marking key:** Shared among 4 gives 3 each; groups of 4 gives 3 groups (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Comparing sharing and grouping language.

### E06 — Draw a fair plan

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw how 18 stickers can be shared equally among 6 children. Label each share and the total.

**Marking key:** Shows 6 equal groups of 3, labelled 3 each and total 18 (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Recording an equal-sharing model.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Explain two correct models

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Luca shows 4 groups of 5 counters. Zoe shows 5 groups of 4 counters. Explain what is the same and different.

**Marking key:** Both total 20; one has 4 groups with 5 in each, the other 5 groups with 4 in each (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Comparing equal-group arrangements with the same total.

### E08 — Design a fair distribution

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** There are 22 craft items for 5 tables. Give the greatest equal whole-number share, report leftovers and explain how you checked.

**Marking key:** 4 per table with 2 left; 5 groups of 4 make 20 and 20 + 2 = 22 (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Modelling and verifying sharing with leftovers.
