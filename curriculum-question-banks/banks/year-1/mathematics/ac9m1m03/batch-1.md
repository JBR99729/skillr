# AC9M1M03 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Key idea:** Sequence tells what happens first, next and last; duration tells how long an event lasts.
- **Method:** Choose a sensible unit: hours for part of a day, days for a week, weeks for longer plans, months and years for long periods.
- **Worked example:** A lesson from 9 am to 10 am lasts 1 hour. Monday to the next Monday is 1 week.
- **Visual model:** Use calendars, day strips, month wheels and simple timelines with movable event cards.
- **Common trap:** A start or finish time is not the duration; duration is the amount of time between them.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Choose a time unit

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Would the length of a school day be best described in hours, months or years?

**Marking key:** Hours (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Selecting a sensible duration unit.

### P02 — Match events and units

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match a birthday-to-birthday, a school term and a night's sleep to year, months and hours.

**Marking key:** Birthday-to-birthday ↔ year; school term ↔ months; sleep ↔ hours (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting familiar durations with units.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Build a week

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Arrange day cards from Monday through Sunday and state how many days make the week.

**Marking key:** Correct Monday–Sunday order and 7 days (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing days and naming week length.

### P04 — Find a one-hour duration

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Art begins at 1 pm and ends at 2 pm. How long does it last?

**Marking key:** 1 hour (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Calculating a whole-hour duration.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order a morning routine

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: arrive at school, wake up, eat breakfast, begin class.

**Marking key:** Wake up → eat breakfast → arrive at school → begin class (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Sequencing familiar daily events.

### P06 — Draw a three-day plan

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw three boxes labelled Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday. Place library on Wednesday and show the day before and after.

**Marking key:** Tuesday before, Wednesday library, Thursday after (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing neighbouring days on a timeline.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Distinguish date and duration

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A camp starts on Monday and lasts 3 days. Explain why ‘Monday’ is not its duration.

**Marking key:** Monday names the start day; 3 days tells how long the camp lasts (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Separating temporal position from duration.

### P08 — Choose units for four events

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Choose hours, days, months or years for a movie, a cold, a season and time at primary school. Explain each choice briefly.

**Marking key:** Movie-hours; cold-days; season-months; primary school-years, with sensible reasoning (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Applying time units across different scales.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Name the next month

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** What month comes after September, and what month comes before it?

**Marking key:** October comes after; August comes before (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using the sequence of months.

### E02 — Match calendar intervals

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match 14 days, 12 months and 24 hours to 2 weeks, 1 year and 1 day.

**Marking key:** 14 days ↔ 2 weeks; 12 months ↔ 1 year; 24 hours ↔ 1 day (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Relating common calendar units.

### E03 — Order a year sequence

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Put March, January, April and February in calendar order.

**Marking key:** January, February, March, April (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Sequencing named months.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Work out elapsed hours

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A visit begins at 10 am and ends at 1 pm. Count the whole hours of duration.

**Marking key:** 3 hours: 10–11, 11–12 and 12–1 (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Counting whole-hour intervals.

### E05 — Demonstrate calendar movement

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** On a calendar, start at Thursday and move forward 5 days. Name the landing day.

**Marking key:** Tuesday, counting Friday 1 through Tuesday 5 (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Moving forward through the repeating week.

### E06 — Draw a simple timeline

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw a timeline for planting a seed, seeing a sprout one week later and seeing a taller plant one month later.

**Marking key:** Events are in chronological order with one week and one month intervals labelled (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing event sequence and duration.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Correct a duration claim

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A student says a weekend lasts 2 months because Saturday and Sunday are two names. Explain the error.

**Marking key:** Names do not determine units; Saturday and Sunday are 2 days, not months (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Reasoning about quantity and time units.

### E08 — Plan and justify a time record

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Choose a suitable unit and simple recording method for a one-hour sport lesson, a two-week reading challenge and a year of birthdays.

**Marking key:** Hours/timeline for lesson; days or weeks/calendar for challenge; months/year calendar for birthdays, with reasons (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Selecting units and representations for varied durations.
