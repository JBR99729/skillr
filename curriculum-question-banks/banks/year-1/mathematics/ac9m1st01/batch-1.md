# AC9M1ST01 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Key idea:** Categorical data sorts answers into named groups, such as favourite fruit or travel-to-school type.
- **Method:** Ask one clear question, use categories that do not overlap, record one response per person, then check the total.
- **Worked example:** For pet type, three dog responses are recorded as |||. Each tally stands for one response.
- **Visual model:** Use labelled sorting hoops, picture cards, lists and tally tables with text labels as well as images.
- **Common trap:** Do not record the same response twice or leave a response between unclear categories.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Name the category variable

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** In the question ‘How do you travel to school?’, what is being sorted into categories?

**Marking key:** Method of travel to school (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Identifying a categorical variable.

### P02 — Match recording methods

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match tally table, picture sort and list to marks in columns, objects under labels and written category names.

**Marking key:** Tally ↔ marks in columns; picture sort ↔ objects under labels; list ↔ written names (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Recognising data-recording formats.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Collect a small set

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Ask 5 people to choose apple or banana and record exactly one response for each person.

**Marking key:** Clear question, 5 single responses and total record count of 5 (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Acquiring and recording categorical data.

### P04 — Read tally marks

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A tally table shows cats |||| and dogs |||. State both frequencies and the total responses.

**Marking key:** Cats 4, dogs 3, total 7 (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Interpreting simple tally records.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Order a data collection

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: check total responses; ask the question; record one mark; choose clear categories.

**Marking key:** Choose categories → ask question → record one mark → check total (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing a reliable data-collection process.

### P06 — Draw a sorting record

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw labelled groups for red, blue and green counters and record 2 red, 3 blue and 1 green.

**Marking key:** Correct labels and one visible record for each of the 6 counters (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Representing categorical data with objects or drawings.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Fix overlapping categories

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A lunch survey uses ‘fruit’, ‘apple’ and ‘sandwich’. Explain the problem and suggest clearer categories.

**Marking key:** Apple overlaps fruit; proposes non-overlapping groups such as apple/banana/other fruit/sandwich (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating category clarity.

### P08 — Plan an inclusive class question

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Write a question with 3 or 4 clear categories about a class topic and explain how every student gets recorded once.

**Marking key:** Question is answerable, categories are mutually exclusive or include other, and method records one mark per student (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Designing a categorical data collection.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Record weather categories

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** For five days the weather is sunny, cloudy, sunny, rainy, cloudy. Write a tally or frequency list.

**Marking key:** Sunny 2, cloudy 2, rainy 1 (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Converting observations to categorical frequencies.

### E02 — Match data to labels

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match bus, walk and car responses—bus, car, walk, walk, car—to frequencies 1, 2 and 2.

**Marking key:** Bus ↔ 1; walk ↔ 2; car ↔ 2 (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Counting categorical responses accurately.

### E03 — Sequence digital entry

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order: select the correct category; submit one response; read the survey question; check confirmation.

**Marking key:** Read question → select category → submit once → check confirmation (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Following a digital categorical-data process.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Check a missing record

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Eight students answered, but a tally table contains only 7 marks. What does this tell you and what should happen next?

**Marking key:** One response is missing or count is wrong; recount records and check the respondent list without guessing an answer (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Auditing completeness of collected data.

### E05 — Demonstrate a picture sort

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Sort 10 provided animal cards into bird, mammal and insect and record category totals.

**Marking key:** Each card appears once in a suitable labelled category and totals sum to 10 (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Sorting and recording categorical observations.

### E06 — Draw a tally table

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Create a tally table for book choices: story 4, facts 3, poetry 2. Include headings and totals.

**Marking key:** Three labelled rows with accurate tallies/frequencies and total 9 (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Constructing a categorical tally record.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Detect double counting

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A student places one scooter picture under both ‘wheels’ and ‘ride to school’ in a single-choice survey. Explain why the total becomes unreliable.

**Marking key:** One response is counted twice and categories use different rules; use one variable and one placement per item (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Reasoning about one-response-per-case rules.

### E08 — Compare two recording plans

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** For 25 lunch choices, compare remembering answers, writing a list and using tally marks. Choose the most reliable plan and justify.

**Marking key:** Tally/list creates a permanent one-per-response record; memory alone is unreliable; choice is justified with counting/checking benefits (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Evaluating data-recording methods.
