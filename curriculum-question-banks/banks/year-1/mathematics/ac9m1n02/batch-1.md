# AC9M1N02 — Pass 1 question bank

## 60-second Quick Read

- **Key idea:** A whole number can be split into parts in more than one correct way.
- **Method:** Use tens and ones first, then exchange one ten for 10 ones to make another partition.
- **Worked example:** 35 is 3 tens and 5 ones. It is also 2 tens and 15 ones because both totals are 35.
- **Visual model:** Build numbers with base-ten sticks, ten-frames or bundled popsticks. Online, move tens and ones with tap and keyboard alternatives.
- **Common trap:** The parts must recombine to the original whole; 3 tens and 4 ones make 34, not 43.

## Practice questions

## Tier 1 — Knowledge and terms

### P01 — Name the tens and ones

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Partition 47 into tens and ones.

**Marking key:** 4 tens and 7 ones, or 40 + 7 (1 mark).

**Coverage:** Reading a standard tens-and-ones partition.

### P02 — Match numbers and parts

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match 23, 40 and 56 to 2 tens 3 ones, 4 tens 0 ones and 5 tens 6 ones.

**Marking key:** 23 ↔ 2 tens 3 ones; 40 ↔ 4 tens 0 ones; 56 ↔ 5 tens 6 ones (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Connecting numerals with base-ten partitions.

## Tier 2 — Core skill practice

### P03 — Build eighteen two ways

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Use counters or ten-frames to show 18 as 10 and 8, then as two different parts.

**Marking key:** Shows 10 + 8 and another valid partition such as 9 + 9 or 12 + 6 (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Representing one number with multiple partitions.

### P04 — Find the missing part

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Complete 10 = 6 + __ and explain how you know.

**Marking key:** 4; 6 and 4 fill a ten-frame or count on from 6 to 10 (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using part-part-whole facts to 10.

## Tier 3 — Guided application

### P05 — Regroup thirty-four

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Order these steps to show 34 as 2 tens and 14 ones: count the whole; exchange one ten for 10 ones; begin with 3 tens and 4 ones.

**Marking key:** Begin with 3 tens and 4 ones → exchange one ten for 10 ones → count the whole (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Sequencing a base-ten regrouping.

### P06 — Draw two partitions

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw 26 as tens and ones, then draw a different partition of 26.

**Marking key:** Shows 2 tens + 6 ones and a valid alternative such as 1 ten + 16 ones (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Recording equivalent partitions visually.

## Tier 4 — Problem solving and reasoning

### P07 — Check a regrouped number

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Noah says 2 tens and 14 ones make 34. Is he correct? Explain.

**Marking key:** Yes; 20 + 14 = 34, or 14 ones can become 1 ten and 4 ones, giving 3 tens and 4 ones (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Reasoning about non-standard partitions.

### P08 — Challenge one-only thinking

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** A student says 42 can only be split as 40 + 2. Give two other partitions and prove each totals 42.

**Marking key:** Two valid examples such as 30 + 12 and 20 + 22, with an addition or materials check for each (4 marks).

**Coverage:** Generating and verifying multiple partitions.

## Exam questions

## Section A — Core knowledge

### E01 — Read a fresh model

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** A model has 6 tens sticks and 3 single cubes. Write the numeral and expanded form.

**Marking key:** 63 and 60 + 3 (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Interpreting a base-ten representation.

### E02 — Match equivalent forms

**Type:** Matching

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students draw lines between matching entries.

**Question:** Match 17, 29 and 50 to 10 + 7, 20 + 9 and 5 tens.

**Marking key:** 17 ↔ 10 + 7; 29 ↔ 20 + 9; 50 ↔ 5 tens (1 mark each).

**Coverage:** Recognising equivalent numerical representations.

### E03 — Exchange within thirty-two

**Type:** Sequencing

**Delivery:** Online, provide movable cards with drag, tap-to-place and keyboard controls. On paper, students number or rewrite the cards in order.

**Question:** Put these actions in order to change 32 into 1 ten and 22 ones: verify the total; exchange one of 3 tens; count 1 ten and 22 ones.

**Marking key:** Exchange one of 3 tens → count 1 ten and 22 ones → verify the total remains 32 (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Following an exchange while preserving value.

## Section B — Application and problem solving

### E04 — Create an alternate partition

**Type:** Short response

**Question:** Write the number made by 5 tens and 8 ones, then partition it without using 5 tens.

**Marking key:** 58; a valid alternative such as 4 tens and 18 ones (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Moving between standard and non-standard partitions.

### E05 — Show twenty-four after exchange

**Type:** Demonstration

**Delivery:** Provide the named classroom materials or an equivalent labelled visual. Accept a teacher-observed demonstration or a written description.

**Question:** Build 24 with tens and ones. Exchange one ten and state the new partition.

**Marking key:** Begins with 2 tens and 4 ones; after exchange shows 1 ten and 14 ones (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Demonstrating a base-ten exchange.

### E06 — Part-part-whole diagram

**Type:** Drawing response

**Delivery:** Online, provide a simple drawing or selectable-diagram tool with a keyboard-labelled alternative. On paper, students draw and label.

**Question:** Draw two part-part-whole diagrams for 37: one using 30 and one using 20.

**Marking key:** Whole 37 with parts 30 and 7; whole 37 with parts 20 and 17 (2 marks).

**Coverage:** Using diagrams to record equivalent partitions.

## Section C — Extended response and analysis

### E07 — Compare two claims

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** Ava writes 3 tens and 12 ones. Eli writes 4 tens and 2 ones. Explain whether their totals are equal.

**Marking key:** They are equal: 30 + 12 = 42 and 40 + 2 = 42; exchanging 10 ones explains the equivalence (3 marks).

**Coverage:** Comparing partitions through regrouping.

### E08 — Find the tens pattern

**Type:** Extended response

**Question:** List the partitions of 46 made by using 4, 3, 2, 1 and 0 tens. Describe what changes each time.

**Marking key:** 40+6, 30+16, 20+26, 10+36, 0+46; each lost ten adds 10 ones while the whole stays 46 (6 marks).

**Coverage:** Generalising the relationship between tens and ones.
