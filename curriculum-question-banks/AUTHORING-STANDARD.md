# Curriculum question-bank authoring standard

## Core purpose

Questions must be worth attempting. They should make students observe, choose, sort, match, calculate, label, predict, build, compare or apply a skill. Do not pad a bank with curriculum-description recall, generic vocabulary checks or repeated question stems.

## Visual progression

Use a deliberate age progression rather than the same text-heavy format in every year.

- **Foundation:** strongly pictorial and object-based. Aim for at least 80% of questions to use a meaningful picture, scene, concrete-object model, pictograph, diagram or interactive visual.
- **Year 1:** visual-first. Aim for at least 70% meaningful visual questions.
- **Year 2:** visuals remain prominent. Aim for at least 60% meaningful visual questions.
- **Year 3:** use visuals regularly but begin increasing independent reading. Aim for at least 50% meaningful visual questions.
- **Year 4 and above:** include a visual only when it is needed to teach or assess the concept accurately. Examples include trees and food webs, photosynthesis, body parts and systems, maps, geometric diagrams, graphs, circuits, scientific apparatus and data displays.

These percentages are minimum quality targets for a complete bank, not permission to attach decorative images. A visual counts only when the student must inspect or use it to answer.

## Avoid empty theory

- Do not ask generic questions such as “What is the main idea?”, “Why is vocabulary important?” or “State one fact about this topic.”
- Avoid isolated definition recall unless the curriculum explicitly requires the term and the term cannot be assessed naturally through an example.
- Prefer a short scenario, photograph, diagram, model, worked example, sorting task, practical observation or data display.
- Embed necessary vocabulary in an applied task: label a diagram, match a word to visible evidence, select the correct explanation for a pictured event, or correct a misconception.
- Extended responses must involve useful reasoning from evidence, not merely repeat the Quick Read.

## What counts as pictorial

A question described as pictorial must supply an actual image or visual representation. Text such as “Which picture would show 3/4?” without visible answer pictures does not count.

Valid visuals include:

- authored SVG or accessible raster illustrations;
- photographs when real-world appearance matters;
- number lines, ten frames, arrays, fraction models, clocks and maps;
- labelled and unlabelled scientific diagrams;
- tables, graphs, pictographs and observation cards;
- image-based sorting, sequencing, matching and hotspot tasks.

Every image needs concise alt text. Drag interactions must also support tap and keyboard input. Do not place the answer in the alt text.

## Subject examples

- **Mathematics F–3:** collections, counters, ten frames, arrays, number lines, fraction shapes, clocks, coins, maps, turns, patterns, graphs and concrete measurement scenes.
- **Science F–3:** real objects or photographs, living/non-living sorting, life-cycle sequencing, observable material changes, day/night or weather scenes, labelled models and simple investigation results.
- **English F–3:** picture-supported sentences, speech bubbles, event sequencing, visual inference, character emotion, phoneme/grapheme cards and short authentic text snippets. Do not add a picture where the assessed skill is reading the words alone.
- **Year 4+:** use diagrams and images when they carry essential information, such as plant structure/photosynthesis, body systems, food chains, geometry, maps, graphs, circuits and experimental apparatus.

## Review gate

Reject or rewrite a bank when any of the following is true:

- it misses the year-level visual target without a curriculum-based reason;
- a claimed picture or diagram is absent;
- theory or definition recall could be replaced by a more engaging application;
- the visual is decorative rather than necessary;
- the same visual, scenario or question pattern is repeatedly reused;
- the task depends on colour alone or lacks an accessible non-drag alternative.
