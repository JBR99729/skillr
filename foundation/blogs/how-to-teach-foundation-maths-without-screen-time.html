How to Teach Foundation Maths Without Screen Time

Screen-free maths can be rich, playful and highly effective when children learn through movement, objects and conversation.

Use real objects first

Start with counters, toy animals, blocks or buttons. Children can sort, compare and count them before moving to written work. This keeps the learning concrete and meaningful.

For early number work, try simple routines such as building a number with objects, then matching it to a numeral and a picture. This helps children connect the idea to the symbol.

Make the lesson active

Short, active lessons often work better than long seated tasks. A simple routine of model, do, talk and record can help children stay focused and understand ideas more clearly.

Parents and teachers can begin by showing one small task, letting the child try it with objects, and then asking them to explain how they know the answer.

Use talking to strengthen understanding

Children often learn more when they are encouraged to explain what they see. Questions such as “How do you know?” and “What is the same?” help build early reasoning without requiring screens or worksheets at every step.

Why this approach works

Hands-on learning gives children time to explore, build confidence and develop language before they move to more formal recording. That makes it easier to support both early maths and future confidence in the subject.
