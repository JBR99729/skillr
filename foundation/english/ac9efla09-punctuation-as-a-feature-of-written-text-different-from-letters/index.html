What students learn

1. Outcome Overview & Core Concepts

Capital letters and punctuation are features of written text. Capitals can begin sentences and names; punctuation can show the sentence has ended.

Learning target: identify punctuation as a feature of written text different from letters; recognise that capital letters are used for names, and that capital letters also signal the beginning of sentences while punctuation marks signal the end

Big idea: Notice capitals and ending punctuation

Learning routine: Find first word → Find name → Find ending mark → Read with sense

3. Common Student Misconceptions

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • Letters and punctuation are the same: Letters make words; punctuation helps organise meaning.
  • Answering without evidence: Point to the word, sound, sentence or text clue that proves the answer.
  • Too much at once: Practise one clear English skill before mixing skills together.
4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence

4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence

Step 1: Explicit Teaching & Modelling

Name the target in child-friendly language and demonstrate one concrete example connected to sentence, punctuation.

Step 2: Guided Analysis

Compare or practise a second example together. Students point to, say or show the clue.

Step 3: Collaborative / Independent Practice

Create or analyse one example together, then use a fresh familiar example independently.

Step 4: Formative Assessment

Use a one-minute check with a new example; require observable evidence rather than a guess.
