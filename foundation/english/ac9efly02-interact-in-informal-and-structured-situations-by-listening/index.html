What students learn in AC9EFLY02

This unit helps students build a clear, usable understanding of Interact in informal and structured situations by listening while others speak and using features of voice including volume levels. The goal is not to memorise one answer pattern. Students should be able to explain the idea, recognise it in a new example and apply it in a short practice or worksheet task.

  • Start with the main idea: interact in informal and structured situations by listening while others speak and using features of voice including volume levels.
  • Use concrete examples, pictures, oral explanation and short written responses before moving to independent practice.
  • exchanging ideas with peers in pairs and small groups, engaging in class discussions, listening to others and contributing ideas showing understanding of appropriate listening behaviour, such as listening without interrupting, and looking at the speaker, if culturally appropriate
  • Finish with a mixed activity so students must choose the correct strategy rather than copy the last example.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

The content description and elaborations below show the curriculum ideas taught in this unit. Items marked as teaching context support lesson planning.

  • Content description: interact in informal and structured situations by listening while others speak and using features of voice including volume levels
  • E1: exchanging ideas with peers in pairs and small groups, engaging in class discussions, listening to others and contributing ideas
  • E2: showing understanding of appropriate listening behaviour, such as listening without interrupting, and looking at the speaker, if culturally appropriate
  • E3: altering volume for inside and outside situations and when speaking to an audience
  • E4: participating in informal interaction situations; for example, play-based experiences that involve the imaginative use of spoken language
  • E5: listening to and following instructions

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Answering from memory without using clues from the text, sentence or image.
  • Knowing the idea orally but not transferring it into reading, writing or speaking.
  • Missing punctuation, word order or vocabulary clues that change meaning.
