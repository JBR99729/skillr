AC9EFLY02 • Foundation English

listening and voice in interactions

I can listen through a whole turn, respond to the same topic and use a voice that listeners can hear comfortably.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

I can listen through a whole turn, respond to the same topic and use a voice that listeners can hear comfortably.

Learning intention: I can listen through a whole turn, respond to the same topic and use a voice that listeners can hear comfortably.

Success criteria

  • I can listen through a whole short turn.
  • I can wait before responding.
  • I can respond on topic.
  • I can use suitable volume.
  • I can explain why the voice suits the listeners.
Key vocabulary
  • interaction — people listening and speaking with one another
  • turn — one person’s time to speak or act
  • listener — a person paying attention to what is being said
  • volume — how loud or quiet a voice is
  • on topic — about the same idea being discussed
Model and guided application

Worked example: listen and take a turn

Worked-model method: listen, wait, respond on topic and choose an audible volume.

1. Mia says, ‘My bean seed has two leaves.’ Sam faces Mia and listens until she finishes.2. Sam waits, then says, ‘My seed has leaves too.’ His response stays on the same topic.3. Sam uses a clear partner voice that Mia can hear without shouting.

Model answer: Sam shows active listening, waits for his turn, responds about the seed and uses comfortable partner volume.

Worked example: change volume for the audience

Worked-model method: listen, wait, respond on topic and choose an audible volume.

1. Nora explains a picture to one partner with a quiet, clear voice.2. Later, Nora reports the same idea to the class and speaks clearly enough for the back row.3. Her message stays the same, but her volume changes because the audience changes.

Model answer: A suitable volume is not always the loudest or quietest voice; it lets the intended listeners hear comfortably.

Learning activities
  1. Listening signal — Listen to one short class message, then point to a picture that matches what was said.
    Ears ready • eyes or body facing the speaker • hands still enough to listen
  2. Turn-and-respond — In pairs, one student shares a favourite playground activity and the other gives one on-topic response.
    Speaker card → wait card → listener response card
  3. Volume match — Say the same greeting to one partner and then to a small group, changing only the volume needed.
    One partner: quiet and clear • small group: clear enough for all
Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Exchange and build ideas in a group

Pair, group and class talk work when learners listen to an idea, contribute a connected idea and share the speaking space.

Teacher: Give one child a seed picture and ask for one observation. Ask a partner to listen until the observation is complete. Prompt the partner to add a question or idea about that same seed.

Ask: What did your partner say, and what connected idea can you add?

Students: Listen until a peer finishes, recall the peer's idea, then add one connected question or detail after waiting for a turn.

Look for: The learner recalls a peer's idea and contributes a relevant question, example or detail after waiting for a turn.

If incorrect: Use a speaker card and a listener card, then offer two pictured responses and ask which one stays on the topic.

E2: Show listening in a respectful way

Appropriate listening means not interrupting and showing attention in a way that is comfortable and culturally appropriate for the speaker and listener.

Teacher: Explain the short listening purpose. Let the speaker finish without interruption. Ask the listener to show understanding by recalling one exact detail.

Ask: Which detail can you repeat to show that you heard the whole message?

Students: Let the speaker finish, show attention in a comfortable and culturally appropriate way, then repeat one exact message detail.

Look for: The learner waits, attends in an appropriate way and accurately recalls or responds to one part of the message.

If incorrect: Shorten the message to one sentence and let the learner point to the matching picture before answering orally.

E3: Adjust volume for place and audience

Voice volume changes with distance, setting and audience size so listeners can hear comfortably without being overwhelmed.

Teacher: Name the place and intended listeners. Model a clear volume for that distance. Ask listeners whether every word was comfortable to hear and adjust once.

Ask: Who needs to hear you here, and how will your voice reach them comfortably?

Students: Name the place and listeners, deliver the same line at a suitable volume, ask whether it was comfortable to hear and adjust once.

Look for: The learner selects and adjusts volume according to the named audience and setting while keeping speech clear.

If incorrect: Move the speaker closer, reduce the audience to one partner and rehearse the sentence before changing the distance again.

E4: Use spoken language in imaginative play

Imaginative play creates informal reasons to ask, explain, negotiate roles and respond in character.

Teacher: Choose roles in a pretend fruit shop. Model a customer asking for an apple and a shopkeeper responding. Invite children to continue the exchange and solve a simple play problem.

Ask: What could your character say next to keep the play going?

Students: Take a pretend-shop role, make or answer a connected request, then adapt the next spoken turn when the play situation changes.

Look for: The learner uses understandable spoken language that suits the pretend role and responds to another player's contribution.

If incorrect: Offer role pictures and a complete oral model such as, 'May I have two apples, please?' or 'We have one apple left.' before fading the prompt.

E5: Listen to and follow instructions

Following an oral instruction requires listening to the whole direction, keeping its order and completing the named action.

Teacher: Say, 'Pick up the blue card, then place it beside the book.' Ask the learner to repeat the two actions in order. Let the learner act and compare the result with the direction.

Ask: What will you do first, and what will you do next?

Students: Listen to the complete two-step direction, repeat what happens first and next, then carry out both actions in order.

Look for: The learner repeats or demonstrates both named actions in the stated order without adding a different action.

If incorrect: Give one instruction at a time with matching object pictures, then reconnect the two steps with first and next.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Waiting silently means listening — Ask the learner to repeat one idea the speaker shared before responding.
  • The loudest voice is always the best voice — Move closer to the listener and adjust volume until every word is comfortable to hear.
  • Any response counts as a turn — Name the topic first, then make the response connect to that same topic.
Quick checks and mastery

Quick checks

  • What did the speaker say about the seed?
  • Which response stays on the speaker’s topic?
  • What volume would suit one nearby partner?
  • Show how you know it is your turn to speak.

Mastery evidence

  • What did the speaker say about the seed?
  • Which response stays on the speaker’s topic?
  • What volume would suit one nearby partner?
  • Show how you know it is your turn to speak.
  • The learner recalls a peer's idea and contributes a relevant question, example or detail after waiting for a turn.
  • The learner waits, attends in an appropriate way and accurately recalls or responds to one part of the message.
  • The learner selects and adjusts volume according to the named audience and setting while keeping speech clear.
  • The learner uses understandable spoken language that suits the pretend role and responds to another player's contribution.
  • The learner repeats or demonstrates both named actions in the stated order without adding a different action.
