Key vocabulary
  • text — something people make to communicate meaning through words, images, sounds or symbols
  • purpose — the main job a text is designed to do
  • story — a literary text with characters and events, often made to entertain
  • informative text — a text made to teach facts or information about a topic
  • symbol — a picture or mark that carries an agreed meaning
  • cover — the front of a book, which can show its title and clues about its content
  • topic — what a text is mainly about
  • clue — a feature that helps a reader identify the text or its purpose
Key concept

I can recognise familiar texts and say what they are for.

Learning goal: I can recognise familiar texts and say what they are for.

I am ready when I can

  • I can name a familiar text such as a story, information page, sign or instruction.
  • I can state whether the text is made to entertain, inform, instruct or guide.
  • I can use a cover, symbol, word, image or layout clue to justify the purpose.
  • I can group texts by topic without assuming they all have the same purpose.
  • I can check an early cover prediction against the content inside the text.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Explain why an everyday symbol is used

Symbols in everyday places can guide, warn, identify or give a shared message quickly.

Think: Name where the symbol appears. Describe its shapes, pictures or colours. Explain what people should know or do.

Try: What does this symbol help people know or do, and which feature gives the clue?

A strong answer: Complete the modelled response and show this evidence in your own example: identifies a familiar symbol and explains its practical purpose using a visible feature.

Check: The learner identifies a familiar symbol and explains its practical purpose using a visible feature.

If you are stuck: Act out the action the symbol requests, then return to the arrow or picture that signalled it.

E2: Group imaginative and informative books by cover clues

Covers can suggest whether a book is an imaginative story or an informative text, and the inside content confirms the choice.

Think: Inspect the title and cover image. Predict Story or Informative. Open the book and confirm with content evidence.

Try: What does the cover make you predict, and what inside the book confirms it?

A strong answer: Complete the modelled response and show this evidence in your own example: groups the text appropriately and cites both a cover clue and a confirming content clue.

Check: The learner groups the text appropriately and cites both a cover clue and a confirming content clue.

If you are stuck: Offer one story cover and one fact cover with strongly contrasting clues, then check one inside page together.

E3: Group texts by a shared topic

Imaginative and informative texts can be grouped together because they share a topic even when their types and purposes differ.

Think: Name each text's topic. Place texts with the same topic together. Compare the purpose of texts inside the group.

Try: Which texts belong in the farm-animal group, and what different jobs do they do?

A strong answer: Complete the modelled response and show this evidence in your own example: groups by shared topic and then distinguishes at least two purposes within the group.

Check: The learner groups by shared topic and then distinguishes at least two purposes within the group.

If you are stuck: Use a large topic picture in the centre and ask whether each cover belongs near it before discussing purpose.

Common misconceptions
  • All books have the same purpose — Place a story and fact book side by side and use inside-text clues to name the different jobs.
  • Pictures always mean a text is imaginative — Compare an illustrated story with a labelled information diagram and ask what each picture helps the text do.
  • Texts about the same topic must be the same type — Keep the topic fixed, then sort by purpose: entertain, inform, instruct or guide.
Explain and apply

Use clues to name the text

Show a story cover, fact page, recipe or sign. Ask: What kind of text is this? Which clue tells you?

Answer and teaching guidance

A strong answer names the text type and one visible clue such as characters, facts, numbered steps, labels, arrows or symbols.

Separate topic from purpose

Compare an animal story and an animal fact page. What are both about? What is each one made to do?

Answer and teaching guidance

Both may share the same topic, but their purposes differ. The story usually entertains with characters and events; the fact page informs with checkable details.

Choose the helpful evidence

A green sign has a running figure, arrow and doorway. What job does the sign do?

Answer and teaching guidance

The sign guides people to an exit. Ask learners to use the visual symbols as evidence, not just guess from colour or page position.

Explain the purpose

Complete: I think this text is made to ____ because ____.

Answer and teaching guidance

Support: offer the purpose words entertain, inform, instruct or guide. Extend: compare two texts on the same topic with different purposes.
