AC9EFLY07 • Foundation English

short spoken texts

I can give a short spoken report that stays on one topic, orders its details and uses a clear voice at a volume suited to the audience.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

I can give a short spoken report that stays on one topic, orders its details and uses a clear voice at a volume suited to the audience.

Learning intention: I can give a short spoken report that stays on one topic, orders its details and uses a clear voice at a volume suited to the audience.

Success criteria

  • I can name one clear topic.
  • I can select relevant details.
  • I can order ideas.
  • I can use a prompt without reading a script.
  • I can speak clearly at suitable volume.
Key vocabulary
  • spoken report — a short talk that shares ideas, information or events
  • topic — what the whole report is about
  • detail — a piece of information that explains the topic
  • sequence — an order that helps ideas make sense
  • rehearse — practise before presenting
  • volume — how loud or quiet a voice is
Model and guided application

Worked example: report a class discovery

Worked-model method: plan one topic, order details and speak clearly.

1. Choose one topic: ‘Our bean seed changed.’2. Order two details: first a root appeared; next two green leaves opened.3. Rehearse, then face the group and deliver the report in a clear classroom voice.

Model answer: The report stays on the seed, gives two ordered details and can be heard comfortably by the group.

Worked example: use visual prompts to stay on topic

Worked-model method: plan one topic, order details and speak clearly.

1. Place three picture cards in order: empty garden bed, planted seed, small shoot.2. Point to each card while saying one connected sentence about what happened.3. Finish with, ‘That is how we planted our seed and saw it begin to grow.’

Model answer: The picture sequence helps the speaker remember the order without adding unrelated details.

Learning activities
  1. Three-card plan — Order three familiar picture cards and say one sentence for each card.
    topic picture • detail one • detail two
  2. Partner rehearsal — Give the report to one partner, who checks topic, order and comfortable volume.
    one helpful comment after listening
  3. Peer report — Deliver a twenty-second report to a small group and answer one on-topic question.
    face listeners • clear voice • stay on topic
Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Share a personal discovery with peers

A semi-formal peer report names one experience, interest or discovery and explains it with relevant details in a clear voice.

Teacher: Choose the discovery: a bean seed has sprouted. Plan an opening topic and two observable details. Rehearse, face the group and speak at a comfortable classroom volume.

Ask: What did you discover, and which two details will help your peers understand it?

Students: Choose one personal discovery, order two relevant details, rehearse the report and deliver it clearly to peers at a suitable volume.

Look for: The learner states one topic, gives relevant details and delivers the report clearly to peers.

If incorrect: Use three picture prompts for topic, detail one and detail two, then rehearse first with one partner.

E2: Share a personal response to a text

A spoken response reports what the learner thought or felt about a text idea or event and gives a story clue.

Teacher: Name the character action or event. State a thought or feeling about it. Explain the text clue and deliver the response to peers.

Ask: What did you think or feel, and which event made you respond that way?

Students: Name the text event, state a connected feeling or thought, give the text-based reason and share the complete response aloud.

Look for: The learner shares a personal response and supports it with an accurate event, word or image clue from the text.

If incorrect: Offer feeling pictures and model a complete response: 'I felt relieved when the boot was found because Niko could go home.'

E3: Use visual prompts to organise a report

A small sequence of visual prompts can help a speaker stay on one topic and recall ideas in order without reading a full script.

Teacher: Arrange three seed pictures from planting to sprouting. Say one connected sentence for each picture. Check that every sentence explains the report topic and that the order makes sense.

Ask: Which picture comes next, and what sentence will you say without reading a script?

Students: Order three visual prompts, point to each in turn and speak one connected report detail before moving to the next prompt.

Look for: The learner follows the visual sequence, stays on topic and expands each prompt with a relevant spoken sentence.

If incorrect: Fix the first and final pictures, rehearse one sentence at a time and let the learner point while speaking.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • A report is a list of unrelated facts — State the topic first and keep only details that explain that topic.
  • Speaking quickly makes a report sound prepared — Pause between ideas and rehearse at a pace listeners can follow.
  • Visual prompts should contain every spoken word — Use one simple picture for each main idea and speak the details aloud.
