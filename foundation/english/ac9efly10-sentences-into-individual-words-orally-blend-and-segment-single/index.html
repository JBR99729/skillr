AC9EFLY10 • Foundation English

words in sentences and sounds in spoken words

I can segment a spoken sentence into words, segment and blend a single-syllable word, and change one speech sound to make a new word.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

I can segment a spoken sentence into words, segment and blend a single-syllable word, and change one speech sound to make a new word.

Learning intention: I can segment a spoken sentence into words, segment and blend a single-syllable word, and change one speech sound to make a new word.

Success criteria

  • I can segment a sentence into words.
  • I can segment a single-syllable word into speech sounds.
  • I can blend speech sounds into a word.
  • I can isolate a first, middle or final sound.
  • I can change one sound and check the new word.
Key vocabulary
  • segment — break spoken language into separate parts
  • blend — join separate speech sounds to say a whole word
  • sentence — a group of words that expresses an idea
  • word — a spoken unit that carries meaning
  • phoneme — one individual speech sound
  • manipulate — add, remove or replace a speech sound
Model and guided application

Worked example: segment a sentence into words

Worked-model method: count spoken parts, blend or change them, then check.

1. Listen to the whole sentence, ‘The dog ran home.’2. Say it again slowly and move one counter for The, one for dog, one for ran and one for home.3. Touch and count the four counters, then say the whole sentence again.

Model answer: The spoken sentence has four words because each meaningful spoken word receives one counter.

Worked example: blend and change a spoken word

Worked-model method: count spoken parts, blend or change them, then check.

1. Listen to three sounds: the humming sound at the start of moon, the short middle sound in apple and the tapping sound at the start of top.2. Slide three counters together and blend the sounds to say mat.3. Replace the first sound with the hissing sound at the start of sun, blend again and say sat.

Model answer: Changing only the first speech sound changes mat to sat while the middle and final sounds stay the same.

Learning activities
  1. Word counters — Move one counter for every word in a short sentence that the teacher says.
    listen first • move once per word • count
  2. Sound slide — Listen to three cue-word sounds and slide three counters together as you blend the word.
    separate sounds → smooth blend → whole word
  3. Change one — Use picture cues to replace the first or final speech sound and say the new word.
    name what changed and what stayed
Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Segment a spoken word into sounds

A single-syllable spoken word can be stretched into its individual speech sounds while the sound order remains unchanged.

Teacher: Say can as a whole word. Stretch it and move counters for the quiet cutting sound at the start of cat, the short middle sound in apple and the humming final sound in nest. Touch the three counters in order and say the whole word again.

Ask: What speech sounds do you hear from the beginning to the end of can?

Students: Say the whole word, move one counter for each heard sound, identify every sound with a cue word and count the separated parts.

Look for: The learner represents each individual speech sound in the spoken word with one ordered counter and recombines the word accurately.

If incorrect: Begin with two-sound words or provide the first counter, then stretch the word together without displaying letters.

E2: Blend separate sounds into a word

Oral blending joins a sequence of separate speech sounds smoothly to make a recognisable single-syllable word.

Teacher: Provide the flowing beginning sound in leaf, the short middle sound in insect and the popping final sound in pig. Touch three counters while repeating the sounds in order. Slide the counters together and say lip as one word.

Ask: Which whole word do these three cue-word sounds make when you join them?

Students: Listen to the separated cue sounds, move one counter for each, slide the counters together and blend the whole spoken word.

Look for: The learner preserves the supplied sound order and blends it into the intended spoken word without adding or omitting a sound.

If incorrect: Use continuous hand movement under the counters, repeat the first two sounds together and then add the final sound.

E3: Replace one sound to make a new word

Changing the beginning, middle or final speech sound in a single-syllable word can make a new spoken word while the other sounds stay fixed.

Teacher: Build three counters for run. Replace only the first counter with the soft blowing beginning sound in fish and blend fun. Keep the first and last sounds, change the middle to the short sound in apple and blend fan.

Ask: Which sound position changed, which sounds stayed and what new word did you make?

Students: Build the starting word with sound counters, replace only the named sound, blend the new word and state what changed and stayed.

Look for: The learner replaces the named speech sound, blends a valid new word and explains which positions changed and stayed.

If incorrect: Hold two counters in place, exaggerate the replacement action and offer two picture choices for the possible new word.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Counting syllables gives the number of words — Repeat the sentence and move a counter for every separate meaningful word, including short words.
  • Speech sounds are the same as letter names — Keep the task oral and use cue words to imitate sounds instead of reciting alphabet names.
  • Changing one sound means changing the whole word — Hold two counters still, replace only the named counter and blend all parts again.

Quick checks

  • How many words do you hear in ‘The dog ran home’?
  • Which spoken word do the moon, apple and top cue sounds blend to make?
  • What changes when mat becomes sat?
  • What stays the same when mat becomes sat?
