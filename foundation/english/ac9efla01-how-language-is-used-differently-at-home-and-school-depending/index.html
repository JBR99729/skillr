AC9EFLA01 • Foundation English

Choose words that fit who you are talking to

At home, at school and out in the community, we can change our words to suit the person and situation.

LearnPrintable worksheet40-question practiceClassroom displayTest
What this skill means

I can choose words that suit who I am talking to.

First think about what is happening, who is listening and what you want to say. Then choose words that fit.

Remember: home language and school language can both be right. The goal is not to find one special way of speaking. The goal is to choose language that works for the relationship and situation.

1. Ask relevant questions

Ask for the information you need

If you cannot find your hat at school, you could ask your teacher, “Where is my hat?”

If you do not know what to do first, you could ask, “What do I do first?”

If you are in a shop, you might say, “Excuse me, where is the milk, please?”

Try it: You do not know where to put your schoolbag. What could you ask?

2. Make requests

Ask clearly for what you need

To a friend: “Can I use the glue when you're finished?”

To a teacher: “Excuse me, can you help me, please?”

To a parent: “Dad, can you help me open this?”

To a shopkeeper: “Can I please have an apple?”

The purpose can stay the same while some of the words change.

3. Express opinions

Tell what you think

An opinion tells what you think, like or prefer.

“I think this book is funny.”

“I like the blue picture best.”

You can sometimes add a reason: “I think the blue picture is best because it is easy to see.”

4. Same message, different person

Think about the relationship

Friend

“Can you help me, please?”

Teacher

“Excuse me, can you help me, please?”

Shopkeeper

“Excuse me, where are the apples, please?”

Ask: Who is listening? What am I trying to say? Do these words fit?

5. Fix words that do not fit

Keep the meaning and improve the wording

“Give me the scissors!” → “Can I please have the scissors?”

“Move! I'm playing!” → “Can I play too?”

“Milk!” → “Excuse me, can you please show me where the milk is?”

Mastery check

Can you do this without a model?

  1. You need your teacher's help. What could you say?
  2. You want to join your friend's game. What could you ask?
  3. Your parent asks which story you liked. Give your opinion.
  4. You cannot find something in a shop. What could you ask the shopkeeper?
  5. A child says to a teacher, “Give me that!” How could they say it better?

A child is secure when they can recognise the situation → think about the listener → choose a question, request or opinion → use words that fit.

Curriculum coverage

AC9EFLA01

Explore how language is used differently at home and school depending on the relationships between people.

  • E1: asking relevant questions, and expressing requests and opinions in ways that suit the contexts
  • E2: learning to use language according to the relationship between people; for example, between a parent and a child, a teacher and a student, siblings or friends, shopkeepers and customers
