Words That Fit Who We Are Talking To

I can notice who I am talking with, where we are and why I am speaking, then choose words that fit. Families and communities may use different words. More than one choice can be kind, clear and correct for its setting.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

I can notice who I am talking with, where we are and why I am speaking, then choose words that fit. Families and communities may use different words. More than one choice can be kind, clear and correct for its setting.

Learning intention: I can choose words that fit who I am talking to.

Success criteria

  • I can name who is speaking and who is listening.
  • I can ask for the same thing in a friend way and a teacher way.
  • I can explain what changed and what stayed the same.
  • I can match a phrase to a friend, teacher or shopkeeper.
Key vocabulary
  • relationship — how people are connected, such as friends, family members or teacher and student
  • context — the place, people and situation around the talk
  • purpose — the reason we are speaking, such as asking, greeting or inviting
  • respectful — showing care for the other person through words, voice and listening
  • choice — one possible way to say something

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Ask, request and share an opinion in a fitting way

A useful question, request or opinion matches what is happening and gives the listener enough information to respond.

Teacher: Use one familiar classroom event and model the difference between asking for information, requesting an action and sharing an opinion. Notice what is happening and what you need to communicate. Choose a question, request or opinion frame. Say it clearly, then listen for the response.

Ask: You cannot find the paint pots. What relevant question could you ask in this classroom?

Students: Complete the modelled response and use the example to show: gives a clear message that fits the classroom event and can name whether it is a question, request or opinion.

Look for: The learner gives a clear message that fits the classroom event and can name whether it is a question, request or opinion.

If incorrect: Offer three oral models: Where is the item? May I use the item? I think this choice fits because it is clear; let the learner adapt one.

E2: Choose language for the relationship

People may express the same purpose in different respectful ways because their relationships and shared experiences are different.

Teacher: Keep the purpose fixed, change the relationship card and rehearse language that is natural, kind and clear for that pair without treating one community's way as better. Name the two people and how they are connected. Keep the purpose the same. Try words, voice and listening that suit this relationship.

Ask: You want to see an item. How might you ask a sibling or friend, and how might you ask a shopkeeper?

Students: Complete the modelled response and use the example to show: changes at least one language feature for the relationship and explains that both versions can be respectful in their settings.

Look for: The learner changes at least one language feature for the relationship and explains that both versions can be respectful in their settings.

If incorrect: Role-play with two labelled puppets and model a complete response, such as: With my friend I could say, Can I see your new card?

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • One sentence must fit everyone — Show friend and teacher scenes side by side. Circle the same purpose and highlight the words that change.
  • Longer words are always better — Say, A short friendly request can fit a friend. The best choice fits the listener and purpose.
  • One way of speaking is wrong — Explain that people and families speak in many correct ways. Today we are choosing words for a particular listener and place.
Quick checks and mastery

Quick checks

  • What changed?
  • Which words suit a teacher?
  • How would you ask a friend?
  • Now ask for a pencil in both ways.

Mastery evidence

  • What changed?
  • Which words suit a teacher?
  • What changed? What stayed the same?
  • Now ask for a pencil in both ways.
  • What changed?
  • Match each phrase to the friend, teacher or shopkeeper.
  • Answer the three picture questions aloud.
