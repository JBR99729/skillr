Key vocabulary
  • word group — words that belong together to add one part of a sentence's meaning
  • sentence — words arranged to communicate a whole idea
  • meaning — the idea the words communicate
  • action — what someone or something does
  • detail — extra information about who, what, where or how

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Connect words by the jobs they do together

Words in a sentence relate to one another: a naming word can connect to an action word, and a describing word can add information to a naming word.

Think: Find the naming word or group. Connect it to the action word or group. Attach a describing word to the person, animal, place or thing it describes.

Try: In “The black cat ate,” which words belong together, and how does each one help the meaning?

A strong answer: Complete the modelled response and use the example to show: connects cat to ate and black to cat, explaining the naming, action and describing relationships in child-friendly language.

Check: The learner connects cat to ate and black to cat, explaining the naming, action and describing relationships in child-friendly language.

If you are stuck: Use a cat picture as the centre, place black beside it and draw an action arrow from cat to ate.

Common misconceptions
  • A word group can be cut anywhere — Read the sentence aloud and keep together the words that answer the same meaning question, such as who or what happened.
  • Every sentence must have exactly two groups — Explain that the two-part model helps us begin, but a sentence can include more meaningful detail groups.
  • Moving one group never changes the message — Swap or move one group, read both sentences aloud and compare what each one now means.
Explain and apply

Keep related words together

Read “The spotted dog carried a stick.” Which words name the dog? Which word tells its action?

Answer and teaching guidance

The spotted dog names the animal; carried tells the action. Spotted describes dog. Use these everyday explanations without requiring formal grammar labels.

Change a description

Change spotted to muddy in “The spotted dog carried a stick.” What changes? What stays the same?

Answer and teaching guidance

The description of the dog changes. The animal still carries a stick. A describing word helps build the naming group rather than telling the action.

Change a place group

Compare “My bag is beside the door.” and “My bag is under the table.”

Answer and teaching guidance

My bag stays the same. Beside the door changes to under the table, so the location changes. Read each complete sentence to check that its groups work together.

Build, then explain

Make a sentence about a bird. Include what it does and where. Point to the words doing each job.

Answer and teaching guidance

For example, The bird rests in a tree. The bird names what it is about; rests tells what happens; in a tree tells where. Support: supply word cards. Extend: change one group and explain the new meaning.

Try these
  1. Human sentence — Students hold word cards and stand close to the words that belong in the who group and action group.
    [The small bird] [sang loudly.]
  2. Swap a group — Keep The fish and swap swims, hides or splashes. Say how each sentence changes.
    Who group: The fish. Try these action groups: swims, hides or splashes.
  3. Hear the join — Teacher pauses between meaningful groups; students repeat the full sentence without losing its meaning.
    meaning group + meaning group → whole sentence

Worked example and reasoning checks

Words work together to make a message

Hear how groups of words tell who or what, and what happens.

Worked example

Compare ‘The little dog sleeps’ with ‘Sleeps dog the little’. Which sounds like a complete message?

  1. Say ‘the little dog’ together. These words tell who the message is about.
  2. Add ‘sleeps’ to tell what the dog does. Read the whole message aloud.
  3. Moving the words randomly makes the message difficult to understand. Word order helps us make meaning.

Watch for this mistake: A row of familiar words does not automatically make a meaningful sentence. Say the complete message and check that the words work together.

Try it, then explain

  1. Use these words to tell a message: bird / sings / The.

    Hint

    Begin with the words naming the animal.

    Answer and explanation

    The bird sings.

  2. Change ‘The dog sleeps’ to tell about a cat. Which word changes?

    Hint

    Keep the action the same.

    Answer and explanation

    Change dog to cat: The cat sleeps.
