AC9EFLA06 • Foundation English

Word Groups Work Together in Sentences

I can hear and see groups of words working together inside a sentence. One group might name who or what the sentence is about; another might tell what happens, what something is like or where it is. We group words by meaning, not by cutting the sentence anywhere.

What students learn

I can hear and see groups of words working together inside a sentence. One group might name who or what the sentence is about; another might tell what happens, what something is like or where it is. We group words by meaning, not by cutting the sentence anywhere.

Learning intention: I can hear and see groups of words working together inside a sentence. One group might name who or what the sentence is about; another might tell what happens, what something is like or where it is. We group words by meaning, not by cutting the sentence anywhere.

Success criteria

  • I can find a group that names who or what.
  • I can find a group that adds action or detail.
  • I can keep words together by meaning.
  • I can change one group to change the message.
  • I can join word groups into a meaningful sentence.
Key vocabulary
  • word group — words that belong together to add one part of a sentence's meaning
  • sentence — words arranged to communicate a whole idea
  • meaning — the idea the words communicate
  • action — what someone or something does
  • detail — extra information about who, what, where or how

Learning activities
  1. Human sentence — Students hold word cards and stand close to the words that belong in the who group and action group.
    [The small bird] [sang loudly.]
  2. Swap a group — Keep The fish and swap swims, hides or splashes. Say how each sentence changes.
    Who group: The fish. Try these action groups: swims, hides or splashes.
  3. Hear the join — Teacher pauses between meaningful groups; students repeat the full sentence without losing its meaning.
    meaning group + meaning group → whole sentence
Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Connect words by the jobs they do together

Words in a sentence relate to one another: a naming word can connect to an action word, and a describing word can add information to a naming word.

Teacher: Find the naming word or group. Connect it to the action word or group. Attach a describing word to the person, animal, place or thing it describes.

Ask: In “The black cat ate,” which words belong together, and how does each one help the meaning?

Students: Complete the modelled response and use the example to show: connects cat to ate and black to cat, explaining the naming, action and describing relationships in child-friendly language.

Look for: The learner connects cat to ate and black to cat, explaining the naming, action and describing relationships in child-friendly language.

If incorrect: Use a cat picture as the centre, place black beside it and draw an action arrow from cat to ate.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • A word group can be cut anywhere — Read the sentence aloud and keep together the words that answer the same meaning question, such as who or what happened.
  • Every sentence must have exactly two groups — Explain that the two-part model helps us begin, but a sentence can include more meaningful detail groups.
  • Moving one group never changes the message — Swap or move one group, read both sentences aloud and compare what each one now means.
Quick checks and mastery

Quick checks

  • In ‘The yellow duck splashed in the pond’, which words name who?
  • Replace the action group in ‘My brother laughed’ and explain the new meaning.
  • Why is the spoken grouping first group little, second group dog ran, third group to not helpful?
  • Say ‘The small bird sang loudly’, pause after bird to separate the who group from the action group, then join the sentence again.
