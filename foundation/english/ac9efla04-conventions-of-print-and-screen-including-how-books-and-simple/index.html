How Books and Screens Are Organised

I can use the organisation clues in a book or a simple screen text. A cover and title help me identify a book; pages and reading direction guide me through it. On a screen, titles, icons and buttons can show where to start and what to do next.

What students learn

I can use the organisation clues in a book or a simple screen text. A cover and title help me identify a book; pages and reading direction guide me through it. On a screen, titles, icons and buttons can show where to start and what to do next.

Learning intention: I can use the organisation clues in a book or a simple screen text. A cover and title help me identify a book; pages and reading direction guide me through it. On a screen, titles, icons and buttons can show where to start and what to do next.

Success criteria

  • I can identify a cover and title.
  • I can find the starting point in a familiar text.
  • I can follow the expected English reading direction.
  • I can use an icon or button for navigation.
  • I can compare one book feature with one screen feature.
Key vocabulary
  • convention — a usual way a text is organised so readers know what to do
  • cover — the outside front of a book
  • title — the name of a text
  • navigation — using text features to move to the part you need
  • icon — a small picture or symbol that shows an action or idea

Learning activities
  1. Book walk — Students point to the cover, title, first page and next-page direction on a familiar book.
    cover → title → first page → next page
  2. Screen tour — On a teacher-controlled page, students locate the title, speaker icon and Next button without pressing yet.
    title • speaker icon • Next button
  3. Tell the path — Students give a partner the steps for beginning and moving through one text.
    start point → reading path → navigation
Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Trace the reading direction in Standard Australian English

Standard Australian English print is usually followed from left to right across a line and from the top line down to the next line.

Teacher: Find the first word at the top left. Sweep from left to right across the line. Return to the left of the next line and move down the page.

Ask: Where does this English sentence begin, and where will your finger move next?

Students: Complete the modelled response and use the example to show: locates the first word and traces left to right, then returns to the start of the next line below.

Look for: The learner locates the first word and traces left to right, then returns to the start of the next line below.

If incorrect: Place a green start dot and a curved return arrow on a two-line text, then let the learner trace over them.

E2: Use tabs in a simple digital text

A tab is a labelled part of a screen that helps a reader move directly to a section of a digital text.

Teacher: Find the row of labelled tabs. Read or hear the tab labels. Choose the label that matches the information you need.

Ask: You want to see what wombats eat. Which tab would you choose: Home, Food or Homes?

Students: Complete the modelled response and use the example to show: selects Food and explains that the label matches the needed information.

Look for: The learner selects Food and explains that the label matches the needed information.

If incorrect: Pair each tab with a picture clue and rehearse a complete question, such as: I need food facts; which label matches?

E3: Notice where images and words are placed

The placement of words near, above, below or inside an image helps a reader connect the right words with the right picture.

Teacher: Find the words. Find the nearby image or image part. Explain how the placement helps connect them.

Ask: A label reading wing sits beside an arrow pointing to a bird's wing. How does the placement help?

Students: Complete the modelled response and use the example to show: follows the spatial clue or arrow and states which words belong with which image or part.

Look for: The learner follows the spatial clue or arrow and states which words belong with which image or part.

If incorrect: Move a loose word card beside the matching picture, then add a yarn line or finger trace between them.

E4: Find a book title and reading start

A book's title names the text, while the first readable page and the top-left starting point show where its English print begins.

Teacher: Point to the front cover. Locate and name the title. Open the book and point to where reading begins.

Ask: Show the title of this book, then open it and point to the first word you would read.

Students: Complete the modelled response and use the example to show: identifies the title and a plausible first reading point rather than pointing only to the cover picture.

Look for: The learner identifies the title and a plausible first reading point rather than pointing only to the cover picture.

If incorrect: Add removable Title and Start here arrows, model once, then move the arrows to a new familiar book.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Start reading at any place on the page — Point to the agreed starting point and trace the reading direction with a finger.
  • Every picture on a screen is a button — Compare a display picture with a clearly labelled button or icon and test only with teacher guidance.
  • Books and screens are organised in exactly the same way — Place them side by side and name one shared clue, such as a title, and one different clue, such as page turn or Next button.
