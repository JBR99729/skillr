AC9EFLA07 • Foundation English

Pictures and Words Build Meaning Together

I can use picture clues and word clues together to understand a story or information text. Sometimes a picture repeats the words; sometimes it adds a place, feeling, action or detail. Words can also tell something that is not visible in the picture.

What students learn

I can use picture clues and word clues together to understand a story or information text. Sometimes a picture repeats the words; sometimes it adds a place, feeling, action or detail. Words can also tell something that is not visible in the picture.

Learning intention: I can use picture clues and word clues together to understand a story or information text. Sometimes a picture repeats the words; sometimes it adds a place, feeling, action or detail. Words can also tell something that is not visible in the picture.

Success criteria

  • I can identify a picture clue.
  • I can identify a word clue.
  • I can explain what an image adds.
  • I can explain what words add.
  • I can combine both clues into one clear meaning.
Key vocabulary
  • image — a picture, photograph, drawing or diagram
  • word clue — meaning communicated by the written or spoken words
  • picture clue — meaning communicated by what an image shows
  • detail — a small piece of information that adds meaning
  • combine — put clues together to make a fuller meaning

Learning activities
  1. Cover and reveal — Show an image without its sentence, then reveal the words. Students name what each part contributes.
    image clue → word clue → combined meaning
  2. Missing clue — Read a sentence while hiding the image. Students predict one useful detail the picture might add.
    words + ? picture detail
  3. Story and information — Compare a story illustration and labelled diagram. Identify how each image helps its text.
    illustration ↔ story • diagram ↔ information
Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Compare meaning from words only and images only

A reader may build different interpretations when only the words or only the images are available because each mode can contribute different evidence.

Teacher: Use the words only and say what they suggest. Use the image only and say what it suggests. Combine both and explain what became clearer or changed.

Ask: The words say, “It was finally here.” The image shows a child beside a birthday cake. What can each part make you think?

Students: Complete the modelled response and use the example to show: gives a text-based idea, an image-based idea and a combined interpretation that remains open to reasonable alternatives.

Look for: The learner gives a text-based idea, an image-based idea and a combined interpretation that remains open to reasonable alternatives.

If incorrect: Use three mats labelled Words, Image and Together and record one clue on each.

E2: Match written details with image details

Some written details are reflected directly in an accompanying image, while other written details such as a name, thought, time or unseen event may not be visible.

Teacher: Name one detail from the words. Check whether the image shows that detail. Find one detail supplied by only one mode.

Ask: The sentence says, “Lina quietly waited until night.” The picture shows a child sitting beside a tent under stars. Which details match, and which detail is not visible?

Students: Complete the modelled response and use the example to show: matches waiting or night with image evidence and identifies Lina's name or quietly as information not securely visible in the image.

Look for: The learner matches waiting or night with image evidence and identifies Lina's name or quietly as information not securely visible in the image.

If incorrect: Cover one phrase at a time and ask Can I still know this from the picture?

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • The picture tells every part of the message — Ask what the words tell that cannot be seen, such as a character's name, time or thought.
  • The words are enough, so the image can be ignored — Cover the image, notice what information disappears, then restore it and combine both clues.
  • Every image only repeats the words — Find one added detail in the image and explain how it extends the sentence or fact.
Quick checks and mastery

Quick checks

  • The words say ‘Lina heard a crash’ and the picture shows a fallen pot. What does each clue add?
  • In a labelled butterfly diagram, what can the image show more quickly than a sentence?
  • Name one detail words could tell that a picture might not show.
  • Combine the clues ‘It was freezing’ and a picture of a child in a snowy park.
