Key vocabulary
  • image — a picture, photograph, drawing or diagram
  • word clue — meaning communicated by the written or spoken words
  • picture clue — meaning communicated by what an image shows
  • detail — a small piece of information that adds meaning
  • combine — put clues together to make a fuller meaning
Key concept

I can use both the words and the picture to understand a text.

Learning goal: I can use both the words and the picture to understand a text.

  • I can identify a word clue.
  • I can explain what an image adds.
  • I can explain what words add.
  • I can combine both clues into one clear meaning.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Compare meaning from words only and images only

A reader may build different interpretations when only the words or only the images are available because each mode can contribute different evidence.

Think: Use the words only and say what they suggest. Use the image only and say what it suggests. Combine both and explain what became clearer or changed.

Try: The words say, “It was finally here.” The image shows a child beside a birthday cake. What can each part make you think?

A strong answer: Complete the modelled response and use the example to show: gives a text-based idea, an image-based idea and a combined interpretation that remains open to reasonable alternatives.

Check: The learner gives a text-based idea, an image-based idea and a combined interpretation that remains open to reasonable alternatives.

If you are stuck: Use three mats labelled Words, Image and Together and record one clue on each.

E2: Match written details with image details

Some written details are reflected directly in an accompanying image, while other written details such as a name, thought, time or unseen event may not be visible.

Think: Name one detail from the words. Check whether the image shows that detail. Find one detail supplied by only one mode.

Try: The sentence says, “Lina quietly waited until night.” The picture shows a child sitting beside a tent under stars. Which details match, and which detail is not visible?

A strong answer: Complete the modelled response and use the example to show: matches waiting or night with image evidence and identifies Lina's name or quietly as information not securely visible in the image.

Check: The learner matches waiting or night with image evidence and identifies Lina's name or quietly as information not securely visible in the image.

If you are stuck: Cover one phrase at a time and ask Can I still know this from the picture?

Common misconceptions
  • The picture tells every part of the message — Ask what the words tell that cannot be seen, such as a character's name, time or thought.
  • The words are enough, so the image can be ignored — Cover the image, notice what information disappears, then restore it and combine both clues.
  • Every image only repeats the words — Find one added detail in the image and explain how it extends the sentence or fact.
Explain and apply

Use a real page

Choose an illustrated story page already available at home or school. Adult: cover the words and ask what the image shows; then read the words.

Answer and teaching guidance

Record one visible detail and one detail stated in words. This activity needs the actual page: a written picture description alone cannot show how a child interprets an image.

Find the evidence

On that story page, name something known from the words, something seen in the picture, and something both tell.

Answer and teaching guidance

Accept answers supported by the selected page. Ask the child to point to each clue. A character name may come from words while clothing or position may come from the image; do not assume every page has all three kinds.

Try an information page

Use an existing labelled photograph or diagram from an information book. Read the caption or labels, then ask what the image adds.

Answer and teaching guidance

The image might show appearance, position or parts; words may name a part or give a fact not visible. Check the actual page rather than accepting a guessed fact.

Check a guess

Ask: “Does this picture tell us the character’s name?” How could we check?

Answer and teaching guidance

A picture of a person alone does not establish their name. Look for words or labels that name them. Support: compare two clear clues. Extend: explain how removing either words or image would change understanding.

Try these
  1. Cover and reveal — Show an image without its sentence, then reveal the words. Students name what each part contributes.
    image clue → word clue → combined meaning
  2. Missing clue — Read a sentence while hiding the image. Students predict one useful detail the picture might add.
    words + ? picture detail
  3. Story and information — Compare a story illustration and labelled diagram. Identify how each image helps its text.
    illustration ↔ story • diagram ↔ information
Check your understanding

Quick checks

  • The words say, ‘The puppy hid.’ The picture shows the puppy under a bed. What does the picture add?
  • The words say, ‘A storm is coming.’ The image shows dark clouds and bending trees. What do they do together?
  • A picture shows three chicks, but the words say, ‘The hen watched her babies.’ What does the picture add?
  • The words say, ‘Sam wore boots.’ The picture also shows a yellow raincoat. What does only the picture tell?
