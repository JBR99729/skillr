Words for Familiar People, Places and Topics

What students learn

I can notice useful words in familiar contexts, connect each word to its meaning and use it when I speak or write. Some words are everyday words; others name an idea precisely in a favourite activity or school topic. Knowing the context helps me choose the word that fits.

Learning intention: I can notice useful words in familiar contexts, connect each word to its meaning and use it when I speak or write. Some words are everyday words; others name an idea precisely in a favourite activity or school topic. Knowing the context helps me choose the word that fits.

Success criteria

  • I can connect a familiar word to its meaning.
  • I can use context to choose a fitting word.
  • I can explain a topic word with an example.
  • I can use new vocabulary in a sentence.
  • I can choose a precise word instead of an unrelated word.
Key vocabulary
  • vocabulary — the words we understand and use
  • context — the situation or topic that helps a word make sense
  • meaning — the idea a word communicates
  • precise — exact and clear for what we mean
  • topic word — a word that is especially useful when learning or talking about one topic

Learning activities
  1. Word detective — Read a familiar sentence and use the picture or situation to infer one new word's meaning.
    sentence + context clue → word meaning
  2. Topic basket — Sort picture-word cards into home, personal interest and school-topic contexts, allowing words to fit more than one.
    home • interests • school topics
  3. Use it clearly — Students choose one word, explain it with a familiar example and use it in a short sentence.
    word → meaning → example sentence
Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Use topic words in learning and imaginative play

Learning areas and imagined roles use precise vocabulary that helps people act, explain and solve problems together.

Teacher: Name the learning or play context. Choose a word that belongs to it. Use the word while doing, asking or explaining something.

Ask: In a pretend shop, which words could a shopkeeper and customer use to buy an apple?

Students: Complete the modelled response and use the example to show: uses relevant words such as price, dollar, customer or receipt in a meaningful shop exchange rather than merely repeating them.

Look for: The learner uses relevant words such as price, dollar, customer or receipt in a meaningful shop exchange rather than merely repeating them.

If incorrect: Give labelled props and two oral models: How much is the apple? and Here is your receipt.

E2: Build a word web for a school topic

Words studied together can form a topic network, with each word naming a person, relationship, place, action or idea connected to the topic.

Teacher: Name the topic in the centre. Add a word that belongs to the topic. Explain its connection with a picture, example or short sentence.

Ask: How could caregiver, sibling, grandparent and home connect to a class topic called Families?

Students: Complete the modelled response and use the example to show: explains a relevant connection for a topic word while respecting that different learners' family experiences vary.

Look for: The learner explains a relevant connection for a topic word while respecting that different learners' family experiences vary.

If incorrect: Offer pictures and blank cards so children can point, name, draw or contribute a word used by their family or community.

E3: Grow vocabulary through people, texts, images and objects

A new word becomes more usable when learners hear it in interaction, see it in a text or image, connect it to an object or action and try it themselves.

Teacher: Hear the word in a meaningful sentence. Connect it to an image, action or object. Say the meaning and use the word in a new context.

Ask: You hear the word fragile, see a picture of a cracked shell and carefully hold an empty eggshell. What does fragile mean?

Students: Complete the modelled response and use the example to show: explains fragile as easy to break or damage and uses it accurately for another suitable object.

Look for: The learner explains fragile as easy to break or damage and uses it accurately for another suitable object.

If incorrect: Contrast a sturdy block and an empty eggshell, then model the sentence The eggshell is fragile because it can break easily.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • A long word is always a better word — Compare long and short choices and select the word whose meaning fits the context most precisely.
  • Repeating a word means understanding it — Ask the learner to point to an example, explain the meaning simply and use the word in a new familiar sentence.
  • A word has exactly the same meaning in every context — Place the word in two familiar sentences and use surrounding clues to discuss how its meaning or use changes.
Quick checks and mastery

Quick checks

  • What does observe mean in ‘We observe the snail closely’, and which clue helps?
  • Choose tiny or enormous for an ant beside a shoe and explain your choice.
  • Name one precise word from a sport, hobby or school topic you know.
  • Use the word stem in a plant sentence so a listener can understand its meaning.
