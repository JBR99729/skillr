CVC words

I can segment a simple consonant–vowel–consonant word, choose a letter for each sound position and blend the written word to check it.

What students learn

I can segment a simple consonant–vowel–consonant word, choose a letter for each sound position and blend the written word to check it.

Learning intention: I can segment a simple consonant–vowel–consonant word, choose a letter for each sound position and blend the written word to check it.

Success criteria

  • I can hear three sound positions.
  • I can choose a letter for each sound.
  • I can keep letters in sound order.
  • I can blend a written CVC word.
  • I can use meaning to confirm the reading.
Key vocabulary
  • CVC word — a three-letter word with consonant, vowel and consonant sound positions
  • consonant — a speech sound made with some closing or narrowing in the mouth
  • vowel — an open speech sound that forms the centre of a syllable
  • segment — break a spoken word into separate speech sounds
  • blend — join letter-linked sounds to read a whole word
  • sound position — the first, middle or final place of a sound in a word
Model and guided application

Worked example: build and read cat

Worked-model method: segment, map letters, blend and check the CVC word.

1. Say cat and move three counters for its first, middle and final speech sounds.2. Choose printed lower-case c for the sound at the start of cat, lower-case a for the short middle sound heard in apple, and lower-case t for the sound at the start of top.3. Place c, a and t in order, then blend the printed word cat to check it.

Model answer: Cat is a CVC word because its three letters represent a consonant sound, a vowel sound and a final consonant sound in order.

Worked example: build and read sun

Worked-model method: segment, map letters, blend and check the CVC word.

1. Say sun slowly and listen for three speech sounds.2. Choose printed lower-case s using sun as its own cue, lower-case u for the short middle sound in up, and lower-case n for the sound at the start of nest.3. Place s, u and n in order and blend the written word sun.

Model answer: Sun has all three sound positions represented, including the vowel letter in the middle.

Learning activities
  1. Three counters — Say a familiar CVC picture name and move one counter for each speech sound.
    first • middle • final
  2. Letter map — Place one printed letter under each counter, then point from left to right.
    sound counter above • letter card below
  3. Blend to check — Blend the three printed letters, choose the matching picture and correct any mismatched letter.
    written word → spoken word → picture meaning
Australian Curriculum elaborations
Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • The middle vowel can be skipped — Hold the middle counter and stretch the vowel sound before choosing its letter.
  • Letter names should be blended into the word — Use each printed letter’s taught sound, then blend the speech sounds smoothly.
  • A picture guess is enough — Point to and blend every printed letter before using the picture to confirm meaning.
Quick checks and mastery

Quick checks

  • How many sound positions are in the CVC word cat?
  • Which letter belongs in the middle of the printed word sun?
  • Why must a CVC word include a vowel sound in the middle?
  • Blend the printed word cat and explain how you checked it.
