What students learn in AC9MFM02

This unit helps students build a clear, usable understanding of Days, routines and time of day. The goal is not to memorise one answer pattern. Students should be able to explain the idea, recognise it in a new example and apply it in a short practice or worksheet task.

  • Start with the main idea: sequence days of the week and times of the day including morning, lunchtime, afternoon and night time, and connect them to familiar events and actions.
  • Use concrete examples, pictures, oral explanation and short written responses before moving to independent practice.
  • ordering images of daily events on a string line across the room, and justifying the placement by referring to morning, lunchtime, afternoon, and night time distinguishing between the days of the school week and weekends, and recognising that the days of the week form a sequence that repeats, with Monday always following on from Sunday
  • Finish with mixed questions so students must choose the correct strategy rather than copy the last example.

Learning intentions

  • sequence days of the week and times of the day including morning, lunchtime, afternoon and night time, and connect them to familiar events and actions
  • ordering images of daily events on a string line across the room, and justifying the placement by referring to morning, lunchtime, afternoon, and night time
  • distinguishing between the days of the school week and weekends, and recognising that the days of the week form a sequence that repeats, with Monday always following on from Sunday
  • sequencing the events from a story in the order in which they occurred using language like, “This happened first” then “This happened next”

Success criteria

  • I can show the skill using objects, pictures, actions or numbers.
  • I can use the key vocabulary accurately.
  • I can answer a new example without copying the previous one.
  • I can explain or demonstrate how I know.

Recommended teaching sequence

  1. 1

    Sequence familiar events within one day.

  2. 2

    Use morning, afternoon and evening accurately.

  3. 3

    Learn the repeating order of days of the week.

  4. 4

    Identify the day before and after a given day.

  5. 5

    Connect yesterday, today and tomorrow.

  6. 6

    Interpret and create a simple weekly routine.

Question 55 Reasoning and explanation 2 marks

Answer and marking guide

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

The content description and elaborations below show the curriculum ideas taught in this unit. Items marked as teaching context support lesson planning but may not appear in the initial eight-question activity.

  • Content description: sequence days of the week and times of the day including morning, lunchtime, afternoon and night time, and connect them to familiar events and actions
  • E1: ordering images of daily events on a string line across the room, and justifying the placement by referring to morning, lunchtime, afternoon, and night time
  • E2: distinguishing between the days of the school week and weekends, and recognising that the days of the week form a sequence that repeats, with Monday always following on from Sunday
  • E3: sequencing the events from a story in the order in which they occurred using language like, “This happened first” then “This happened next”
  • E4: creating, interpreting and discussing classroom rosters; for example, a roster for watering the classroom garden and asking, “Who watered the garden yesterday?” or “Whose turn is it today?”
  • E5: creating a pictorial diary to show the important events that happen on the various days of the week

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Rushing to a rule before checking the concrete model, drawing or number sentence.
  • Using the correct answer once but not being able to explain why it works.
  • Mixing up similar vocabulary such as more/less, before/after, longer/shorter or equal groups/sharing.
