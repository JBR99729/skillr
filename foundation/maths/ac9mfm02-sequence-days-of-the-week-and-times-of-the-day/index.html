Days, Routines and Time of Day

Daily events can be sequenced by time of day, and the seven days of the week repeat in a fixed cycle.

What students learn

Daily events can be sequenced by time of day, and the seven days of the week repeat in a fixed cycle.

Learning intention: I can daily events can be sequenced by time of day, and the seven days of the week repeat in a fixed cycle.

Success criteria

  • I can sequence dayparts.
  • I can order days of week.
  • I can use before / after.
  • I can use yesterday / today / tomorrow.
  • I can explain a routine sequence.
Key vocabulary
  • sequence — an order that makes sense
  • daypart — a part of the day, such as morning
  • yesterday — the day before today
  • tomorrow — the day after today

Learning activities
  • Order picture cards from morning through night and explain the order.
  • Build a class week strip and identify school days and weekend days.
  • Use a today marker to answer yesterday/today/tomorrow questions.
Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Order events by time of day

Times of day provide a broad order for familiar events. Morning comes before lunchtime, followed by afternoon, evening and night, although particular routines can differ.

🌅 morning→🥪 lunchtime→☀️ afternoon→🌙 night

Teacher: Choose familiar event pictures and name each event. Place each event beneath its likely time-of-day label. Read the sequence from morning to night and justify one placement.

Ask: I am using the time of day to order the events, not choosing which event I like best.

Look for: Order breakfast, lunch, play after school and bedtime, then explain one placement using a time-of-day word.

E2: Build the repeating week

The seven days occur in a fixed repeating cycle. After Sunday, the sequence returns to Monday and begins another week.

Mon–Fri
weekdaysSat–Sun
weekend↻Sunday → Monday

Teacher: Arrange cards for all seven days in order. Connect the final day back to the first to show the cycle. Use the sequence to name the day before or after a given day.

Ask: The week does not stop at Sunday. Monday begins the next cycle of the same seven day names.

Look for: What follows Sunday, and how does the week model show that the sequence continues?

E3: Sequence a story with order words

Temporal order describes what happened first and what followed. Words such as first, next, then and last communicate the sequence clearly.

first→next→then→last

Teacher: Study all story pictures and identify clues about cause or change. Arrange the pictures from earliest to latest. Retell the sequence using first, next, then and last.

Ask: I am explaining what happened in time order, not the order I happened to pick up the cards.

Look for: Put four familiar-event pictures in order and retell them using all four sequence words.

E4: Read a roster across changing days

A roster connects people or jobs to ordered days. Yesterday, today and tomorrow are relative terms that shift when the today marker moves.

yesterday
Samtoday
Miatomorrow
Leo→move today

Teacher: Locate today on the weekly roster. Read the person or job beside yesterday, today and tomorrow. Move the today marker forward and update all three relative-day statements.

Ask: The roster stays in day order, but the words yesterday, today and tomorrow move with our today marker.

Look for: If Mia is on today and Leo is on tomorrow, whose turn comes next? Move the marker and describe the change.

E5: Make a pictorial weekly diary

A pictorial diary records events beneath day labels so a reader can see when events occur and compare their order across a week.

Mon
📚Tue
⚽Wed
🎵Thu
🏊Fri
📚

Teacher: Lay out the day labels in the correct weekly order. Choose a clear picture or symbol for each important event. Place each symbol beneath its day and read the diary using day names.

Ask: The picture tells the event; its position under the day label tells when it happens.

Look for: Add two events to a week strip and use day names to explain when they happen and which comes first.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Stops at Sunday — Show that Monday follows Sunday because the week repeats.
  • Yesterday/tomorrow reversed — Anchor the language to the movable today card.
  • Orders by preference — Use when events happen, not which event the child likes most.
Quick checks and mastery

Quick checks

  • Put in order: night, morning, afternoon, lunchtime.
  • What day comes after Sunday?
  • If today is Wednesday, what was yesterday?
  • Which usually comes first: breakfast or dinner? Explain.

Mastery

  • Sequence dayparts
  • Order days of week
  • Use before / after
  • Use yesterday / today / tomorrow
  • Explain a routine sequence
