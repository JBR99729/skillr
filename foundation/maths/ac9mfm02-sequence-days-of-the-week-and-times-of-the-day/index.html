AC9MFM02: Sequence days of the week and times of the day...

sequence days of the week and times of the day including morning, lunchtime, afternoon and night time, and connect them to familiar events and actions

Start lessonTeacher slidesWorksheetPracticeTest
🎯 Learn the key ideaStart here

Learning intention: Daily events can be sequenced by time of day, and the seven days of the week repeat in a fixed cycle.

Success looks like: Sequence dayparts; Order days of week; Use before / after; Use yesterday / today / tomorrow; Explain a routine sequence.

Learning routine: Place event → Name daypart → Order → Before/After → Repeat week → Explain

👀 Model and worked examplesLearn

Build the day and week sequences

DAY
morning → lunchtime → afternoon → night

Place familiar events in the correct part of the day.

WEEK
Mon → Tue → Wed → Thu → Fri → Sat → Sun → Mon

The sequence repeats.

RELATIVE DAYS
yesterday ← today → tomorrow

Move the “today” marker each day.

Think aloud: Build or point to the model first, explain what each part shows, then state the answer.

Before and after in a repeating cycle

Sunday → Monday → Tuesday

Ask: “What comes after Sunday? What comes before Tuesday?”

⚠️ Common mistakes and fixesFix errors
  • Stops at Sunday: Show that Monday follows Sunday because the week repeats.
  • Yesterday/tomorrow reversed: Anchor the language to the movable today card.
  • Orders by preference: Use when events happen, not which event the child likes most.
✅ Quick checkCheck
  • Put in order: night, morning, afternoon, lunchtime.
  • What day comes after Sunday?
  • If today is Wednesday, what was yesterday?
  • Which usually comes first: breakfast or dinner? Explain.

These are curriculum teaching examples used as a breadth check, not separate mandatory outcomes.

E1

ordering images of daily events on a string line across the room, and justifying the placement by referring to morning, lunchtime, afternoon, and night time

Learning connection: Daily events can be sequenced by time of day, and the seven days of the week repeat in a fixed cycle.

E2

distinguishing between the days of the school week and weekends, and recognising that the days of the week form a sequence that repeats, with Monday always following on from Sunday

Learning connection: Daily events can be sequenced by time of day, and the seven days of the week repeat in a fixed cycle.

E3

sequencing the events from a story in the order in which they occurred using language like, “This happened first” then “This happened next”

creating a pictorial diary to show the important events that happen on the various days of the week
