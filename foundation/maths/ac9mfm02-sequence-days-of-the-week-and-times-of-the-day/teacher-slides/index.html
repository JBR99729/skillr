AC9MFM02 - AC9MFM02: Sequence days of the week and times of the day...
Example 1

ordering images of daily events on a string line across the room, and justifying the placement by referring to morning, lunchtime, afternoon, and night time Learning connection: Daily events can be sequenced by time of day, and the seven days of the week repeat in a fixed cycle.

Example 2

distinguishing between the days of the school week and weekends, and recognising that the days of the week form a sequence that repeats, with Monday always following on from Sunday Learning connection: Daily events can be sequenced by time of day, and the seven days of the week repeat in a fixed cycle.

Example 3

sequencing the events from a story in the order in which they occurred using language like, “This happened first” then “This happened next” Learning connection: Daily events can be sequenced by time of day, and the seven days of the week repeat in a fixed cycle.

Example 4

creating, interpreting and discussing classroom rosters; for example, a roster for watering the classroom garden and asking, “Who watered the garden yesterday?” or “Whose turn is it today?” Learning connection: Daily events can be sequenced by time of day, and the seven days of the week repeat in a fixed cycle.

Review hints and exit ticketReady for practice

Assessment-style questions and review hints 1

Questions

  • What day comes after Sunday?
  • If today is Wednesday, what was yesterday?

Review hints

  • Main idea: Daily events can be sequenced by time of day, and the seven days of the week repeat in a fixed cycle.
  • Useful routine: Place event → Name daypart → Order → Before/After → Repeat week → Explain

Assessment-style questions and review hints 2

Questions

  • Which usually comes first: breakfast or dinner? Explain.

Review hints

  • sequence: an order that makes sense
  • daypart: a part of the day, such as morning

Exit ticket

  • Put in order: night, morning, afternoon, lunchtime.
