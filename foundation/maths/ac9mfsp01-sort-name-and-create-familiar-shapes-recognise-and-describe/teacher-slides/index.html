Clean one-page examples

AC9MFSP01 - AC9MFSP01: Sort, name and create familiar shapes; recognise and describe familiar...
Example 1

sorting a collection of shapes into groups based on different features such as the number of sides, colour or size, and describing how they have been sorted Learning connection: Recognise familiar shapes by their features, even when they are turned, resized or seen inside everyday objects.

Example 2

creating a picture using a variety of shapes and a range of materials, including objects to trace around, describing the shapes they have created or used and sharing why they chose each shape in their picture Learning connection: Recognise familiar shapes by their features, even when they are turned, resized or seen inside everyday objects.

Example 3

creating familiar shapes using groups of people; for example, holding hands and creating a circle Learning connection: Recognise familiar shapes by their features, even when they are turned, resized or seen inside everyday objects.

Example 4

recognising and naming shapes that are (close to) rectangles, squares, triangles and circles in component parts of everyday items; for example, on bicycles, toy vehicles or kitchen pantry items Learning connection: Recognise familiar shapes by their features, even when they are turned, resized or seen inside everyday objects.

Elaboration expansion and examples 3

  • E5: describing and naming shapes within objects that can be observed on Country/Place, recreating and sorting into groups based on their shape Learning connection: Recognise familiar shapes by their features, even when they are turned, resized or seen inside everyday objects.
  • Describe features, not just appearance: TRIANGLE △ 3 straight sides and 3 corners. SQUARE □ 4 straight equal sides and 4 corners. CIRCLE ○ Curved boundary and no corners. Think aloud: Build or point to the model first, explain what each part shows, then state the answer.
