What students learn in AC9MFSP02

This unit helps students build a clear, usable understanding of Position, location and movement. The goal is not to memorise one answer pattern. Students should be able to explain the idea, recognise it in a new example and apply it in a short practice or worksheet task.

  • Start with the main idea: describe the position and location of themselves and objects in relation to other people and objects within a familiar space.
  • Use concrete examples, pictures, oral explanation and short written responses before moving to independent practice.
  • describing the position of an item in relation to other items in the space using language like “inside”, “underneath” and “on top of”; for example, when asked “Where are the scissors kept?”, responding with “They are in a box, on the bottom shelf at the back of the classroom” describing where they have moved themselves and items in relation to other items within a space, using familiar terms; for example, playing a hiding game and when asked “Where did you hide the ball?”, responding, “I hid it behind the garbage bin over there near the bench”
  • Finish with mixed questions so students must choose the correct strategy rather than copy the last example.

Learning intentions

  • describe the position and location of themselves and objects in relation to other people and objects within a familiar space
  • describing the position of an item in relation to other items in the space using language like “inside”, “underneath” and “on top of”; for example, when asked “Where are the scissors kept?”, responding with “They are in a box, on the bottom shelf at the back of the classroom”
  • describing where they have moved themselves and items in relation to other items within a space, using familiar terms; for example, playing a hiding game and when asked “Where did you hide the ball?”, responding, “I hid it behind the garbage bin over there near the bench”
  • describing the position of a robotic toy in relation to other objects as it moves around a familiar space; for example, describing the position of a robotic car as being under the desk or next to the chair

Success criteria

  • I can show the skill using objects, pictures, actions or numbers.
  • I can use the key vocabulary accurately.
  • I can answer a new example without copying the previous one.
  • I can explain or demonstrate how I know.

Recommended teaching sequence

  1. 1

    Describe position relative to the student.

  2. 2

    Describe one object's position relative to another.

  3. 3

    Follow one-step positional instructions.

  4. 4

    Follow and give a short movement sequence.

  5. 5

    Use several spatial words to describe one scene.

  6. 6

    Check whether instructions lead to the intended location.

Differentiation

Support

Use one spatial word at a time with real objects and model the movement.

Extension

Use multi-step routes, changing viewpoints and precise relational descriptions.
