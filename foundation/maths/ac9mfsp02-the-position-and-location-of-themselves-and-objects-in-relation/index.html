AC9MFSP02: Describe position and location in familiar spaces

describe the position and location of themselves and objects in relation to other people and objects within a familiar space

Start lessonTeacher slidesWorksheetPracticeTest
🎯 Learn the key ideaStart here

Learning intention: Position words describe where something is in relation to another person, object or place.

Success looks like: Use position words; Name reference object; Follow instructions; Give movement instructions; Describe a precise location.

Learning routine: Choose reference → Say position → Move → Describe again → Follow route → Check

👀 Model and worked examplesLearn

Always name the reference object

INSIDE
[ ● ]

The ball is inside the box.

UNDER
TABLE
─────
  ●

The ball is under the table.

BESIDE
CHAIR ●

The ball is beside the chair.

Think aloud: Build or point to the model first, explain what each part shows, then state the answer.

Use more than one clue

“The scissors are in the box, on the bottom shelf, at the back of the classroom.”

Specific relational words make a location easier to find.

⚠️ Common mistakes and fixesFix errors
  • Says “over there”: Name a reference object: beside the chair, under the desk, inside the box.
  • Changes viewpoint silently: Keep the same reference point or explain the viewpoint change.
  • Follows only first step: Repeat a short route in order and check each move.
✅ Quick checkCheck
  • Where is a ball if it is inside a box?
  • Give one instruction that places a toy under a chair.
  • Which is more precise: “there” or “beside the desk”?
  • Describe a two-step route from a door to a table.

Ready for Practice when: Use position words; Name reference object; Follow instructions; Give movement instructions; Describe a precise location.

🇦🇺 Australian Curriculum coverageE1–E4

Content description: describe the position and location of themselves and objects in relation to other people and objects within a familiar space

These are curriculum teaching examples used as a breadth check, not separate mandatory outcomes.

First Nations teaching guidance: Use a locally appropriate, community-approved source; name the relevant Peoples and Country/Place; respect cultural authority and permissions; and do not present one example as universal across First Nations Australians.

E1

describing the position of an item in relation to other items in the space using language like “inside”, “underneath” and “on top of”; for example, when asked “Where are the scissors kept?”, responding with “They are in a box, on the bottom shelf at the back of the classroom”

Learning connection: Position words describe where something is in relation to another person, object or place.

E2

describing where they have moved themselves and items in relation to other items within a space, using familiar terms; for example, playing a hiding game and when asked “Where did you hide the ball?”, responding, “I hid it behind the garbage bin over there near the bench”

Learning connection: Position words describe where something is in relation to another person, object or place.

E3

describing the position of a robotic toy in relation to other objects as it moves around a familiar space; for example, describing the position of a robotic car as being under the desk or next to the chair

Learning connection: Position words describe where something is in relation to another person, object or place.

E4

exploring First Nations Australians’ instructive games; for example, Thapumpan from the Wik-Mungkan Peoples of Cape Bedford in north Queensland, describing position and movement of self in relation to other participants, objects or locations

Learning connection: Position words describe where something is in relation to another person, object or place.
