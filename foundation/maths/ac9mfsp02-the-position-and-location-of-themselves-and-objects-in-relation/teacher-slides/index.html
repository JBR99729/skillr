AC9MFSP02 - AC9MFSP02: Describe position and location in familiar spaces
Example 1

describing the position of an item in relation to other items in the space using language like “inside”, “underneath” and “on top of”; for example, when asked “Where are the scissors kept?”, responding with “They are in a box, on the bottom shelf at the back of the classroom” Learning connection: Position words describe where something is in relation to another person, object or place.

Example 2

describing where they have moved themselves and items in relation to other items within a space, using familiar terms; for example, playing a hiding game and when asked “Where did you hide the ball?”, responding, “I hid it behind the garbage bin over there near the bench” Learning connection: Position words describe where something is in relation to another person, object or place.

Example 3

describing the position of a robotic toy in relation to other objects as it moves around a familiar space; for example, describing the position of a robotic car as being under the desk or next to the chair Learning connection: Position words describe where something is in relation to another person, object or place.

Example 4

exploring First Nations Australians’ instructive games; for example, Thapumpan from the Wik-Mungkan Peoples of Cape Bedford in north Queensland, describing position and movement of self in relation to other participants, objects or locations Learning connection: Position words describe where something is in relation to another person, object or place.

AC9MFSP02 · AC9MFSP02: Describe position and location in familiar spaces

Learning focus: describe the position and location of themselves and objects in relation to other people and objects within a familiar space

Model it: exploring First Nations Australians’ instructive games; for example, Thapumpan from the Wik-Mungkan Peoples of Cape Bedford in north Queensland, describing position and movement of self in relation to other participants, objects or locations Learning connection: Position words describe where something is in relation to another person, object or place.

Ask the learner: Where is a ball if it is inside a box?

Look for: Where is a ball if it is inside a box? Which is more precise: “there” or “beside the desk”?

If they are stuck: If the learner guesses or repeats a rule without evidence, return to the concrete or visual model and ask them to explain what each part shows.

Assessment-style questions and review hints 1

Questions

  • Where is a ball if it is inside a box?
  • Which is more precise: “there” or “beside the desk”?

Review hints

  • Main idea: Position words describe where something is in relation to another person, object or place.
  • Useful routine: Choose reference → Say position → Move → Describe again → Follow route → Check

Assessment-style questions and review hints 2

Questions

  • Give one instruction that places a toy under a chair.
  • Describe a two-step route from a door to a table.

Review hints

  • position: where something is
  • location: the place where something can be found
