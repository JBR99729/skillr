Foundation Maths · AC9MFN02

Subitising to 5

See a small group and know how many without counting every object one by one.

Core conceptSubitising means recognising how many objects are in a small collection with a quick look. Learners may see the whole amount at once or notice helpful smaller parts, such as 4 and 1 making 5.

Learn from example

Teach the idea through one clear example before asking learners to respond.

4 and 1 make 5

How to teach it

Show the dots for a moment, then cover them.

Say: “I did not count every dot. I saw four around the outside and one in the middle. Four and one make five.”

Then ask: “What did your eyes notice?”

Key idea: the learner is recognising the amount from the arrangement, not pointing and counting one by one.

I do · 2 teacher-modelled questions

I do 1

See 4 quickly

Show 4 dots in a square for about two seconds, then cover them.

Model: “I saw 2 on top and 2 underneath, so I knew it was 4.”

Teaching point: seeing 2 and 2 is a valid subitising strategy.
I do 2

Same number, new arrangement

Show 5 counters. Move the same 5 counters into a new arrangement.

Model: “It is still 5 because I did not add any counters or take any away.”

Teaching point: arrangement can change while quantity stays the same.

You do · 2 learner questions

You do 1

How many?

Flash a new arrangement of 5 counters.

Ask: “How many did you see? What helped you know?”

Look for: “5”, with an explanation such as “4 and 1”, “3 and 2”, or recognition of the whole familiar pattern.
You do 2

Did the amount change?

Show 4 counters in a square, then spread the same 4 counters into a line.

Ask: “Is it still 4? How do you know?”

Look for: yes, because no counters were added or removed.

Exit ticket · 2 quick checks

Exit ticket 1

Quick recognition

Flash 3, 4 and then 5 in different arrangements.

Can the learner name each amount without one-by-one counting?

Exit ticket 2

Explain the idea

Move the same 5 counters into a different arrangement.

Ask: “Why is it still 5?”

Ready to move on when the learner recognises the total across more than one arrangement and explains that moving objects does not change how many there are.
If the learner counts every object, go back to familiar patterns of 2 or 3 and build up again. Subitising is recognition, not a speed contest.
