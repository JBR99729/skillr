Subitising to 5

See a small group, know how many, and explain what your eyes noticed

Ready to project and teach

Learning goalsStart here
Learning intention

I can look at a small group and know how many without counting each object.

LookSee partsSay totalExplain
Success looks like
  • Recognise 1–5 in more than one arrangement.
  • Say the total after a brief look instead of pointing to every object.
  • Explain helpful parts such as “3 and 2 make 5”.
  • Know that rearranging the same objects does not change the quantity.
Australian CurriculumExact wording and teacher focus
Content description

Recognise and name small collections

Recognise and name the number of objects within a collection up to 5 using subitising.

Teacher focus
  • A quick-look answer demonstrates subitising; one-by-one pointing demonstrates counting.
  • Familiar dice, domino and five-frame arrangements help children recognise quantities.
  • Children can recognise a whole directly or notice smaller parts within it.
I doModel the skill

Teacher talk: “I used a quick look. I did not point to every dot. For 5, I saw four around the outside and one in the middle.”

Worked examplesWe do

Same amount, different arrangement

Ask: “Did the number change?” Then: “What did your eyes notice in each arrangement?”

Key idea: moving the same objects changes the arrangement, not how many there are.

Compare after a quick look

Flash a group of 5, then a group of 4. Ask which has more and how the child knows. Encourage “I saw 5 and 4, so 5 has one more” rather than guessing from spacing.

Teacher promptsBuild understanding

Useful prompts

  • How many did you see?
  • What pattern helped you know?
  • Did you see the whole number, or smaller parts?
  • Can you make the same number in a different arrangement?
  • If I spread the counters out, does the amount change?

Do not turn subitising into a speed contest. If a child needs to count, reduce the collection and use a familiar pattern before trying again.

Check understandingQuestions and answers

Question 1

I flash four dots arranged as a square, then cover them. How many?

Answer

4. The child may recognise the whole square pattern or see 2 and 2.

Question 2

I move the same 5 counters into a new arrangement. Is it still 5? How do you know?

Answer

Yes. No counter was added or removed, so the quantity stayed 5.

Question 3

One child says “5” after pointing to every dot. Another says “5” after a quick look. Which response demonstrates subitising?

Answer

The quick-look response. Both totals can be correct, but subitising means recognising the quantity without counting each object one by one.

Exit ticketReview and assess
Review hints
  • If the child counts every dot, return to familiar patterns of 2 or 3.
  • If rearranging changes the answer, move the same counters while the child watches.
  • Use dice, domino or five-frame patterns before irregular arrangements.
Exit ticket

Flash a new arrangement of 5 counters for about two seconds.

Cover it. Ask: “How many? What helped you see it?” Then rearrange the same counters and ask again.

Ready to move on when the child recognises the total across more than one arrangement without relying on one-by-one counting.
