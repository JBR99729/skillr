1 · Understand

What does “subitise” mean?

Subitising means seeing “how many” immediately instead of touching and counting each object. Adults often do this with dice. Foundation students build the same skill with small collections, learning to see number structure such as 5 as 4 and 1 or 3 and 2.

See 5 as 4 and 1

● ●
● ●   ●

4 + 1 = 5
Say: “I saw four together and one more. I knew it was five without counting each dot.”

Why this matters

Children begin seeing number structure instead of treating every quantity as a long counting task.

5 can look different and still be 5.

This supports later addition, subtraction and part-part-whole thinking.

2 · Teach

Teach it in three moves

1

Flash

Show 1–5 counters or dots briefly, then cover them.

2

Name

Ask, “How many?” Do not turn it into a speed race.

3

Explain

Ask, “What did your eyes notice?”

Teacher model: “I did not count every dot. I saw 4 and 1 more. Four and one make 5.”
3 · Compare

Different arrangement, same number

Move the same counters without adding or removing any. The arrangement changes, but the amount does not.

Try it

Show differently arranged cards for 3, 4 and 5. Match equal quantities and compare which has more.

What mastery sounds like

“It is still 4. You moved them, but you didn’t add or take any away.”

4 · Practise

Quick-look practice

1

Recognise

Flash collections of 1 to 5. Say the total without pointing to every object.

2

Match

Match two different arrangements showing the same quantity.

3

Compare

Explain which card has more, fewer or the same number.

5 · Fix misconceptions

When a learner gets stuck

Counts every dot
Use familiar 2-, 3- and 4-dot patterns. Flash, cover, then ask what pattern or parts they noticed.
Thinks rearranging changes the number
Move the same counters and ask, “Did I add any? Did I take any away?”
Guesses without explaining
Ask, “What smaller groups did your eyes notice?”

Curriculum coverage · AC9MFN02

Content description: recognise and name the number of objects within a collection up to 5 using subitising.

  • E1: recognising how many objects are in a collection or in images on a card with a quick look and saying the associated number without counting.
  • E2: playing instructive card games that rely on recognising numbers represented in different ways; matching pairs of quantities shown differently; using subitising to compare and order collections and to say who has more when sharing items in a game.

Success criteria: recognise collections up to 5 without one-by-one counting; recognise the same quantity after rearrangement; explain useful parts; compare small collections using quick recognition.
