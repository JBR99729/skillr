AC9MFN02: Recognise and name the number of objects within a collection up to 5 using subitising

recognise and name the number of objects within a collection up to 5 using subitising

Topic guideWorksheetPracticeTest

Teach subitising to 5

Learning intention: students recognise a collection of up to 5 objects with a quick look, say how many there are without counting each object, and explain the smaller parts they noticed.

What subitising means

Subitising means seeing “how many” immediately. An adult usually recognises the dots on a die without touching and counting every dot. Foundation students develop the same skill with small collections. This strengthens number sense because they begin to see that 5 can be made from 4 and 1, 3 and 2, or other combinations.

E1 visual teaching: recognise a quantity with a quick look

Flash for two seconds:
● ●
● ●   ●
What the eyes can see: [● ● / ● ●] + [●] → 4 + 1 → 5

Teacher model: cover the dots and say, “I did not count every dot. I saw a group of 4 and 1 more. Four and one make 5.” Flash a new arrangement and ask the student to name the total and the parts they noticed.

E1 guided check: briefly show ● ● ● with ● ● underneath. Cover it. Ask, “How many? What groups did you see?” A secure response names 5 without recounting and explains “3 and 2”.

E2 visual teaching: match, compare and order different arrangements

Same quantity:
Card A: ● ● ● ● ●   =   Card B: ● ● / ● ● ●
Compare:
Card B: ● ● / ● ● ●   >   Card C: ● ● / ● ●
Conclusion: 5 is one more than 4.

Teacher model: say, “A and B look different, but both show 5 because no dot was added or removed. C shows 4, so A and B have more.” Match cards with equal quantities first, then arrange cards from fewer to more.

E2 guided check: show differently arranged cards for 3, 4 and 5. Ask the student to match equal quantities, order them and explain which collection has more using the visual evidence.

You do: quick-look practice

  1. Flash collections of 1 to 5 counters. The student says the total and explains the parts they saw.
  2. Match two cards that show the same quantity in different arrangements.
  3. Compare two quick-look cards and explain which has more, fewer or the same number.

Success criteria

  • I can recognise collections up to 5 without counting one by one.
  • I can recognise the same quantity when objects are rearranged.
  • I can explain the parts I saw, such as “3 and 2 make 5”.
  • I can use quick recognition to compare two small collections.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

The content description and elaborations below show the curriculum ideas taught in this unit. Items marked as teaching context support lesson planning.

  • Content description: recognise and name the number of objects within a collection up to 5 using subitising
  • E1: recognising how many objects are in a collection or in images on a card with a quick look and saying the associated number without counting
  • E2: playing instructive card games that rely on the recognition of numbers represented in different ways; for example, playing memory games, matching pairs of quantities on dot cards or similar where the arrangement on each is different; using subitising to compare and order collections and to say who has more when sharing items in a game

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Counting every dot: use brief flashes and structured arrangements, then ask what parts the student saw.
  • Thinking a new arrangement means a new number: rearrange the same counters in front of the student and confirm that none were added or removed.
  • Guessing without explaining: ask, “What smaller groups did your eyes notice?”
