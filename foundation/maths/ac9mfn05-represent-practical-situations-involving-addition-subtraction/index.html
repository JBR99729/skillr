AC9MFN05: Represent practical situations involving addition, subtraction and quantification with physical...

represent practical situations involving addition, subtraction and quantification with physical and virtual materials and use counting or subitising strategies

🎯 Learn the key ideaStart here

Learning intention: Use objects, actions, drawings and numbers to represent what happens when quantities join or separate.

Success looks like: Model joining; Model separating; Connect story to drawing; Record result with numeral; Explain why model matches story.

Learning routine: Act → Show → Draw → Say → Record → Check

Draw before → action → after. Then write the result with a numeral.

Think aloud: Build or point to the model first, explain what each part shows, then state the answer.

Match the model to the action

Story A: 4 blocks. 3 more are added.
Story B: 7 blocks. 3 are removed.

Ask: “Which story is joining? Which is separating? Show each with counters.”

⚠️ Common mistakes and fixesFix errors
  • Chooses operation from one word: Focus on what physically happens to the collection.
  • Skips the action: Act it out before drawing or recording.
  • Recounts from the wrong start: Keep the starting collection visible until the change is made.
✅ Quick checkCheck
  • 3 bears are playing. 2 more join. How many now?
  • 8 counters are shown. 3 are removed. How many are left?
  • Is “2 more arrive” a joining or separating action?
  • Draw a story that shows 5 becoming 7.

🇦🇺 Australian Curriculum coverageE1–E4

Content description: represent practical situations involving addition, subtraction and quantification with physical and virtual materials and use counting or subitising strategies

These are curriculum teaching examples used as a breadth check, not separate mandatory outcomes.

First Nations teaching guidance: Use a locally appropriate, community-approved source; name the relevant Peoples and Country/Place; respect cultural authority and permissions; and do not present one example as universal across First Nations Australians.

E1

using role-play and materials to represent mathematical relationships in stories; for example, “Eight kangaroos were drinking at the river and 3 hopped away”; drawing a picture and using materials to represent the situation, discussing, and recording the result of the action with a numeral

Learning connection: Use objects, actions, drawings and numbers to represent what happens when quantities join or separate.

E2

role playing or actively engaging in situations that involve quantifying or comparing collections of items or simple money transactions; for example, “Do we have enough scissors for our group so that each person has their own pair?”; role-playing using \$1 coins to pay for items in a shop where items are priced in whole dollars

Learning connection: Use objects, actions, drawings and numbers to represent what happens when quantities join or separate.

E3

representing situations expressed in First Nations Australians’ stories, such as “Tiddalick, the greedy frog”, that describe additive situations and their connections to Country/Place

Learning connection: Use objects, actions, drawings and numbers to represent what happens when quantities join or separate.

E4

representing addition and subtraction situations found in leaf games involving sets of objects used to tell stories, such as games from the Warlpiri Peoples of Yuendumu in the Northern Territory

Learning connection: Use objects, actions, drawings and numbers to represent what happens when quantities join or separate.
