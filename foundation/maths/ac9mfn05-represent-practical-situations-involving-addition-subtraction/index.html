Addition and Subtraction Stories

Use objects, actions, drawings and numbers to represent what happens when quantities join or separate.

What students learn

Use objects, actions, drawings and numbers to represent what happens when quantities join or separate.

Learning intention: I can use objects, actions, drawings and numbers to represent what happens when quantities join or separate.

Success criteria

  • I can model joining.
  • I can model separating.
  • I can connect story to drawing.
  • I can record result with numeral.
  • I can explain why model matches story.
Key vocabulary
  • join — put more objects into a group
  • separate — take objects away from a group
  • represent — show an idea with objects, pictures or numbers
  • result — how many there are after the action

Learning activities
  • Tell a toy story and have students physically perform every action.
  • Show a story and choose: join or separate? Then explain why.
  • Create a short story to match a given counter model.
Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Act, draw and record joining or separating stories

Addition stories join or increase a collection; subtraction stories separate or decrease it. Acting out the change makes the mathematical relationship visible before it is recorded.

8 objects− 3 move away5 remain
start → action → result

Teacher: Build and count the starting collection. Perform exactly the action described in the story. Draw before and after, then record the result with a numeral.

Ask: I watched what happened to the collection. Objects moved away, so the collection became smaller.

Look for: Model a story that starts with 8 objects and removes 3; explain why 5 remain.

E2: Quantify practical needs and simple money transactions

Counting and one-to-one matching solve everyday quantity problems. Whole-dollar prices can be paid by matching one $1 coin to each dollar in the price.

4 people ↔ 4 scissors$1 + $1 + $1 ↔ $3

Teacher: Identify what must be counted or matched. Build the required collection one item at a time. Check the match and explain whether there is enough, too much or too little.

Ask: I know there are enough because every person—or every dollar—has exactly one matching item.

Look for: Supply one item to each of 4 people and show how to pay $3 using $1 coins.

E3: Represent an additive situation from a sourced First Nations story

A story can contain a mathematical change: a starting quantity, an action that joins or separates, and a resulting quantity. The cultural story remains more than a maths prompt and must be used respectfully.

sourced story start→ joining or separating action →represented result

Teacher: Choose an authorised or reliable version and preserve its cultural context. Identify one visible additive action without reducing the whole story to arithmetic. Represent that action with counters, drawings or movement and explain the result.

Ask: We are representing one mathematical change in this specific story; the story itself carries cultural meaning beyond our number model.

Look for: Identify the start, action and result in the selected passage and build a matching material model.

E4: Represent sets and actions from a sourced leaf-game story

Sets of objects can be joined, separated or compared as a story unfolds. Physical sets make each change observable and explainable.

leaf set Ajoin / separateleaf set B→new set

Teacher: Use an accurately sourced leaf-game example and follow its stated teaching guidance. Build the sets with permitted classroom materials. Perform one story action and describe how the set changed.

Ask: My model matches the sets and action described in this sourced game example.

Look for: Build one sourced leaf-set situation, perform its action and explain what changed.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Chooses operation from one word — Focus on what physically happens to the collection.
  • Skips the action — Act it out before drawing or recording.
  • Recounts from the wrong start — Keep the starting collection visible until the change is made.
Quick checks and mastery

Quick checks

  • 3 bears are playing. 2 more join. How many now?
  • 8 counters are shown. 3 are removed. How many are left?
  • Is “2 more arrive” a joining or separating action?
  • Draw a story that shows 5 becoming 7.

Mastery

  • Connect story to drawing
