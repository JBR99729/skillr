AC9MFN06: Represent practical situations that involve equal sharing and grouping with...

represent practical situations that involve equal sharing and grouping with physical and virtual materials and use counting or subitising strategies

Topic GuideClassroom ViewWorksheetPracticeTest
🎯 Learn the key ideaStart here

Learning intention: A fair share gives each person the same amount, and equal grouping makes groups with the same number of items.

Success looks like: Make a fair share; Check equal amounts; Make equal groups; Describe groups and group size; Explain an unfair share.

Learning routine: Share one each → Check equal → Count each → Group → Explain → Check

Explain, check and applyReason

Sharing and grouping ask different questions

Share 8 counters equally between 2 mats: find how many on each mat. Then make groups of 2 from 8 counters: find how many groups. The same collection can answer different questions.

Name what the answer counts

Share 8 between 2 mats. Then arrange 8 in groups of 2. What does 4 mean in each case?

Answer and teaching guidance

Sharing: 4 counters on each mat. Grouping: 4 groups, with 2 counters in each. Say the unit with the answer.

Repair an unequal share

One mat has 4 counters and another has 2. Move counters to make equal shares without changing the total.

Answer and teaching guidance

Move one from the group of 4 to the group of 2. Each mat now has 3, and the total stays 6.

Notice a leftover

Share 5 whole counters between 2 mats, one at a time. Can you use all five and give both mats the same number?

Answer and teaching guidance

No. Two on each mat leaves one counter. Moving that last counter between mats does not make equal shares. Keep counters whole; fractions are not required.

Ready to move on

Use 6 counters. Show equal shares between 3 people, then make groups of 3.

Answer and teaching guidance

Each person gets 2 in the sharing task. There are 2 groups of 3 in the grouping task. Check both the number of groups and the number in each.

⚠️ Common mistakes and fixesFix errors
  • Shares in chunks: Deal one item to each recipient in turn.
  • Confuses groups with items per group: Ask both: “How many groups?” and “How many in each?”
  • Does not check equality: Count every share at the end and compare.
✅ Quick checkCheck
  • Share 6 counters equally between 3 people. How many each?
  • Make 2 equal groups from 8 objects. How many in each group?
  • Is 3 counters and 2 counters a fair share? Why?
  • Show another equal grouping for a collection you choose.

🇦🇺 Australian Curriculum coverageE1–E3

Content description: represent practical situations that involve equal sharing and grouping with physical and virtual materials and use counting or subitising strategies

These are curriculum teaching examples used as a breadth check, not separate mandatory outcomes.

First Nations teaching guidance: Use a locally appropriate, community-approved source; name the relevant Peoples and Country/Place; respect cultural authority and permissions; and do not present one example as universal across First Nations Australians.

E1

using materials to role-play equal sharing; for example, sharing pieces of fruit or a bunch of grapes between 4 people and discussing how you would know they have been shared equally; when playing card games where each player is dealt the same number of cards, and counting the number of cards after the deal to ensure they have the same amount

Teaching example: Deal 8 cards to 4 players, one each in turn. Count every share to verify that each player receives 2.

E2

representing situations that involve counting several items; for example, 9 beads or 6 \$1 coins, and sharing them equally between 3 people by subitising or counting each group by ones to decide how many beads or coins each person will receive

Teaching example: Share 9 beads between 3 mats, then separately make groups of 3. Ask what each answer counts: beads per mat or number of groups.

E3

exploring instructive games of First Nations Australians that involve sharing; for example, playing Yangamini of the Tiwi Peoples of Bathurst Island to investigate and discuss equal sharing

Teaching example: Use an authorised explanation of Yangamini from the Tiwi Peoples. Follow the source and permissions when exploring its sharing actions.
