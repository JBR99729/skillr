Elaboration expansion and examples 1

  • E1: using language to describe the measurement attributes of length, mass, capacity and duration, and connecting the words with the appropriate attribute; for example, using words like “tall”, “short”, “wide”, “long”, “high” to describe the attribute of length Learning connection: Choose the attribute you are comparing and use a fair direct comparison before saying longer, shorter, heavier, lighter, holds more or takes longer.

Elaboration expansion and examples 2

  • E2: directly comparing pairs of objects to say which is longer/shorter, and explaining or demonstrating how they know; for example, standing back-to-back to determine who is taller; choosing to line up the bases of a spoon and fork to decide which is longer and explaining why Learning connection: Choose the attribute you are comparing and use a fair direct comparison before saying longer, shorter, heavier, lighter, holds more or takes longer.

Elaboration expansion and examples 3

  • E3: starting 2 events at the same time to decide which takes longer; for example, putting on a pair of sandals with buckles or Velcro, describing the duration using familiar terms and reasoning, “I took a longer time because I’m still learning to do up my buckles” Learning connection: Choose the attribute you are comparing and use a fair direct comparison before saying longer, shorter, heavier, lighter, holds more or takes longer.

Elaboration expansion and examples 4

  • E4: directly comparing pairs of everyday objects from the kitchen pantry to say which is heavier/lighter; for example, hefting a tin of baked beans and a packet of marshmallows; comparing the same pair of objects to say which is longer/shorter and discussing comparisons Learning connection: Choose the attribute you are comparing and use a fair direct comparison before saying longer, shorter, heavier, lighter, holds more or takes longer.

Elaboration expansion and examples 5

  • Use a fair comparison method: LENGTH |──── spoon |──────── fork Line up the same starting point. MASS Heft two objects or place them on a balance. Do not judge only by size. CAPACITY / DURATION Pour between containers to compare capacity. Start two events together to compare duration. Think aloud: Build or point to the model first, explain what each part shows, then state the answer.
  • Say what you are comparing: longer / shorter → length heavier / lighter → mass holds more / less → capacity takes longer / shorter → duration Ask: “What must stay fair in this comparison?”
