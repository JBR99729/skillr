Learn the idea

Build understanding one step at a time

Learning goal: Connect spoken number names, numerals and amounts. Make collections, explore zero, and put numbers in order through at least 20. Open a focus, follow its example, then try the question before checking the answer. An adult can read instructions aloud.

1. Say the name and match the numeral

A numeral is a written sign for a number. The spoken name “fourteen”, the numeral 14 and a collection of fourteen objects can describe the same amount.

Worked example: Match “fourteen” to 14.

  1. Say the number name: fourteen.
  2. Find 14, then make a collection of fourteen counters.
  3. Point to the collection and its numeral: both represent fourteen.

Why it works: Matching all three forms gives the numeral a meaning beyond its appearance.

Watch for: Fourteen and forty sound similar. Use the quantity to check; this lesson works with numbers through 20.

Try it: An adult says “seventeen”. Which numeral matches: 7, 17 or 19?

Check the answer

17. Seventeen names seventeen objects; it is different from seven.

2. See quantities, including zero

Count the named objects. An empty collection has zero objects, written 0. The tray holding the collection is not an extra object.

Worked example: Read fourteen in two ten-frames.

  1. The first frame has ten counters.
  2. The second has four more.
  3. Ten and four make fourteen. Match the model to 14.

Why it works: Organised groups help you see a quantity. You can check by counting every counter once.

Watch for: Moving the counters changes their arrangement, not their number. Adding or removing counters changes the quantity.

Try it: Make an empty tray and a tray with 3 counters. Label each amount.

Check the answer

The empty tray is 0 and the other tray is 3. Count counters, not trays.

3. Make the requested amount

Making a collection means choosing how many to put in it, then stopping at the target. You do not always use everything available.

Worked example: Collect 12 counters from a supply of 20.

  1. Name the target: twelve.
  2. Move one counter for each count word until you reach twelve.
  3. Stop. Check the new collection and label it 12.

Why it works: Each object contributes one to the amount; stopping at twelve makes the requested collection.

Watch for: Taking all twenty does not answer a request for twelve. Keep the supply separate from the collection you made.

Try it: Draw or collect 9 objects from a supply of at least 10.

Check the answer

Any collection with exactly 9 named objects is correct. One object remains if the supply was 10.

4. Follow the number order

When counting forwards by ones, each next number is one more. Counting backwards by ones gives one less. A number line makes the direction visible.

Worked example: Find the neighbours of 14.

  1. Find 14 on the line.
  2. Move one place towards smaller numbers: 13 is immediately before 14.
  3. Move one place towards larger numbers: 15 is immediately after 14.

Why it works: Adjacent positions are consecutive numbers. There is no skipped number between them.

Watch for: “Next” needs a direction. When asked for the next numbers counting backwards from 18, do not repeat 18 as a new answer.

Try it: Count backwards from 18. Say the next three numbers.

Check the answer

17, 16, 15. Each new number is one less than the previous number.

5. Order numbers and describe positions

Order numerals by their values. First, second and third describe positions in a line with a known starting end.

Worked example: Order 12, 5 and 18 from smallest to largest.

  1. Find the smallest number: 5.
  2. Compare the remaining values: 12 comes before 18.
  3. Place them as 5, 12, 18 and check on a number line.

Why it works: Numerical order depends on the amounts, not the printed size of the numeral.

Watch for: For an ordinal position, mark where the line starts. “Third” is ambiguous if the starting end is missing.

Try it: A line starts at the red counter, followed by blue, then green. Which is second?

Check the answer

Blue is second: red is first and green is third. Starting at the marked end makes the position clear.

For teachers and parents: Say number names aloud and let children point, collect or draw. If the task asks them to name a numeral, let them name it before you read it. Handwriting and spelling can be supported separately from number understanding.

Ready to try it independently?

Show 18 with objects, name its numeral and say the numbers immediately before and after it. Explain how you checked.

Use fresh objects or examples. Notice what the child can do independently and what needs another model. A correct choice alone may not show the practical action or reasoning.
