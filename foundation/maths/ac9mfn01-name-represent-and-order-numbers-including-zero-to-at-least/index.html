Numbers to 20

Name, represent and order numbers

Connect real quantities, visual models and written numerals from 0 to 20 in one focused lesson.

Lesson at a glance

25–30 minutes
Learning intention: I can build, see, say, write and order numbers from 0 to 20.
You need

20 counters or craft sticks, numeral cards 0–20 and a ten-frame.

Success looks like

The student connects a quantity, visual model, number name and numeral without guessing.

1. See the numbers

Embedded visual models

Use these models while students build the same quantities with counters or sticks.

One less and one more on a 0–20 number line

Teen numbers: bundle the ten first

10 + 4 = 14 → one ten comes first, then four ones.

2. Teach it in 3 steps

Concrete → Pictorial → Abstract
1

Concrete — hands-on

Give each student 15 counters or craft sticks. Count them, then make one bundle of 10 and leave 5 singles.

Say: “This bundle is one ten. How many ones are left?”

2

Pictorial — see it

Show one full ten-frame and 5 dots in the next frame. Students draw or place the same model.

Say: “I can see 10 without recounting. Five more makes 15.”

3

Abstract — write it

Connect the model to the numeral 15 and the word fifteen. Place 15 correctly on the number line.

Say: “The picture, word and numeral all show the same amount.”

Do not rush: if a student cannot build or explain the model, return to the concrete step before asking for more written numerals.

3. Misconceptions and rapid fixes

Use during the lesson

✕ Writes 41 instead of 14

Why: the student hears “four” before “teen” or has not connected the digits to place value.

Rapid fix: point to the full ten-frame first. Say, “One ten comes first, then four ones.” Rebuild 14, say it, then write it.

✕ Forgets 0 when ordering

Why: the student starts the memorised count at 1 and does not treat an empty collection as a number.

Rapid fix: begin with an empty mat labelled 0. Add the 0 card to the starting point of every number track.

✕ Counts an object twice

Why: counted and uncounted objects are mixed together.

Rapid fix: move each object across a line as its number word is said. Finish with, “There are ___ altogether.”

4. Five-minute warm-up

Zero prep
Game 1

One More / One Less Flashcards

  1. Show any numeral card from 1 to 19.
  2. Point left: students call the number that is one less.
  3. Point right: students call the number that is one more.
  4. Ask one student to prove it on the number line.

Example: show 12 → “One less is 11; one more is 13.”

Game 2

Collect the Quantity

  1. Call a number from 0 to 20.
  2. Students collect that many cubes or counters.
  3. They organise the quantity on ten-frames.
  4. Partners check by naming the full ten first, then the extra ones.

Example: 8 fills eight ten-frame spaces; 14 is one full frame and four more.

Quick check before Practice

2 minutes
BuildShow 14 as 1 ten and 4 ones.
OrderPlace 0, 7, 15 and 20 from least to greatest.
ExplainWhy is 13 one less than 14?

📘 Australian Curriculum description & elaborationsReference

Content description: name, represent and order numbers including zero to at least 20, using physical and virtual materials and numerals.

  • E1: collect a requested quantity or match a numeral to a quantity; for example, collecting 9 paintbrushes after hearing “nine”.
  • E2: recognise number order to 20 and identify one less and one more; use card games, songs, storybooks and rhymes.
  • E3: use ordinal and positional language such as first, second, before, after and between.
  • E4: recognise, write and read numerals on familiar objects, images, texts and labels.
  • E5: connect quantities to number names and numerals through stories, counting games and culturally responsive contexts.
