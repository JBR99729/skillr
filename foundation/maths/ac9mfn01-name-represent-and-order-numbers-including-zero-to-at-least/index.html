What students learn in AC9MFN01

This unit helps students build a clear, usable understanding of Numbers to 20. The goal is not to memorise one answer pattern. Students should be able to explain the idea, recognise it in a new example and apply it in a short practice or worksheet task.

  • Start with the main idea: name, represent and order numbers including zero to at least 20, using physical and virtual materials and numerals.
  • Use concrete examples, pictures, oral explanation and short written responses before moving to independent practice.
  • responding to a request to collect a quantity of objects or reading a numeral and selecting the associated quantity of items from a collection to match the number required; for example, collecting 9 paint brushes after hearing the word ‘nine’ recognising the order in the sequence of numbers to 20 and identifying the number that is “one less” than a given number and the number that is “one more”; for example, playing instructive card games that involve reading and ordering number cards; using counting songs, story books and rhymes to establish the forwards and backwards counting sequence of numbers in the context of active counting activities
  • Finish with mixed questions so students must choose the correct strategy rather than copy the last example.

Learning intentions

  • name, represent and order numbers including zero to at least 20, using physical and virtual materials and numerals
  • responding to a request to collect a quantity of objects or reading a numeral and selecting the associated quantity of items from a collection to match the number required; for example, collecting 9 paint brushes after hearing the word ‘nine’
  • recognising the order in the sequence of numbers to 20 and identifying the number that is “one less” than a given number and the number that is “one more”; for example, playing instructive card games that involve reading and ordering number cards; using counting songs, story books and rhymes to establish the forwards and backwards counting sequence of numbers in the context of active counting activities
  • understanding and using terms such as “first”, “second”, “third”, … “fifth”… to indicate ordinal position in a sequence; for example, creating a number track using cards with the numerals zero to 20 and describing positions using terms such as first, last, before, after, between

Success criteria

  • I can show the skill using objects, pictures, actions or numbers.
  • I can use the key vocabulary accurately.
  • I can answer a new example without copying the previous one.
  • I can explain or demonstrate how I know.

Recommended teaching sequence

  1. 1

    Match spoken number names, numerals and collections.

  2. 2

    Count forwards and backwards with objects and number tracks.

  3. 3

    Find one more, one less, before, after and between.

  4. 4

    Order numeral cards and use ordinal language.

  5. 5

    Read, write and label quantities with numerals.

  6. 6

    Apply the ideas in mixed practical and reasoning tasks.

Hands-on activities

Activity 1

Build numeral–quantity matching trays.

Activity 2

Make a floor number track from 0 to 20.

Activity 3

Hide one card and identify the missing number.

Activity 4

Order classroom collections and explain the order.

Differentiation

Support

Work within 0–5 or 0–10 first; use ten-frames, counters and a visible number track.

Extension

Use teen numbers, backwards counting and more than one possible ordering clue.

Assessment plan

  1. Before teaching: use a few practical prompts to check prior knowledge.
  2. During teaching: select questions from the 56-question Practice bank across multiple response types.
  3. Readiness check: Can the student name, represent, read, write and order numbers to at least 20 without relying on one fixed arrangement?
  4. After teaching: administer the separate 24-question Test bank without previewing its answers with students.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

The content description and elaborations below show the curriculum ideas taught in this unit. Items marked as teaching context support lesson planning but may not appear in the initial eight-question activity.

  • Content description: name, represent and order numbers including zero to at least 20, using physical and virtual materials and numerals
  • E1: responding to a request to collect a quantity of objects or reading a numeral and selecting the associated quantity of items from a collection to match the number required; for example, collecting 9 paint brushes after hearing the word ‘nine’
  • E2: recognising the order in the sequence of numbers to 20 and identifying the number that is “one less” than a given number and the number that is “one more”; for example, playing instructive card games that involve reading and ordering number cards; using counting songs, story books and rhymes to establish the forwards and backwards counting sequence of numbers in the context of active counting activities
  • E3: understanding and using terms such as “first”, “second”, “third”, … “fifth”… to indicate ordinal position in a sequence; for example, creating a number track using cards with the numerals zero to 20 and describing positions using terms such as first, last, before, after, between
  • E4: recognising, writing and reading numerals written on familiar objects; for example, in images, text or illustrations in story books; writing a numeral on a container as a label to show how many objects it contains
  • E5: connecting quantities to number names and numerals when reading and reciting stories and playing counting games or determining and reasoning about the size of sets of objects within First Nation Australians’ instructive games; for example, Segur etug from Mer Island in the Torres Strait region (teaching context)

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Rushing to a rule before checking the concrete model, drawing or number sentence.
  • Using the correct answer once but not being able to explain why it works.
  • Mixing up similar vocabulary such as more/less, before/after, longer/shorter or equal groups/sharing.
