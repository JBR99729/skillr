AC9MFA01 - AC9MFA01: Recognise, copy and continue repeating patterns represented in different ways
Example 1

recognising, copying and describing different repeating patterns using materials, shapes, sounds and movements during activities and play; for example, making a bead necklace and describing the pattern they have created, such as, “red, blue, green, red, blue, green, red, blue, green”; copying repeating patterns of drumbeats or dance moves during music activities Learning connection: A repeating pattern has a smallest unit that repeats again and again in the same order.

Example 2

recognising repeating patterns used at home and in daily activities to help make tasks easier or to solve problems; for example, setting the table to eat Learning connection: A repeating pattern has a smallest unit that repeats again and again in the same order.

Example 3

recognising and discussing repeating patterns in images created using dynamic geometric software or a generative artificial intelligence tool, describing what has been repeated in the pattern Learning connection: A repeating pattern has a smallest unit that repeats again and again in the same order.

Example 4

recognising and describing repeating patterns that can be observed on Country/Place and in First Nation Australians artwork, cultural performances and material cultures; for example, shell and seed necklaces, dances and songs Learning connection: A repeating pattern has a smallest unit that repeats again and again in the same order.
