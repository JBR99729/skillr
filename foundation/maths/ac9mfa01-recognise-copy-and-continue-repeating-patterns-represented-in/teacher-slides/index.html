Elaboration expansion and examples 1

  • E1: recognising, copying and describing different repeating patterns using materials, shapes, sounds and movements during activities and play; for example, making a bead necklace and describing the pattern they have created, such as, “red, blue, green, red, blue, green, red, blue, green”; copying repeating patterns of drumbeats or dance moves during music activities Learning connection: A repeating pattern has a smallest unit that repeats again and again in the same order.
  • E2: recognising repeating patterns used at home and in daily activities to help make tasks easier or to solve problems; for example, setting the table to eat Learning connection: A repeating pattern has a smallest unit that repeats again and again in the same order.

Elaboration expansion and examples 2

  • E3: recognising and discussing repeating patterns in images created using dynamic geometric software or a generative artificial intelligence tool, describing what has been repeated in the pattern Learning connection: A repeating pattern has a smallest unit that repeats again and again in the same order.
  • E4: recognising and describing repeating patterns that can be observed on Country/Place and in First Nation Australians artwork, cultural performances and material cultures; for example, shell and seed necklaces, dances and songs Learning connection: A repeating pattern has a smallest unit that repeats again and again in the same order.

Elaboration expansion and examples 3

  • Find the repeating unit: SEE 🔴 🔵 🔴 🔵 🔴 🔵 Something repeats. FIND THE CORE [🔴 🔵] [🔴 🔵] [🔴 🔵] The smallest repeating unit is red-blue. CONTINUE 🔴 🔵 🔴 🔵 ___ ___ Next: red, blue. Think aloud: Build or point to the model first, explain what each part shows, then state the answer.
  • Same pattern, new representation: 🔴 🔵 🔴 🔵 → clap, stamp, clap, stamp The objects changed, but the repeating structure stayed AB.
