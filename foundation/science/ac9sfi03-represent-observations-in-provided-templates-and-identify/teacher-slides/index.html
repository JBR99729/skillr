Elaboration expansion and examples 1

  • E1: using provided tables or graphic organisers to sort images or models of plants and animals into groups based on external features Learning connection: We can record observations using pictures, marks, words or simple tables. Looking across the records can help us notice a pattern.
  • E2: collaborating to create a floor or wall display to link images or samples of materials to images of observed objects Learning connection: We can record observations using pictures, marks, words or simple tables. Looking across the records can help us notice a pattern.

Elaboration expansion and examples 2

  • E3: identifying common features of familiar groups of animals, such as fish, birds or reptiles Learning connection: We can record observations using pictures, marks, words or simple tables. Looking across the records can help us notice a pattern.
  • E4: identifying patterns of movement of objects, with guidance, such as that balls roll easily in a straight line when pushed, or toy cars move in certain ways because of their wheels Learning connection: We can record observations using pictures, marks, words or simple tables. Looking across the records can help us notice a pattern.

Elaboration expansion and examples 3

  • E5: identifying patterns in uses of everyday objects made of similar materials, such as wood, plastic, metal or glass Learning connection: We can record observations using pictures, marks, words or simple tables. Looking across the records can help us notice a pattern.
  • Record observations so they can be compared: Day 1: 2 leaves Day 3: 3 leaves Day 5: 4 leaves Pattern: the number of leaves increased. Think aloud: Describe what is observed first, then use that evidence to explain the idea.
  • A pattern must come from the records: Point to the observations that support the pattern. Do not choose a pattern just because it sounds likely.
