What students learn in AC9SFI01

This unit helps students build a clear, usable understanding of Questions and make predictions based on experiences. The goal is not to memorise one answer pattern. Students should be able to explain the idea, recognise it in a new example and apply it in a short practice or worksheet task.

  • Start with the main idea: pose questions and make predictions based on experiences.
  • Use concrete examples, pictures, oral explanation and short written responses before moving to independent practice.
  • posing questions based on experiences, such as: ‘What part of a plant is broccoli?’ or ‘How high do balls bounce?’ posing questions about everyday objects and the materials that they may be made of
  • Finish with mixed questions so students must choose the correct strategy rather than copy the last example.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

The content description and elaborations below show the curriculum ideas taught in this unit. Items marked as teaching context support lesson planning but may not appear in the initial eight-question activity.

  • Content description: pose questions and make predictions based on experiences
  • E1: posing questions based on experiences, such as: ‘What part of a plant is broccoli?’ or ‘How high do balls bounce?’
  • E2: posing questions about everyday objects and the materials that they may be made of
  • E3: making predictions before field work, such as which plants and animals they may observe in the school grounds
  • E4: making predictions about how an unusually shaped object such as an egg or a hexagonal block might move down a slope

How to use this unit

Read the topic guide, use the teacher slide for instruction, then complete the Worksheet, Practice and Test. These three activities use the same eight-question unit bank.

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Describing what is seen without connecting it to a question, pattern or prediction.
  • Using everyday words only and missing simple scientific vocabulary.
  • Changing more than one thing in an investigation, which makes the comparison unfair.
