FOUNDATION SCIENCE · AC9SFI02

Look closely. Investigate safely.

Follow instructions, use tools carefully and make observations with safe senses.

More examples ↓Help with gaps ↓10-minute activity ↓

WORKED EXAMPLE

Which observations are safe?

With adult guidance: look at the leaf, use a magnifying glass and draw the lines you see.
Do not: taste the leaf, touch an unfamiliar plant or look at the Sun through the magnifying glass.

Choose senses and tools that are safe for this investigation. You do not need to use every sense to make a useful observation.

Learn the skill

1. Listen before starting

Check the instructions, boundaries and equipment with an adult. Walk with tools. Outdoors, follow sun protection and leave living things undisturbed.

Stop and ask

You find an unfamiliar berry on a nature walk.

StopAsk
Unfamiliar berryTell the adult; leave it alone
Try it · Should you taste it to investigate?

No. Leave it alone and tell the adult. Do not taste investigation materials.

2. Match the sense to the evidence

Sight helps with colour, shape and movement. Hearing helps with sounds. Only touch or smell when an adult says it is safe; do not bring unknown substances to your nose.

Two safe observations

An adult provides a safe block. You see a brown surface and, with permission, feel a rough edge.

EvidenceSense
BrownSight
Rough edgePermitted touch
Try it · Which sense tells you the edge is rough?

Touch. Seeing brown does not tell you how the edge feels.

3. Use a tool and record

Tools can make details easier to observe. Use them as directed, then draw, count, dictate or photograph what you actually noticed.

Choose a safe tool

A bird is far away. An adult helps you use binoculars from a safe place.

Try it · Do you need to approach or touch the bird?

No. Observe without disturbing it. Never point binoculars towards the Sun.

Common mistakes

“Science means using all five senses.” Choose only safe, permitted senses. Do not taste investigation materials.

“I can handle equipment before hearing instructions.” Wait, listen and ask if unsure.

10-MINUTE ACTIVITY

Safe sense detectives

2 minutes An adult checks a block and fabric scrap and explains safe handling. 5 minutes Look and, when permitted, gently feel. 3 minutes Draw or dictate two observations and name the sense used.

Adjust the challenge Support: choose sight or touch for each observation. Extend: use a magnifying glass and add a detail that becomes easier to see.

No tasting. Keep tools away from eyes and sunlight. Stop and ask when unsure.

Three tricky questions

1. A berry looks familiar. Is that enough reason to taste it during an investigation?

No. Do not taste investigation materials. Leave it alone and ask the adult.

2. Can you tell that cloth feels soft just because it is blue?

No. Colour is a sight observation. Touch needs permission and provides different evidence.

3. You want a closer view of a bird. Must you go closer?

No. An adult can help you use binoculars safely from a suitable place. Do not disturb the bird.

FAQ

Can we use smell?

Only when the adult explicitly approves and explains the method. Do not smell unknown substances.

What counts as a record?

An accurate drawing, number, dots, dictated words, photograph or voice recording can record an observation.

🎯 Learn the key ideaStart here

Learning intention: Foundation scientists follow instructions, use equipment carefully and make safe observations with senses such as sight, hearing, touch and smell when a teacher says it is safe.

Success looks like: Follow safety instructions; Use senses safely; Handle simple tools; Describe observations; Ask when unsure.

Learning routine: Listen → Check safety → Investigate → Observe → Report

Explain, check and applyReason

Choose safe observations

Follow the adult’s instructions before handling objects or tools. Use sight and hearing at a safe distance; touch or smell only as directed. Do not taste investigation materials. Stop and ask when unsure.

Make a safe decision

You find an unfamiliar berry during a plant walk. A friend suggests tasting it to learn about it. What should you do instead?

Answer and teaching guidance

Do not taste it. Leave it alone and tell the adult. You can describe its visible colour and shape from a safe distance when directed.

Match a sense to evidence

An adult provides a safe wooden block. You see a brown surface and, when allowed to touch it, feel a rough edge. Which sense supports each observation?

Answer and teaching guidance

Sight supports brown; touch supports rough. Name the sense that supplied the evidence. Colour alone does not tell you how the surface feels.

Use a tool without causing harm

You want to see a leaf’s tiny lines with a magnifying glass. How should you use it?

Answer and teaching guidance

Follow adult guidance, carry it carefully and observe the leaf without damaging it. Never look at the Sun through it or focus sunlight onto objects or skin.

Ready to move on

With an adult-approved object, state one safety instruction, make two safe observations and record one using a drawing, marks or words.

Answer and teaching guidance

Accept observations actually made. Check that the child follows instructions and asks before using an uncertain sense or tool. A detailed observation does not need a long written answer.

⚠️ Common mistakes and fixesFix errors
  • Using every sense automatically: Only use a sense when the activity instructions say it is safe.
  • Touching before listening: Wait for instructions before handling materials or equipment.
  • Observation without evidence: Say what you actually noticed with a safe sense.

🇦🇺 Australian Curriculum coverageE1–E4

Content description: engage in investigations safely and make observations using their senses

These are curriculum teaching examples used as a breadth check, not separate mandatory outcomes.

E1

discussing ways to conduct investigations safely, such as by being sun safe, not running with equipment, not tasting objects or materials, and following teacher instructions

Teaching example: Agree on boundaries, sun protection, careful walking with equipment and no tasting of investigation materials before an outdoor activity.

E2

explaining safety considerations for using the senses of touch, smell, sight and hearing, and discussing why we do not use taste to make observations in science

Teaching example: Discuss an unfamiliar berry: observe from a safe distance, do not taste or touch it, and ask an adult. Choose senses according to the instructions.

E3

using provided tools such as binoculars, magnifying glasses, digital photography or video to enhance their observations of plants and animals

Teaching example: Use a magnifying glass on a leaf with adult guidance, or binoculars for a distant bird. Never look at the Sun through optical tools.

E4

recording observations using numbers, dots, drawings, voice recordings, digital photography or video

Teaching example: Record an actual observation with a labelled drawing, dots or an adult-assisted voice recording. Say which safe sense provided the information.
