Elaboration expansion and examples 1

  • E1: revisiting their predictions and with guidance identifying whether their predictions matched their observations Learning connection: After an investigation, compare the prediction with the observation. A different result is useful because it helps us update our thinking.
  • E2: comparing, with guidance, observations of plants or animals made during field work with their predictions Learning connection: After an investigation, compare the prediction with the observation. A different result is useful because it helps us update our thinking.

Elaboration expansion and examples 2

  • E3: using a provided table to draw or dictate their prediction and their observation and identifying whether they are the same or different Learning connection: After an investigation, compare the prediction with the observation. A different result is useful because it helps us update our thinking.
  • Prediction and observation have different jobs: Prediction: “The large marble will roll farther.” Observation: “The small marble rolled farther.” Compare: The observation was different from the prediction. Think aloud: Describe what is observed first, then use that evidence to explain the idea.
  • Use evidence, not embarrassment: We do not change the prediction after the test. We keep it, record the observation and explain whether they matched.

Assessment-style questions and review hints 1

Questions

  • What do we compare after an investigation?
  • Can a prediction be different from the observation?

Review hints

  • Main idea: After an investigation, compare the prediction with the observation. A different result is useful because it helps us update our thinking.
  • Useful routine: Recall prediction → Observe result → Compare → Say same/different → Learn
