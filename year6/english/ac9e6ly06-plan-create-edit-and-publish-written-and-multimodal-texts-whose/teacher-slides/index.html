CurriculumStart here

Students learn to plan, create, edit and publish written and multimodal texts whose purposes may be imaginative, informative and persuasive, using paragraphs, a variety of complex sentences, expanded verb groups, tense, topic-specific and vivid vocabulary, punctuation, spelling and visual features. They move from recognising the feature to explaining how it works in context, then applying it deliberately in their own reading, speaking, writing or multimodal creation.

Core idea: In English, choices in language, structure, literature and mode create meaning for a particular audience and purpose. A strong answer names the choice, gives evidence and explains the effect.

Success looks like

  • identify the relevant feature, strategy or text choice
  • use precise English metalanguage
  • support the answer with specific evidence
  • explain the effect in context
  • apply or revise the choice for a new audience and purpose

Assessment and reviewReady for practice
  • Naming the feature is enough — Explain how it changes meaning, clarity, emphasis or correctness.
  • Rules used mechanically — Check the sentence structure and intended meaning before applying a rule.
  • Longer wording is better — Prefer precise wording that suits the audience and purpose.
  • No proofreading evidence — Read the sentence aloud and check grammar, punctuation, spelling and meaning together.
