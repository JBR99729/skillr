What students learn in AC9S6I01

Students convert observations into focused variable questions, select measurable outcomes and write directional predictions grounded in prior evidence or a scientific model.

Learning routine: Observe → Model → Investigate → Analyse → Explain → Evaluate

Success looks like

  • Represent or identify the concept
  • Explain the underlying relationship
  • Select an appropriate strategy or feature
  • Apply it in a new context
  • Justify and verify the response

A prediction is recorded before evidence collection and evaluated afterward; it is not rewritten to match the result.

  1. Identify the system, variables, evidence or quantities shown.
  2. Explain the relationship represented by the model.
  3. Check whether the conclusion is supported and stays within the evidence.

Improve questions that cannot be tested fairly

weak questionimproved questionWhich circuit is best?How does lamp arrangement affect brightness with the same cells?Do plants like shade?How does light level affect growth rate?Does everything dissolve?How does water temperature affect dissolving time for equal samples?

Words such as best need criteria. Complex systems may require observational studies rather than simple fair tests.

Apply the same reasoning to an unfamiliar case, then identify limitations or a useful next investigation.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Content description: pose investigable questions to identify patterns and test relationships and make reasoned predictions

  • E1: posing investigable questions to identify patterns, such as: ‘What type of material is the best conductor and what is the best insulator?’
  • E2: posing investigable questions to test relationships, such as: ‘Will more salt dissolve in warm water than in cold water?’
  • E3: discussing and refining questions to enable scientific investigation
  • E4: making reasoned predictions about the physical conditions that will result in the largest mould colonies growing on bread
  • E5: making reasoned predictions about electrical circuit function based on a picture or diagram of a circuit

Avoid this trap
  • Question asks for preference — Define a measurable relationship.
  • Prediction lacks direction — State increase, decrease or difference.
  • Several variables change together — Focus or use a design that can separate them.
  • Conclusion written as prediction — Keep stages distinct.
