Concept modelTeach from the board

Compare group results and method differences

groupmean risemethod noteA8.2°Clamp 20 cm; 3 trialsB8.5°Clamp 20 cm; 3 trialsC12.9°Clamp 10 cm; 1 trialevaluationA/B comparableC method differs

A different result is not automatically wrong. Group C’s changed distance and single trial provide plausible reasons for the discrepancy.

  1. Identify the system, variables, evidence or quantities shown.
  2. Explain the relationship represented by the model.
  3. Check whether the conclusion is supported and stays within the evidence.

Use an evidence evaluation sequence

compare question→compare variables/controls→inspect equipment and precision→compare raw data→identify error/variation→decide repeat/combine→conclude cautiously→pose next question

Systematic error is not removed by averaging repeated biased measurements; random variation can be described through spread and repetitions.

Apply the same reasoning to an unfamiliar case, then identify limitations or a useful next investigation.

Elaboration examplesCopied content

Content description: compare methods and findings with those of others, recognise possible sources of error, pose questions for further investigation and select evidence to draw reasoned conclusions

  • E1: working collaboratively to identify the strengths and weaknesses of their own and others’ investigations including where testing was not fair and practices could be improved
  • E2: recognising errors that could have occurred during investigations, including changing too many variables, incorrect or misreading of measurements, or changes in environmental factors
  • E3: comparing and contrasting data collected by different individuals or groups to discuss similarities and differences in their findings and posing questions about differences for further investigation
  • E4: comparing and contrasting evidence selected by different individuals or groups from similar data
  • E5: evaluating the inferences made from observations and analysis of the data to draw a reasoned conclusion

  • Pose next question. Answer with evidence from a model, observation or fair comparison.
Assessment and reviewReady for practice
  • Different finding equals misconduct — Check method and variation.
  • Outlier removed because inconvenient — Investigate first.
  • More repeats fix calibration bias — Systematic error remains.
  • Conclusion ignores scope — State conditions and limitations.
