What students learn in AC9S6I06

Students organise reports, explanations and presentations around purpose, use precise scientific vocabulary, integrate labelled representations and adapt detail without altering evidence or overstating conclusions.

Learning routine: Observe → Model → Investigate → Analyse → Explain → Evaluate

Success looks like

  • Represent or identify the concept
  • Explain the underlying relationship
  • Select an appropriate strategy or feature
  • Apply it in a new context
  • Justify and verify the response

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Content description: write and create texts to communicate ideas and findings for specific purposes and audiences, including selection of language features, using digital tools as appropriate

  • E1: constructing a scientific report to share findings, such as how plants responded to changes in physical conditions such as temperature or salinity, and using appropriate vocabulary, data representations, units and sentence structures
  • E2: creating an imaginative text about a future in which humans live on other planets with differing day lengths, and how they manage the social implications of this, such as keeping track of time or calculating human ages
  • E3: acknowledging and exploring First Nations Australians’ ways of representing and communicating understandings of the night sky and its use for timekeeping purposes through rock paintings, paintings and stone arrangements
  • E4: designing a product that uses electrical circuits and performing a sales pitch to have the product mass produced
  • E5: constructing a poster or slideshow comparing everyday examples of reversible and irreversible change

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Results displayed without interpretation — State the pattern and relevance.
  • Conclusion repeats prediction only — Use actual evidence.
  • Audience adaptation changes facts — Preserve accuracy.
  • Sources and image/data origins omitted — Attribute external material.
  • Slide becomes a paragraph wall — Use models and concise cues.
