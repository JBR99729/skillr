What students learn in AC9S6U04

Students distinguish physical changes that can be reversed by practical processes from changes producing new substances, using observations such as gas formation, colour change, temperature change and new properties cautiously.

Learning routine: Observe → Model → Investigate → Analyse → Explain → Evaluate

Success looks like

  • Represent or identify the concept
  • Explain the underlying relationship
  • Select an appropriate strategy or feature
  • Apply it in a new context
  • Justify and verify the response

Reversible means the original material can be recovered under suitable conditions; it does not mean the change reverses by itself or without energy.

  1. Identify the system, variables, evidence or quantities shown.
  2. Explain the relationship represented by the model.
  3. Check whether the conclusion is supported and stays within the evidence.

Use evidence without overclaiming

bubblesmay indicate gas formation, but boiling also bubbles
colour changemay support new substance, but mixing colours is physical
temperature changemay indicate energy change, but heating source matters
new propertiesstrong evidence when combined with controlled observations
separationfiltration/evaporation can recover components of some mixtures

Use several observations and controls. A single sign is rarely enough to identify a chemical change conclusively.

Apply the same reasoning to an unfamiliar case, then identify limitations or a useful next investigation.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Content description: compare reversible changes, including dissolving and changes of state, and irreversible changes, including cooking and rusting that produce new substances

  • E1: discussing what makes a change reversible or irreversible, using everyday examples
  • E2: examining the substances produced in cooking and rusting and comparing them with the original substances
  • E3: comparing how the amount of heat energy added affects whether a change in state or an irreversible change occurs
  • E4: describing how dissolved substances are reclaimed from solutions
  • E5: exploring how reversible changes can be used to recycle materials
  • E6: investigating First Nations Australians’ knowledges of reversible processes such as the application of adhesives and of irreversible processes such as the use of fuels for torches

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Dissolved material disappears — Particles remain and can often be recovered.
  • All bubbles prove chemical change — Boiling and trapped air can produce bubbles.
  • Reversible means no energy involved — Melting and freezing exchange energy.
  • Irreversible means impossible under any technology — At this level, focus on practical recovery under ordinary conditions.
