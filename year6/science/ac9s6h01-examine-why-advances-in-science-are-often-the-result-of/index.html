Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Content description: examine why advances in science are often the result of collaboration or build on the work of others

  • E1: investigating how contemporary restorative ecology adapts and builds on the traditional ecological knowledges of First Nations Australians
  • E2: exploring how international scientific collaboration can answer complex questions about the abiotic factors that affect the growth and survival of living things in Antarctica
  • E3: examining why ecologists collaborate with engineers and computer scientists to develop remote sensing techniques, identify patterns in habitat change and make predictions
  • E4: constructing a timeline to show how contributions and collaboration of scientists, mathematicians and astronomers from many countries have advanced our ideas about space and the solar system through development of models, gathering of evidence and, more recently, space exploration
  • E5: investigating how astronauts and scientists from many different countries have collaborated in the International Space Station program
  • E6: investigating why scientists changed the phosphate levels in detergents to prevent algal blooms

Avoid this trap
  • Advance credited to one visible person only — Identify teams and prior contributions.
  • New technology assumed automatically accurate — Validate and calibrate it.
  • Collaboration means agreement without critique — Constructive challenge improves reliability.
  • More data always better — Data quality and relevance matter.
