What students learn in AC9S6H02

Students explain what science can inform, identify stakeholder values and constraints and evaluate benefits, risks, uncertainty and unintended consequences before recommending action.

Learning routine: Observe → Model → Investigate → Analyse → Explain → Evaluate

Success looks like

  • Represent or identify the concept
  • Explain the underlying relationship
  • Select an appropriate strategy or feature
  • Apply it in a new context
  • Justify and verify the response
Concept models and worked thinking

Use evidence in a community decision

Science describes likely effects and uncertainty; communities also make value judgements about fairness, priority and acceptable risk.

  1. Identify the system, variables, evidence or quantities shown.
  2. Explain the relationship represented by the model.
  3. Check whether the conclusion is supported and stays within the evidence.

Monitor decisions and adapt

define problem→gather reliable evidence→identify stakeholders→compare options→decide transparently→monitor outcomes→respond to unintended effects

A responsible decision includes measures of success and a plan to revise action if evidence changes.

Apply the same reasoning to an unfamiliar case, then identify limitations or a useful next investigation.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Content description: investigate how scientific knowledge is used by individuals and communities to identify problems, consider responses and make decisions

  • E1: exploring how communities consider the impact of aquatic noise pollution when designing guidelines for water sports
  • E2: investigating how people use knowledge of conditions that reduce mould or bacterial growth when considering food packaging and storage
  • E3: considering how people use electrical device guidelines to help ensure safety of children
  • E4: investigating why underground power cables were developed and how local government authorities use scientific knowledge about power safety when considering converting to underground power

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Science supplies one value-free answer — Criteria and values also shape decisions.
  • Most popular option assumed scientifically best — Popularity is not evidence of effectiveness.
  • Uncertainty used as reason to ignore all evidence — Decisions can state uncertainty and monitor.
  • Only immediate effects considered — Include long-term and indirect consequences.
