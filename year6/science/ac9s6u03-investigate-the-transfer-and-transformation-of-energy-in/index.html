What students learn in AC9S6U03

Students construct safe low-voltage circuits, distinguish energy transfer from current flow language, trace transformations and investigate how open paths, component number and series or parallel arrangements affect operation.

Learning routine: Observe → Model → Investigate → Analyse → Explain → Evaluate

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Content description: investigate the transfer and transformation of energy in electrical circuits, including the role of circuit components, insulators and conductors

  • E1: identifying necessary components for an electric circuit such as a source of electrical energy and conducting material such as metal wires
  • E2: constructing a real or virtual circuit to examine requirements to allow the flow of electricity, including exploring the construction and role of switches
  • E3: constructing representations of electrical circuits and their components using accepted conventions
  • E4: examining the purpose of different components such as switches and bulbs and exploring use of ammeters to measure current
  • E5: investigating different electrical conductors and insulators and examining why they may be used
  • E6: exploring how electricity is used in the home and identifying electrical hazards and safety measures used to mitigate these hazards

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Current is used up by the lamp — Current is not a fuel; energy is transferred at components.
  • Open switch still completes circuit — A gap stops the closed path.
  • Series and parallel judged from page appearance — Trace actual connection points and paths.
  • More cells always safe — Respect component ratings and classroom limits.
