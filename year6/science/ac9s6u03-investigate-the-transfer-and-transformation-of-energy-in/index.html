YEAR 6 SCIENCE · AC9S6U03

Trace a complete circuit and its energy transfers

Learning intention: Explain component roles, energy transfer and transformation, and the evidence for conductors and insulators in a simple circuit.

WORKED EXAMPLE

A lamp needs a continuous conducting path

The labelled model connects one cell, a closed switch and a suitable intact incandescent lamp. A conducting path runs from one cell terminal through the lamp filament to the other terminal. The cell supplies energy; the lamp transforms transferred electrical energy into light and heating. The model is not a wiring instruction for household electricity.

ComponentRole
CellSupplies energy through chemical processes
Conducting wiresProvide connections in the circuit
SwitchCompletes or breaks the intended path
Incandescent lampProduces light and heating when the suitable circuit operates

Illustrations provide context. Use the supplied labelled records and models for exact values; do not measure the artwork.

Learn the skill

1. Check the actual connections

Trace the loop through each component and both cell terminals. Nearby objects or wires touching only plastic coating do not establish a conducting connection. An open switch breaks this simple path.

A diagnostic question

If the lamp does not light, inspect the path, contacts, cell and lamp. A gap is one possible cause, not the only explanation.

Try it · If both leads connect to one lamp contact and the other contact is unconnected, is there a path through the filament?

No. The intended circuit must connect through the filament between the lamp’s two contacts. Do not test a shorted arrangement physically.

2. Separate energy from charge

Energy is transferred from the cell to components. A lamp gives light and heating; a motor can produce movement and heating; a buzzer can produce sound and heating. Charge is not used up by a lamp.

An energy chain

Stored chemical energy → electrical transfer in the circuit → light and heating at the lamp. A simple loop model does not explain every microscopic detail.

Try it · Does a switch create a fresh supply of energy when it closes?

No. It completes the intended conducting path. The source supplies energy; the switch controls the connection.

3. Test materials and interpret the evidence

Check a working reference circuit before replacing one link with a test sample. Keep contacts, cell and lamp the same. A metal core can conduct while dry plastic coating provides insulation under ordinary intended conditions.

Hypothetical observationSupported interpretation
Clean copper link: lamp lightsSuitable conductor in this setup
Dry plastic link: lamp does not lightNot a suitable conducting link in this setup
Reference also stops workingApparatus or contact problem needs checking
Try it · Does a lamp failing to light prove a material has zero conductivity in every situation?

No. Poor contacts, failed equipment or the sensitivity of the setup can affect the result. Limit the claim to the tested conditions.

Common mistakes

“The lamp uses up the current.” The lamp transforms energy; charge is not consumed by the component.

“Low voltage means there is no hazard.” Use only teacher-selected matched school equipment. Short circuits, damaged cells and hot components can still be hazardous.

10-MINUTE ACTIVITY

Explain and diagnose a circuit model

2 minutes: trace the supplied closed loop. 4 minutes: explain each component’s role and one energy transformation. 2 minutes: identify what opening the switch changes. 2 minutes: suggest a reference check for a material test.

Adjust the challenge Support: follow source → connections → lamp → return. Extend: distinguish a broken circuit from an intact circuit with a depleted cell.

Paper analysis is sufficient. Any practical must use teacher-approved matched school cells and components; never mains, damaged batteries, deliberate short circuits or live household wiring.

Three tricky questions

1. Can an intact incandescent lamp usually operate with the cell reversed?

Yes, with suitable matched equipment. Do not generalise this to LEDs or every electronic component, which may need a particular polarity and protection.

2. Why can a wire have both metal and plastic?

The metal core provides a conducting path, while the dry insulating covering helps keep unintended contacts apart in its designed use.

3. If a test lamp is off, why restore the reference link?

It checks whether the circuit still works. A failed reference weakens a claim that the test material alone caused the result.

FAQ

Does a cell contain a fixed supply of current to pour out?

No. It supplies energy through chemical processes. Current describes charge flow when a suitable circuit operates.

Must we calculate current or resistance here?

No. Focus on conducting paths, component functions, materials and qualitative energy transfers.

What students learn in AC9S6U03

Learning intention: Build and diagnose complete low-voltage circuits and explain how components transfer and transform energy.

Curriculum focus: Investigate the transfer and transformation of energy in electrical circuits, including the role of circuit components, insulators and conductors.

Success criteria

  • trace an unbroken conducting path
  • explain the roles of cells, switches, wires and loads
  • distinguish conductors from insulators and identify energy transformations
Key vocabulary
circuit
a complete path through which electric current can flow
cell
a component that supplies energy to a circuit
load
a component such as a lamp, buzzer or motor that transforms electrical energy
conductor
a material that permits current under the tested conditions
insulator
a material used to resist current and separate or cover conductors
energy transformation
a change from one energy form to another
Investigation and reasoning routine
  1. Trace the path from one cell terminal back to the other.
  2. Check every connection and switch position.
  3. Name each component’s function.
  4. Identify the input energy and observable outputs.
  5. Change one factor and repeat to gather evidence.

complete circuit

Task: A cell, closed switch and lamp are joined by metal wires in one loop. What must be true for the device to operate?

Model reasoning: There must be an unbroken conducting loop from one cell terminal through the lamp to the other terminal. A working simple circuit needs an unbroken conducting path.

component function

Task: A cell, closed switch and lamp are joined by metal wires in one loop. What is the main role of the cell?

Model reasoning: The cell supplies energy to the circuit.

energy transformation

Task: A cell, closed switch and lamp are joined by metal wires in one loop. Which energy transformation occurs in the load?

Model reasoning: Electrical energy is transformed mainly into light and thermal energy. The cell supplies energy that the load changes into observable forms.

conductors and insulators

Task: A lamp lead needs a safe flexible wire. Which design choice is best and why?

Model reasoning: Metal provides the conducting path while plastic reduces contact with the conductor.

diagnose evidence

Task: The lamp turns off when one wire is lifted from its terminal. What is the best first explanation?

Model reasoning: The observation directly shows that breaking the loop stops operation.

fair investigation

Task: Students compare copper and plastic strips in the gap of the same lamp circuit. Which method gives the fairest evidence?

Model reasoning: Keep the cell, lamp, strip dimensions and contacts the same; change only the material and repeat trials. Changing one factor and repeating the test makes the comparison more dependable.

Curriculum coverage and concept boundary

Complete paths

A lamp, buzzer or motor works only when its connections form an unbroken conducting loop. An open switch or loose connection creates a gap.

Component roles

The cell supplies energy, wires connect components, the switch controls the path and a load transforms electrical energy to light, sound, motion and thermal energy. Current is not simply used up at the load.

Materials and safety

Metals commonly form conducting cores and contacts. Plastics and rubber commonly provide insulating covers. Material claims apply to the tested conditions, and only safe low-voltage classroom circuits should be investigated.

Evidence and diagnosis

A device being off has competing explanations: open path, flat cell, failed load, unsuitable material or poor contact. Change one factor at a time before deciding.

Important questions and answers
  • Does a battery contain current? A cell stores chemical energy and creates conditions for current in a complete circuit; current is not a substance stored inside.
  • Why does an open switch stop a lamp? It creates a gap, so there is no complete conducting path.
  • Is plastic always an insulator? Use material claims in context; ordinary plastic is chosen as an insulator in simple low-voltage circuits.
Assessment-style questions and review hints
  • A lamp circuit has a closed switch but one wire touches the plastic coating instead of the metal core. Why is the lamp off? Hint: Inspect material at each connection.
  • A wind turbine charges a cell that later powers a buzzer. Which chain is best? Hint: Name each device’s input and output.
  • What changes when a simple switch opens? Hint: Think path, not electricity creation.
  • A steel spoon and aluminium foil light a tester lamp; wood and rubber do not. What is supported? Hint: Report what the circuit showed without absolutes.
  • One lamp in a two-branch circuit is removed and the other remains lit. Why? Hint: Trace each branch independently.
  • Why do both lamps stop when one is removed from a one-loop circuit? Hint: Trace the single path.
Support, core and extend
  • Support: highlight the changed feature, observation and comparison before writing a claim.
  • Core: solve an unseen context and justify the answer with precise evidence.
  • Extend: evaluate a limitation, competing explanation or misleading model while staying within Year 6 science.
Exit ticket and mastery evidence

For a cell-switch-motor circuit, trace the path, name each role, state the energy transformation and diagnose one possible fault.

Evidence of mastery: The student explains operation using path continuity and energy transfer, selects conductor/insulator roles safely and proposes a fair diagnostic test.
