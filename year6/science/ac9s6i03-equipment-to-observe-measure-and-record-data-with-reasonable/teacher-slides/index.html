A digital display can show many decimals without the method being equally accurate. Precision should reflect the weakest part of measurement.

  1. Identify the system, variables, evidence or quantities shown.
  2. Explain the relationship represented by the model.
  3. Check whether the conclusion is supported and stays within the evidence.

Elaboration examplesCopied content

Content description: use equipment to observe, measure and record data with reasonable precision, using digital tools as appropriate

  • E1: selecting and using instruments with the correct scale for measuring data with appropriate accuracy, such as a multimeter
  • E2: recording data in tables and diagrams or electronically as digital images and spreadsheets
  • E3: discussing why precision is important in measurement, and the possible effect of low precision on investigation findings
  • E4: recording data using standard units, such as volt, ampere, gram, second and metre, and developing the use of standard prefixes for metric units such as kilo- and milli-
  • E5: using digital tools such as digital thermometers or soil moisture probes to collect data over time and record data in spreadsheets

Assessment and reviewReady for practice
  • More decimal places means accuracy — Display resolution may exceed method quality.
  • One reading accepted — Repeat where variation is plausible.
  • Outlier deleted automatically — Investigate and document.
  • Precision and accuracy treated as synonyms — They describe different qualities.
