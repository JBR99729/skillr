What students learn in AC9S6I04

Students select representations by variable type, show raw and processed data transparently, use labels and scales and distinguish relationships visible in a graph from causal explanations.

Learning routine: Observe → Model → Investigate → Analyse → Explain → Evaluate

Success looks like

  • Represent or identify the concept
  • Explain the underlying relationship
  • Select an appropriate strategy or feature
  • Apply it in a new context
  • Justify and verify the response
Concept models and worked thinking

Match evidence to representation

evidence purposerepresentationexact repeated measurementsraw-data tablechange through timeline graphcompare categoriescolumn graphrelationship between two numbersscatter-style plotsystem or processlabelled model/flow diagram

Processed values such as means should be traceable to raw measurements and calculation rules.

  1. Identify the system, variables, evidence or quantities shown.
  2. Explain the relationship represented by the model.
  3. Check whether the conclusion is supported and stays within the evidence.

Construct a cautious pattern statement

PatternTemperature rise decreased as lamp distance increased
→
Data12.1°C at 10 cm; 6.8°C at 20 cm; 3.2°C at 30 cm
→
Scopesame lamp, materials and 10-minute period
→
Limitationfew distances and possible room-light effects

A graph can show association. A causal explanation also requires design, controls and scientific reasoning.

Apply the same reasoning to an unfamiliar case, then identify limitations or a useful next investigation.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Content description: construct and use appropriate representations, including tables, graphs and visual or physical models, to organise and process data and information and describe patterns, trends and relationships

  • E1: exploring how different representations can be used to show different aspects of relationships, processes and trends
  • E2: representing circuits using virtual simulations or circuit diagrams and indicating the direction of electricity flow
  • E3: using line graphs to show changes in growth over time under different physical conditions
  • E4: developing a physical model of the sun and Earth using objects or role-play to describe their relative positions when a place on Earth is in day or night
  • E5: organising information in graphic organisers to describe patterns and trends

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Graph type chosen by appearance — Match variables and purpose.
  • Axis starts or intervals conceal change — Use clear scales and disclose breaks.
  • Mean shown without raw evidence — Retain source data.
  • Trend stated as proof of cause — Consider investigation design.
