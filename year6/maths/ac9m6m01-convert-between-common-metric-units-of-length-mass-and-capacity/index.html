YEAR 6 MATHS · AC9M6M01

Convert metric units

Convert common metric units of length, mass and capacity and choose useful decimal representations.

Practice on app ↗Help with gaps ↓10-minute activity ↓

WORKED EXAMPLE

Convert metres to centimetres

A smaller unit takes more units to describe the same length.

Learn the skill

1. Identify the unit relationship

Write how many smaller units make one larger unit.

Try it · How many grams make 1 kg?

1,000 g.

2. Convert to a smaller unit

Multiply by the number of smaller units in one larger unit.

Try it · How many millilitres are in 2.4 L?

2,400 mL.

3. Convert to a larger unit

Divide by the number of smaller units in one larger unit.

Try it · Write 85 cm in metres.

0.85 m, because 85 ÷ 100 = 0.85.

Common mistakes

Using the same conversion factor for every pair of units. Write the relationship first. For example, 1 m = 100 cm and 1 L = 1,000 mL.

10-MINUTE HOME ACTIVITY

Compare measurements

Find labels or measures in different units, such as 750 mL and 1.2 L. Convert both to the same unit before comparing.

Adjust the challenge: Start with whole litres and millilitres. Extend by comparing 1.05 kg and 1,005 g.

FAQ

Which capacity is greater?

1.2 L, because it is 1,200 mL.

Learning goals

Learning goal

Students connect metric prefixes and powers of ten, convert without changing the measured quantity and choose a decimal representation appropriate to the context and instrument precision.

Success criteria

  • I can represent or identify the concept.
  • I can explain the underlying relationship.
  • I can select an appropriate strategy or feature.
  • I can apply it in a new context.
  • I can justify and verify the response.

Key concept

Convert across related metric units

Use the visual model first. Ask students to identify the quantities, structure or conditions before calculating or explaining.

quantitylarger unitsmaller unitlength2.375 m2 375 mmmass3.48 kg3 480 gcapacity1.625 L1 625 mLlength0.045 km45 m

Multiplying or dividing the numeral compensates for changing unit size. A smaller unit needs a larger numeral for the same measurement.

Worked examples

Choose useful decimal precision

Connect the central relationship to a new context, then verify the conclusion with a second representation, estimate, inverse operation or reasonableness check.

road distance2.35 km more useful than 2 350 m
object thickness4.6 mm more useful than 0.0046 m

Do not report more decimal places than the instrument supports. Preserve exact conversions before rounding for presentation.

Curriculum elaborations explicitly taught

AC9M6M01: convert between common metric units of length, mass and capacity; choose and use decimal representations of metric measurements relevant to the context of a problem

  • E1: recognising the significance of the prefixes in units of measurement
  • E2: identifying and using the correct operations when converting between units including millimetres, centimetres, metres, kilometres, milligrams, grams, kilograms, tonnes, millilitres, litres, kilolitres and megalitres
  • E3: recognising the equivalence of measurements, such as 1.25 metres is the same as 125 centimetres

Use the central and application models above to connect each elaboration to the same underlying concept.

Classroom activities

Build and annotate the model

Represent metric conversions with decimal measurements and label the important parts, quantities or choices.

Compare and reason

Use the application model to compare two cases, explain the relationship and identify a likely error.

Transfer and verify

Apply the idea in an unfamiliar context and use a second method, evidence source or text feature to check it.

Revision Notes and quick mastery check

Check understanding

  • Convert 2.375 m to mm.
  • Convert 3.48 kg to g.
  • Choose a unit for thickness.
  • Explain conversion direction.
  • State precision limit.

Evidence of mastery

  • Represent or identify the concept
  • Explain the underlying relationship
  • Select an appropriate strategy or feature
  • Apply it in a new context
