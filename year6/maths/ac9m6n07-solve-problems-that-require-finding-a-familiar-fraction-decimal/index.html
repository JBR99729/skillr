YEAR 6 MATHS · AC9M6N07

Find fractions, decimals and percentages of amounts

Find parts of a quantity and calculate discounts using efficient strategies.

Practice on app ↗Help with gaps ↓10-minute activity ↓

WORKED EXAMPLE

Find the price after a discount

The discount is what you save. The final price is what you pay.

Learn the skill

1. Connect equivalent forms

A quarter, 0.25 and 25% name the same part of a whole.

Try it · Which fraction equals 50%?

One half.

2. Choose an efficient calculation

For 10% divide by ten. For 25% divide by four.

Try it · What is 25% of 48?

12.

3. Interpret the result

Identify whether the problem asks for the amount saved or the final price.

Try it · An $80 item is discounted by 10%. What do you pay?

$72 after an $8 discount.

Common mistakes

Reporting the discount as the sale price. Subtract the amount saved from the original price.

10-MINUTE HOME ACTIVITY

Work out a sale price

Find a 25% discount on a $36 item and calculate the price after the discount.

Adjust the challenge: Start with 50% off. Extend by comparing the 25% discount with $10 off.

FAQ

What is the sale price?

$27. One quarter of $36 is $9, so subtract $9.

Learning goals

Learning goal

Students connect equivalent fractions, decimals and percentages, find benchmark parts by division and multiplication and distinguish discount amount from sale price.

Success criteria

  • I can represent or identify the concept.
  • I can explain the underlying relationship.
  • I can select an appropriate strategy or feature.
  • I can apply it in a new context.

Key concept

Find 25%, 40% and 75% of $240

Use the visual model first. Ask students to identify the quantities, structure or conditions before calculating or explaining.

partstrategyamount25% = 1/4240 ÷ 4$6040% = 4/10240 ÷ 10 × 4$9675% = 3/4240 ÷ 4 × 3$180

Use a familiar equivalent form that makes the quantity easy to partition. A 25% discount is $60; the sale price is $180.

Curriculum elaborations explicitly taught

AC9M6N07: solve problems that require finding a familiar fraction, decimal or percentage of a quantity, including percentage discounts, choosing efficient calculation strategies and using digital tools where appropriate

  • E1: explaining how \frac13 of a quantity can be achieved by dividing by 3, and how knowledge of \frac13 of a quantity can be used to find \frac23 or \frac43 of the same quantity using situations involving money, length, duration, mass or capacity
  • E2: investigating percentage discounts of 15%, 30% and 45% in an online toy sale, using their equivalent decimal representations of 0.15, 0.3 and 0.45 to calculate the amount of discount on sale items, with and without digital tools
  • E3: linking percentages to their decimal equivalent of tenths and hundredths and using these to determine percentage discounts; for example, finding 30% discount by using its equivalence to 0.3, dividing by 10 and multiplying the result by 3 to give 30%
  • E4: explaining the equivalence between percentages and fractions; for example, 33\frac13% and \frac13; keeping to percentages that are equivalent to fractions with small denominators such as 66\frac23% and 12.5%
  • E5: representing a situation with a mathematical expression; for example, numbers and symbols such as \frac14\times24, that involve finding a familiar fraction or percentage of a quantity; using mental strategies or a calculator and explaining the result in terms of the situation in question

Use the central and application models above to connect each elaboration to the same underlying concept.
