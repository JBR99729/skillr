Timetables and journey durations

Read timetable entries and calculate travel, waiting and arrival times.

Practice on app ↗Help with gaps ↓10-minute activity ↓

WORKED EXAMPLE

Count waiting time in a journey

Include travel and waiting when finding the total elapsed time.

Learn the skill

1. Read the timetable

Match the service column with the correct stop row.

Try it · Which two times are needed to calculate time aboard a service?

Departure from your starting stop and arrival at your destination.

2. Calculate across an hour

Jump to the next hour and then to the end time.

Try it · How long is 09:48 to 10:12?

24 minutes.

3. Allow time to transfer

Compare the arrival and next departure times.

Try it · A bus arrives at 10:12 and the train leaves at 10:25. How long is the wait?

13 minutes.

Common mistakes

Adding only time spent aboard vehicles. Include the time between arrival and the next departure.

10-MINUTE HOME ACTIVITY

Plan a connection

A bus leaves at 09:48 and arrives at 10:12. A train leaves at 10:25 and arrives at 10:53. Draw a timeline and label the three intervals.

Adjust the challenge: Use a time line marked at each hour. Extend by deciding whether a 15-minute transfer is possible.

FAQ

How long is the whole journey including the wait?

65 minutes, or 1 hour 5 minutes.

Learning goals

Learning goal

Students read 12- and 24-hour schedules, connect services, calculate elapsed time across noon or midnight and test whether an itinerary meets transfer, opening-time and duration constraints.

Success criteria

Worked examples

Plan activities under timetable constraints

Connect the central relationship to a new context, then verify the conclusion with a second representation, estimate, inverse operation or reasonableness check.

identify fixed times→calculate durations→include travel/transition→check overlap→allow buffer→select feasible itinerary→communicate plan

A plan may be mathematically possible but impractical without preparation or delay allowance. State assumptions about time zones and dates where relevant.

Curriculum elaborations explicitly taught

AC9M6M03: interpret and use timetables and itineraries to plan activities and determine the duration of events and journeys

  • E1: planning a trip involving one or more modes of public transport
  • E2: developing a timetable of daily activities for a planned event; for example, a sports carnival
  • E3: investigating different ways duration is represented in timetables and using different timetables to plan a journey

Use the central and application models above to connect each elaboration to the same underlying concept.

Common misconceptions
Travel times added but waits omittedInclude every interval from start to finish.
Clock digits subtracted as decimalsUse 60-minute hours and timelines.
Arrival before departure ignoredCheck date, midnight and time zone.
Minimum transfer assumed safeConsider practical buffer when planning.
