Area of rectangles

Use rows of unit squares to explain and apply the rectangle area formula.

Practice on app ↗Help with gaps ↓10-minute activity ↓

WORKED EXAMPLE

Find the area of a rectangle

Area measures what the shape covers. Perimeter measures the distance around it.

Learn the skill

1. Count rows

Each equal row contains the same number of unit squares.

Try it · How many squares are in three rows of five?

15.

2. Use the formula

Multiply length by width in the same units.

Try it · What is the area of a 9 m by 4 m rectangle?

36 m².

3. Find a missing dimension

Divide area by the known side length.

Try it · A rectangle has area 30 cm² and length 6 cm. What is its width?

5 cm.

Common mistakes

Giving the area in centimetres. Use square centimetres because area counts how many 1 cm by 1 cm squares cover the surface.

10-MINUTE HOME ACTIVITY

Measure a tabletop

Measure the length and width in centimetres. Draw a sketch and calculate the area in square centimetres.

Adjust the challenge: Start by counting squares on a grid. Extend by finding a width from a given area.

FAQ

What is the area of a 7 cm by 4 cm rectangle?

28 cm².

Learning goals

Learning goal

Students derive area from rows and columns of square units, explain A = length × width and apply it to missing dimensions, composite spaces and material estimates.

Key concept

Derive A = l × w from a square-unit array

Use the visual model first. Ask students to identify the quantities, structure or conditions before calculating or explaining.

The formula summarises repeated counting of square units. Units are squared because area measures two-dimensional coverage.

Curriculum elaborations explicitly taught

AC9M6M02: establish the formula for the area of a rectangle and use it to solve practical problems

  • E1: using one centimetre grid paper to construct a variety of rectangles, recording the side lengths and the related areas of the rectangles in a table to establish the formula for the area of a rectangle by recognising the relationship between the length of the sides and its calculated area
  • E2: using the relationship between the length and area of square units and the array structure to derive a formula for calculating the area of a rectangle from the lengths of its sides
  • E3: solving problems involving the comparison of lengths and areas using appropriate units
  • E4: investigating the connection between the perimeters of different rectangles with the same area and between the areas of rectangles with the same perimeter

Use the central and application models above to connect each elaboration to the same underlying concept.
