Probability on a numerical scale

Use fractions, decimals and percentages to describe chances from impossible to certain.

Practice on app ↗Help with gaps ↓10-minute activity ↓

WORKED EXAMPLE

Place a chance on a scale

Probability describes a share of possible outcomes and runs from 0 to 1.

Learn the skill

1. Set the endpoints

Zero means impossible and one means certain.

Try it · What probability represents an even chance?

0.5, or one half.

2. Count equally likely outcomes

Divide favourable outcomes by total outcomes when individual outcomes are equally likely.

Try it · What is the chance of drawing the only blue counter from four equally likely counters?

One quarter.

3. Rename the probability

The same chance can be shown in three forms.

Try it · Write three quarters as a decimal and percentage.

0.75 and 75%.

Common mistakes

Treating 0.25 as 25 on a zero-to-one scale. Place 0.25 one quarter of the way along. Its percentage is 25%.

10-MINUTE HOME ACTIVITY

Build a chance scale

Place impossible, even chance and certain on a line from 0 to 1. Add the chance of drawing red from a bag with three red counters and one blue counter.

Adjust the challenge: Use a line with 0, 0.5 and 1 marked. Extend by writing every chance as a percentage.

FAQ

Where does the red draw sit?

At 0.75, or 75%, between even chance and certain.

Learning goals

Learning goal

Students place impossible, unlikely, even-chance, likely and certain events on a numerical scale and convert among equivalent probability forms.

Success criteria

  • I can represent or identify the concept.
  • I can explain the underlying relationship.
  • I can select an appropriate strategy or feature.
  • I can apply it in a new context.
  • I can justify and verify the response.

Key concept

Locate probability values on one scale

Use the visual model first. Ask students to identify the quantities, structure or conditions before calculating or explaining.

impossible 0unlikely 0.2even 0.5likely 0.8certain 1

Probability 0 means impossible under the model and 1 means certain. Values between express relative likelihood, not a guarantee of short-run frequency.

Curriculum elaborations explicitly taught

AC9M6P01: recognise that probabilities lie on numerical scales of 0 - 1 or 0% - 100% and use estimation to assign probabilities that events occur in a given context, using common fractions, percentages and decimals

  • E1: recognising that the probability of an event occurring can be represented numerically as either a number ranging from zero to one or a percentage from 0% to 100% where zero or 0% means it won’t happen and one or 100% means it is certain to happen
  • E2: using a scale of zero to one or 0% to 100% to estimate chances of events
  • E3: listing the different possible outcomes for rolling a dice and using a scale to locate the relative probability by considering the chance of more or less than for each possible event; for example, the probability of getting a number greater than 4
  • E4: recognising the language used to describe situations involving uncertainty, such as what it means to be lucky, a 75% chance of rain or a 1-in-100 years flood
  • E5: exploring how probabilities are used in artificial intelligence for machine learning and decision-making; for example, when choosing a video on a streaming service or travelling in a self-driving autonomous car, where artificial intelligence algorithms estimate the probability of a pedestrian crossing the road, which helps the autonomous car make decisions about when to stop or slow down
  • E6: exploring First Nations Australian children’s instructive games, such as Weme from the Warlpiri Peoples of Central Australia, to investigate and assign probabilities that events will occur, indicating their estimated likelihood

Use the central and application models above to connect each elaboration to the same underlying concept.
