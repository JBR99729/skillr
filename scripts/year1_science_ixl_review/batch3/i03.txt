# Practice
A child sees three holes in a leaf. Which note records only what was seen?|The leaf has three holes.|A caterpillar made every hole.|The leaf will get more holes.|The first note records visible evidence. The others add a cause or prediction.
A seedling's height will be checked each week. What should each record include?|the date and height|the pot's colour only|the child's favourite plant|Dates and heights allow the observations to be compared over time.
Four birds land on a fence during an observation. Which entry matches?|Birds on fence: 4|Birds on fence: many more tomorrow|Birds on fence: probably hungry|The count records what was observed, without adding an explanation or prediction.
A leaf spans five identical cubes laid end to end. How should its length be recorded?|5 cubes long|5 steps long|5 leaves long|The recorded unit must match the objects used to measure.
The weather is cloudy at the morning check. Which note is useful?|Morning check: cloudy|The clouds will bring rain later|Cloudy days are the best|The first note reports the weather at the stated observation time.
A toy stops spinning. Which tool can help record how long it spun?|a timer|a magnifier|a balance|A timer measures duration; the other tools measure or reveal different features.
A shell has a dark stripe near its edge. Which drawing would preserve this observation?|a drawing with the stripe in its observed place|a drawing with stripes added all over|a drawing showing only the background|A science drawing should show the observed features accurately.
A class photographs the same plant on different days. What should be saved with each photo?|the date|a prediction written as a result|the name of an unrelated plant|Dates help identify when each photograph was taken.
Use the length model. Which record matches the leaf?|Leaf length: 4 cubes|Leaf length: 5 cubes|Leaf length: 3 cubes|Four equal cubes span the leaf from one end to the other.
A child writes '6' after counting snails. What should be added?|what was counted|a different number|a guess about tomorrow|Writing '6 snails' makes the number meaningful.
Two plants are being measured with blocks. What helps make their records comparable?|use the same-sized blocks for both|use large blocks for one and small blocks for the other|record one in blocks and one as 'tall'|Equal-sized units allow the lengths to be compared directly.
A child sees water dripping from a wet cloth. Which record is an observation?|Drops are falling from the cloth.|The cloth is upset.|The cloth will be dry by lunch.|Falling drops can be seen. The other statements add an imagined cause or a prediction.
A class checks a puddle before and after lunch. What record helps show the change?|labelled drawings from both times|one drawing labelled 'sometime'|a drawing of a different puddle|Matching the same puddle to both times helps preserve its change.
A child counts visiting butterflies. How can tally marks help?|add one mark for each visit|add marks only when remembering later|use one mark for any number of visits|Recording each visit as it happens reduces reliance on memory.
A child wants to record the pattern of spots on a leaf. Which digital tool is useful?|a camera to photograph the leaf|a timer running beside the leaf|a calculator with no leaf information|A photograph can preserve the arrangement of visible spots.
A leaf is measured with cubes, but gaps are left between them. What should be done before recording its length?|line the cubes up without gaps and check again|count each gap as another cube|keep the count without checking|Repeated units should touch end to end so the count represents the length.
Read the observation card. Which notebook entry keeps all the information?|Tuesday morning: 3 snails on the wall|Tuesday: 3 animals somewhere|Morning: snails might visit the wall|The complete entry records when, what, how many and where.
A child records two flowers, then notices another on the same plant. What should the record show?|3 flowers|2 flowers because that was written first|4 flowers in case another appears|The record should be updated to match what is actually observed.
A plant looks taller than yesterday. How could the child make the comparison clearer?|measure from the same starting point with the same units|measure from a new starting point with bigger units|replace both measurements with 'looks good'|Keeping the measurement method consistent makes the comparison more useful.
A sound lasts until a timer reads 7 seconds. What should be recorded?|Sound duration: 7 seconds|Sound length: 7 cubes|Sound count: 7 sounds|The number and unit must describe the duration measured.
The two records show a plant changing. Which record matches?|Two leaves first; four leaves later|Four leaves first; two leaves later|Four leaves in both records|The record must preserve the number and order of the observed leaves.
A child records 'smooth' for one rock and 'heavy' for another. What extra observation helps compare texture?|how the second rock's surface feels, if safe to touch|the second rock's colour only|how far the first rock rolls|Texture should be recorded for both objects when comparing texture.
A ruler reading is unclear. What should the child do?|ask for help checking it before recording|write the largest visible number|copy a friend's different object measurement|Checking the reading is better than inventing or borrowing a result.
A video shows a ball rolling, slowing, then stopping. Which note records that order?|rolled, slowed, stopped|stopped, sped up, rolled|rolled at the same speed throughout|The note should match the sequence visible in the video.
# Test
A child sees two white stripes on a rock. Which entry records that observation?|Rock: two white stripes|Rock: stripes made by an insect|Rock: more stripes tomorrow|The visible stripes are an observation; the other statements add unsupported ideas.
A twig spans six equal blocks without gaps. Which length entry fits?|6 blocks long|6 twigs long|6 steps long|The units are blocks, so the record needs the number and that unit.
A child records a plant's height but forgets the date. What useful information is missing?|when the height was measured|why every plant grows|how tall the plant will become|The date links a measurement to its place in a record over time.
Read the counting card. Which note matches?|Friday: 5 ants beside the pot|Friday: 4 ants beside the pot|Thursday: 5 ants beside the pot|The note must match both the count and the day provided.
A class wants to record a cloud's shape before it changes. Which tool helps?|a camera|a balance|a timer alone|A photograph preserves visible shape at a particular time.
A child measures two shells with different-sized cubes. What should they do to compare lengths?|measure both with the same-sized cubes|compare only the two cube counts|choose the count that is larger|Different-sized units make the counts unsuitable for direct comparison.
A class hears five bird calls. What should its tally show?|five marks, one per call|one mark for all five calls|six marks to allow for the next call|A one-to-one tally records each observed call once.
A child notes 'The soil is dark and damp.' What kind of record is this?|an observation of the soil|a prediction of tomorrow's weather|a measurement of plant height|The note describes observed soil features.
The model shows a feather measured with equal blocks. What should be recorded?|Feather length: 5 blocks|Feather length: 4 blocks|Feather length: 6 blocks|Five blocks span the feather in the model.
A child draws a plant with three leaves but the plant has four. What should change?|add the missing observed leaf|erase the whole record and guess|add two leaves to make it look fuller|A record should match the plant being observed.
A timer shows a toy spun for 9 seconds. Which record is useful?|Toy spin: 9 seconds|Toy spin: 9 blocks|Toy spin: a nice long time|The specific duration and correct unit allow comparison with later spins.
A group records rain each morning. What should accompany each weather note?|the day of the observation|the favourite weather of the group|a guaranteed forecast for next week|The day helps arrange and compare repeated observations.
One child writes 'The leaf has a hole.' Another writes 'A beetle ate it.' Which is directly observable?|the hole in the leaf|which animal made the hole without seeing it happen|the animal's reason for eating|The hole can be seen; its cause needs further evidence.
A child wants to compare water levels in two clear cups. What record helps?|mark and label the level in each cup|write 'water' beside only one cup|record the colour of the table|Both labelled levels are needed for the comparison.
Read the before-and-after card. Which note matches?|The puddle was larger before lunch.|The puddle was larger after lunch.|The puddle stayed the same size.|The note preserves the direction of change shown in the record.
A child cannot remember whether there were four or five birds. What is the honest next step?|say the count is uncertain and observe again|record five as certain|record whichever number was predicted|Uncertainty should be acknowledged rather than turned into an invented observation.
