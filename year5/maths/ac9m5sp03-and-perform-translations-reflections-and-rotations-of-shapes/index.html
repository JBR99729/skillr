Learn the skill

Four focused steps

Learning goal: Perform and describe shape movements, then test what stays the same and whether the shape has symmetry.

Study a worked example, explain the reasoning and try its short check before moving on.

1. Translate every point by the same move

A translation slides a shape without turning or flipping it. Every point moves the same distance in the same direction.

Move a triangle three grid units right.

  1. Choose a vertex and count three equal spaces right.
  2. Move each remaining vertex three spaces right, then join corresponding vertices.
  3. Lengths, angles, size and orientation stay the same; position changes.

Watch for this: Moving different vertices by different amounts distorts the shape instead of translating it.

Try it: A shape moves two units up. What happens to its side lengths?

Check the answer

They stay unchanged. Every vertex moves the same two-unit displacement.

2. Reflect across a stated mirror line

A reflection flips a shape. Corresponding points lie on opposite sides of the mirror line at equal perpendicular distances.

Reflect the triangle in the vertical dashed line.

  1. For each vertex, count its horizontal distance to this vertical mirror line.
  2. Mark a point the same distance across the line at the same height.
  3. Join the new vertices. The shape is the same size but its orientation is reversed.

Watch for this: The nearest edge of the drawing is not automatically the mirror line; use the stated line.

Try it: What happens to a vertex exactly on the mirror line?

Check the answer

It stays in the same position.

3. Rotate about a stated centre

A rotation turns every point around the same centre by a stated amount and direction. Distances from the centre stay fixed.

Turn a paper arrow a quarter-turn clockwise about its tail.

  1. Pin or mark the tail as the centre.
  2. Rotate through 90° clockwise; an arrow initially pointing up now points right.
  3. Keep the centre fixed and trace the image. A further clockwise quarter-turn points down.

Watch for this: “Rotate 90°” is incomplete if the centre or direction is missing.

Try it: An up-pointing arrow makes a half-turn about its tail. Which way does it point?

Check the answer

Down. A half-turn is 180° in either direction.

4. Compare sequences and test symmetries

A symmetry maps a shape exactly onto itself. Reflection symmetry uses a mirror line; rotational symmetry uses a turn smaller than 360°.

Test a non-square rectangle.

  1. It has two mirror lines through the midpoints of opposite sides.
  2. A 180° turn about its centre matches it to itself; a 90° turn does not.
  3. Two successive half-turns about the same centre return any shape to its start, as do two reflections in the same line. Use tracing or geometry software to test.

Watch for this: A transformation and a symmetry are different ideas: a move may produce a new position without matching the original.

Try it: How many mirror lines does an unmarked square have?

Check the answer

Four: two through opposite side midpoints and two diagonals. It also matches after 90°, 180° and 270° centre turns.

Vocabulary and teaching help

Before this lesson: Recognise a shape’s vertices, equal lengths and quarter/half turns.

Help a learner get started

Open one focus at a time. Read the worked steps together, ask the learner to explain what each quantity means, then let them try the short check before opening its answer. Use paper, objects or a drawing when that makes the idea clearer.

Keep the challenge meaningful: A practical construction, investigation or digital activity needs the learner’s actual work. A correct spoken answer alone does not demonstrate that practical skill.
