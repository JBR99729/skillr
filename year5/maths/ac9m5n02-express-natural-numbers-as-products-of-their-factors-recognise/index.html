Learn the skill

Four focused steps

Learning goal: Build factor pairs, recognise multiples and explain why a number divides exactly.

Study a worked example, explain the reasoning and try its short check before moving on.

1. Build all the factor pairs

A factor divides a positive whole number exactly. Arrays connect the factors to the number of rows and the number in each row.

Find the factors of 24.

  1. Arrange 24 counters as 1 × 24, 2 × 12, 3 × 8 and 4 × 6.
  2. Check candidate row sizes systematically. After 4, 5 fails and 6 repeats the 4 × 6 pair.
  3. List both numbers from every pair: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24.

Watch for this: Listing only small factors misses their partners. Square pairs such as 6 × 6 contribute the factor 6 once.

Try it: List the factor pairs of 18.

Check the answer

1 × 18, 2 × 9, 3 × 6; factors 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18.

2. Generate and combine multiples

A positive multiple is a number multiplied by 1, 2, 3 and so on. Equal groups can be split without changing their total.

Build multiples of 7 from groups of 5 and 2.

  1. 4 × 7 = 4 × (5 + 2).
  2. Combine 4 × 5 = 20 and 4 × 2 = 8: the total is 28.
  3. The first five positive multiples are 7, 14, 21, 28, 35.

Watch for this: A factor belongs to the number’s factor set; its multiples continue without a last positive multiple.

3. Explain tests for 2, 5 and 10

Base-ten groups explain the last-digit tests. Every full ten can be split into pairs or groups of five, so the ones decide whether anything is left over.

Is 340 divisible by 2, 5 and 10?

  1. The ones digit 0 leaves no extra ones after its 34 tens.
  2. It is even, ends in 0 or 5, and ends in 0: all three tests pass.
  3. Check: 340 = 2 × 170 = 5 × 68 = 10 × 34.

Watch for this: A divisibility test means no remainder, not merely a close answer.

Try it: Which of 2, 5 and 10 divide 135?

Check the answer

Only 5. The last digit is odd and is not zero.

4. Use digit sums and check the result

For 3 and 9, sum the digits. Each ten is one more than a multiple of 9, so replacing tens by ones leaves the same remainder for these tests.

Test 4 572 for divisibility by 3 and 9.

  1. 4 + 5 + 7 + 2 = 18, which is divisible by both 3 and 9.
  2. 4 572 = 3 × 1 524 and 9 × 508 confirm both tests.

Watch for this: The digit-sum rule for 3 or 9 is not a rule for every divisor.

A divisibility test is a reasoned shortcut. Confirm unfamiliar cases with multiplication or division rather than relying on a guessed pattern.

Try it: Is 2 314 divisible by 3?

Check the answer

No: 2 + 3 + 1 + 4 = 10, which is not a multiple of 3.

Vocabulary and teaching help

Before this lesson: Recall multiplication facts and distinguish a whole group from a remainder.

Useful vocabulary

factor
whole number that divides another exactly
multiple
product of a number and a whole number
divisible
able to be divided with no remainder

Help a learner get started

Open one focus at a time. Read the worked steps together, ask the learner to explain what each quantity means, then let them try the short check before opening its answer. Use paper, objects or a drawing when that makes the idea clearer.

Common errors and quick fixes

  • Factors and multiples reversed — Factors fit into a number; multiples extend outward from it.
  • Only small factor pairs listed — Continue systematically until the pair order repeats.
  • Divisible means close to a multiple — The quotient must be a whole number with no remainder.
  • One is forgotten — 1 and the number itself are factors of every positive natural number.

Keep the challenge meaningful: A practical construction, investigation or digital activity needs the learner’s actual work. A correct spoken answer alone does not demonstrate that practical skill.
