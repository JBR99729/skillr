Learn the skill

Four focused steps

Learning goal: Identify the missing quantity and use inverse operations and number properties to keep equations balanced.

Study a worked example, explain the reasoning and try its short check before moving on.

1. Identify what the missing number represents

An unknown can be a factor, a total or a divisor. Its position determines the related calculation.

Find the blank in 72 ÷ □ = 8.

  1. The blank is the group size when eight groups make 72.
  2. Use 8 × □ = 72, giving □ = 9.
  3. Substitute: 72 ÷ 9 = 8.

Watch for this: 72 ÷ □ = 8 and □ ÷ 8 = 72 are different equations.

Try it: Find □ in □ × 7 = 91.

Check the answer

13; 13 × 7 = 91.

2. Use equality as a balance

Both sides of an equation can contain calculations. Find a value that makes both sides equal rather than treating the equals sign as “write the answer next”.

Solve 4 × 6 = 48 ÷ □.

  1. The left side is 24.
  2. 48 ÷ 2 = 24, so the blank is 2.
  3. Check the original equation: both sides have value 24.

Watch for this: Scaling dividend and divisor together preserves a quotient: 15 ÷ 3 = 30 ÷ 6.

The inverse operation finds the missing factor. Substitution confirms that the equality is true.

Try it: Solve 5 × 4 = □ × 2.

Check the answer

10, because 5 × 4 = 20 and 10 × 2 = 20.

3. Use order and grouping carefully

Multiplication permits swapping factors and regrouping them. Division does not generally permit the same changes.

Explain 3 × 8 = 6 × 4.

  1. Replace 8 by 2 × 4.
  2. 3 × (2 × 4) = (3 × 2) × 4 = 6 × 4.
  3. Compare division: (24 ÷ 6) ÷ 2 = 2, but 24 ÷ (6 ÷ 2) = 8. The grouping matters.

Watch for this: The multiplication properties are not permission to swap or regroup division at will.

Try it: Complete 5 × 12 = 10 × □.

Check the answer

6: split 12 into 2 × 6 and regroup 5 × 2.

4. Distribute over parts and check the unknown

A partitioned array lets you multiply each part and combine the products. This explains the distributive property.

Solve 7 × 14 = 7 × 10 + 7 × □.

  1. Split 14 into 10 + 4.
  2. 7 × 14 = 70 + 28, so the blank is 4.
  3. Both sides equal 98.

Watch for this: Every part must be multiplied: 7 × (10 + 4) is not 70 + 4.

Identify whether the unknown is a factor, dividend or divisor. Multiplication can be regrouped; division order cannot be swapped casually.

Try it: Find □ in 6 × 18 = 6 × 20 − 6 × □.

Check the answer

2, because 18 = 20 − 2; both sides equal 108.

Vocabulary and teaching help

Before this lesson: Know multiplication/division facts and that an equals sign means “has the same value”.

Useful vocabulary

equation
statement that two expressions have equal value
unknown
value to be determined
substitution
replacing a symbol with a value to test the equation

Help a learner get started

Open one focus at a time. Read the worked steps together, ask the learner to explain what each quantity means, then let them try the short check before opening its answer. Use paper, objects or a drawing when that makes the idea clearer.

Common errors and quick fixes

  • Same inverse used for every unknown position — Identify factor, dividend or divisor first.
  • Equals sign read as an instruction only — It states balance between expressions.
  • Division reversed — a ÷ b is not generally b ÷ a.
  • Solution not checked — Substitute into the original equation.

Keep the challenge meaningful: A practical construction, investigation or digital activity needs the learner’s actual work. A correct spoken answer alone does not demonstrate that practical skill.
