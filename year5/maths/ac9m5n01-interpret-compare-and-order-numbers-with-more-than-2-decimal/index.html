Learn the skill

Four focused steps

Learning goal: Read, compare and locate decimals by naming the value of each digit, including thousandths.

Study a worked example, explain the reasoning and try its short check before moving on.

1. Name each decimal place

Each place to the right is one tenth of the place before it. One hundredth is ten thousandths. The whole must stay the same when a model is subdivided.

Read 4.307 using place value.

  1. 4 ones + 3 tenths + 0 hundredths + 7 thousandths.
  2. The 7 is worth 0.007, not 0.07. Say “four point three zero seven”.

Watch for this: A zero inside the numeral holds a place; 4.307 and 4.037 are different.

Try it: What is the value of 8 in 2.086?

Check the answer

Eight hundredths: 0.08.

2. Rename without changing the amount

Splitting a unit makes more, smaller pieces of the same amount. Appending a zero after the final decimal digit changes its name, not its value.

Explain 0.4 = 0.40 = 0.400.

  1. Four tenths are forty hundredths or four hundred thousandths.
  2. To divide 0.4 by ten, share 40 hundredths into ten equal groups: 4 hundredths = 0.04.

Watch for this: Dividing by ten makes the amount smaller; do not just erase or add a zero.

Another worked model

Interpret 3.407 by place value

onestenthshundredthsthousandths3407value0.400.007

The zero holds the hundredths place. Read 3.407 as three and four hundred seven thousandths, or 3 + 4/10 + 7/1000.

Try it: Write 0.7 ÷ 10.

Check the answer

0.07; seven tenths shared into ten groups gives seven hundredths.

3. Compare and order using common places

Compare the ones first, then tenths, hundredths and thousandths until the first difference. Trailing zeros can help align the places.

Order 2.58, 2.508 and 2.580.

  1. Write 2.580, 2.508 and 2.580.
  2. Ones and tenths agree. At hundredths, 0 < 8, so 2.508 < 2.58 = 2.580.

Watch for this: More decimal digits do not automatically mean a larger number.

Try it: Which is larger: 1.709 kg or 1.79 kg?

Check the answer

1.79 kg = 1.790 kg; nine hundredths is greater than zero hundredths.

4. Locate a decimal between close endpoints

Zoom in on a number line. Ten equal intervals from 2.33 to 2.34 are thousandths because the total gap is one hundredth.

Locate 2.335.

  1. The left endpoint is 2.330 and the right is 2.340.
  2. 2.335 is five thousandths after 2.330, halfway through the interval.

Watch for this: Count intervals, not tick marks. A diagram of a length needs its unit as well as its number.

Vocabulary and teaching help

Before this lesson: Know tenths and hundredths, whole-number place value and equal number-line intervals.

Useful vocabulary

zero showing an empty place without changing value
decimal place
position to the right of the decimal point

Help a learner get started

Open one focus at a time. Read the worked steps together, ask the learner to explain what each quantity means, then let them try the short check before opening its answer. Use paper, objects or a drawing when that makes the idea clearer.

Common errors and quick fixes

  • More digits means greater value — 3.407 is less than 3.42 because 3.407 < 3.420.
  • Zero ignored inside a decimal — The zero in 3.407 keeps 7 in the thousandths place.
  • Decimal points not aligned — Compare like place values by aligning the decimal points.
  • Number-line intervals assumed unequal — Equal numerical steps require equal spatial intervals.

Keep the challenge meaningful: A practical construction, investigation or digital activity needs the learner’s actual work. A correct spoken answer alone does not demonstrate that practical skill.
