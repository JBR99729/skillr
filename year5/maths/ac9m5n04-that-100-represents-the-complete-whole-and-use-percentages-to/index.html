Percentages, fractions and decimals

Treat one complete whole as 100%, then connect familiar fractions, decimals and percentages. One-quarter is 25% or 0.25 of the same whole; compare proportions while keeping the whole clear.

Learn the skill

Four focused steps

Learning goal: Treat one complete whole as 100%, then connect familiar proportions and compare their relative size.

Study a worked example, explain the reasoning and try its short check before moving on.

1. Identify the whole and show percent

Percent means “per hundred”. A complete hundred-cell grid represents 100%; each equal cell represents 1%.

Read a grid with 35 of its 100 cells shaded.

  1. The whole is all 100 cells.
  2. 35 shaded cells represent 35/100 = 35%.
  3. The 65 unshaded cells represent 65%; 35% + 65% = 100%.

Watch for this: Two different-sized complete containers are both 100% full; they need not contain the same amount.

Try it: If 20% of the original juice is used, what percentage remains?

Check the answer

80%, because used and remaining portions make the same original 100%.

2. Connect familiar fractions to percentages

Rename a fraction in hundredths, or subdivide a grid into equal groups. The proportion stays the same.

Write 3/4 as a percentage.

  1. Divide 100 cells into four equal groups of 25.
  2. Three groups contain 75 cells: 3/4 = 75/100 = 75%.

Watch for this: The denominator does not tell you the percentage on its own.

Another worked model

Represent 35% on a hundred grid

35% = 35/100 = 0.35

The whole must be identified. Thirty-five shaded cells out of 100 represent 35%, but the actual quantity depends on what one whole represents.

Try it: Write 2/5 as a percentage.

Check the answer

40%, because 2/5 = 40/100.

3. Connect percentages and decimal place value

A decimal names parts of one whole. Hundredths link it directly with a percentage.

Connect 0.6, 6/10 and a percentage.

  1. 0.6 = 6/10 = 60/100.
  2. Sixty per hundred is 60%. Similarly, 0.25 = 25% = 1/4.

Watch for this: 0.05 is 5%, while 0.5 is 50%. The zero matters.

Try it: Write 75% as a decimal.

Check the answer

0.75, because seventy-five per hundred is seventy-five hundredths.

4. Compare proportions and read everyday claims

Use a common representation when fractions, decimals and percentages are mixed. A larger proportion does not guarantee a larger actual amount if the wholes differ.

Compare 50%, 0.7 and 3/4.

  1. Write 50%, 70% and 75%.
  2. The order is 50% < 0.7 < 3/4.
  3. On the same device, a 70% indicator represents a greater proportion of capacity than 50%.

Watch for this: A sale sign saying 25% off describes a proportion of the original price; it does not mean $25 off.

Check the answer

Vocabulary and teaching help

Before this lesson: Recognise halves, quarters, tenths and hundredths of a stated whole.

Useful vocabulary

percentage
number of parts per hundred
relative size

equivalent representation

Help a learner get started

Open one focus at a time. Read the worked steps together, ask the learner to explain what each quantity means, then let them try the short check before opening its answer. Use paper, objects or a drawing when that makes the idea clearer.

Common errors and quick fixes

  • 100% means 100 objects — It means the complete whole, whatever its size.
  • Percent and decimal copied without place-value change — 35% = 0.35, not 35.0.
  • Percentages compared without the whole — Equal percentages of different wholes can be different amounts.
  • 25% treated as 25/10 — Percent means denominator 100 before simplification.

Keep the challenge meaningful: A practical construction, investigation or digital activity needs the learner’s actual work. A correct spoken answer alone does not demonstrate that practical skill.
