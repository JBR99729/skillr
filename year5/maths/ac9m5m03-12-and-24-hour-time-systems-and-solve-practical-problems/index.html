12-hour and 24-hour time

Convert between 12-hour and 24-hour time, including noon and midnight. For an afternoon time such as 3:25 pm, add 12 to the hour to get 15:25; noon is 12:00.

Learn the skill

1. Match the same time in two systems

3. Handle noon and midnight explicitly

Noon is 12:00 PM or 12:00. Midnight at the start of a stated day is 12:00 AM or 00:00. These are exceptions to a simple “add 12” rule.

Common mistakes

Subtracting the minute numbers as though an hour had 100 minutes. Use forward jumps through the hour boundary or convert to minutes first.

Learn the skill

Four focused steps

Learning goal: Connect clock faces, AM/PM times and 24-hour timetables, including noon and midnight.

Study a worked example, explain the reasoning and try its short check before moving on.

1. Match the same time in two systems

The 12-hour clock repeats twice each day. The 24-hour clock numbers the hours from 00 to 23. Minutes keep the same meaning.

Match 16:30 to a 12-hour time and clock face.

  1. 16 − 12 = 4, so 16:30 is 4:30 PM.
  2. The minute hand is at 6 and the hour hand is halfway between 4 and 5.
  3. The same face could show 4:30 AM; the day period distinguishes them.

Watch for this: The hour hand moves continuously; at half past it is not exactly on the hour numeral.

Try it: Write 08:15 in 12-hour time.

Check the answer

8:15 AM.

2. Convert in both directions

For PM hours 1 to 11, add 12 to get 24-hour time. For AM hours 1 to 11, keep the hour and use a leading zero when needed.

Convert 7:45 PM and 21:05.

  1. 7:45 PM → 19:45, because 7 + 12 = 19.
  2. 21:05 → 9:05 PM, because 21 − 12 = 9.

Watch for this: Add or subtract 12 hours, not 12 minutes; minutes are not decimal fractions of an hour.

3. Handle noon and midnight explicitly

Noon is 12:00 PM or 12:00. Midnight at the start of a stated day is 12:00 AM or 00:00. These are exceptions to a simple “add 12” rule.

Convert 12:20 AM and 12:20 PM.

  1. 12:20 AM is twenty minutes after midnight: 00:20.
  2. 12:20 PM is twenty minutes after noon: 12:20.

Watch for this: 12 AM is midnight; 12 PM is noon. State the day when a journey crosses midnight.

Try it: What is 00:35 in 12-hour time?

Check the answer

12:35 AM.

4. Use a timetable and check the date

Read the correct service and day, then convert as needed. A clock time is a position in the day; duration is the time between positions.

A bus leaves Monday at 23:40 and arrives Tuesday at 00:15.

  1. Departure is 11:40 PM Monday; arrival is 12:15 AM Tuesday.
  2. There are 20 minutes to midnight and 15 more to arrival.
  3. The journey lasts 35 minutes.

Watch for this: Subtracting the displayed hours without the day change can produce a meaningless negative duration.

Another worked model

Use timelines for schedule duration

depart 14:35→to 15:00: +25 min→to 17:00: +2 h→arrive 17:18: +18 min→duration 2 h 43 min

Do not subtract time digits as ordinary decimals. Bridge through whole hours and check whether the interval crosses noon or midnight.

Try it: A service leaves at 13:25 and arrives at 14:05 on the same day. How long?

Check the answer

40 minutes: 35 minutes to 14:00 and 5 more.

Vocabulary and teaching help

Before this lesson: Read an analogue clock and know that one hour contains 60 minutes.

Help a learner get started

Open one focus at a time. Read the worked steps together, ask the learner to explain what each quantity means, then let them try the short check before opening its answer. Use paper, objects or a drawing when that makes the idea clearer.

Common errors and quick fixes

  • 12 am and 12 pm reversed — 12 am is midnight; 12 pm is noon.
  • Add 12 to every pm hour — Do not add 12 to 12 pm.
  • Minutes treated as hundredths — An hour has 60 minutes.
  • Leading zero omitted in formal 24-hour time — Write 07:05 for schedule clarity.

Keep the challenge meaningful: A practical construction, investigation or digital activity needs the learner’s actual work. A correct spoken answer alone does not demonstrate that practical skill.
