AC9S5I01: Pose investigable questions to identify patterns and test relationships and make reasoned predictions

What students learn in AC9S5I01

Students move from observations to focused questions naming variables, select measurable outcomes and write predictions that state direction and scientific reasoning.

A strong investigation question identifies a relationship that can be explored through controlled observation or measurement.

The prediction should be testable and based on evidence or a scientific explanation, not a preferred result.

Define criteria for words such as best or strong and isolate one relationship per investigation.

Learning routine: Observe → Model → Investigate → Analyse → Explain → Evaluate

Success looks like

  • Identify system components
  • Explain relationships with a model
  • Use evidence from a fair method
  • Apply to a new context
  • Evaluate limits and further questions

The prediction should be testable and based on evidence or a scientific explanation, not a preferred result.

  1. Read every label and identify the quantities, parts or evidence.
  2. Explain the relationship shown—not just the final answer.
  3. Check the conclusion against the original question and units.

Improve vague or multi-variable questions

Guided learning activities

1. Model and annotate

Build or examine the visual model for scientific questions, variables and predictions, label the parts and explain the relationship.

2Variablessurface colour; temperature increase
3QuestionHow does surface colour affect temperature rise in equal light?
4Predictiondarker surfaces will warm more because they absorb more light

2. Investigate or analyse evidence

Use a controlled comparison, data set or source evidence to test the application and record a cautious conclusion.

3. Transfer and evaluate

Apply the explanation to a new case, identify limitations and propose a next question or design improvement.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Content description: pose investigable questions to identify patterns and test relationships and make reasoned predictions.

  • E1: posing questions that can be investigated scientifically, such as: ‘Do all animals which live in desert habitats have ways to survive without water?’
  • E2: acknowledging and using information from First Nations Australians to guide the formulation of investigable questions about structural features and behaviours of living things
  • E3: posing investigable questions about landscape features and how they were changed by weathering, erosion, transportation or deposition
  • E4: asking questions and making predictions to test relationships, such as: ‘Will there be more erosion of steeper slopes? Will this organisation of mirrors enable me to see around corners? Are animals that camouflage well more likely to survive predation?’
  • E5: making reasoned predictions about the habitat a plant or animal lives in or the observable effect of light interacting with an object

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Question asks opinion — Use measurable evidence.
  • Prediction gives no direction — State what is expected to increase, decrease or differ.
  • Several variables change together — Focus the relationship.
  • Prediction rewritten after results — Record it before testing and evaluate later.
