AC9S5I03 - Use equipment to observe, measure and record data with reasonable precision, using digital tools as appropriate

CurriculumStart here

Students follow procedures in order, read instrument scales, record quantitative and qualitative observations with units and use digital tools without losing awareness of precision and error.

A reliable record states what was observed, when and how, with units and sufficient precision for comparison.

Use consistent units and decimal precision supported by the instrument. Record immediately and do not replace unexpected values silently.

Precision is limited by the instrument and method. More decimal places do not automatically mean greater accuracy.

Learning routine: Observe → Model → Investigate → Analyse → Explain → Evaluate

Success looks like

  • Identify system components
  • Explain relationships with a model
  • Use evidence from a fair method
  • Apply to a new context
  • Evaluate limits and further questions

Elaboration examplesCopied content

Content description: use equipment to observe, measure and record data with reasonable precision, using digital tools as appropriate.

  • E1: exploring which equipment gives the most reasonable precision for the measurements of data required in the investigation
  • E2: exploring the precision of measurements of different equipment such as a cup compared with a measuring jug and discussing why precision is important in measurement
  • E3: recording data using standard units, such as grams, second and metre, and developing the use of standard prefixes for metric units such as kilo- and milli-
  • E4: recording data in tables and diagrams or electronically as digital images and spreadsheets

Assessment and reviewReady for practice
  • Unexpected data changed — Record and investigate it.
  • Digital decimals assumed accurate — Check instrument precision.
  • Interpretation written as observation — Separate what happened from why.
  • Different precision used inconsistently — Use a justified common recording format.
