YEAR 5 SCIENCE · AC9S5I03

Measure carefully and record useful data

Learning intention: Choose suitable equipment, read its intervals and reference points, and record values and units with reasonable precision.

WORKED EXAMPLE

A reading is not always a length

A card edge begins at 12.0 cm on a ruler and ends at 18.4 cm. Its length is 18.4 − 12.0 = 6.4 cm. The enlarged scale section shows only the end position; the starting reading is supplied separately. Each small interval is 0.2 cm, so the stated reading is supported by the scale.

Learn the skill

1. Choose a tool for the quantity and range

Match the quantity, expected size and precision to the equipment. Use a ruler or tape for distance, a balance for mass and suitable timing or temperature equipment when needed.

QuestionSuitable choice
How tall is the shadow?A ruler/tape covering its full height
What is the mass of a sample?A balance with suitable range and resolution
How long does an event take?An appropriate timer
Try it · Is a finer digital display alone proof of a more accurate measurement?

No. Check suitability, units, zero, stability and method. Extra displayed digits do not guarantee accuracy.

2. Find intervals and use reference points

Work out the value of each interval, align the agreed edge and look straight at the scale. If the start is not zero, find the difference between the two positions.

Reading the supplied scale

Between 18 and 19 cm are five equal intervals, each 0.2 cm. Two intervals after 18 cm give 18.4 cm. Subtract the supplied 12.0 cm starting reading to obtain 6.4 cm.

Try it · If the last reading is 18.4 cm, must the object be 18.4 cm long?

No. That is a position reading. Here the starting position is 12.0 cm, so the length is 6.4 cm.

3. Record supported precision and recheck

State the value, unit, trial and conditions. A digital tool can help organise or measure, but check that the reading is stable and that the right unit and zero/tare were used.

A useful record

Card length: 6.4 cm; start 12.0 cm, end 18.4 cm; ruler intervals 0.2 cm; edges and viewing position checked.

Try it · Would recording 6.40000 cm be justified by this supplied scale?

No. It suggests more detail than the model supports. Use the stated measurement precision and report the unit.

Common mistakes

“The last number always gives the length.” Use the span between agreed start and end reference points.

“All repeats remove a zero error.” A consistent zero or setup error can affect every repeat. Check the method and instrument.

10-MINUTE ACTIVITY

Audit a measurement record

2 minutes: read the enlarged scale and supplied start. 3 minutes: explain the 0.2 cm interval and calculate the length. 3 minutes: write a unit-labelled record. 2 minutes: name one equipment check and one method check.

Adjust the challenge Support: count the intervals between 18 and 19 first. Extend: explain why a stable display and repeated identical values do not alone guarantee accuracy.

Use the supplied drawing or a safe classroom ruler. Keep equipment stable; use liquids, balances and thermometers only with an approved method.

Three tricky questions

1. A ruler has five equal intervals from 18 to 19 cm. Is each interval 1 cm?

No. The 1 cm span is divided into five equal parts, so each is 0.2 cm.

2. Why state whether shadow height is read to the top of a fuzzy edge?

Different edge rules can produce different readings. A shared rule improves consistency and makes uncertainty visible.

3. A balance reads 12 g before a container is added. Can you ignore the zero check?

No. Investigate and correct the setup or zero as appropriate before measuring. Do not assume the displayed mass represents only the intended sample.

FAQ

What is reasonable precision?

Detail that the equipment, conditions and task can support. It is not simply the greatest number of decimal places.

Can digital tools help?

Yes. They can measure or organise records, but users must check units, setup, stable readings and faithful data entry.

What students learn in AC9S5I03

Students follow procedures in order, read instrument scales, record quantitative and qualitative observations with units and use digital tools without losing awareness of precision and error.

A reliable record states what was observed, when and how, with units and sufficient precision for comparison.

Use consistent units and decimal precision supported by the instrument. Record immediately and do not replace unexpected values silently.

Precision is limited by the instrument and method. More decimal places do not automatically mean greater accuracy.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Content description: use equipment to observe, measure and record data with reasonable precision, using digital tools as appropriate.

  • E1: exploring which equipment gives the most reasonable precision for the measurements of data required in the investigation
  • E2: exploring the precision of measurements of different equipment such as a cup compared with a measuring jug and discussing why precision is important in measurement
  • E3: recording data using standard units, such as grams, second and metre, and developing the use of standard prefixes for metric units such as kilo- and milli-
  • E4: recording data in tables and diagrams or electronically as digital images and spreadsheets

Common misconceptions
  • Unexpected data changed — Record and investigate it.
  • Digital decimals assumed accurate — Check instrument precision.
  • Interpretation written as observation — Separate what happened from why.
  • Different precision used inconsistently — Use a justified common recording format.
