What students learn in AC9S5U01

Students distinguish structural features from behaviours, connect them to environmental conditions and explain how a feature can improve—not guarantee—survival and reproduction.

An adaptation is an inherited structural or behavioural feature that increases success in a particular environment across generations.

A feature is not automatically adaptive in every habitat. State the environmental pressure, mechanism and likely effect on survival or reproduction.

Correlation alone may not prove the feature caused success. Consider other traits, changing environments and whether the evidence comes from repeated observations.

Learning routine: Observe → Model → Investigate → Analyse → Explain → Evaluate

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Content description: examine how particular structural features and behaviours of living things enable their survival in specific habitats.

  • E1: identify physical and behavioural characteristics that enable a plant or animal to survive, such as being able to see in dim light and being nocturnal
  • E2: exploring features of plants and animals that enable them to survive in Australia’s desert environments, such as bottle (or boab) trees and the water-holding frog
  • E3: investigating how camouflage is used by animals to hide from predators and to ambush prey
  • E4: using physical or digital simulations to explore how the shape of animals’ body parts, such as the beak of a particular bird species, influence their ability to find food and survive in a given environment
  • E5: investigating First Nations Australians’ knowledges of the structural features of certain species and how those features can be exploited

Common misconceptions
  • Any useful action is an inherited adaptation — Some behaviours are learned or immediate responses.
  • Organisms choose adaptations because they need them — Adaptations become common across generations through differential survival and reproduction.
  • One feature guarantees survival — Several conditions and trade-offs affect success.
  • Feature described without mechanism — Explain how it changes access, protection, temperature, water or reproduction.
