YEAR 5 SCIENCE · AC9S5U01

Explain how living things survive in their habitats

Learning intention: Link a particular body or plant feature, or a behaviour, to how it helps survival under specific habitat conditions.

WORKED EXAMPLE

A feature needs a function and a habitat

Mulga has narrow, leaf-like structures called phyllodes. Their features can help conserve water in dry conditions. Learn the skill

1. Explain a structural feature

A structural feature is part of an organism’s body. Explain what it does before saying it helps survival. Useful explanations follow feature → function → benefit in the named habitat.

A mechanism matters

An insulating feather layer slows heat transfer from a warm penguin to colder surroundings. It does not produce heat.

Try it · Would “a penguin has feathers” fully explain survival?

No. Explain that the insulating layer slows heat loss in cold conditions. Naming a feature alone leaves out its function.

2. Explain a behaviour in context

A behaviour is an action. Seasonal movement can take animals to places with more suitable conditions or resources. Not every species migrates or becomes dormant.

Seasonal contrast

Bogong moths spend summer in cool alpine shelters. Mountain pygmy-possums hibernate through winter. The behaviours respond to different seasonal challenges.

Try it · Are summer sheltering and winter hibernation the same action?

No. Both can support survival, but the species, season and behaviour differ. Describe the particular action and condition.

3. Compare benefits and limits

A useful feature in one habitat is not automatically best everywhere. Compare the challenge, mechanism and trade-off. A model can help test an idea, but it cannot establish an animal’s survival on its own.

Try it · Does a wet-cloth model prove how much water every mulga loses?

No. Cloth lacks living plant structures and regulation. It models one influence on water loss under the tested conditions.

Common mistakes

“The plant chose to grow this feature today.” A useful feature is not a deliberate instant invention. Describe the feature and its function without giving the plant a conscious plan.

“Every animal in a cold place hibernates.” Species use different structures and behaviours. Check the particular animal and seasonal conditions.

10-MINUTE ACTIVITY

Build three survival explanations

2 minutes: choose mulga, little penguin or bogong moth from the supplied table. 4 minutes: write feature/action → function → habitat benefit. 2 minutes: compare a second example. 2 minutes: name one limit of a cloth or container model.

Adjust the challenge Support: use the three-part sentence frame. Extend: explain why a helpful feature does not guarantee survival after a major habitat change.

Use pictures and supplied information. Do not handle wildlife, disturb shelters or remove plant material.

Three tricky questions

1. Is thick insulation always the best feature in every habitat?

No. It slows heat transfer and can be useful in cold conditions, but heat loss can be helpful in hot conditions. The habitat and other ways the animal manages temperature matter.

2. A moth moves to a cooler shelter. Is the movement a structural feature?

No. Movement is a behaviour. A wing is a structural feature that can support that movement.

3. Does a useful survival feature make food and water unnecessary?

No. Features and behaviours help organisms meet needs; they do not remove those needs or guarantee survival.

FAQ

Can plants have adaptations?

Yes. Plant structures such as water-conserving surfaces can support survival in particular conditions.

Must I explain evolution in detail here?

No. Focus on the particular feature or behaviour, its function and the habitat benefit. Do not suggest individuals invent inherited traits because they need them.

What students learn in AC9S5U01

Students distinguish structural features from behaviours, connect them to environmental conditions and explain how a feature can improve—not guarantee—survival and reproduction.

An adaptation is an inherited structural or behavioural feature that increases success in a particular environment across generations.

A feature is not automatically adaptive in every habitat. State the environmental pressure, mechanism and likely effect on survival or reproduction.

Correlation alone may not prove the feature caused success. Consider other traits, changing environments and whether the evidence comes from repeated observations.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Content description: examine how particular structural features and behaviours of living things enable their survival in specific habitats.

  • E1: identify physical and behavioural characteristics that enable a plant or animal to survive, such as being able to see in dim light and being nocturnal
  • E2: exploring features of plants and animals that enable them to survive in Australia’s desert environments, such as bottle (or boab) trees and the water-holding frog
  • E3: investigating how camouflage is used by animals to hide from predators and to ambush prey
  • E4: using physical or digital simulations to explore how the shape of animals’ body parts, such as the beak of a particular bird species, influence their ability to find food and survive in a given environment
  • E5: investigating First Nations Australians’ knowledges of the structural features of certain species and how those features can be exploited

Common misconceptions
  • Any useful action is an inherited adaptation — Some behaviours are learned or immediate responses.
  • Organisms choose adaptations because they need them — Adaptations become common across generations through differential survival and reproduction.
  • One feature guarantees survival — Several conditions and trade-offs affect success.
  • Feature described without mechanism — Explain how it changes access, protection, temperature, water or reproduction.
