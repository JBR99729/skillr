Point of view and reader response

Recognise that the point of view in a literary text influences how readers interpret and respond to events and characters.

Core idea: Point of view controls what readers know, notice and trust, so the same event can feel different when told from another perspective.

Key conceptTeach from the board

Routine

who tells → what they know → evidence → interpretation → reader response

Direct IXL evidence: IXL directly supports comparing and contrasting points of view using evidence from two viewpoint texts.

Coverage boundary: IXL evidence is strongest for viewpoint comparison; extended discussion of varied reader responses is a curriculum-level extension.

Worked examplesModel thinking
  • two students describing the same school musical: identify the decisive clue, name it precisely and explain what it does.
  • siblings remembering a camping trip differently: identify the decisive clue, name it precisely and explain what it does.
  • a goalkeeper and striker describing one match: identify the decisive clue, name it precisely and explain what it does.
  • a child and parent describing a storm: identify the decisive clue, name it precisely and explain what it does.
Questions and answersCheck understanding
  • What evidence matters most, and why?
  • Which term fits the evidence precisely?
  • What is the likely misconception?
  • How would the answer change in a new context?
MisconceptionsCatch the trap
  • confusing viewpoint with a character trait
  • assuming the narrator is neutral
  • using only one passage when comparing two viewpoints
  • claiming every reader must respond identically
