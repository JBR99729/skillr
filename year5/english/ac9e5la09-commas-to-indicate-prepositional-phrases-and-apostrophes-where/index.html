AC9E5LA09: use commas to indicate prepositional phrases, and apostrophes where there is multiple possession

Identify the whole prepositional phrase before punctuating it, and decide exactly who owns what before placing an apostrophe.

Classroom ViewWorksheetBrowse app practice →Browse app practice →
Key concept: prepositional phrases

A prepositional phrase begins with a preposition and includes its object: under the cushion, before sunrise, across the court. It can express place, time, movement, accompaniment or another relationship.

Trap: do not select only the preposition. In “under the old bridge”, the whole phrase is under the old bridge.

Introductory phrases and commas

When a prepositional phrase comes before the main clause, a comma can make the boundary clear, especially when the phrase is substantial: During the final minutes of the match, the crowd became silent.

Direct IXL evidence in the research supports identifying prepositional phrases, but it did not directly provide a mapped skill for comma placement after them. SkillrHub therefore teaches the comma component as a curriculum extension and avoids claiming direct IXL verification for it.

Possessive apostrophes
  • one dog → the dog’s collar
  • several dogs → the dogs’ leashes
  • irregular plural children → the children’s games

Never add an apostrophe just to make a plural: three dogs, not three dog’s.

Shared or separate ownership?

Sam and Priya’s project can show one jointly owned project. Sam’s and Priya’s projects makes separate ownership clear when each has a different project. Work out the ownership relationship before punctuating.

Common traps
  • preposition alone mistaken for the full phrase
  • phrase confused with a dependent clause
  • apostrophe added to an ordinary plural
  • regular and irregular plural possessives confused
  • shared possession and separate possession treated as identical
  • comma placed inside rather than after the introductory phrase
Research note

IXL directly supports prepositional phrase identification and possessive classification/formation. Direct IXL evidence for the comma-after-prepositional-phrase part of the descriptor was not observed, so that component remains explicitly identified as curriculum-extension teaching.
