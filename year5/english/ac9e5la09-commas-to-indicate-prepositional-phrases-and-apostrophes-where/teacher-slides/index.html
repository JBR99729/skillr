Year 5 English · AC9E5LA09

Phrase boundary first; punctuation second

Identify the complete prepositional phrase and the ownership meaning before making comma or apostrophe decisions.

Learning intention
  • I can select a complete prepositional phrase.
  • I can identify its preposition and object.
  • I can use a comma to clarify an introductory phrase boundary.
  • I can distinguish plurals from possessives.
  • I can show singular, plural, shared and separate possession.
Board 1: complete phrase

under the old bridge = preposition under + object noun group the old bridge.

Do not select only under.

Board 2: introductory comma

During the final minutes of the match, the crowd became silent.

Mark the whole introductory phrase first; then place the comma at its end when the boundary needs clarification.

Board 3: apostrophe meaning
  • dogs = more than one dog
  • dog’s collar = one dog owns the collar
  • dogs’ leashes = several dogs own the leashes
  • children’s games = irregular plural possessive
Board 4: shared versus separate

Sam and Priya’s project → one shared project.

Sam’s and Priya’s projects → separate projects.

Meaning first, apostrophe placement second.

Quick checks
  1. Full phrase in “beside the creek” → beside the creek.
  2. Simple plural: dog’s or dogs? → dogs.
  3. Several teachers own the lounge → teachers’ lounge.
  4. Irregular plural: women’s team.
Exit ticket

Edit: Before the concert the musicians instruments were checked.

Students should add the phrase-boundary comma and correct the plural possessive: Before the concert, the musicians’ instruments were checked.
