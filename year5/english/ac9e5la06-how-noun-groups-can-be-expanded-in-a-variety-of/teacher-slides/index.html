Expand the noun, not the clutter

Teach students to identify the head noun, then add only details that improve precision, identification or effect.

Learning intention

We are learning to build expanded noun groups for fuller description.

  • I can find the head noun.
  • I can identify what each modifier contributes.
  • I can order adjectives naturally.
  • I can add qualifiers after the noun.
  • I can remove vague or repetitive description.
Board 1: unpack the noun group

the three curious young magpies beside the gate

  • the → determiner
  • three → quantity
  • curious young → description
  • magpies → head noun
  • beside the gate → qualifier
Board 2: adjective order
Natural

a small red ball

an old wooden chair

three tiny green frogs

Awkward

a red small ball

a wooden old chair

green three tiny frogs

Board 3: precision versus pile-up

Weak: the amazing wonderful fantastic incredible garden

Stronger: the shaded native garden beside the library

Ask: what distinct information does each modifier add?

Board 4: purpose changes description
Science

the small wingless insect

Narrative

the narrow rusted door at the end of the corridor

Instructions

the fragile glass instrument

Different purposes favour different kinds of precision.

Quick checks
  1. Head noun: “those two ancient stone towers” → towers.
  2. Qualifier: “the museum near the river” → near the river.
  3. More natural: “old wooden chair” or “wooden old chair”? → old wooden chair.
  4. Why remove repeated adjectives? → They add clutter rather than distinct meaning.
Exit ticket

Expand the bird for two different purposes:

  1. a factual field guide
  2. a suspenseful story

Underline the head noun and circle the detail that most strongly suits each purpose.
