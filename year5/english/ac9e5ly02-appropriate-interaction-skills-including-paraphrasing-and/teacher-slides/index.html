CurriculumStart here

Students learn to use appropriate interaction skills including paraphrasing and questioning to clarify meaning, make connections to own experience, and present and justify an opinion or idea. They move from recognising the feature to explaining how it works in context, then applying it deliberately in their own reading, speaking, writing or multimodal creation.

Core idea: In English, choices in language, structure, literature and mode create meaning for a particular audience and purpose. A strong answer names the choice, gives evidence and explains the effect.

Success looks like

  • identify the relevant feature, strategy or text choice
  • use precise English metalanguage
  • support the answer with specific evidence
  • explain the effect in context
  • apply or revise the choice for a new audience and purpose

Assessment and reviewReady for practice
  • First draft is final — Plan, draft, revise for meaning, edit for accuracy and publish for audience.
  • More features automatically improve a text — Choose features that serve purpose and audience.
  • Presentation means reading slides — Use voice, pace, visuals and structure to develop the idea.
  • Feedback treated as correction only — Use criteria to decide what to keep, improve or remove.
