AC9E5LE02 • Year 5 English

AC9E5LE02: present an opinion on a literary text using specific terms about literary devices, text structures and language features, and reflect on the viewpoints of others

Move beyond “I liked it” by naming literary devices, structures or language features, explaining their effects and considering how another reader may interpret the text.

Classroom ViewWorksheetBrowse app practice →Browse app practice →
Response model

Opinion → literary term/evidence → effect → another viewpoint → reflection.

Example: “The scene feels threatening because the personification ‘branches clawed at the windows’ makes the setting seem hostile. Another reader might see the storm as exciting, but the repeated references to darkness support my interpretation.”

Useful terms

simile, metaphor, personification, sensory imagery, point of view, setting, plot, conflict, dialogue, tone, mood and theme.

Naming a device is only the first step. Explain what the feature suggests, how it shapes a character or setting, or how it affects the reader.

Reflect on viewpoints

Two readers can reach different interpretations when each is supported by relevant textual evidence. Reflection means considering another view fairly, then explaining whether it strengthens, changes or challenges your own response.

Common traps
  • device label with no effect
  • plot summary instead of opinion
  • personal preference treated as proof
  • dismissing another reader rather than responding to evidence
  • claiming a tone or theme without textual clues
Evidence note

IXL directly supports sensory detail, simile/metaphor, figure-of-speech effects, point of view and story elements.
