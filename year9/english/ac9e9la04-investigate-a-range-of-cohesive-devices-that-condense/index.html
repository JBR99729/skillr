Cohesive devices that condense and connect ideas

This topic helps students understand how mature writing holds complex ideas together. Cohesive devices link sentences and paragraphs, avoid unnecessary repetition, and condense information so writing sounds precise and fluent.

Start practiceTake testOpen worksheet

Core understanding

What students need to know

Reference

Pronouns and noun groups refer back to earlier ideas, such as this issue, these actions or such evidence.

Substitution

Writers can replace repeated wording with shorter references so paragraphs do not sound clumsy.

Nominalisation

Actions or descriptions can be turned into noun groups to summarise earlier information.

Logical links

Connectives such as however, therefore and consequently show relationships between ideas.

English language tools

Useful terms and ways to analyse the idea

reference: this, these, such, it, they
ellipsis: leaving out repeated words when meaning remains clear
connectives: however, therefore, meanwhile, consequently
nominalisation: decided → the decision; pollute → pollution

Worked example

Worked example: condensing information

  1. Repetitive: “The committee delayed the decision. The committee delayed the decision because the committee wanted more evidence.”
  2. Improved: “The committee delayed the decision because it wanted more evidence.”
  3. More condensed: “The delay reflected the committee's need for more evidence.”
  4. The noun group “the delay” summarises the action and helps the sentence flow.
  5. A strong writer chooses cohesion without making meaning unclear.

Common misconceptions

What to watch for

  • Using pronouns when the reader cannot tell what they refer to.
  • Adding connectives randomly instead of matching the logical relationship.
  • Thinking longer sentences are automatically better.
  • Condensing so much that the meaning becomes vague.

Quick check

Try these before moving on

  1. Rewrite “the school changed the rule and the rule upset students” using a cohesive reference.
  2. Choose a connective that shows contrast: therefore, however or meanwhile.
  3. Turn “students protested” into a noun group that could summarise the action.

Support tip

For parents and teachers

Ask students to highlight repeated words in a paragraph. Then have them decide which repetition is useful for clarity and which can be replaced, condensed or linked.
