Year 9 English • AC9E9LA01 • Topic guide

Language, power, relationships and roles

This topic helps students notice how language does social work. Word choice, tone, formality, modality and address terms can show authority, build closeness, exclude people, challenge power or position a reader to agree.

Start practiceTake testOpen worksheet

Core understanding

What students need to know

Power

Commands, high modality and expert vocabulary can establish authority or control.

Relationship

Pronouns, greetings and formality can create closeness, distance, respect or conflict.

Role

Language can signal whether someone is speaking as a friend, leader, expert, witness or critic.

Positioning

Readers can be guided to see a person, issue or group in a particular way.

English language tools

Useful terms and ways to analyse the idea

modality: must, should, might, could
tone: respectful, sarcastic, urgent, authoritative
inclusive pronouns: we, us, our
register: formal, informal, technical, conversational

Worked example

Worked example: analysing power in a sentence

  1. Sentence: “You must submit the report by 5 pm.”
  2. The modal verb “must” creates obligation and high authority.
  3. The direct address “you” clearly positions the reader as responsible.
  4. The exact deadline makes the instruction firm and non-negotiable.
  5. A strong answer links language choices to the relationship between speaker and reader.

Common misconceptions

What to watch for

  • Only naming a technique without explaining its effect.
  • Assuming formal language is always more powerful than informal language.
  • Ignoring context, audience and purpose.
  • Confusing the speaker's role with the author's own identity.

Quick check

Try these before moving on

  1. How does “we need to act now” position the audience?
  2. Rewrite a command so it sounds more collaborative.
  3. Identify one language choice that could show respect in a formal email.

Support tip

For parents and teachers

Ask students to use the sentence frame: “The language choice ___ positions the audience as ___ because ___.” This keeps analysis focused on effect, not just technique spotting.
