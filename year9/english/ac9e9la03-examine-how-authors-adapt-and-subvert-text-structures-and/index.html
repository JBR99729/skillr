Adapting and subverting text structures

This topic helps students analyse how writers work with genre expectations. Authors may follow familiar structures to guide readers, adapt them for a new purpose, or subvert them to surprise, challenge or critique expectations.

Core understanding

What students need to know

Genre convention

A convention is an expected feature, such as a mystery clue, persuasive call to action or news headline.

Adaptation

An author adapts structure when they reshape a familiar form for a new audience, context or purpose.

Subversion

An author subverts structure when they deliberately break expectations to create meaning or surprise.

Effect

The key question is not just what changed, but why the author changed it and how readers respond.

English language tools

Useful terms and ways to analyse the idea

orientation, complication, climax, resolution
chronological, circular or fragmented structure
genre conventions and reader expectations
hybrid texts combine forms, modes or purposes

Worked example

Worked example: explaining subversion

  1. A detective story usually reveals clues gradually and solves the mystery at the end.
  2. A writer might reveal the criminal in the opening paragraph.
  3. This subverts the usual mystery structure because the question changes from “who did it?” to “why did they do it?”
  4. The effect is to shift reader attention from solution to motive and psychology.
  5. A strong response links the structural change to meaning and reader response.

Common misconceptions

What to watch for

  • Calling any unusual feature a subversion without identifying the normal convention.
  • Describing structure without explaining effect.
  • Forgetting that adaptation can be subtle, not always dramatic.
  • Ignoring multimodal structures in digital texts, films and webpages.

Quick check

Try these before moving on

  1. Name one convention of a persuasive speech.
  2. How could a news article subvert a reader's expectation?
  3. Explain why a circular ending might suit a reflective story.

Support tip

For parents and teachers

Use a three-step prompt: identify the expected convention, describe what the author does differently, then explain the effect on meaning or audience response.
