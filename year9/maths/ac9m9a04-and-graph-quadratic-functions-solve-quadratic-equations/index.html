Quadratic functions, graphs and equations

Quadratics are the next major step after linear relationships. Instead of a constant rate of change, a quadratic creates a curved graph called a parabola. Students connect tables, equations and graphs, then use factorising and graph features to solve equations.

Core understanding

What students need to understand

A quadratic has a squared variable

A relation such as y = x² or y = x² + 3x + 2 is quadratic because the highest power of x is 2.

The graph is a parabola

A positive x² coefficient opens upward; a negative x² coefficient opens downward. The turning point is the minimum or maximum.

Roots are x-intercepts

Solving a quadratic equation often means finding where the graph crosses the x-axis.

Factorising links algebra and graphing

If y = (x − 2)(x + 5), the x-intercepts are x = 2 and x = −5.

Key tools

Formulas and representations

standard form: y = ax² + bx + c

factorised form: y = a(x − r)(x − s)

x-intercepts occur where y = 0

Choose the formula or representation that matches the question. In Year 9, marks often come from defining variables, showing substitutions and interpreting the result.

Worked example

Worked example: solve x² − 5x + 6 = 0

  1. Look for two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to −5: −2 and −3.
  2. Factorise: x² − 5x + 6 = (x − 2)(x − 3).
  3. Set each factor to zero: x − 2 = 0 or x − 3 = 0.
  4. Solutions: x = 2 or x = 3.
  5. Graph interpretation: the parabola crosses the x-axis at 2 and 3.

Quick check

Try these before practice

  1. For y = x² − 4, state the two x-intercepts.
  2. Explain why y = −x² opens downward.
  3. Factorise x² + 7x + 12 and identify the roots.

Common misconceptions

Watch for these mistakes

  • Treating x² as if it were just 2x.
  • Forgetting that a quadratic can have two, one or no real x-intercepts.
  • Thinking the y-intercept and x-intercepts are the same thing.
  • Solving only one factor after factorising.

Support tip

For parents and teachers

Encourage students to move between table, graph and equation. The best understanding comes from seeing that the same quadratic can be represented in several connected ways.

Next steps

Build fluency

Use the topic guide first, then practise until the method feels natural. The test should be used only after students can explain the method, not just copy steps.
