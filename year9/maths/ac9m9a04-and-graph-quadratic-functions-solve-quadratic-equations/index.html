Quadratic Functions and Equations

Tables, parabolas, factors and roots are connected representations of a quadratic relationship.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

Tables, parabolas, factors and roots are connected representations of a quadratic relationship.

Learning intention: Tables, parabolas, factors and roots are connected representations of a quadratic relationship.

Success criteria

  • Explain spot constant second differences using the approved model.
  • Apply graph changing moon illumination to a new case.
  • Verify a conclusion with a second representation, estimate or test case.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • identify and graph quadratic functions, solve quadratic equations graphically and numerically, and solve monic quadratic equations with integer roots algebraically, using graphing software and digital tools as appropriate

Prerequisites

  • Relevant Year 8 algebra representations and vocabulary.

May support informally

  • Digital tools may test, visualise and verify after the mathematical structure is explicit.

Do not overteach

  • Do not introduce later-year formalism that is unnecessary for this content description.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • A rule is applied without preserving equivalence or its conditions — Return to the linked table, graph or expanded form and verify by substitution.
  • The quadratic functions and equations model is copied without interpretation — Connect every label in ‘Recognise that in a table of values, if the second difference between consecutive values of the dependent variable is constant, then it is a quadratic.’ to its mathematical role.
  • The result is not verified — Use a second representation, boundary case, inverse operation or simulation check.
Support, core and extend

Support: Reduce numerical or representational load while retaining the same decision and explanation.

Core: Connect at least two representations, justify the method and verify independently.

Extend: Test a boundary case, counterexample or model limitation without introducing later-year procedures.
