Choosing Data Displays

The most useful display depends on data type, question, audience and the comparison that must remain visible.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
What students learn

The most useful display depends on data type, question, audience and the comparison that must remain visible.

Learning intention: The most useful display depends on data type, question, audience and the comparison that must remain visible.

Success criteria

  • Explain match display to centre and spread using the approved model.
  • Apply critique an ai-generated visualisation to a new case.
  • Verify a conclusion with a second representation, estimate or test case.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • choose appropriate forms of display or visualisation for a given type of data; justify selections and interpret displays for a given context

Prerequisites

  • Relevant Year 8 statistics representations and vocabulary.

May support informally

  • Digital tools may test, visualise and verify after the mathematical structure is explicit.

Do not overteach

  • Do not introduce later-year formalism that is unnecessary for this content description.

Worked examples

Match display to centre and spread

Compare data displays using mean, median and range to describe and interpret numerical data sets in terms of centre and spread using histograms, dot plots, or stem-and-leaf plots.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • A display or sample is treated as proof of a stronger claim — Link the inference to sampling, variation, context and uncertainty.
  • The choosing data displays model is copied without interpretation — Connect every label in ‘Compare data displays using mean, median and range to describe and interpret numerical data sets in terms of centre and spread using histograms, dot plots, or stem-and-leaf plots.’ to its mathematical role.
  • The result is not verified — Use a second representation, boundary case, inverse operation or simulation check.
Support, core and extend

Support: Reduce numerical or representational load while retaining the same decision and explanation.

Core: Connect at least two representations, justify the method and verify independently.

Extend: Test a boundary case, counterexample or model limitation without introducing later-year procedures.
