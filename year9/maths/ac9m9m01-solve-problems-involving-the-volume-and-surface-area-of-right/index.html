AC9M9M01 • Year 9 Maths • Measurement

Surface Area and Volume

Surface area measures exposed covering while volume measures occupied space; equal volume does not imply equal surface area.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
What students learn

Surface area measures exposed covering while volume measures occupied space; equal volume does not imply equal surface area.

Learning intention: Surface area measures exposed covering while volume measures occupied space; equal volume does not imply equal surface area.

Success criteria

  • Explain build surface-area formulas from nets using the approved model.
  • Apply connect capacity, surface area and design to a new case.
  • Verify a conclusion with a second representation, estimate or test case.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • solve problems involving the volume and surface area of right prisms and cylinders using appropriate units

Prerequisites

  • Relevant Year 8 measurement representations and vocabulary.

May support informally

  • Digital tools may test, visualise and verify after the mathematical structure is explicit.

Do not overteach

  • Do not introduce later-year formalism that is unnecessary for this content description.

Worked examples

Build surface-area formulas from nets

Analyse nets of objects to generate short cuts and establish formulas for surface area.

Teacher prompt: For build surface-area formulas from nets, which algebraic step preserves equivalence, and why?

Minimise material for a cylinder

Determine the amount of material needed to make can-coolers for a class fundraising project and working out the most cost-efficient way to cut out the pieces.

Teacher prompt: For minimise material for a cylinder, what changes, what stays invariant, and why?

Hold volume constant and vary surface area

Find different prisms that have the same volume but different surface areas, making conjectures as to what type of prism would have the smallest or largest surface area.

Teacher prompt: For hold volume constant and vary surface area, which units, bounds or accuracy statement belong in the result?

Connect capacity, surface area and design

Explore objects and technologies of First Nations Australians, analysing and connecting surface area and volume, and exploring their relationship to their capacity.

Teacher prompt: For connect capacity, surface area and design, which units, bounds or accuracy statement belong in the result?

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Dimensions, units or accuracy are mixed — Label every measure and convert before calculating.
  • The surface area and volume model is copied without interpretation — Connect every label in ‘Analyse nets of objects to generate short cuts and establish formulas for surface area.’ to its mathematical role.
  • The result is not verified — Use a second representation, boundary case, inverse operation or simulation check.
Support, core and extend

Support: Reduce numerical or representational load while retaining the same decision and explanation.

Core: Connect at least two representations, justify the method and verify independently.

Extend: Test a boundary case, counterexample or model limitation without introducing later-year procedures.
