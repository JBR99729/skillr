Surface area and volume of right prisms and cylinders

This topic develops practical measurement skills for solids such as boxes, tanks, pipes, prisms and cylinders. Students learn to separate volume from surface area, choose the correct formula and interpret answers using appropriate units.

Core understanding

What students need to know

Key idea

Volume measures the space inside a solid and uses cubic units such as cm³ or m³.

Key idea

Surface area measures the exposed outside area and uses square units such as cm² or m².

Key idea

Right prisms have a constant cross-section, so volume can be found using cross-section area × length.

Key idea

Cylinder problems often require π and careful rounding.

Formulas and representations

Useful rules and ways to represent the idea

Prism volume: V = area of cross-section × length
Cylinder volume: V = πr²h
Cylinder curved surface area: 2πrh
Total surface area adds all exposed faces

Worked example

Worked example: cylinder volume

  1. A cylinder has radius 4 cm and height 10 cm.
  2. Use V = πr²h.
  3. V = π × 4² × 10 = 160π cm³.
  4. So V ≈ 502.7 cm³, depending on the required rounding.

Common misconceptions

What to watch for

  • Using square units for volume or cubic units for area.
  • Forgetting to include circular ends when total surface area is needed.
  • Using diameter as radius without halving it.
  • Rounding too early, especially when π is involved.

Support tip

For parents and teachers

Encourage students to draw and label the solid, identify whether the question asks for inside space or outside covering, then choose volume or surface area before substituting numbers.
