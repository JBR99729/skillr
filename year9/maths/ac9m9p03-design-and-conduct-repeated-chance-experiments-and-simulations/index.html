Chance Experiments and Simulations

A simulation must reproduce the rules and dependencies of the real chance process before its frequencies can estimate probability.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
What students learn

A simulation must reproduce the rules and dependencies of the real chance process before its frequencies can estimate probability.

Learning intention: A simulation must reproduce the rules and dependencies of the real chance process before its frequencies can estimate probability.

Success criteria

  • Explain simulate simple and compound events using the approved model.
  • Apply estimate winning in a repeated game to a new case.
  • Verify a conclusion with a second representation, estimate or test case.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • design and conduct repeated chance experiments and simulations, using digital tools to compare probabilities of simple events to related compound events, and describe results

Prerequisites

  • Relevant Year 8 probability representations and vocabulary.

May support informally

  • Digital tools may test, visualise and verify after the mathematical structure is explicit.

Do not overteach

  • Do not introduce later-year formalism that is unnecessary for this content description.

Worked examples

Simulate simple and compound events

Use digital tools to conduct probability simulations that demonstrate the relationship between the probability of compound events and the individual probabilities.

Teacher prompt: For simulate simple and compound events, which outcomes belong in the denominator, and why?

Compare replacement methods

Compare experiments which differ only by being undertaken with replacement or without replacement.

Teacher prompt: For compare replacement methods, which outcomes belong in the denominator, and why?

List favourable outcomes systematically

Conduct two-step chance experiments using systematic methods to list outcomes of experiments and to list outcomes favourable to an event.

Teacher prompt: For list favourable outcomes systematically, which outcomes belong in the denominator, and why?

Estimate winning in a repeated game

Gorri from all parts of Australia, to calculate the probabilities of winning and not winning.

Teacher prompt: For estimate winning in a repeated game, what changes, what stays invariant, and why?

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • The sample space or dependency changes without being noticed — Rebuild the outcomes and label replacement or conditioning explicitly.
  • The chance experiments and simulations model is copied without interpretation — Connect every label in ‘Use digital tools to conduct probability simulations that demonstrate the relationship between the probability of compound events and the individual probabilities.’ to its mathematical role.
  • The result is not verified — Use a second representation, boundary case, inverse operation or simulation check.
Support, core and extend

Support: Reduce numerical or representational load while retaining the same decision and explanation.

Core: Connect at least two representations, justify the method and verify independently.

Extend: Test a boundary case, counterexample or model limitation without introducing later-year procedures.
