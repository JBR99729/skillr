Year 9 Maths • AC9M9A01 • Topic guide

Index laws, integer exponents and scientific notation

Index laws help students simplify repeated multiplication and work fluently with very large and very small quantities. Year 9 students need more than memorised rules: they should understand why powers combine, why a zero power equals 1, and why negative powers create reciprocals.

What students need to understand

Core ideas

Multiplying powers

When bases are the same, add indices: a^m × a^n = a^(m+n).

Dividing powers

When bases are the same, subtract indices: a^m ÷ a^n = a^(m−n), where a is not zero.

Power of a power

Multiply the indices: (a^m)^n = a^(mn).

Zero and negative powers

a^0 = 1 for a ≠ 0. A negative exponent means reciprocal: a^-n = 1/a^n.

Worked thinking

How the idea works

Simplify with the same base

2^5 × 2^3 = 2^8 because there are five factors of 2 and three more factors of 2. The result has eight factors of 2.

Negative exponent

3^-2 = 1/3^2 = 1/9. The negative index does not make the answer negative; it moves the power to the denominator.

Skill building

Quick checks before practice

  1. Simplify x^4 × x^7.
  2. Simplify 5^6 ÷ 5^2.
  3. Evaluate 10^-3 as a decimal.
  4. Write 72 000 in scientific notation.

These questions are designed to expose the common gap before students begin the SkillrHub practice set.

Common mistakes

Misconceptions to fix early

  • Do not add indices when the bases are different. 2^3 × 3^3 is not 6^6.
  • A negative exponent does not mean a negative number. It means reciprocal.
  • a^0 = 1, not 0, when a is not zero.

Support at home

Parent and teacher tip

Check whether the student explains the rule or only quotes it. Ask them to expand 2^3 × 2^2 as factors of 2; this quickly reveals whether the index law makes sense.
