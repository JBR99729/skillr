Integer Exponents and Variables

Exponent laws extend to negative integer powers and algebraic variables when bases, operations and non-zero conditions are respected.

What students learn

Exponent laws extend to negative integer powers and algebraic variables when bases, operations and non-zero conditions are respected.

Learning intention: Exponent laws extend to negative integer powers and algebraic variables when bases, operations and non-zero conditions are respected.

Success criteria

  • Explain write decimals with powers of ten using the approved model.
  • Apply connect si prefixes to powers of ten to a new case.
  • Verify a conclusion with a second representation, estimate or test case.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • apply the exponent laws to numerical expressions with integer exponents and extend to variables

Prerequisites

  • Relevant Year 8 algebra representations and vocabulary.

May support informally

Do not overteach

  • Do not introduce later-year formalism that is unnecessary for this content description.

Worked examples

Write decimals with powers of ten

0.475 can be represented as 0.475 = 4/10+7/100+5/1000 = 4×10^(-1)+7×10^(-2)+5×10^(-3) and 0.00023 as 23×10^(-5).

Teacher prompt: For write decimals with powers of ten, which algebraic step preserves equivalence, and why?

Interpret negative exponents

5^(-3)=(1)/(5³)=(1/5)³=1/125 and connecting terms of the sequence 125, 25, 5, 1, 1/5, 1/25, 1/125 and so on to terms of the sequence 5³, 5², 5^1, 5^0,5^(-1),5^(-2),5^(-3).

Teacher prompt: For interpret negative exponents, which algebraic step preserves equivalence, and why?

Connect calculations to exponent laws

2³÷2^5 = 2^(-2) = (1)/(2²)=1/4 and (3×5)² = 3²×5² = 9×25 = 225.

Teacher prompt: For connect calculations to exponent laws, which algebraic step preserves equivalence, and why?

Apply exponent conventions to variables

Recognise exponents in algebraic expressions and applying the relevant exponent laws and corresponding conventions.

Teacher prompt: For apply exponent conventions to variables, which algebraic step preserves equivalence, and why?

Build algebraic laws from first principles

(a²)³ = (a× a) × (a× a) × (a× a) = a× a× a× a× a× a = a^6.

Teacher prompt: For build algebraic laws from first principles, which algebraic step preserves equivalence, and why?

Simplify products, quotients and powers

((2xy)³)/(xy^4) = (8x³y³)/(xy^4) = 8x²y^(-1).

Teacher prompt: For simplify products, quotients and powers, which algebraic step preserves equivalence, and why?

Connect SI prefixes to powers of ten

One pico-gram = 10^(-12) gram and one terabyte = 10^(12) bytes.

Teacher prompt: For connect si prefixes to powers of ten, what changes, what stays invariant, and why?

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Write decimals with powers of ten

Curriculum: representing decimals in exponential form; for example, 0.475 can be represented as 0.475\;=\;\frac4{10}+\frac7{100}+\frac5{1000}\;=\;4\times10^{-1}+7\times10^{-2}+5\times10^{-3} and 0.00023 as 23\times10^{-5}

Look for: Use the model evidence: 0.475 can be represented as 0.475 = 4/10+7/100+5/1000 = 4×10^(-1)+7×10^(-2)+5×10^(-3) and 0.00023 as 23×10^(-5). Then name the deciding relationship or limitation in a complete mathematical sentence.

E2: Interpret negative exponents

Curriculum: simplifying and evaluating numerical expressions, involving both positive and negative integer exponents, explaining why; for example, 5^{-3}=\frac1{5^3}=(\frac15)^3=\frac1{125} and connecting terms of the sequence 125, 25, 5, 1, \frac15, \frac1{25}, \frac1{125}… to terms of the sequence 5^3, 5^2, 5^1, 5^0,5^{-1},5^{-2},5^{-3}...

E4: Apply exponent conventions to variables

Curriculum: recognising exponents in algebraic expressions and applying the relevant exponent laws and corresponding conventions; for example, for any non-zero natural number a, a^0\;=\;1, x^1\;=\;x, r^2\;=\;r\times r, h^3\;=\;h\times h\times h, y^4\;=\;y\times y\times y\times y, and \frac1{w} \times \frac1{w}=\frac1{w^2} = w^{-2}

E5: Build algebraic laws from first principles

Curriculum: relating simplification of expressions from first principles and counting to the use of exponent laws; for example, (a^2)^3\;=\;(a\times a)\;\times\;(a\times a)\;\times\;(a\times a)\;=\;a\times a\times a\times a\times a\times a\;=\;a^6; b^2\times b^3\;=\;(b\times b)\times(b\times b\times b)\;=\;b\times b\times b\times b\times b\;=\;b^5; \frac{y^4}{y^2}\;=\;\frac{y\times y\times y\times y}{y\times y}\;=\;\frac{y^2}1\;=\;y^2 and (5a)^2\;=\;(5\times a)\times(5\times a)\;=\;5\times5\times a\times a\;=\;25\times a^2\;=\;25a^2

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • A rule is applied without preserving equivalence or its conditions — Return to the linked table, graph or expanded form and verify by substitution.
  • The integer exponents and variables model is copied without interpretation — Connect every label in ‘0.475 can be represented as 0.475 = 4/10+7/100+5/1000 = 4×10^(-1)+7×10^(-2)+5×10^(-3) and 0.00023 as 23×10^(-5).’ to its mathematical role.
  • The result is not verified — Use a second representation, boundary case, inverse operation or simulation check.
