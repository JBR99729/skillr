Sampling methods, bias and data representation

This topic focuses on how data is collected. Students compare random, convenience, volunteer and stratified sampling, and explain how sample choice can change survey results.

Start practiceTake testOpen homework

Core understanding

What students need to know

Key idea

A sample should represent the population being studied.

Key idea

Convenience and volunteer samples are often easier to collect but may be biased.

Key idea

Random sampling can reduce bias when done carefully.

Key idea

Data representation choices can make results look more or less dramatic.

Formulas and representations

Useful rules and ways to represent the idea

population = the whole group of interest
sample = the smaller group actually surveyed
sampling bias occurs when some groups are over-represented or under-represented
a fair graph uses clear labels, sensible scale and honest categories

Worked example

Worked example: identifying sampling bias

  1. A school asks only students in the chess club whether the school should buy more chess sets.
  2. The population may be all students, but the sample is only chess club members.
  3. Chess club members are more likely to support buying chess sets.
  4. The sample is biased because it does not represent the whole school.
  5. A better method would sample students from different year levels and interest groups.

Common misconceptions

What to watch for

  • Thinking a large sample is always fair even if it comes from one biased group.
  • Using “random” to mean “whoever was nearby”.
  • Forgetting to define the population before judging the sample.
  • Using graph scales that exaggerate small differences.

Quick check

Try these before moving on

  1. Identify the population in a survey about Year 9 students’ homework habits.
  2. Why might an online poll be biased?
  3. Describe one way to make a school survey more representative.

Support tip

For parents and teachers

Have students compare two ways of sampling the same population. The key question is: who might be missed by this method?
