Relative frequency, Venn diagrams and simulation

This topic connects collected data with probability. Students calculate relative frequency, compare it with theoretical probability, and use representations such as Venn diagrams, tables and simulations to reason about chance.

Start practiceTake testOpen homework

Core understanding

What students need to know

Key idea

Relative frequency is the fraction or proportion of trials where an event happens.

Key idea

Experimental probability comes from results; theoretical probability comes from the expected model.

Key idea

As trials increase, relative frequency often becomes more stable.

Key idea

Venn diagrams help show overlapping events such as students who play sport, music or both.

Formulas and representations

Useful rules and ways to represent the idea

relative frequency = number of times event occurs ÷ total number of trials
P(A or B) counts all outcomes in A, B or both
P(A and B) counts outcomes in the overlap
expected frequency ≈ probability × number of trials

Worked example

Worked example: calculate relative frequency

  1. A spinner lands on blue 18 times in 60 spins.
  2. Relative frequency of blue = 18 ÷ 60.
  3. 18 ÷ 60 = 0.3.
  4. The experimental probability of blue is 0.3, or 30%.
  5. If the theoretical probability was 1/4, the experiment was close but not exact.

Common misconceptions

What to watch for

  • Thinking experimental results must exactly match theoretical probability.
  • Using the number of successful trials as the denominator instead of total trials.
  • Double-counting the overlap in a Venn diagram.
  • Assuming a short simulation is always reliable.

Quick check

Try these before moving on

  1. A coin lands heads 47 times in 100 tosses. Find the relative frequency of heads.
  2. Explain why 20 trials may be less reliable than 500 trials.
  3. In a Venn diagram, why should the overlap be counted carefully?

Support tip

For parents and teachers

Use real class data or quick digital simulations. Students should compare the relative frequency with what they expected and explain why they may differ.
