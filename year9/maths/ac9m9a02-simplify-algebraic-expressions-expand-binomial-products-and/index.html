Quadratic Expressions

Expansion and factorisation are inverse ways to represent the same quadratic structure.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

Expansion and factorisation are inverse ways to represent the same quadratic structure.

Learning intention: Expansion and factorisation are inverse ways to represent the same quadratic structure.

Success criteria

  • Explain expand two binomials using the approved model.
  • Apply reverse expansion to factorise to a new case.
  • Verify a conclusion with a second representation, estimate or test case.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • simplify algebraic expressions, expand binomial products and factorise monic quadratic expressions

Prerequisites

  • Relevant Year 8 algebra representations and vocabulary.

May support informally

  • Digital tools may test, visualise and verify after the mathematical structure is explicit.

Do not overteach

  • Do not introduce later-year formalism that is unnecessary for this content description.

Worked examples

Expand two binomials

(x+7)(x+8), (x+7)(x-8), (x-7)(x+8), (x-7)(x-8) to identify expansion and factorisation patterns related to (x+a)(x+b) = x²+(a+b)x+ab, where a and b are integers.

Use an area model for quadratics

(x+1)(x+3) = x²+4x+3, (x-5)² = x²-10x+25 or (x-3)²+4 = x²-6x+9+4 = x²-6x+13.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
