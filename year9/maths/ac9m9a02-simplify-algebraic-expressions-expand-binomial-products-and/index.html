Algebraic expressions, expansion and factorising

This topic develops algebra as a flexible language. Students simplify expressions, expand brackets, multiply binomials and reverse the process by factorising. The goal is not just procedural fluency; students should understand that expanding and factorising are inverse ways of writing equivalent expressions.

What students need to understand

Core ideas

Like terms

Terms can be combined only when the variable part is identical, such as 3x and 5x.

Expansion

Use the distributive law: a(b + c) = ab + ac. For binomials, multiply every term in one bracket by every term in the other.

Factorising

Reverse expansion by taking out a common factor or recognising a structure.

Equivalent expressions

Different forms can represent the same quantity, such as 2(x+3) and 2x+6.

Worked thinking

How the idea works

Expand a binomial product

(x + 4)(x + 3) = x² + 3x + 4x + 12 = x² + 7x + 12. The middle terms combine because they are like terms.

Factorise with a common factor

6x² + 9x = 3x(2x + 3). Check by expanding: 3x × 2x = 6x² and 3x × 3 = 9x.

Skill building

Quick checks before practice

  1. Simplify 4x + 7x − 3.
  2. Expand 3(x − 5).
  3. Expand (x + 2)(x + 6).
  4. Factorise 8x + 12.

These questions are designed to expose the common gap before students begin the SkillrHub practice set.

Common mistakes

Misconceptions to fix early

  • x² and x are not like terms and cannot be combined.
  • When expanding two brackets, every term must multiply every term.
  • Always check factorising by expanding back to the original expression.

Support at home

Parent and teacher tip

Ask the student to expand, then factorise back. This back-and-forth check is one of the best signs that the concept is understood rather than memorised.

Next step

Practice pathway

Use the topic guide first, then homework for written reasoning, practice for feedback, and the test when the student is ready to check retention.
