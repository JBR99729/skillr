Enlargement, similarity and scale factor

This topic develops spatial reasoning through enlargement. Students compare shapes before and after enlargement, identify scale factor, and explain why similar figures keep the same shape even when their size changes.

Core understanding

What students need to know

Key idea

An enlargement changes lengths by a scale factor but preserves angles.

Key idea

A scale factor greater than 1 enlarges a shape; a scale factor between 0 and 1 reduces it.

Key idea

Similar figures have equal corresponding angles and proportional corresponding side lengths.

Key idea

Area changes by the square of the scale factor, not just the scale factor.

Formulas and representations

Useful rules and ways to represent the idea

new length = original length × scale factor
scale factor = image length ÷ original length
if length scale factor is k, area scale factor is k²
corresponding sides must be matched carefully

Worked example

Worked example: use a scale factor

  1. A rectangle is enlarged by scale factor 3. Its original length is 5 cm and width is 2 cm.
  2. New length = 5 × 3 = 15 cm.
  3. New width = 2 × 3 = 6 cm.
  4. The angles are unchanged, so the new rectangle is similar to the original.
  5. The area scale factor is 3² = 9.

Common misconceptions

What to watch for

  • Multiplying angles by the scale factor.
  • Using different scale factors for different corresponding sides.
  • Assuming area and length scale by the same factor.
  • Matching the wrong pair of corresponding sides when finding scale factor.

Quick check

Try these before moving on

  1. A triangle side changes from 4 cm to 10 cm. What is the scale factor?
  2. If a shape is enlarged by scale factor 2, what happens to its angles?
  3. Explain why a 6 cm by 9 cm rectangle is similar to a 2 cm by 3 cm rectangle.

Support tip

For parents and teachers

Use tracing paper, grids or digital graphing tools so students can see that enlargement changes distance from a centre but preserves angle size.
