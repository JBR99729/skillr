Trigonometric ratios in similar right triangles

This topic introduces the reason trigonometry works. Students learn that for any fixed acute angle, similar right triangles have the same side ratios, so sine, cosine and tangent can be used to find unknown sides and angles.

Core understanding

What students need to know

Key idea

Similar right triangles have equal corresponding angles and proportional corresponding sides.

Key idea

For a fixed angle, opposite/hypotenuse, adjacent/hypotenuse and opposite/adjacent stay constant.

Key idea

The hypotenuse is always opposite the right angle and is the longest side.

Key idea

The ratio needed depends on which sides are known and which side or angle is required.

Formulas and representations

Useful rules and ways to represent the idea

sin θ = opposite ÷ hypotenuse
cos θ = adjacent ÷ hypotenuse
tan θ = opposite ÷ adjacent
SOH CAH TOA helps match sides to ratios

Worked example

Worked example: identify the correct ratio

  1. A right triangle has angle θ, opposite side 7 cm and hypotenuse 12 cm.
  2. The sides involved are opposite and hypotenuse.
  3. The matching ratio is sine.
  4. sin θ = 7 ÷ 12.
  5. This ratio is the same for any similar right triangle with the same angle θ.

Common misconceptions

What to watch for

  • Using the side opposite the right angle as the opposite side for every angle.
  • Choosing a ratio before identifying opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse.
  • Thinking sine, cosine and tangent change when a similar triangle is enlarged.
  • Forgetting that the adjacent side depends on the chosen acute angle.

Quick check

Try these before moving on

  1. Label opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse for an angle of 35° in a right triangle.
  2. Which ratio uses adjacent and hypotenuse?
  3. Explain why enlarging a right triangle does not change the sine ratio for a fixed angle.

Support tip

For parents and teachers

Ask students to mark the angle first, then label sides. Most errors in early trigonometry come from labelling sides before choosing the reference angle.
