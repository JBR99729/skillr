Gradient, midpoint and distance on the Cartesian plane

This topic turns coordinate geometry into a practical toolkit. Students learn how to describe the steepness of a line, locate the exact halfway point between two coordinates, and measure the distance between points on a grid. These skills prepare students for linear graphs, analytic geometry and modelling movement or location.

Core understanding

What students need to understand

Gradient measures steepness

Gradient compares vertical change with horizontal change. A positive gradient rises left to right; a negative gradient falls left to right; a zero gradient is horizontal.

Midpoint finds the halfway coordinate

The midpoint is found by averaging the x-coordinates and averaging the y-coordinates separately.

Distance uses Pythagoras

The distance between two points is the hypotenuse of a right triangle formed by the horizontal and vertical changes.

Coordinate geometry models real movement

Maps, routes, slopes, paths and screen coordinates all use the same ideas: position, change and distance.

Key tools

Formulas and representations

gradient = rise/run = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁)

midpoint = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2)

distance = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²)

Choose the formula or representation that matches the question. In Year 9, marks often come from defining variables, showing substitutions and interpreting the result.

Worked example

Worked example: from A(2, 3) to B(8, 11)

  1. Horizontal change: 8 − 2 = 6. Vertical change: 11 − 3 = 8.
  2. Gradient = 8/6 = 4/3.
  3. Midpoint = ((2 + 8)/2, (3 + 11)/2) = (5, 7).
  4. Distance = √(6² + 8²) = √100 = 10 units.
  5. Interpretation: the segment rises 4 units for every 3 units across, has centre (5, 7), and length 10 units.

Quick check

Try these before practice

  1. Find the gradient from (1, 4) to (5, 12).
  2. Find the midpoint of (−2, 7) and (6, 1).
  3. Explain why the distance formula is based on Pythagoras.

Common misconceptions

Watch for these mistakes

  • Reversing the point order in only one part of the gradient calculation.
  • Forgetting that a negative gradient is normal when the line falls left to right.
  • Averaging all four numbers together instead of averaging x-values and y-values separately.
  • Using x-change + y-change instead of Pythagoras for distance.

Support tip

For parents and teachers

Ask students to explain what each coordinate represents before calculating. A quick sketch often prevents sign errors and helps students choose gradient, midpoint or distance correctly.

Next steps

Build fluency

Use the topic guide first, then practise until the method feels natural. The test should be used only after students can explain the method, not just copy steps.
