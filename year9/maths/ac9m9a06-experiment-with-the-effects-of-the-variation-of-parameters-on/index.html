Function Transformations

Changing a function's parameters produces predictable transformations that can be tested graphically and explained algebraically.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

Changing a function's parameters produces predictable transformations that can be tested graphically and explained algebraically.

Learning intention: Changing a function's parameters produces predictable transformations that can be tested graphically and explained algebraically.

Success criteria

  • Explain transform y=x using the approved model.
  • Apply use parameter variation in predictive models to a new case.
  • Verify a conclusion with a second representation, estimate or test case.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • experiment with the effects of the variation of parameters on graphs of related functions, using digital tools, making connections between graphical and algebraic representations, and generalising emerging patterns

Prerequisites

  • Relevant Year 8 algebra representations and vocabulary.

May support informally

  • Digital tools may test, visualise and verify after the mathematical structure is explicit.

Do not overteach

  • Do not introduce later-year formalism that is unnecessary for this content description.

Worked examples

Transform y=x

Y=x → y=2x (vertical enlargement as a>1) → y=2x-1 (vertical translation) and y=x → y=1/2x (vertical compression as a<1) → y=-1/2x (reflection in the horizontal axis) → y=-1/2x+3 (vertical translation).

Teacher prompt: For transform y=x, which labelled relationship justifies the conclusion?

Transform y=x²

Start with y = x²; a multiplier changes vertical scale or reflection, while y = a(x − h)² + k translates the parabola by (h, k).

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • A rule is applied without preserving equivalence or its conditions — Return to the linked table, graph or expanded form and verify by substitution.
  • The function transformations model is copied without interpretation — Connect every label in ‘Y=x → y=2x (vertical enlargement as a>1) → y=2x-1 (vertical translation) and y=x → y=1/2x (vertical compression as a<1) → y=-1/2x (reflection in the horizontal axis) → y=-1/2x+3 (vertical translation).’ to its mathematical role.
  • The result is not verified — Use a second representation, boundary case, inverse operation or simulation check.
