Year 9 Science • AC9S9U01 • Topic guide

Body systems, coordination and regulation

This topic helps students understand how the human body detects change, communicates information and coordinates responses. It connects body systems to homeostasis, feedback and everyday examples such as temperature control and blood glucose regulation.

Core understanding

What students need to know

Nervous system

Sends fast electrical messages through neurons.

Endocrine system

Sends slower chemical messages called hormones through the blood.

Receptors and effectors

Receptors detect changes and effectors carry out responses.

Homeostasis

Keeps internal conditions within a suitable range.

Scientific representations

stimulus → receptor → coordinator → effector → response
negative feedback reduces change and helps restore balance

Worked example: temperature regulation

  1. Body temperature rises during exercise.
  2. Receptors detect the change and send information to the brain.
  3. The brain coordinates sweating and increased blood flow to the skin.
  4. Heat loss lowers body temperature.
  5. The response reduces the original change, so it is negative feedback.

Common misconceptions

  • Thinking one organ system works alone.
  • Confusing hormones with nerves.
  • Thinking homeostasis means the body never changes.
  • Describing a response without explaining the stimulus or feedback pathway.
