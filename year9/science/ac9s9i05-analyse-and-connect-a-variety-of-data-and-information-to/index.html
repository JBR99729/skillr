AC9S9I05 • Year 9 Science

Connect Evidence Across Data Sets

Analysis explains patterns, relationships and anomalies by comparing data sources and scientific models.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain connect evidence across data sets using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect source A, source B, shared pattern.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Comparable data can reveal converging patterns or conflicts.
  • Anomalies and uncertainty affect interpretation.
  • Explanations connect data patterns to scientific mechanisms or models.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not combine incompatible data without transformation or caveat.
  • Do not infer cause from pattern alone without design or mechanism.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Describe each data set
    Use the shared model to foreground source A.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving source A.
  2. 2. Align variables and scales
    Use the shared model to foreground source B.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving source B.
  3. 3. Find convergence or conflict
    Use the shared model to foreground shared pattern.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving shared pattern.
  4. 4. Explain with a model
    Use the shared model to foreground anomaly.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving anomaly.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

Two reaction-rate trials show faster gas production at higher temperature, but one run plateaus early.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Discuss: source A

discussing the validity of the data when extrapolating from a graph

Use the validity of the data when extrapolating to make the role of source A visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E2: Analyse: source B

analysing representations of data from atmospheric monitoring and ice cores to identify patterns and trends in the amount of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere, highlighting inconsistencies

Use representations of data from atmospheric monitoring and ice cores to make the role of source B visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E3: Compare: shared pattern

comparing published data with experimental data such as the sound-insulating levels of different materials and identifying any trends or patterns in differences, such as: ‘The published sound levels are usually higher than the experimentally determined levels’

Use published data with experimental data to make the role of shared pattern visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E4: Analyse: anomaly

analysing data on heat transfer through multiple layers of an insulating material and identifying patterns and proportional relationships, such as: ‘When the thickness of the material is doubled the amount of heat transferred is halved’

Use data on heat transfer through multiple layers to make the role of anomaly visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E5: Examine: model-based explanation

examining tables, graphs and digital simulations of radioactive decay half-life to predict changes in mass over time

Use tables, graphs and digital simulations of radioactive decay half-life to make the role of model-based explanation visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

Common misconceptions
  • Different sources can be averaged immediately.
  • An anomaly invalidates the whole data set.
