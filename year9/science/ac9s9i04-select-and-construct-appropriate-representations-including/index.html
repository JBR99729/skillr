AC9S9I04 • Year 9 Science

Represent Data Without Distortion

Tables, graphs, statistics, models and equations should reveal the relevant relationship and communicate uncertainty honestly.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain represent data without distortion using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect data purpose, table or graph, descriptive statistic.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Representation choice is purpose- and data-dependent.
  • Conventions and scale affect interpretation.
  • Statistics and models summarise evidence but have limitations.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not use visual effects that distort magnitude.
  • Do not report statistics without units, context or distribution where relevant.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Define the message
    Use the shared model to foreground data purpose.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving data purpose.
  2. 2. Select the form
    Use the shared model to foreground table or graph.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving table or graph.
  3. 3. Apply conventions
    Use the shared model to foreground descriptive statistic.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving descriptive statistic.
  4. 4. Test interpretation
    Use the shared model to foreground model or equation.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving model or equation.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

Reaction time falls as temperature rises.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Use: data purpose

using spreadsheet software to present data in tabular and graphical forms

Use spreadsheet software to present data in tabular and graphical to make the role of data purpose visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E2: Identify: table or graph

identifying which sample properties, such as mean, median and range, are the most appropriate to use to make generalisations

Use which sample properties to make the role of table or graph visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E3: Explore: descriptive statistic

applying algorithms to measure carbon storage of different vegetation types

Use applying algorithms to measure carbon storage of different vegetation to make the role of descriptive statistic visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E4: Explore: model or equation

applying ratios to accurately represent usable and waste energy in transfer and transformation diagrams such as Sankey diagrams

Use applying ratios to accurately represent usable and waste energy to make the role of model or equation visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E5: Compare: scale and limitation

comparing the information provided by molecular models and word and balanced symbolic chemical equations when examining the law of conservation of mass

Use the information provided by molecular models and word to make the role of scale and limitation visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

Common misconceptions
  • The representation with the most features is best.
  • Changing an axis only changes appearance.
