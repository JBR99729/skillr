Year 9 Science • AC9S9H03 • Topic guide

How science knowledge and practices are adopted by society

This topic helps students evaluate why some scientific ideas, technologies and practices are adopted quickly while others are debated or delayed. Students consider evidence, communication, cost, safety, ethics, cultural values and practical usefulness.

Core understanding

What students need to know

Key idea

Strong evidence is important, but adoption also depends on social, ethical and economic factors.

Key idea

Clear communication affects whether people understand and trust scientific advice.

Key idea

New practices may require technology, funding, regulation or behaviour change.

Key idea

Different groups may weigh risks and benefits differently.

Scientific language and representations

Useful terms and ways to represent the idea

adoption depends on evidence, trust, cost, access and values
risk-benefit analysis compares possible advantages and harms
scientific consensus can support decision-making
policy and regulation can shape how science is used

Worked example

Worked example: adopting a public health practice

  1. Scientists gather evidence that a practice reduces disease spread.
  2. The evidence is reviewed and communicated to health authorities.
  3. Decision-makers consider cost, access, safety and public trust.
  4. The practice may be recommended, regulated or adapted for different communities.
  5. Adoption depends on both science evidence and social factors.

Common misconceptions

What to watch for

  • Thinking good evidence automatically means immediate adoption.
  • Ignoring cost, access, ethics and public trust.
  • Treating disagreement as proof that the science is worthless.
  • Assuming one community’s response applies everywhere.

Quick check

Try these before moving on

  1. Name two non-scientific factors that affect adoption of a science-based practice.
  2. Why does communication matter when science informs society?
  3. Explain why a useful technology might not be adopted immediately.

Support tip

For parents and teachers

Ask students to use “evidence plus context”. They should identify the science evidence, then explain the social or practical factors that influence adoption.
