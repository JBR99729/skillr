Evaluating investigation validity and reproducibility

This topic helps students judge the quality of an investigation. Students assess whether the method tested the intended question, whether the results could be repeated, and whether the conclusion is strong enough for the evidence collected.

Start practiceTake testOpen homework

Core understanding

What students need to know

Validity

A valid method tests the intended relationship and controls other variables that could affect the result.

Reproducibility

A reproducible method is clear enough for another person to repeat and obtain similar results.

Reliability

Repeated trials and consistent results increase confidence in the data.

Evaluation

An evaluation identifies strengths, weaknesses and realistic improvements to the investigation.

Scientific language and representations

Useful terms and ways to represent the idea

validity: does the method test the question?
reproducibility: can the method be repeated?
uncertainty: how much doubt is attached to a measurement or conclusion?
improvement: a specific change that would produce stronger evidence

Worked example

Worked example: evaluating a dissolving investigation

  1. A student changes water temperature but also changes the amount of stirring.
  2. This weakens validity because more than one variable changed.
  3. The method should keep stirring constant for each trial.
  4. Repeating each temperature condition would improve confidence in the trend.
  5. A strong evaluation names the problem and explains how the improvement helps.

Common misconceptions

What to watch for

  • Writing “do more trials” without explaining why it helps.
  • Confusing validity with whether the answer sounds correct.
  • Ignoring uncontrolled variables when judging a conclusion.
  • Claiming the hypothesis is proven when the evidence only supports it.

Quick check

Try these before moving on

  1. What is one difference between validity and reproducibility?
  2. Why can an uncontrolled variable weaken a conclusion?
  3. Write one specific improvement for a fair-test investigation.

Support tip

For parents and teachers

Ask students to use this evaluation sentence frame: “This method was limited because…, so the conclusion is…, and it could be improved by…”.
