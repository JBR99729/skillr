AC9S9I06 • Year 9 Science

Judge Validity, Reproducibility and Claims

Evaluation distinguishes error types, assumptions and uncertainty to decide how strongly evidence supports a conclusion.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain judge validity, reproducibility and claims using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect method validity, reproducibility, random and systematic error.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Validity concerns whether the design supports the intended inference.
  • Reproducibility and error patterns affect confidence.
  • Conclusions must reflect assumptions, uncertainty and conflicting evidence.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not call every difference ‘human error’.
  • Do not use uncertainty as a reason to treat all claims as equally plausible.

Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

A group concludes a catalyst increases final product because gas formed faster during the first minute.
