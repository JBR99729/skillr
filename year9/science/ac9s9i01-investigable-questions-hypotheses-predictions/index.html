Year 9 Science • AC9S9I01

Investigable questions, hypotheses and predictions

Students turn broad ideas into testable questions, identify variables, make reasoned predictions and construct hypotheses that link expected relationships to scientific understanding.

PracticeTestHomeworkTeacher Slides

Core understanding

Investigable question

A useful question can be answered by collecting evidence and clearly identifies what will be changed and measured.

Prediction

A prediction states the expected outcome and should be reasoned from prior scientific knowledge.

Hypothesis

A hypothesis proposes a testable explanation for the expected relationship between variables.

Variables

The independent variable is changed, the dependent variable is measured, and relevant controlled variables are kept consistent.

Worked example

Question: How does water temperature affect the time taken for a fixed mass of sugar to dissolve? Prediction: Sugar will dissolve faster at higher temperatures. Hypothesis: Higher temperature increases particle motion and collision frequency, so the sugar should dissolve in less time.

Curriculum mapping

  • Australian Curriculum v9: AC9S9I01 — develop investigable questions, reasoned predictions and hypotheses to guide investigations.
  • Victoria: closest alignment is Levels 9–10 Science Inquiry, including identifying questions, formulating hypotheses and planning investigations.
  • NSW: closest alignment is Stage 5 Working Scientifically, particularly questioning, predicting and planning valid investigations.
  • International: aligns closely with NGSS planning and carrying out investigations and England KS3/KS4 working scientifically expectations for hypotheses, variables and testable questions.

Quick check

  1. Turn “temperature and dissolving” into a measurable investigable question.
  2. Identify the independent, dependent and two controlled variables.
  3. Write a hypothesis that explains the prediction using scientific reasoning.
