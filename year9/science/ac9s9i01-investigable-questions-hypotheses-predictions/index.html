AC9S9I01 • Year 9 Science

Investigable questions, hypotheses and predictions

Turn broad ideas into testable questions, use reasoned predictions and hypotheses to test relationships, and use evidence to develop and refine explanatory models.

Teacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
Core understanding
  • An investigable question can be answered by collecting evidence rather than opinion alone.
  • A reasoned prediction states the expected outcome and is based on scientific understanding.
  • A hypothesis proposes a testable explanation for a relationship between variables.
  • Hypotheses are tested to examine relationships and to develop, evaluate or refine explanatory models.
  • Clear independent, dependent and controlled variables make evidence easier to interpret.
Scientific language and representations
  • Independent variable: the factor deliberately changed.
  • Dependent variable: the factor measured or observed.
  • Controlled variables: relevant factors kept consistent so the relationship can be tested fairly.
  • Hypothesis: a testable explanation linking variables and scientific reasoning.
  • Explanatory model: a representation or explanation used to account for observed relationships and refined when new evidence is collected.

Reasoning chain: question → prediction/hypothesis → investigation → evidence → evaluate relationship → develop or refine explanatory model.

Worked example
  1. Broad idea: plants and light.
  2. Investigable question: How does the number of hours of light each day affect plant height over two weeks?
  3. Prediction: Plants receiving more light each day will grow taller over the two-week period.
  4. Hypothesis: If daily light exposure increases, then plant growth should increase because more light can support greater photosynthesis.
  5. Test the relationship: vary light exposure, measure growth and keep other important conditions consistent.
  6. Develop the explanatory model: compare the evidence with the proposed photosynthesis–growth explanation. If the pattern differs from the prediction, refine the model by considering limiting factors such as water, nutrients or excessive light.
Common misconceptions and quick checks
  • A vague question is not investigable just because it is about science.
  • A prediction states an expected outcome; a hypothesis explains the expected relationship.
  • Testing a hypothesis is not only about proving it correct; evidence can support, challenge or refine an explanatory model.
  • Changing several independent variables at once makes relationships harder to interpret.
  • The dependent variable is measured; it is not the variable deliberately changed.

Check: Turn “temperature and dissolving” into an investigable question. Identify the variables. Write a hypothesis with scientific reasoning. Explain what evidence would make you refine your explanatory model.

Support for teaching

Start with “How does changing ___ affect ___?” Then add “I predict ___ because ___.” After collecting evidence, ask students whether the observed relationship supports the explanation and what should change in their model.

Curriculum mapping

  • Victoria: closest Levels 9–10 Science Inquiry alignment includes identifying questions, formulating hypotheses, testing relationships and using evidence to develop explanations.
  • NSW: closest Stage 5 Working Scientifically alignment includes questioning, predicting, hypothesising, planning valid investigations and using evidence to develop explanations.
  • NGSS: closest practices include asking questions, planning investigations, analysing data and developing and using models.
  • England: KS3/KS4 working scientifically includes hypotheses, variables, testable questions, data interpretation and explanation.
  • New Zealand: Nature of Science alignment includes investigating, interpreting evidence and developing scientific explanations and models.
