AC9S9H01 • Year 9 Science

Validating and refining scientific knowledge

Scientific claims become stronger when evidence is checked, methods are repeated, experts review the work and explanations are refined when better evidence appears.

Teacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
Core understanding
  • Scientific knowledge is based on evidence, not opinion alone.
  • Replication and reproducibility help scientists check whether results are reliable.
  • Peer review allows experts to question methods, evidence and conclusions.
  • Models and explanations can change when better evidence becomes available.
Scientific language and representations
  • Claim + evidence + reasoning: evidence must support the stated conclusion.
  • Peer review: experts scrutinise method, data and interpretation.
  • Replication: repeating a method tests whether results are consistent.
  • Refinement: improving an explanation as evidence accumulates.
Worked example
  1. A scientist makes a claim about a new material.
  2. Other scientists examine the method, measurements and conclusion.
  3. They check whether the evidence actually supports the claim.
  4. If weaknesses are found, the method or explanation may be revised.
  5. This makes accepted knowledge more reliable.
Common misconceptions and quick checks
  • One experiment is not enough to prove a claim forever.
  • Peer review is expert scrutiny, not a popularity vote.
  • Scientific knowledge changing is evidence of self-correction, not guessing.
  • Uncertainty, limitations and sample size matter.

Check: Why is replication important? What does peer review check? How can new evidence refine a model?

Support for teaching

Ask students to separate claim, evidence and reasoning. Strong answers explain how evidence changed or strengthened an accepted explanation.

Support, core and extend

Support: Give students a short claim and ask them to highlight the claim, evidence, method and conclusion separately.

Core: Explain how replication and peer review each improve confidence in a claim, using a different reason for each process.

Extend: Compare two claims with different sample sizes or methods and decide which should be trusted more, while naming remaining uncertainty.

Mastery evidence

Exit ticket: A result disagrees with a published explanation. What should scientists check before changing the explanation?

Look for: Method quality, repeated evidence, peer review and whether the new data explains the pattern better than the old model.

Curriculum mapping

  • Victoria: closest Years 9–10 Science alignment includes evaluating evidence, reliability and development of scientific explanations.

  • NGSS: closest practices include analysing evidence, argument from evidence and evaluating explanations.
  • England: working scientifically includes peer review, reproducibility and evaluating claims.
  • New Zealand: Nature of Science includes investigating and understanding how scientific knowledge develops.
