Year 9 Science • AC9S9I08 • Topic guide

Communicating scientific ideas, findings and arguments

This topic helps students turn science understanding into clear communication. Students choose structure, language, evidence, diagrams and representations to suit the audience, purpose and scientific message.

Core understanding

What students need to know

Audience

The same finding should be explained differently for classmates, teachers, parents or the public.

Purpose

A scientific text may explain, argue, report, compare, persuade or evaluate.

Evidence

Findings and claims should be supported with relevant data, observations or source information.

Representation

Tables, graphs, labelled diagrams and models can make complex ideas clearer.

Scientific language and representations

Useful terms and ways to represent the idea

formal science writing uses precise, objective language
captions explain what a figure shows and why it matters
paragraphs should move from claim to evidence to reasoning
technical terms should be used accurately and defined when needed

Worked example

Worked example: improving a conclusion sentence

  1. Weak: “The hot water worked best.”
  2. Better: “The data suggests that higher water temperature decreased dissolving time.”
  3. Add evidence: “At 70 °C, the solid dissolved in 28 s compared with 95 s at 20 °C.”
  4. Add reasoning: “Higher temperature increases particle motion, increasing collision frequency.”
  5. The final sentence is clearer, more precise and evidence-based.

Common misconceptions

What to watch for

  • Using casual language where scientific precision is needed.
  • Adding graphs or diagrams without captions or explanation.
  • Reporting results without explaining what they mean.
  • Using technical words without understanding them.

Quick check

Try these before moving on

  1. Rewrite “it went up heaps” in formal scientific language.
  2. Why should a graph have a caption or clear title?
  3. What would you change when explaining the same finding to a younger student?

Support tip

For parents and teachers

Ask students to identify the audience first. Then check whether the vocabulary, evidence and representation match that audience and purpose.
