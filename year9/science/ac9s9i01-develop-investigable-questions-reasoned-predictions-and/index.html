AC9S9I01 • Year 9 Science

Develop Testable Explanatory Questions

Questions, predictions and hypotheses connect measurable relationships to an explanatory model.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain develop testable explanatory questions using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect observation, question, variables.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Questions identify measurable relationships or model tests.
  • Hypotheses include scientific reasoning.
  • Predictions follow logically from a hypothesis under stated conditions.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not state causation from observational correlation alone.
  • Do not force every inquiry into an ‘if–then–because’ sentence when another precise form is clearer.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Observe carefully
    Use the shared model to foreground observation.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving observation.
  2. 2. Define variables
    Use the shared model to foreground question.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving question.
  3. 3. Propose a mechanism
    Use the shared model to foreground variables.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving variables.
  4. 4. Predict a pattern
    Use the shared model to foreground hypothesis.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving hypothesis.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

A tablet appears to dissolve faster in warm water.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Discuss: observation

discussing what is needed for a question to be investigable or a prediction to be reasoned

Use what is needed for a question to be investigable to make the role of observation visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E2: Explore: question

generating investigable questions about the relationships between human body systems and everyday events, such as: ‘How does the intensity of exercise affect heart rate and breathing rate?’

Use generating investigable questions about the relationships between human body to make the role of question visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E3: Develop: variables

developing investigable questions to explore an explanatory model, such as: ‘How is sound wave transfer affected by the density of the medium through which it travels? What causes our body temperature to rise when we are ill?’

Use investigable questions to explore an explanatory model to make the role of variables visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E4: Discuss: hypothesis

discussing why a scientific hypothesis has to be able to be supported or refuted through evidence

Use a scientific hypothesis has to be able to be to make the role of hypothesis visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E5: Explore: model-based prediction

proposing a hypothesis to test an identified relationship, such as: ‘If objects of different temperatures are placed in contact, heat energy will transfer from an object of higher temperature to an object of lower temperature until both objects reach the same temperature’

Use proposing a hypothesis to test an identified relationship to make the role of model-based prediction visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

Common misconceptions
  • A prediction and hypothesis are identical.
  • A testable question must always be causal.
