Planning valid and reproducible investigations

This topic helps students design investigations that can actually test a claim. Students focus on validity, reproducibility, controlled variables, repeated trials, risk management and clear procedures.

Start practiceTake testOpen homework

Core understanding

What students need to know

Key idea

Validity means the investigation tests the question it claims to test.

Key idea

Reproducibility means another person could repeat the method and obtain similar results.

Key idea

Controlled variables help make a fair comparison.

Key idea

Repeated trials and clear measurements improve confidence in the results.

Scientific language and representations

Useful terms and ways to represent the idea

validity: how well the method tests the intended relationship
reproducibility: whether the method can be repeated with similar results
risk assessment: identifying hazards and reducing harm
method: a clear, ordered procedure that another person can follow

Worked example

Worked example: making a method more valid

  1. Question: How does water temperature affect dissolving time?
  2. Only change the water temperature.
  3. Keep solute mass, water volume, stirring method and container type the same.
  4. Measure dissolving time using the same timing rule each trial.
  5. Repeat each temperature condition and calculate a typical result.

Common misconceptions

What to watch for

  • Thinking a method is valid just because it gives data.
  • Leaving controlled variables unstated.
  • Writing instructions that are too vague for another student to repeat.
  • Ignoring safety and ethical considerations in the plan.

Quick check

Try these before moving on

  1. Name two controlled variables in a dissolving-rate investigation.
  2. Explain why repeated trials can improve confidence.
  3. Write one safety control for heating water in a classroom investigation.

Support tip

For parents and teachers

Ask students to swap methods with a partner. If the partner cannot repeat the investigation without asking questions, the method needs more detail.
