AC9S9I02 • Year 9 Science

Design Valid, Reproducible Investigations

A strong plan aligns variables, controls, sampling, risk, ethics and cultural responsibilities with the question.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain design valid, reproducible investigations using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect question, variables and controls, sampling and repeats.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Validity depends on alignment and control of relevant variables.
  • Reproducibility requires transparent procedures and useful sample sizes.
  • Risk, ethics and Country/Place protocols can change or prevent a method.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not treat repetition as a cure for every design problem.
  • Do not access heritage material, sites or knowledge without correct authority and protocols.

Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

To test temperature and reaction rate, measure gas volume over time at several temperatures.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Discuss: question

discussing what is meant by validity and reproducibility and how they relate to the method used in an investigation

Use what is meant by validity and reproducibility and how to make the role of question visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E2: Explore: variables and controls

determining the reproducibility of a field investigation using survey techniques that seek to answer a question such as: ‘How much traffic passes the school during a designated period of time?’

Use determining the reproducibility of a field investigation using survey to make the role of variables and controls visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E3: Identify: sampling and repeats

identifying possible sources of error in methods then examining if further testing or extra variable control is needed

Use possible sources of error in methods then examining if to make the role of sampling and repeats visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E4: Identify: risk and ethics

identifying the potential hazards of chemicals or biological materials and processes used in experimental investigations and identifying how these should be addressed

Use the potential hazards of chemicals or biological materials to make the role of risk and ethics visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E5: Discuss: reproducible method

discussing the ethical and social issues involved in the care and use of animals for scientific purposes before starting an investigation involving animals

Use the ethical and social issues involved in the care to make the role of reproducible method visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E6: Recognise: question

recognising First Nations Australians’ heritage laws and public responsibilities to report new sites or artefacts, and developing awareness of the consequences for disturbing heritage sites on, above or below the land surface, or in waters

Use First Nations Australians’ heritage laws and public responsibilities to make the role of question visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

Common misconceptions
  • Many repeats fix systematic bias.
  • A detailed method is automatically valid.
