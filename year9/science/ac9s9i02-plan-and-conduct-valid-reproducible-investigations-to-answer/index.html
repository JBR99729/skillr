What students learn in AC9S9I02

This unit helps students build a clear, usable understanding of Plan and conduct valid, reproducible investigations to answer questions and test hypotheses, including identifying and controlling for possible sources of error and, as appropriate, developing and following risk assessments, considering ethical issues, and addressing key considerations regarding heritage sites and artefacts on Country/Place. The goal is not to memorise one answer pattern. Students should be able to explain the idea, recognise it in a new example and apply it in a short practice or worksheet task.

  • Start with the main idea: plan and conduct valid, reproducible investigations to answer questions and test hypotheses, including identifying and controlling for possible sources of error and, as appropriate, developing and following risk assessments, considering ethical issues, and addressing key considerations regarding heritage sites and artefacts on Country/Place.
  • Use concrete examples, pictures, oral explanation and short written responses before moving to independent practice.
  • discussing what is meant by validity and reproducibility and how they relate to the method used in an investigation determining the reproducibility of a field investigation using survey techniques that seek to answer a question such as: ‘How much traffic passes the school during a designated period of time?’
  • Finish with a mixed activity so students must choose the correct strategy rather than copy the last example.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

The content description and elaborations below show the curriculum ideas taught in this unit. Items marked as teaching context support lesson planning.

  • Content description: plan and conduct valid, reproducible investigations to answer questions and test hypotheses, including identifying and controlling for possible sources of error and, as appropriate, developing and following risk assessments, considering ethical issues, and addressing key considerations regarding heritage sites and artefacts on Country/Place
  • E1: discussing what is meant by validity and reproducibility and how they relate to the method used in an investigation
  • E2: determining the reproducibility of a field investigation using survey techniques that seek to answer a question such as: ‘How much traffic passes the school during a designated period of time?’
  • E3: identifying possible sources of error in methods then examining if further testing or extra variable control is needed
  • E4: identifying the potential hazards of chemicals or biological materials and processes used in experimental investigations and identifying how these should be addressed
  • E5: discussing the ethical and social issues involved in the care and use of animals for scientific purposes before starting an investigation involving animals
  • E6: recognising First Nations Australians’ heritage laws and public responsibilities to report new sites or artefacts, and developing awareness of the consequences for disturbing heritage sites on, above or below the land surface, or in waters (teaching context)

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Describing what is seen without connecting it to a question, pattern or prediction.
  • Using everyday words only and missing simple scientific vocabulary.
  • Changing more than one thing in an investigation, which makes the comparison unfair.
