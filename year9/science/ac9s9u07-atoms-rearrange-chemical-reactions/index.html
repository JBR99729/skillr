Atoms Rearrange in Reactions

Chemical equations represent the same atoms rearranged into new substances, conserving mass in a closed system.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain how atoms rearrange in reactions using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify reactants and products and connect particle models, word equations and balanced equations.
  • Use a model to explain atom rearrangement rather than only describe it.
  • Apply conservation of mass to closed and open-system examples.
  • Evaluate a model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Atoms are rearranged, not created or destroyed, in chemical reactions.
  • Word, particle and balanced symbolic equations represent the same event.
  • Mass is conserved when the whole system is considered.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not change chemical formulas to make an equation balance.
  • Do not introduce mole calculations or redox formalism as the target.
Teaching progression
  1. Name reactants and products. Identify the starting and resulting substances.
  2. Model particle rearrangement. Match atoms before and after the reaction.
  3. Count each atom. Check that each element has the same atom count on both sides.
  4. Balance the representation. Change coefficients, never subscripts.
Worked examples

Hydrogen and oxygen form water

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. The equation has four hydrogen atoms and two oxygen atoms on both sides. The atoms are rearranged into water molecules; none are created or destroyed.

If a reaction in an open container appears to lose mass, check whether a gas escaped. Conservation applies to the complete system.

Australian Curriculum elaborations
  • Identify reactants and products in chemical reactions.
  • Use models and representations to show rearrangement of atoms.
  • Investigate reactions in closed and open systems and relate measurements to conservation of mass.
  • Write simple balanced symbolic equations and explain why balancing is required.
  • Investigate why many elements occur in compounds and how elements may be obtained.
  • Connect green-chemistry choices to waste, energy use and environmental effects.
Common misconceptions
  • Balancing an equation does not create extra atoms.
  • Changing a subscript changes the substance and is not a valid balancing method.
  • Mass does not disappear when a gas escapes; the gas is outside the measured open system.
