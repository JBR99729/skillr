Atoms Rearrange in Chemical Reactions

Chemical reactions rearrange existing atoms into new substances. Balanced equations and particle models show that atoms, and therefore mass in a closed system, are conserved.

Teacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
Core understanding
  • Atoms are rearranged, not created or destroyed, in chemical reactions.
  • Reactants and products can be represented with particle models, word equations and balanced symbolic equations.
  • Coefficients are changed to balance equations; chemical formulas are not changed.
  • Mass is conserved when the whole closed system is considered.
Worked example

For hydrogen reacting with oxygen to form water, the balanced equation is 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. There are 4 hydrogen atoms and 2 oxygen atoms on both sides, so the representation is consistent with conservation of mass.

Common misconceptions
  • Balancing an equation does not create extra atoms.
  • Changing a subscript changes the substance and is not a valid balancing method.
  • Apparent mass loss in an open system can occur when a gas escapes; this does not violate conservation of mass.
