AC9S9U07 • Year 9 Science

Atoms Rearrange in Reactions

Chemical equations represent the same atoms rearranged into new substances, conserving mass in a closed system.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain atoms rearrange in reactions using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect reactant particles, product particles, word equation.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Atoms are rearranged, not created or destroyed, in chemical reactions.
  • Word, particle and balanced symbolic equations represent the same event.
  • Mass is conserved when the whole system is considered.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not change chemical formulas to make an equation balance.
  • Do not introduce mole calculations or redox formalism as the target.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Name reactants and products
    Use the shared model to foreground reactant particles.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving reactant particles.
  2. 2. Model particle rearrangement
    Use the shared model to foreground product particles.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving product particles.
  3. 3. Count each atom
    Use the shared model to foreground word equation.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving word equation.
  4. 4. Balance the representation
    Use the shared model to foreground balanced equation.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving balanced equation.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

Hydrogen reacts with oxygen to form water.
