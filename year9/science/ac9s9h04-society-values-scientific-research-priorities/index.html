Year 9 Science • AC9S9H04

Society, values and scientific research priorities

Students examine how social needs, values, ethics, funding, risk and environmental priorities influence what scientists investigate and how technologies are developed or used.

PracticeTestHomeworkTeacher Slides

Core understanding

Social needs

Research priorities can shift toward urgent issues such as health, clean water, food security, energy and climate.

Values and ethics

Values influence what investigations and applications are considered acceptable or responsible.

Evidence and decisions

Science informs public decisions, but social, cultural and economic factors also matter.

Technology impacts

New technologies can create benefits, risks and new questions for society.

Worked example

Renewable-energy research may receive greater funding when communities prioritise lower emissions and energy security. Scientists provide evidence on efficiency and durability, while society also weighs cost, land use, access and environmental trade-offs.

Curriculum mapping

  • Australian Curriculum v9: AC9S9H04 — examine how the values and needs of society influence scientific research and technological development.
  • Victoria: closest alignment is within Levels 9–10 Science as a Human Endeavour, including interactions between science, technology, society and values.
  • NSW: closest alignment is Stage 5 Science outcomes addressing how scientific knowledge and applications affect, and are affected by, society.
  • International: aligns with NGSS science-and-society connections and England KS3/KS4 expectations around social, ethical and environmental implications of science.

Quick check

  1. Give one social need that could influence research priorities.
  2. Explain why ethics can affect how a technology is used.
  3. Describe why scientific evidence may be necessary but not sufficient for a public decision.
