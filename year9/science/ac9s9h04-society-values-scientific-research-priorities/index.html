AC9S9H04 • Year 9 Science

Society, values and scientific research priorities

Social needs, values, ethics, funding, risk and environmental priorities can influence the focus of scientific research.

Core understanding
  • Society can direct scientific research toward urgent problems such as health, water, food, energy and climate.
  • Values and ethics influence what investigations are considered acceptable or important.
  • Science can inform public decisions, while social, cultural and economic factors also shape priorities.
  • Technological applications can be examples or consequences of research priorities, but the assessed content description focuses on the focus of scientific research.
Scientific language and representations
  • Public need: a problem society wants scientific research to help address.
  • Values: beliefs about what is important, fair or responsible.
  • Ethics: questions about harm, fairness, consent and responsibility.
  • Research priority: an area receiving attention, resources or funding because of identified needs or values.
Worked example
  1. A community identifies reliable lower-emission energy as an important need.
  2. This can increase research funding for solar materials, batteries and grid science.
  3. Scientists investigate efficiency, durability and environmental effects.
  4. Communities and funders also weigh cost, land use, access and environmental priorities.
  5. A strong answer links the social need or value to the focus of the scientific research.
Common misconceptions and quick checks
  • Society can influence research priorities even though scientific conclusions still depend on evidence.
  • Social opinion does not replace scientific evidence.
  • Ethics can influence which research questions or methods are pursued.
  • Do not misquote AC9S9H04 as a technology-development content description; technology can be discussed as an elaboration or application.

Check: Give one social need that could guide research. Why might ethics affect research priorities? Why may evidence alone not determine funding priorities?

Support for teaching

Use examples such as clean water, renewable energy, vaccines, assistive technology or food production. Ask: what problem was society trying to solve, and how did that need influence what scientists investigated?

Support, core and extend

Support: Identify who benefits from a research priority and what need it responds to.

Core: Explain how health, environment, industry, safety or cultural needs can influence which questions receive funding.

Extend: Compare two research priorities and justify which should receive limited resources using evidence and social need.

Mastery evidence

Exit ticket: How can society influence science without changing what counts as scientific evidence?

Look for: Students distinguish choosing research questions from deciding whether results are valid.

  • NSW: closest Stage 5 Science alignment addresses how scientific work is influenced by societal needs and contexts.
  • NGSS: closest connections include societal needs influencing scientific questions and research priorities.
  • England: KS3/KS4 working scientifically includes social and ethical contexts for scientific work.
  • New Zealand: Nature of Science alignment includes participation in socio-scientific issues and understanding science in society.
