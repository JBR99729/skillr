How the atomic model changed with evidence

Trace how experimental evidence led scientists to refine atomic models, from early particle ideas to electrons and the nuclear atom.

PracticeTestHomeworkTeacher Slides

Core understanding

  • Scientific models are revised when new evidence explains observations better.
  • Dalton described matter as made of atoms.
  • Thomson's experiments provided evidence for negatively charged electrons inside atoms.
  • Rutherford's gold-foil experiment showed that most of the atom is empty space with a small, dense, positively charged nucleus.
  • Model change is a normal feature of science, not evidence that earlier scientific work was pointless.

Worked reasoning

Most alpha particles passed through gold foil with little deflection, but a small number were strongly deflected. Rutherford reasoned that most atomic volume must be empty space while positive charge and much of the mass are concentrated in a very small nucleus. The older plum-pudding model could not explain the rare large deflections.

Common misconceptions

  • Scientific models are evidence-based explanations, not unsupported guesses.
  • Only a small proportion of Rutherford's alpha particles were strongly deflected.
  • New models usually retain useful parts of earlier models while explaining additional evidence.
