Year 9 Science • AC9S9H03

Science adoption, evidence, trust and values

Students analyse why some scientific knowledge, technologies and practices are adopted quickly while others are debated or delayed, considering evidence, trust, communication, cost, safety, access, ethics and cultural values.

PracticeTestHomeworkTeacher Slides

Core understanding

Evidence

Strong evidence supports adoption, but evidence alone does not determine social decisions.

Trust and communication

People are more likely to understand and use scientific advice when evidence and uncertainty are communicated clearly.

Practical factors

Cost, access, infrastructure, regulation and behaviour change can affect adoption.

Values and ethics

Different groups may weigh benefits, risks and ethical concerns differently.

Worked example

A public-health practice may have strong evidence behind it, yet adoption can still depend on affordability, accessibility, trust in institutions, clear communication and community values. A strong response distinguishes the scientific evidence from the contextual factors affecting uptake.

Quick check

  1. Name two non-scientific factors that can affect adoption of a science-based practice.
  2. Explain why strong evidence does not always produce immediate adoption.
  3. Describe how trust and communication can influence a scientific decision.
