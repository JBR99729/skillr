AC9S9H03 • Year 9 Science

Science adoption, evidence, trust and values

Why scientific knowledge and practices are adopted depends on evidence as well as communication, trust, cost, safety, access, ethics and cultural values.

Teacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
Core understanding
  • Strong evidence is important, but adoption also depends on social, ethical and economic factors.
  • Clear communication affects whether people understand and trust scientific advice.
  • New practices may require technology, funding, regulation or behaviour change.
  • Different groups may weigh risks and benefits differently.
Scientific language and representations
  • Adoption: evidence, trust, cost, access and values can all affect uptake.
  • Risk-benefit analysis: compares possible advantages and harms.
  • Scientific consensus: can support decision-making while uncertainty is still communicated.
  • Policy and regulation: can shape how science-based practices are used.
Worked example
  1. Scientists gather evidence that a public-health practice reduces disease spread.
  2. The evidence is reviewed and communicated to health authorities.
  3. Decision-makers consider cost, access, safety and public trust.
  4. The practice may be recommended, regulated or adapted for different communities.
  5. Adoption depends on both scientific evidence and social context.
Common misconceptions and quick checks
  • Good evidence does not automatically mean immediate adoption.
  • Cost, access, ethics and public trust cannot be ignored.
  • Disagreement does not by itself make the science worthless.
  • One community’s response may not apply everywhere.

Check: Name two non-scientific factors affecting adoption. Why does communication matter? Why might a useful technology not be adopted immediately?

Support for teaching

Use an “evidence plus context” frame: identify the scientific evidence, then explain the social or practical factors that influence uptake.
