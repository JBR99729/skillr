AC9S9U01 • Year 9 Science

Control Systems Keep the Body Stable

Nervous and endocrine pathways coordinate responses, while negative feedback restores a variable toward its set range.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain control systems keep the body stable using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect stimulus, receptor and coordinator, effector.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Body systems detect stimuli and coordinate responses.
  • Nervous and endocrine systems differ in signal, speed and duration.
  • Negative feedback counteracts change to maintain a stable internal range.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not describe homeostasis as an unchanging exact value.
  • Do not teach detailed hormone pathways beyond the selected feedback examples.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Detect change
    Use the shared model to foreground stimulus.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving stimulus.
  2. 2. Send information
    Use the shared model to foreground receptor and coordinator.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving receptor and coordinator.
  3. 3. Coordinate response
    Use the shared model to foreground effector.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving effector.
  4. 4. Restore the range
    Use the shared model to foreground response.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving response.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

Body temperature rises above its normal range.

Common misconceptions
  • Negative feedback makes a response worse.
  • The nervous and endocrine systems act independently.
