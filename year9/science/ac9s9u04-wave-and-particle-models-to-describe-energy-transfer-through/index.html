Two Models for Energy Transfer

Wave and particle models explain different aspects of energy transfer through matter and space.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain two models for energy transfer using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect wave, amplitude and frequency, particle vibration.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Waves transfer energy without net transfer of matter.
  • Particle interactions explain transfer through a medium.
  • Models are evaluated by what they explain and what they omit.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not use a transverse sketch to claim sound particles move up and down.
  • Do not introduce senior wave equations as the central target.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Observe the phenomenon
    Use the shared model to foreground wave.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving wave.
  2. 2. Apply a wave model
    Use the shared model to foreground amplitude and frequency.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving amplitude and frequency.
  3. 3. Apply a particle model
    Use the shared model to foreground particle vibration.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving particle vibration.
  4. 4. Judge usefulness
    Use the shared model to foreground medium.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving medium.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

Sound travels through air as a disturbance.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Explain: wave

describing the processes underlying convection and conduction of heat in terms of the particle model

Use the processes underlying convection and conduction of heat to make the role of wave visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E2: Model: amplitude and frequency

modelling the transfer of sound energy as waves using slinky springs and relating to the medium through which the sound is transferred

Use the transfer of sound energy as waves using slinky to make the role of amplitude and frequency visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E3: Examine: particle vibration

examining how the particle model of electricity explains static electricity and electrical current and relating this to voltage, conductors and insulators

Use how the particle model of electricity explains static electricity to make the role of particle vibration visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E4: Discuss: medium

discussing the wave and particle models of energy transfer, including the concept of photons, and how they are useful for understanding aspects of light and other forms of electromagnetic radiation

Use the wave and particle models of energy transfer, to make the role of medium visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E5: Investigate: model strength and limit

investigating aspects of heat transfer and conservation in the design of First Nations Australians’ bedding and clothing in the various climatic regions of Australia

Use aspects of heat transfer and conservation in the design to make the role of model strength and limit visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E6: Investigate: wave

investigating the impact of material selection on the transfer of sound energy in First Nations Australians’ traditional musical, hunting and communication instruments

Use the impact of material selection on the transfer to make the role of wave visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E7: Examine: amplitude and frequency

examining the forms of electromagnetic radiation that are used in different modern communication technologies and identifying any limitations

Use the forms of electromagnetic radiation that are used to make the role of amplitude and frequency visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

Common misconceptions
  • Particles travel with the wave from source to receiver.
  • One model must be the true picture.
