YEAR 9 SCIENCE

Trace the two directions of development AC9S9H02

Learning intention: Investigate how technologies enable new scientific evidence and how scientific questions and knowledge guide technological and engineering advances.

More examples ↓Help with gaps ↓10-minute activity ↓

WORKED EXAMPLE

A scientific question can create an engineering need

In the historical development of penicillin, an inhibition observation raised questions about the active material. Biological and chemical investigation required sensitive tests and usable preparations. Oxford team members developed complementary methods and apparatus: Heatley’s assays, extraction equipment and culture vessels helped make preparation and comparison possible, while others improved apparatus or tested biological properties. Later structural work, including Hodgkin’s crystallography, helped distinguish proposed molecular structures. The useful account is a loop of questions, knowledge, tools and evidence, rather than one inventor or one observation proving a finished medicine.

Historical basis: University of Oxford Dunn School, “The discovery of penicillin”; Howard W. Florey, Nobel lecture, 11 December 1945, selected coordinated-methods discussion. These are historical explanations, not present-day treatment advice or a practical protocol.

Learn the skill

1. Explain technology enabling science

Name the capability that changed and the evidence it allowed. Greater sensitivity can reveal smaller effects; better preparation can make a comparison possible; a new detector can observe what unaided senses cannot. In the historical case, sensitive assays and extraction apparatus supported investigation of active preparations. “A machine was invented” is not a complete causal explanation.

A student writes: “Better equipment helped the penicillin team.”
Try it · Add the missing scientific link using the supplied history.

For example: sensitive assays let investigators compare biological activity during isolation, while extraction apparatus helped obtain material suitable for further tests. State what was measured or prepared rather than merely praising equipment.

2. Explain science guiding engineering

Scientific explanations and questions identify what a device must do. Engineering then develops and tests solutions under constraints such as sensitivity, capacity, safety, reliability and cost. Biological and chemical investigation of an active material created practical needs for assays, culture vessels and extraction methods; design choices were directed toward those needs.

An investigation needs comparable preparations and a test sensitive enough to distinguish their activity.
Try it · What design criteria follow from that scientific need?

The preparation method should produce usable, comparable material, and the assay should detect the relevant activity consistently. Capacity, contamination control and reliable measurement can matter. A larger apparatus is not automatically a better scientific solution.

3. Close the reciprocal loop and test limits

Scientific need guides a tool; improved capability provides evidence; interpretation refines questions or models; those changes can guide further tool development. Team roles, calibration, controls and interpretation remain necessary. A stronger detector can reveal a real new pattern or an artefact, so newness alone does not settle the science.

A new sensor reveals an unexpected pattern. No independent instrument check or alternative measurement is yet available.
Try it · What should scientists and engineers do next?

Check calibration, settings, sampling and processing; compare suitable independent measurements and model predictions. Engineers can investigate instrument effects while scientists test explanations. Treat the pattern as a question to investigate, not automatic proof of a new phenomenon.

Common mistakes

“The relationship only runs science → technology.” Tools can also produce evidence that changes scientific understanding.

“Newer equipment guarantees a valid conclusion.” Method, calibration and interpretation still matter.

“One laboratory observation proves a finished application.” Preparation, further tests, context and other evidence may still be needed.

10-MINUTE ACTIVITY

Build a reciprocal evidence chain

Use the historical table to draw scientific question → design need → changed tool or method → new evidence → revised question. Name two complementary contributions and one limit of the evidence. Then adapt the chain to a familiar instrument without inventing its history.

Adjust the challenge Support: use verbs such as enabled comparison and guided design. Core: show both directions. Extend: identify an engineering constraint that could produce a new scientific uncertainty.

Use the historical text and paper diagrams. Do not grow mould, culture microorganisms, handle antibiotics or infer a personal treatment from this case.

Three tricky questions

1. Do science, technology and engineering mean exactly the same thing?

No. Science develops evidence-based explanations; technologies extend capabilities; engineering designs and tests solutions under constraints. Their work can overlap.

2. Does a timeline alone establish that one invention caused a discovery?

No. Explain the changed capability and the evidence link, and consider other contributions and conditions.

3. Must every useful technology begin with a formal scientific discovery?

No. Practical design and experience can also generate technologies and new scientific questions. Avoid a universal one-way origin story.

FAQ

Why discuss several people and methods?

Specific complementary work explains how a development became possible and avoids treating shared progress as one person’s isolated achievement.

Why was an early inhibition observation not enough to establish a useful medicine?

It showed a biological effect under particular conditions. Isolation, preparation, further biological testing and evidence about an application were still needed; an observation cannot establish every later claim.

More teaching examples and curriculum coverage
Learning goals
  • Distinguish science, technology and engineering while recognising overlap.
  • Explain how a technological advance can produce new scientific evidence.
  • Explain how scientific knowledge can enable a technological or engineering advance.
  • Build a reciprocal chain rather than a one-way “science causes technology” story.
Prerequisite knowledge

Know that science builds evidence-based explanations, technologies extend human capability, and engineering designs solutions under constraints.

Key concept

New instruments can reveal evidence that was previously inaccessible: microscopes reveal smaller structures, detectors record wavelengths outside human senses, sensors collect faster and more precise data. New evidence can then refine scientific models.

The relationship also runs the other way. Understanding electromagnetic waves enabled communication and imaging technologies; advances in materials science enable stronger, lighter or more specialised engineered devices. A strong explanation identifies what changed, what new evidence/capability followed, and how that fed back into further development.

Worked examples

Example 1 — microscopy

Improved lenses and imaging technology increased resolution → scientists observed previously unseen structures → cell explanations improved → scientific knowledge guided further imaging and biomedical design.

Example 2 — medical imaging

Scientific knowledge of waves and interactions with matter enabled imaging systems. Better detectors and computing then generated richer data, allowing new medical and scientific questions.

Example 3 — astronomy

More sensitive telescope detectors reveal faint or non-visible radiation. Those observations test astronomical models; model predictions then influence instrument design and where engineers optimise future observations.

Common misconceptions
  • Technology is just applied science. Technology also develops through design, materials, constraints and engineering knowledge.
  • The relationship is one-way. Instruments generate evidence that can transform science.
  • A newer device automatically creates better science. Data still require valid methods and interpretation.
  • Science, technology and engineering are identical. Their purposes differ even when projects overlap.
Guided practice
  1. Classify a telescope detector improvement as technology/engineering and state the new scientific capability.
  2. Complete: scientific understanding → ______ → new observations → refined scientific understanding.
  3. Explain one constraint an engineer might face when turning science into a device.
  4. Identify the feedback step in a medical-imaging example.
Independent practice
  1. Define science, technology and engineering in one sentence each.
  2. Give an example of technology enabling new evidence.
  3. Give an example of science enabling a technology.
  4. Explain why improved measurement does not automatically guarantee a valid conclusion.
  5. Trace a two-way chain for satellites, gene sequencing or sensors.
  6. Evaluate the claim “all technological progress begins with a scientific discovery”.
Reasoning/problem-solving

A new sensor detects a pattern no existing model predicted. Explain how scientists and engineers should work together to decide whether the pattern is a real phenomenon, an instrument artefact or evidence requiring model revision.

Questions and answers
  1. How can technology advance science? By extending observation, measurement, analysis and experimentation.
  2. How can science advance technology? Explanations and discoveries provide principles designers can use.
  3. Why “reciprocal”? Each can enable advances in the other over repeated cycles.
  4. What makes an example strong? A specific causal chain, not merely naming a device.
Practice and review
  1. Explain how one technological advance changed scientific understanding.
    Name the new observation or measurement it enabled.
  2. Explain how scientific knowledge contributed to one engineering or technological development.
    State the scientific principle and the design capability it enabled.
  3. Analyse a two-way relationship between a technology and a field of science.
    Include at least one technology → science link and one science → technology link.
Check understanding
  • I can distinguish the three domains.
  • I can build a technology→science chain.
  • I can build a science→technology chain.
  • I can explain feedback between them.

Exit ticket: Use one instrument to show a complete reciprocal relationship in 4–5 sentences.

Teacher + parent guidance

Teacher

Require arrows with verbs: “enabled measurement of”, “provided evidence for”, “guided design of”. This prevents vague timelines of inventions.

Parent/carer

Choose a familiar technology and ask both directions: what science made it possible, and what new science can it help us discover?
