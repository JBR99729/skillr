AC9S9I07 • Year 9 Science

Argue from Multiple Lines of Evidence

Strong scientific arguments synthesise credible evidence, address alternatives and observe ethical and cultural protocols.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain argue from multiple lines of evidence using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect claim, evidence set, source credibility.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Arguments synthesise multiple credible evidence lines.
  • Reasoning addresses alternatives and limitations.
  • Secondary evidence is accessed and cited ethically, including cultural protocols.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not use culturally restricted information without permission.
  • Do not hide counter-evidence or exceed the evidence boundary.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Define the claim
    Use the shared model to foreground claim.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving claim.
  2. 2. Triangulate evidence
    Use the shared model to foreground evidence set.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving evidence set.
  3. 3. Address alternatives
    Use the shared model to foreground source credibility.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving source credibility.
  4. 4. State a bounded conclusion
    Use the shared model to foreground reasoning and rebuttal.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving reasoning and rebuttal.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

Claim: A local lake’s algal bloom is linked to nutrient runoff.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Examine: claim

examining secondary data to determine the credibility of the source and the validity and reproducibility of the data

Use secondary data to determine the credibility of the source to make the role of claim visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E2: Identify: evidence set

identifying multiple sources of evidence that are consistent with a claim such as the effectiveness of a vaccine

Use multiple sources of evidence that are consistent to make the role of evidence set visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E3: Explore: source credibility

interrogating the evidence and reasoning used to justify claims regarding the age of ancient artefacts

Use interrogating the evidence and reasoning used to justify claims to make the role of source credibility visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E4: Research: reasoning and rebuttal

researching the methods used by scientists in studies reported in the media to evaluate the validity of the headlines

Use the methods used by scientists in studies reported to make the role of reasoning and rebuttal visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E5: Examine: ethical use and boundary

examining secondary data to ensure it does not contain personal information which could potentially harm individuals, is correctly cited and is relevant to the investigation question or claim

Use secondary data to ensure it does not contain personal to make the role of ethical use and boundary visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E6: Acknowledge: claim

acknowledging and identifying the relationship between First Peoples’ knowledges and contemporary science and the co-contributions in arriving at shared understandings when working ‘both ways’

Use identifying the relationship between First Peoples’ knowledges and contemporary to make the role of claim visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E7: Acknowledge: evidence set

acknowledging and constructing an argument for the contributions to medicine of First Nations Australians’ knowledges of physiological pathways and contemporary medicinal delivery systems

Use constructing an argument for the contributions to medicine to make the role of evidence set visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

Common misconceptions
  • A persuasive tone strengthens scientific evidence.
  • Acknowledging uncertainty weakens an argument.
