Year 9 Science • AC9S9I07 • Topic guide

Constructing evidence-based scientific arguments

This topic helps students build clear scientific arguments. Students use evidence from experiments, secondary sources, graphs and models to support a claim, while acknowledging limitations and alternative explanations.

Core understanding

What students need to know

Claim

A claim is the argument or conclusion being supported.

Evidence

Evidence is specific data, observations or source information that supports the claim.

Reasoning

Reasoning explains why the evidence supports the claim using scientific ideas.

Limitations

Strong arguments consider uncertainty, bias, assumptions and possible alternative explanations.

Scientific language and representations

Useful terms and ways to represent the idea

claim → evidence → reasoning
counter-claim: a different explanation that should be considered
source quality: how trustworthy and relevant the evidence is
weighing evidence means comparing strength, consistency and relevance

Worked example

Worked example: arguing from carbon-cycle evidence

  1. Claim: burning fossil fuels increases carbon dioxide in the atmosphere.
  2. Evidence: combustion releases carbon dioxide and measured atmospheric carbon dioxide has increased over time.
  3. Reasoning: fossil fuels store ancient carbon; burning them transfers this carbon to the atmosphere.
  4. Limitation: other sources and sinks also affect atmospheric carbon dioxide.
  5. A strong argument uses evidence while avoiding overclaiming.

Common misconceptions

What to watch for

  • Giving an opinion without evidence.
  • Listing evidence without explaining how it supports the claim.
  • Using weak or irrelevant evidence because it sounds scientific.
  • Ignoring evidence that does not fit the preferred claim.

Quick check

Try these before moving on

  1. What is the difference between a claim and evidence?
  2. Why should a scientific argument mention limitations?
  3. Write one reasoning sentence that begins with “This supports the claim because…”.

Support tip

For parents and teachers

Use a three-colour check: underline the claim, circle the evidence, and highlight the reasoning. If one part is missing, the argument is not yet complete.
