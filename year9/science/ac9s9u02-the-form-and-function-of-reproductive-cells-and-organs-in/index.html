AC9S9U02 • Year 9 Science

Reproduction and Species Survival

Reproductive structures support sexual or asexual reproduction, creating different patterns of variation and population growth.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain reproduction and species survival using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect gametes and organs, fertilisation, sexual reproduction.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Reproductive cells and organs have structures suited to their functions.
  • Sexual reproduction involves gamete fusion and usually increases genetic variation.
  • Asexual reproduction uses one parent and can rapidly increase numbers.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not turn this lesson into detailed human sexuality or embryology.
  • Do not claim one reproductive strategy is universally better.

Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

Strawberry runners produce genetically similar plants without gamete fusion.

Common misconceptions
  • Sexual reproduction always means mating.
  • Asexual offspring are always perfectly identical.
