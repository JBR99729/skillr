Year 9 Science • AC9S9U03 • Topic guide

Carbon cycle processes and human impacts

This topic develops systems thinking. Students represent carbon moving between the atmosphere, organisms, soil, oceans and fossil fuels, then examine how human activities can change the balance of the cycle.

Core understanding

Carbon stores

Carbon is stored in the atmosphere, organisms, soils, oceans and fossil fuels.

Photosynthesis

Removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere and stores carbon in biomass.

Respiration and decomposition

Return carbon dioxide to the atmosphere.

Combustion

Releases carbon stored in fuels.

Scientific representations

photosynthesis: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen
a carbon-cycle diagram should show stores and labelled process arrows

Worked example: explaining an arrow

  1. An arrow from atmosphere to plants represents photosynthesis.
  2. Plants take in carbon dioxide.
  3. Carbon becomes part of glucose and plant biomass.
  4. This moves carbon from an atmospheric store to a living store.

Common misconceptions

  • Drawing arrows without naming the process.
  • Thinking carbon is created or destroyed in the cycle.
  • Confusing respiration with breathing only.
  • Ignoring long-term stores such as oceans and fossil fuels.

Curriculum mapping

Australian and international alignment

  • Australian Curriculum v9: AC9S9U03 — represent the carbon cycle and examine how key processes move carbon between Earth systems, including human influences.
  • Victoria: closest Years 9–10 Earth and Biological Sciences alignment through biogeochemical cycles, ecosystems and human impacts on Earth systems.
  • NSW: closest Stage 5 Science alignment through ecosystems, Earth systems, matter cycling and human impacts.
  • International: closely aligns with lower-secondary Earth and life science expectations on cycling of matter, photosynthesis, respiration and human effects on the carbon cycle, including NGSS-style LS2 and ESS3 concepts.

Mappings are closest curriculum alignments rather than claims that jurisdictions use identical wording.
