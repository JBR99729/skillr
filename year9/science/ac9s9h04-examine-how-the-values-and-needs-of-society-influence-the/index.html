AC9S9H04 • Year 9 Science

How Society Shapes Research Priorities

Needs, values, funding, ethics and power influence which scientific questions receive attention.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain how society shapes research priorities using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect societal need, value or concern, funding and capacity.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Societal needs and values influence research focus.
  • Funding and infrastructure enable some questions and constrain others.
  • Priority decisions have benefits, ethical dimensions and opportunity costs.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not imply all funding influence invalidates findings.
  • Do not treat ‘society’ as one group with one value set.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Identify the need
    Use the shared model to foreground societal need.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving societal need.
  2. 2. Locate influence
    Use the shared model to foreground value or concern.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving value or concern.
  3. 3. Trace the funding choice
    Use the shared model to foreground funding and capacity.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving funding and capacity.
  4. 4. Evaluate consequences
    Use the shared model to foreground research question.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving research question.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

Increasing antibiotic resistance creates a health need for new diagnostics and treatments.

Common misconceptions
  • Scientists freely choose research without constraints.
  • Funded research is automatically biased or invalid.
