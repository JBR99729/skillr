How Atomic Models Changed

New evidence changed atomic models, and unstable nuclei can decay toward more stable arrangements.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain how atomic models changed using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect Dalton, electron, nucleus.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Scientific atomic models changed when evidence could not be explained by an older model.
  • Atoms contain electrons and a nucleus with protons and neutrons.
  • Natural radioactive decay changes unstable nuclei toward more stable forms.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not teach quantum orbitals as the target model.
  • Do not imply electron-shell diagrams show literal paths.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Start with each model
    Use the shared model to foreground Dalton.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving Dalton.
  2. 2. Add the new evidence
    Use the shared model to foreground electron.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving electron.
  3. 3. Revise the structure
    Use the shared model to foreground nucleus.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving nucleus.
  4. 4. Connect instability to decay
    Use the shared model to foreground proton and neutron.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving proton and neutron.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

Rutherford’s scattering results showed most particles passed through foil while a few deflected strongly.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Compare: Dalton

comparing the mass and charge of protons, neutrons and electrons

Use the mass and charge of protons, neutrons and electrons to make the role of Dalton visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E2: Examine: electron

examining how the discovery of electrons, protons and neutrons resulted from experimental evidence and answered questions related to properties and behaviours of atoms

Use how the discovery of electrons, protons and neutrons resulted to make the role of electron visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E3: Explain: nucleus

explaining that differences in the number of neutrons in atoms of the same element results in isotopes and that naturally occurring isotopes of some elements are unstable

Use differences in the number of neutrons in atoms to make the role of nucleus visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E4: Explain: proton and neutron

describing in simple terms how different unstable isotopes decay such as radon-222 releasing an alpha particle, iodine-131 releasing a beta particle and cobalt-60 releasing gamma radiation to form stable atoms

Use in simple terms how different unstable isotopes decay to make the role of proton and neutron visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E5: Define: radioactive decay

defining half-life, examining the timescales of decay of different elements such as carbon-14 and uranium-238 and simulating or using digital simulations to examine radioactive decay including half-life

Use half-life, examining the timescales of decay of different elements to make the role of radioactive decay visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E6: Investigate: Dalton

investigating how radiocarbon and other dating methods have been used to establish that First Peoples of Australia have been present on the Australian continent for more than 60,000 years

Use how radiocarbon and other dating methods have been used to make the role of Dalton visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E7: Identify: electron

identifying where applications of radioactivity are used in medicine and industry such as diagnosing and treating cancer and checking for faults in materials used in aircraft and spacecraft

Use where applications of radioactivity are used in medicine to make the role of electron visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E8: Discuss: nucleus

discussing how mass and energy are connected at all scales and energy conversion processes within atomic nuclei

Use how mass and energy are connected at all scales to make the role of nucleus visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

Common misconceptions
  • Scientists simply guessed newer models.
  • Radioactive atoms choose to decay at a predictable time.
