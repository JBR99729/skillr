Year 9 Science • AC9S9U06 • Topic guide

How the atomic model changed with evidence

This topic shows science as a changing body of knowledge. Students examine how atomic models developed when new evidence challenged older explanations.

Start practiceTake testOpen homework

Core understanding

What students need to know

Key idea

Scientific models change when new evidence explains observations better.

Key idea

Dalton described atoms as tiny indivisible particles.

Key idea

Thomson’s electron evidence showed atoms contain smaller charged particles.

Key idea

Rutherford’s gold-foil evidence led to a nuclear model with a small dense positive nucleus.

Scientific language and representations

Useful rules and ways to represent the idea

model: a simplified explanation of something difficult to observe directly
evidence can support, refine or replace a model
Rutherford model: mostly empty space with a small positive nucleus
electrons are negatively charged particles within atoms

Worked example

Worked example: explaining Rutherford’s evidence

  1. Alpha particles were fired at thin gold foil.
  2. Most passed straight through, suggesting atoms are mostly empty space.
  3. A few were strongly deflected or bounced back.
  4. This suggested positive charge and most mass were concentrated in a small nucleus.
  5. The earlier plum-pudding model could not explain these rare large deflections well.

Common misconceptions

What to watch for

  • Thinking scientific models are guesses without evidence.
  • Saying Rutherford discovered that all particles bounced back.
  • Forgetting that most alpha particles passed through the foil.
  • Treating model change as failure rather than improvement.

Quick check

Try these before moving on

  1. What evidence showed atoms contain electrons?
  2. Why did Rutherford infer that atoms are mostly empty space?
  3. Explain why scientific models can change over time.

Support tip

For parents and teachers

Use a timeline and ask: what evidence did this model explain, and what could it not explain? This keeps the focus on evidence rather than memorising names.
