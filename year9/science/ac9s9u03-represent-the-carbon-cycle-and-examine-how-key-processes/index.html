Year 9 Science • AC9S9U03 • Topic guide

Carbon cycle processes and human impacts

This topic develops systems thinking. Students represent carbon moving between the atmosphere, organisms, soil, oceans and fossil fuels, then examine how human activities can change the balance of the cycle.

Core understanding

What students need to know

Key idea

Carbon moves between stores through processes.

Key idea

Photosynthesis removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere and stores carbon in biomass.

Key idea

Respiration, decomposition and combustion release carbon dioxide.

Key idea

Human activities can increase carbon dioxide release or reduce carbon storage.

Scientific language and representations

Useful rules and ways to represent the idea

photosynthesis: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen
respiration releases energy and carbon dioxide
combustion releases carbon stored in fuels
a carbon-cycle diagram should show stores and arrows for processes

Worked example

Worked example: explaining an arrow in the carbon cycle

  1. An arrow from atmosphere to plants represents photosynthesis.
  2. Plants take in carbon dioxide from the air.
  3. Carbon becomes part of glucose and plant biomass.
  4. This moves carbon from an atmospheric store to a living store.
  5. A strong explanation names both the process and the stores.

Common misconceptions

What to watch for

  • Drawing arrows without naming the process.
  • Thinking carbon is created or destroyed in the cycle.
  • Confusing respiration with breathing only.
  • Ignoring long-term stores such as fossil fuels and oceans.

Quick check

Try these before moving on

  1. Name two carbon stores.
  2. Which process moves carbon dioxide from air into plants?
  3. Explain how burning fossil fuels affects the carbon cycle.

Support tip

For parents and teachers

Ask students to label every arrow with a process. This turns a diagram from decoration into a scientific explanation.
