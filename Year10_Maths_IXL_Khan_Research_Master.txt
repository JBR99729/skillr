AC9M10N01
OFFICIAL DESCRIPTOR
Recognise the effect of using approximations of real numbers in repeated calculations and compare the results when using exact representations.

RESEARCH STATUS: AUTHORING-READY

CURRICULUM INTENT AND BOUNDARY
This is not simply “round a number” or “identify an irrational number”. The central idea is error propagation: replacing an exact real-number representation by a decimal approximation can change a final result, and repeated operations can accumulate or magnify the difference. Students should compare an exact pathway with one or more rounded/truncated pathways, state the size and direction of error, and decide when an approximation is reasonable. Exact representations may include fractions, radicals and symbols such as π. Required depth includes calculator/digital-tool comparison and contextual judgement; formal numerical-analysis error bounds are beyond scope.

PREREQUISITES
- Rational and irrational numbers; terminating, recurring and non-terminating decimals.
- Exact fractions and surds; index laws and square roots.
- Rounding to decimal places/significant figures.
- Order of operations, percentage error and calculator fluency.

IXL AUSTRALIA MAPPING
DIRECT FULL-CODE MATCH: SOURCE-NOT-MAPPED.
Component mappings in the Year 10 catalogue:
- V.4 Simplify radical expressions.
- V.5 Simplify radical expressions involving fractions.
- V.6 Multiply radical expressions.
- V.7 Add and subtract radical expressions.
- V.8 Simplify radical expressions using the distributive property.
- V.9 Simplify radical expressions using conjugates.
- V.10 Simplify radical expressions: mixed review.
- V.11 Convert between radical expressions and expressions with rational exponents.
These preserve and manipulate exact values, but the catalogue does not directly teach accumulated error from repeated approximation. Therefore authoring must supply the missing comparison experiments.

IXL LEARN-WITH-AN-EXAMPLE TRANSFER
The mapped radical sequence foregrounds maintaining exact symbolic form, applying one transformation at a time, and checking equivalence. Useful authoring transfer: show an exact expression beside its rounded decimal, delay rounding until the final line, and verify both on a calculator. Do not misrepresent the radical skills as full AC9M10N01 coverage.

KHAN ACADEMY EVIDENCE
Relevant foundation: Introduction to rational and irrational numbers
https://www.khanacademy.org/math/algebra/x2f8bb11595b61c86%3Airrational-numbers/x2f8bb11595b61c86%3Airrational-numbers-intro/v/introduction-to-rational-and-irrational-numbers
Khan supports classification and the meaning of irrational decimal expansions. It does not, in this mapping, provide the complete repeated-calculation investigation required by the code. Authoring gap: construct explicit exact-versus-approximate chains.

OTHER TRUSTED SOURCE USE
- Australian Curriculum v9.0: scope anchor.
- GeoGebra/Desmos calculators: repeat an operation with stored exact input and with rounded input; display tables of iteration number, exact value, approximate value, absolute error and relative error.
- Calculator documentation/conventions: distinguish internal stored precision from a rounded display.

QUESTION / APPLICATION PATTERNS
1. Compare (√2)^n computed exactly with a pathway that rounds √2 first.
2. Repeated percentage growth using an exact multiplier versus a rounded multiplier.
3. Perimeter/area/volume using π versus 3.14 or 22/7.
4. Recurrence sequences where each term is rounded, versus rounding only the final term.
5. Currency or measurement contexts where intermediate rounding is mandated.
6. Decide whether two displayed calculator answers are genuinely equal or only appear equal at the shown precision.
7. Measure accumulated absolute and percentage error.
8. Explain why premature rounding can change a decision threshold.

PROGRESSION
A. Identify exact and approximate representations.
B. Perform one calculation both ways.
C. Repeat a calculation and tabulate divergence.
D. Compare rounding at every step with rounding only at the end.
E. Interpret error in context and justify an appropriate precision.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS / DISTRACTOR LOGIC
- Treating a displayed decimal as exact.
- Believing more repeated steps cannot affect error.
- Rounding the exact and approximate results to such low precision that the difference disappears.
- Confusing truncation with rounding.
- Assuming approximation always overestimates.
- Comparing only decimal places, not absolute/relative error.
- Re-entering the displayed calculator value instead of using stored precision.
- Using 22/7 as an exact value of π.

TOPIC PAGE / CLASSROOM VIEW NEEDS
- Exact-versus-approximate split-screen examples.
- “Keep exact until the end” decision rule, with exceptions such as required currency rounding.
- Iteration/error table and an error-growth graph.
- Vocabulary: exact value, approximation, rounding, truncation, accumulated error, absolute error, relative/percentage error, significant figures.
- Teacher demonstration using calculator memory and a deliberately re-entered rounded display.

AUTHORING COVERAGE PLAN
48 practice: 8 recognition/classification; 10 single-chain comparisons; 12 repeated-calculation tables; 8 geometry/π/surd applications; 6 error measures; 4 written judgements.
16 test: 4 exact/approximate comparisons; 4 repeated-operation analyses; 4 contextual precision decisions; 4 multi-step explanations. Test stems and values must be independent of practice.

AC9M10A01
OFFICIAL DESCRIPTOR
Expand, factorise and simplify expressions and solve equations algebraically, applying exponent laws involving products, quotients and powers of variables, and the distributive law, including completing the square.

RESEARCH STATUS: AUTHORING-READY

CURRICULUM INTENT AND BOUNDARY
Students fluently transform algebraic expressions while preserving equivalence, then select transformations to solve equations. Coverage must integrate exponent laws, distributive expansion, common factors, quadratic factorisation and completing the square. The code requires connected reasoning, not isolated tricks: expansion verifies factorisation; completing the square reveals structure and supports solving.

PREREQUISITES
- Integer/rational arithmetic and algebraic notation.
- Like terms; substitution; inverse operations.
- Basic index laws and distributive property.
- Binomial multiplication and zero-product property.

IXL AUSTRALIA MAPPING
- P.3 Factor out a monomial.
- P.4 Factorise quadratics with leading coefficient 1.
- P.5 Factorise quadratics with other leading coefficients.
- P.6 Factorise special cases.
- P.7 Factorise using algebra tiles.
- P.8 Factorise by grouping.
- P.9 Factorise polynomials.
- Q.7 Solve quadratic equations using square roots.
- Q.8 Solve quadratic equations using the zero-product property.
- Q.9–Q.10 Solve quadratics by factorising.
- Q.12–Q.13 Solve quadratics by completing the square.
- Q.14 Solve quadratics using the quadratic formula.
Use the catalogue’s exponent-law and polynomial-expansion skills as prerequisites/components; Q.14 is useful comparative extension but not a substitute for the descriptor’s named methods.

ACTUAL IXL LEARN-WITH-AN-EXAMPLE EVIDENCE
Observed P.4 example: factorise f² + 5f + 6.
Paraphrased teaching sequence:
1. Match the trinomial to x² + bx + c.
2. Seek two integers whose product is c and whose sum is b.
3. Compare factor pairs of 6: 1 and 6 sum to 7; 2 and 3 sum to 5.
4. Because the sum and product are positive, both constants are positive.
5. Write (f + 2)(f + 3).
6. Expand/FOIL to verify the original expression.
Representation and vocabulary: trinomial, coefficient, constant, factor pair, product, sum, binomial factors, expand, verify.
Feedback design: diagnose whether the learner chose numbers with the right product but wrong sum, or correct magnitudes with wrong signs.

KHAN ACADEMY EVIDENCE
Relevant sequence includes factoring quadratics, completing the square and solving quadratic equations. Completing-the-square worked teaching explicitly uses half the x-coefficient, squares it, and adds the same quantity to preserve equality:
https://www.khanacademy.org/math/algebra/x2f8bb11595b61c86%3Aquadratic-functions-equations/x2f8bb11595b61c86%3Acompleting-square-quadratics/v/solving-quadratic-equations-by-completing-the-square
Khan adds area-model/structure explanations and method comparison. Authoring should connect x² + bx to a perfect-square trinomial and show why (b/2)² is required, rather than presenting a memorised rule alone.

OTHER TRUSTED SOURCE USE
- Australian Curriculum v9.0: descriptor boundary.
- GeoGebra CAS: verify equivalence of expanded/factorised/completed-square forms after students show algebraic working.
- Desmos: graph equivalent quadratic forms to confirm the same curve while exposing different useful features.

QUESTION / APPLICATION PATTERNS
- Simplify products/quotients/powers of monomials with numerical coefficients.
- Expand single and double brackets, including negative/fractional coefficients.
- Factor common monomial and binomial factors.
- Factor monic/non-monic trinomials, difference of squares and perfect squares.
- Complete the square for x²+bx+c and ax²+bx+c.
- Solve equations using factorisation, square roots or completing the square.
- Identify and correct a false algebraic transformation.
- Choose the most efficient form/method and justify.

PROGRESSION
A. One index law or distributive step.
B. Multi-law simplification with restrictions noted where relevant.
C. Expand and factor as inverse operations.
D. Factor quadratics and verify.
E. Complete the square and solve.
F. Compare methods and explain preserved equivalence.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS / DISTRACTOR LOGIC
- Adding exponents when adding like bases rather than multiplying.
- Applying a power to the variable but not the coefficient.
- Losing a negative sign when expanding.
- Factoring only one term or failing to take the greatest common factor.
- Choosing factor-pair numbers with correct product but incorrect sum.
- Omitting ± after taking square roots.
- Adding (b/2)² to one side only.
- Dividing by a leading coefficient but not every term.
- Cancelling across addition.

TOPIC PAGE / CLASSROOM VIEW NEEDS
- Exponent-law table with worked/non-example pairs.
- Area model and symbolic distributive expansion.
- Factor-pair decision tree, sign logic and FOIL verification.
- Completing-square geometric model plus algebraic algorithm.
- Method-selection comparison for quadratic equations.
- Error-analysis cards and equivalence checks.

AUTHORING COVERAGE PLAN
48 practice: 8 exponent laws; 8 expand/simplify; 8 common/special factors; 10 monic/non-monic quadratics; 8 completing square; 6 solve/method selection.
16 test: balanced four-domain set with at least 6 multi-step items and 4 reasoning/error-analysis items; no reused practice stems.

AC9M10A02
OFFICIAL DESCRIPTOR
Solve linear inequalities and simultaneous linear equations in 2 variables; interpret solutions graphically and communicate solutions in terms of the situation.

RESEARCH STATUS: AUTHORING-READY

CURRICULUM INTENT AND BOUNDARY
Students solve algebraically, connect the solution to a graph, and translate it back into contextual meaning. Linear inequalities require correct boundary and direction; simultaneous equations require an ordered-pair intersection and a contextual statement. Systems of two-variable inequalities are useful extension but must not displace the stated core.

PREREQUISITES
- Solve linear equations and graph on number lines/cartesian planes.
- Gradient, intercept and equation of a line.
- Substitute values and rearrange formulas.
- Ordered pairs and coordinate-plane intersections.

IXL AUSTRALIA MAPPING
Inequalities:
- E.1–E.12: graph/write/solve one-step, two-step and multi-step inequalities, including word problems and advanced forms.
Simultaneous equations:
- H.1–H.11: check solutions, solve by graphing, substitution, elimination and any method, including word problems.
Extension:
- I.1–I.6: graph two-variable inequalities and systems of inequalities.

ACTUAL IXL LEARN-WITH-AN-EXAMPLE EVIDENCE
Observed H.10 example with −5x + y = −15 and y = 5.
Paraphrased substitution sequence:
1. Select an equation already isolated for one variable.
2. Substitute y = 5 into the other equation.
3. Solve −5x + 5 = −15 to obtain x = 4.
4. Pair the values as (4, 5).
5. Check the ordered pair in both original equations and interpret it as their common solution.
The panel frames substitution as a four-stage method: isolate, substitute, solve, back-substitute/check. Authoring feedback should pinpoint the stage where an error arose.

KHAN ACADEMY EVIDENCE
Khan’s systems work develops graphing, substitution and elimination as equivalent routes to the intersection. Its inequality material emphasises that multiplying or dividing by a negative reverses the inequality and that graphs represent solution sets, not a single answer. Contextual systems-of-inequalities interpretation is useful extension:
https://www.khanacademy.org/math/algebra/x2f8bb11595b61c86%3Ainequalities-systems-graphs/x2f8bb11595b61c86%3Amodeling-with-linear-inequalities/v/interpreting-system-of-inequalities-in-context
Authoring transfer: require a final sentence with units and explain whether boundary points are included.

OTHER TRUSTED SOURCE USE
- Desmos: display two lines, estimate the intersection, then verify the algebraic exact solution.
- GeoGebra: dynamically compare coefficient changes and solution type.
- Australian Curriculum v9.0: scope anchor.

QUESTION / APPLICATION PATTERNS
- Solve and graph single-variable inequalities.
- Build an inequality from a verbal constraint.
- Reverse inequality after multiplication/division by a negative.
- Solve systems by graphing, substitution and elimination.
- Classify one, no or infinitely many solutions.
- Derive systems from price, mixture, distance/rate or geometry contexts.
- Interpret an intersection with units and feasibility constraints.
- Compare approximate graphical and exact algebraic solutions.
- Diagnose a boundary-line or substitution/elimination error.

PROGRESSION
A. Inequality language and number-line representations.
B. Algebraic inequalities, including negative coefficient reversal.
C. Graphical intersection of two lines.
D. Substitution, then elimination.
E. Select an efficient method.
F. Model and interpret contextual solutions; identify infeasible answers.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS / DISTRACTOR LOGIC
- Not reversing the sign after multiplying/dividing by a negative.
- Reversing it after addition/subtraction.
- Using an open point for ≤ or ≥.
- Reporting x and y separately rather than an ordered pair.
- Substituting into only part of an expression.
- Adding equations without aligning like terms.
- Treating parallel lines as “zero” rather than no common solution.
- Accepting a non-integer or negative answer that violates context.
- Reading a pixel-level graph estimate as exact.

TOPIC PAGE / CLASSROOM VIEW NEEDS
- Inequality symbol/phrase/boundary table.
- Number-line and algebra displayed together.
- Three-method systems comparison.
- Colour-coded substitution and elimination.
- Graphical meanings of one/no/infinitely many solutions.
- Context checklist: define variables, form equations, solve, check, state with units.

AUTHORING COVERAGE PLAN
48 practice: 14 inequalities; 8 graphical interpretation; 8 substitution; 8 elimination; 6 method choice/solution type; 4 contextual modelling.
16 test: 4 inequalities; 6 systems across methods; 4 contexts; 2 error-analysis/graph interpretation. Independent stems/values.

- Rewriting bases incorrectly.
- Equating exponents when bases are not equal/equivalent.

AC9M10A04
OFFICIAL DESCRIPTOR
Use mathematical modelling to solve applied problems involving growth and decay, including financial contexts; formulate problems, choosing to apply linear, quadratic or exponential models; interpret solutions and evaluate model reasonableness.

RESEARCH STATUS: AUTHORING-READY

CURRICULUM INTENT AND BOUNDARY
This is a full modelling-cycle code. Students formulate variables/assumptions, choose among linear, quadratic and exponential models, solve with appropriate technology, interpret results and evaluate reasonableness/limitations. Exponential growth and decay, including compound-interest contexts, are required but model selection is equally important.

PREREQUISITES
- Linear, quadratic and exponential representations.
- Percent change and compound multipliers.
- Function evaluation and graph interpretation.
- Units, rounding and reasonableness checks.

IXL AUSTRALIA MAPPING
- B.2–B.3 Compound interest word problems.
- M.9 Exponential growth and decay.
- M.10 Exponential growth and decay word problems.
- R.1–R.3 Identify/write linear, quadratic and exponential functions.
- R.6 Describe linear and exponential growth and decay.
Component limitation: no single IXL skill spans the complete formulate–choose–solve–interpret–evaluate modelling cycle, so the authored tasks must integrate it.

ACTUAL IXL LEARN-WITH-AN-EXAMPLE EVIDENCE
Observed M.10 radioactive-decay example: 56 g of potassium with a 12-hour half-life, find the amount after 24 hours.
Paraphrased teaching sequence:
1. Determine elapsed half-lives: 24 ÷ 12 = 2.
2. Multiply the starting amount by 1/2 once per half-life.
3. Express compactly as 56(1/2)^2.
4. Evaluate to obtain 14 g.
The example connects repeated multiplication, exponential notation and units. Feedback should distinguish an incorrect number of periods from an incorrect decay factor.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS / DISTRACTOR LOGIC
- Using r instead of 1+r or 1−r.
- Applying simple interest in a compound context.
- Mismatching annual/monthly rates and period counts.
- Choosing a model from appearance alone without checking differences/ratios/context.

- Treating a negative quadratic prediction as meaningful in context.
- Omitting units or rounding currency incorrectly.
- Extrapolating far beyond observed data without qualification.
- Declaring a calculator output reasonable without checking magnitude/direction.
