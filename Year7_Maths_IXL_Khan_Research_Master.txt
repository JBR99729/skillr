AC9M7N01 — PERFECT SQUARES AND SQUARE ROOTS
STATUS: AUTHORING-READY

CURRICULUM DESCRIPTION
Describe the relationship between perfect square numbers and square roots, and use squares of numbers and square roots of perfect square numbers to solve problems.

CURRICULUM BOUNDARY
Teach squares of whole numbers, recognition of perfect squares, the principal square root and inverse reasoning in meaningful problems. Do not drift into simplifying surds, irrational-number approximation, quadratic equations or difference-of-squares algebra. The radical symbol denotes the non-negative principal root; x² = a may have two solutions, but √a names the principal root.

IXL MAPPING AND LIVE EVIDENCE
Primary mapping: J.7 “Square roots of perfect squares” (skill code JKM), https://au.ixl.com/maths/year-7/square-roots-of-perfect-squares
Supporting mapping: J.1 “Evaluate powers”, https://au.ixl.com/maths/year-7/evaluate-powers
Live question observed: “What is the square root of 0?” This verifies direct retrieval includes boundary cases, not only 1–144.
Learn-with-an-example observed: “What is the square root of 100?” IXL teaches the learner to find the number which, multiplied by itself, equals the radicand. It displays 10² = 10 × 10 = 100, then reverses the relationship to √100 = 10. The explanation explicitly frames squaring and square root as opposite operations.
IXL progression inferred from the live skill: direct perfect-square roots; zero and one; fluency with square notation; inverse-operation checks; later items can place the same knowledge in area or missing-side settings.
High-value IXL misconception evidence: confusing √100 with 50 or with ±10; reading the radical as division by two; treating n² as 2n; failing to check by squaring.

KHAN ACADEMY EVIDENCE
Authenticated page inspected: “Intro to square roots”, https://www.khanacademy.org/math/algebra/x2f8bb11595b61c86:rational-exponents-radicals/x2f8bb11595b61c86:radicals/v/introduction-to-square-roots
Khan develops the inverse relationship from 3² = 9 to √9 = 3 and repeats with 4² = 16 and 5² = 25. It distinguishes the principal root √9 = 3 from solving x² = 9, where x can be 3 or −3. This distinction belongs in teacher notes and feedback, but test questions must remain inside the Year 7 perfect-square boundary.

AUTHORING TRANSFER
Topic Page: begin with square arrays and the vocabulary factor, square, perfect square, radical, square root and principal root. Build a table n, n × n, n² for n = 0–20. State the inverse test: if a² = b, then √b = a for a ≥ 0.
Classroom View: slide 1 visual square array; slide 2 notation connection; slide 3 worked example “A square garden has area 196 m². Side = √196 = 14 m”; slide 4 misconception contrast √81 = 9, not ±9; exit ticket combines recognition and context.
Homework: mix fact fluency, missing entries in square/root tables, area-to-side, side-to-area, perimeter after finding a side and explain-your-reasoning items.
Feedback pattern: name the inverse relation, show the matching multiplication fact, then verify by squaring the answer.

48-PRACTICE BLUEPRINT
P01–P08 identify perfect squares and match n² to values.
P09–P16 evaluate squares including 0, 1 and two-digit bases within fluent range.
P17–P24 evaluate principal square roots of perfect squares.
P25–P32 complete inverse-operation and missing-number statements.
P33–P40 solve square-area, tile-array and side-length problems.
P41–P48 multi-step perimeter, scaling, error analysis and justification.
Difficulty: 16 accessible, 16 developing, 12 challenging, 4 stretch. Use original values/contexts.

16-TEST BLUEPRINT
T01–T04 direct square/root fluency; T05–T08 inverse statements and perfect-square classification; T09–T12 original area/perimeter applications; T13–T16 independent multi-step reasoning and misconception diagnosis. No Practice stem may be reused with cosmetic number changes.

AC9M7N02 — PRIME FACTORISATION WITH EXPONENT NOTATION
STATUS: AUTHORING-READY

CURRICULUM DESCRIPTION
Represent natural numbers as products of powers of prime numbers using exponent notation.

CURRICULUM BOUNDARY
The required endpoint is a unique prime factorisation written as ascending prime powers. HCF and LCM are useful applications/elaborations but should support, not replace, the representation requirement. Distinguish factors from prime factors and exponents from multiplication coefficients.

IXL MAPPING AND LIVE EVIDENCE
Primary mapping: A.2 “Prime factorisation” (skill code RLB), https://au.ixl.com/maths/year-7/prime-factorisation
Supporting mappings: A.1 “Prime or composite”; A.5 “Highest common factor”; A.6 “Lowest common multiple”; A.7 “HCF and LCM: word problems”; Year 6 prerequisite “Write multiplication expressions using exponents”.
Learn-with-an-example observed: write the prime factorisation of 12, use exponents when appropriate and order factors least to greatest. IXL’s method repeatedly divides by prime factors until the quotient is 1: 12 ÷ 2 = 6, 6 ÷ 2 = 3, 3 ÷ 3 = 1; then writes 2 × 2 × 3 and compresses the repeated 2 as 2² × 3.
IXL design evidence: directions explicitly constrain canonical answer form; answer checking must accept mathematically equivalent ordering internally while feedback teaches least-to-greatest convention.
Common traps: stopping at a composite factor (for example 4 × 6); writing 2 × 6; using 2 × 3² for 12; exponent counts the repeated factors, not their sum; treating 1 as prime.

KHAN ACADEMY EVIDENCE
Mapped Khan resource: “Prime factorization”, which teaches factor trees/repeated decomposition and writing repeated prime factors with exponent notation: https://www.khanacademy.org/math/class-6-tg/x47164056520f4c0a%3Aplaying-with-numbers/x47164056520f4c0a%3Aprime-factorisation/v/prime-factorization-indian-accent
Supporting Khan concept: “Exponent example 2” explains that an exponent gives the number of repeated factors, not a multiplier.
Khan page access was checked in the single authenticated tab. Public metadata confirms prime definition and exponential-notation endpoint.

AUTHORING TRANSFER
Topic Page: define prime/composite; show divisibility checks; model both a factor tree and repeated division; circle prime leaves; regroup equal primes into powers; verify by expanding and multiplying.
Classroom View: use 180 as a teacher model, deliberately show two different factor trees converging on the same prime-power product; include “Is 4 × 45 finished?” hinge question; finish with reverse reconstruction from 2³ × 3².
Homework: prime/composite warm-up, factor trees, repeated-division ladders, exponent compression, expansion checks, HCF/LCM applications and explain why factor-tree shape does not change the final product.
Feedback pattern: identify the first composite factor remaining, decompose it, regroup repeated primes, expand to verify the original number.

48-PRACTICE BLUEPRINT
P01–P08 prime/composite and factor vocabulary prerequisites.
P09–P16 complete factor trees.
P17–P24 repeated division by primes.
P25–P32 write canonical prime-power products.
P33–P36 reconstruct numbers from prime powers.
P37–P40 compare two decompositions and detect errors.
P41–P44 use prime powers for HCF.
P45–P48 use prime powers for LCM and contextual cycles/grouping.
Ensure products vary materially and include repeated powers of 2, 3 and 5 plus at least one larger prime.

16-TEST BLUEPRINT
T01–T04 prerequisite discrimination and direct factorisation; T05–T08 exponent-form conversion/reconstruction; T09–T12 error analysis and uniqueness reasoning; T13–T16 original HCF/LCM or multi-step applications. Test contexts, values and reasoning structures must be independent from Practice.

AC9M7N03 — EXPANDED NOTATION USING PLACE VALUE AND POWERS OF 10
STATUS: AUTHORING-READY

CURRICULUM DESCRIPTION
Represent natural numbers in expanded notation using place value and powers of 10.

CURRICULUM BOUNDARY
Use natural numbers and non-negative whole-number powers of 10. Connect standard form, place-value expansion, multiplication form and exponent form. Scientific notation with non-integer coefficients and negative exponents belongs later and is out of scope.

IXL MAPPING AND LIVE EVIDENCE
Primary mapping: J.4 “Powers of ten” (skill code FQ6), https://au.ixl.com/maths/year-7/powers-of-ten
Supporting mapping: J.1 “Evaluate powers”; relevant place-value review skills on the Year 7 list.
Learn-with-an-example observed: find the missing exponent in 10^? = 100 000. IXL defines an exponent as the number of times the base is used as a factor, then tabulates 10¹ = 10 through 10⁴ = 10 000. For powers of 10, the exponent equals the number of zeros after the leading 1; therefore 100 000 = 10⁵.
This shortcut must be taught after the repeated-factor meaning, not as an unexplained trick.
Question families required beyond the observed missing exponent: value ↔ exponent matching; digit value; full expanded form; missing place; standard-form reconstruction; zero internal digits.

KHAN ACADEMY EVIDENCE
Authenticated Khan search for “powers of 10 expanded form” did not yield a clean age-matched lesson in its first results: SOURCE-NOT-MAPPED for a single exact Year 7 lesson.
Relevant Khan foundation remains exponent meaning (“Exponent example 2”) and place-value/expanded-form material in elementary arithmetic. Use it only for conceptual cross-checking; IXL and AC v9/QCAA provide the direct mapping.
Public Khan evidence reinforces: b^n means n factors of b. Therefore 10⁵ means five factors of 10 and has place value one hundred thousand.

AUTHORING TRANSFER
Topic Page: place-value chart from ones to millions; align each column with 10⁰, 10¹, …; demonstrate 604 032 = 6×10⁵ + 0×10⁴ + 4×10³ + 0×10² + 3×10¹ + 2×10⁰, then show the conventional omission of zero terms.
Classroom View: physically shift a digit one place left and multiply its value by 10; compare 10³ and 3¹⁰ to prevent base/exponent reversal; reconstruct a number from unordered place-value terms; include zeros as placeholders.
Homework: read/write powers of ten, value-of-digit questions, expansions, reconstructions, missing exponent/coefficients, error correction and large-number contexts.
Feedback pattern: locate the digit’s column, name its value, write digit × matching power of 10, and verify by evaluating the sum.

48-PRACTICE BLUEPRINT
P01–P08 evaluate powers of 10 and identify exponents.
P09–P16 match place names with powers of 10.
P17–P24 expand natural numbers with multiplication by powers.
P25–P32 reconstruct standard form, including internal/trailing zeros.
P33–P40 find missing digits, coefficients, exponents or terms.
P41–P48 compare representations, order terms, analyse errors and apply to populations/distances/data counts.
Use 10⁰ explicitly in some items and accept omission of zero-coefficient terms where appropriate.

16-TEST BLUEPRINT
T01–T04 powers/place-value fluency; T05–T08 two-way conversion; T09–T12 missing-part and zero-placeholder reasoning; T13–T16 independent contextual and error-analysis items. No scientific notation or negative exponents.

AC9M7N04 — EQUIVALENT REPRESENTATIONS OF RATIONAL NUMBERS
STATUS: AUTHORING-READY

CURRICULUM DESCRIPTION
Find equivalent representations of rational numbers and represent rational numbers on a number line.

CURRICULUM BOUNDARY
Cover equivalent fractions, simplest form, improper fractions/mixed numbers and decimal/fraction equivalence where denominators permit terminating decimals; locate and compare positive and negative rational numbers on number lines as demanded by the descriptor. Do not restrict the bank to part-whole diagrams between 0 and 1.

IXL MAPPING AND LIVE EVIDENCE
Primary mappings:
- F.2 “Graph equivalent fractions on number lines”, https://au.ixl.com/maths/year-7/graph-equivalent-fractions-on-number-lines
- F.3 “Equivalent fractions”, https://au.ixl.com/maths/year-7/equivalent-fractions
- F.4 “Write fractions in lowest terms”, https://au.ixl.com/maths/year-7/write-fractions-in-lowest-terms
Supporting: F.7 compare/order fractions, F.9 mixed/improper conversion, and decimal number-line skills.

IXL Learn-with-an-example evidence inspected sequentially:
1. Number-line equivalence: top line partitioned into fourths and bottom into eighths; the same point shows 1/4 = 2/8. The method is positional: equal rational numbers occupy exactly the same point even when partitions differ.
2. Missing equivalent numerator: 1/4 = ?/8. Parallel number lines show that the denominator doubled and the numerator also doubled, producing 2/8.
3. Lowest terms: simplify 2/6. IXL defines lowest terms as the smallest possible numerator and denominator, finds the largest common divisor 2, then divides both by 2 to obtain 1/3.
Critical authoring rule: representational equivalence must be justified by scaling both numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number or by coincidence at the same number-line point. “Make denominators the same” is not itself a proof.
Common traps: changing only numerator or denominator; counting marks rather than equal intervals; assuming larger denominator means larger fraction; plotting negative rationals in the wrong direction; mistaking simplest form for a decimal approximation.

KHAN ACADEMY EVIDENCE
Mapped resource: “Creating equivalent fractions”, https://www.khanacademy.org/math/arithmetic/fraction-arithmetic/x18ca194a%3Aequivalent-fractions/v/generating-equivalent-fractions
Public metadata states the lesson uses 2/3, 4/6 and 6/9 and represents equivalence on a number line.
Supporting Khan resource: “Equivalent fractions with models”, which uses area/tape models; authenticated page access was checked in the single Khan tab.
Khan’s useful teaching move is to connect visual subdivision to multiplying numerator and denominator by the same factor, rather than presenting a rule alone.

AUTHORING TRANSFER
Topic Page: open with one point represented as 1/2, 2/4, 0.5 and 50%; show fraction strips and aligned number lines; formalise k·a/k·b = a/b for non-zero k; simplify via greatest common divisor; extend the number line left of zero and beyond one.
Classroom View: compare aligned fourth/eighth number lines; jointly construct an equivalence chain; model −3/4 between −1 and 0; use a double number line or hundred grid for fraction-decimal-percent links; exit ticket must require both a representation and a justification.
Homework: fraction-wall/number-line interpretation, missing equivalent terms, simplest form, mixed/improper conversions, terminating decimal equivalents, positive/negative plotting and contextual comparisons.
Feedback pattern: identify the scale factor or common divisor, apply it to both terms, then verify with cross-products or the same number-line location.

48-PRACTICE BLUEPRINT
P01–P08 read fraction strips and aligned number lines.
P09–P16 generate equivalent fractions and fill missing terms.
P17–P24 simplify using common divisors/GCD.
P25–P30 convert improper fractions and mixed numbers.
P31–P36 convert familiar fractions, decimals and percentages.
P37–P42 plot/order positive rational numbers including values >1.
P43–P46 plot/order negative rational numbers.
P47–P48 multi-representation error analysis and contextual justification.
Models must have mathematically correct partition counts and labelled endpoints.

16-TEST BLUEPRINT
T01–T04 direct equivalence/simplest form; T05–T08 conversion among forms; T09–T12 number-line placement including negative and >1 values; T13–T16 independent comparison, error analysis and contextual reasoning. Avoid reusing Practice visuals, numbers or story structures.
