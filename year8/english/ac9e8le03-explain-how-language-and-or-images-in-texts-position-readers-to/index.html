Explain how language and/or images in texts position readers to respond and form viewpoints

1. Outcome Overview & Core Concepts

We are learning to explain how deliberate language and text choices shape meaning for an audience or reader.

Australian Curriculum target: explain how language and/or images in texts position readers to respond and form viewpoints

Core concepts

  • explain how language and/or images in texts position readers to respond and form viewpoints
  • choice → context → effect reasoning

Success criteria

  • Identify a precise language or text choice.
  • Use the surrounding context as evidence.
  • Explain the effect on audience, relationship, purpose or reader response.
  • Transfer the explanation to a new example.

Useful prior knowledge

  • Read a short text closely.
  • Locate a phrase, structural choice or visual/text feature.
  • Use a complete sentence to explain an effect.

2. Subject-Specific Content & Key Examples

Choice → context → effect

Teacher model: Do not stop at naming a technique. Show the evidence, place it in context and explain the effect.

  1. Choice: Identify the exact word, structure or text feature connected to explain how language and/or images in texts position readers to respond and form viewpoints.
  2. Context: Explain who is speaking or writing, for whom, and what the text is doing.
  3. Effect: Explain how the choice shapes meaning, relationship, purpose or reader response.

E1: Connect voice to meaning and audience effect

A writer or speaker can use voice to shape discussing how a complex picture book combines words and images to position readers to respond and influence how an audience understands the text.

Teacher move: Highlight the exact wording that shows voice, then connect it to discussing how a complex picture book combines words and images to position readers to respond.

Ask: Which choice is evidence, and what effect does it create for the audience?

E2: Connect representation to meaning and audience effect

A writer or speaker can use representation to shape sharing opinions about how a film positions the viewer to respond to a character and influence how an audience understands the text.

Teacher move: Highlight the exact wording that shows representation, then connect it to sharing opinions about how a film positions the viewer to respond to a character.

Ask: Which choice is evidence, and what effect does it create for the audience?

One word, different effect

Show two short versions of the same message. Students underline the changed choice and predict the audience effect.

Share: Pairs share the choice before the interpretation.

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • A feature name is enough to prove an interpretation.: Use the frame: ‘The choice ___ suggests/positions ___ because ___.’
  • Effect means whether the student personally likes the text.: Replace preference with an audience effect supported by the exact wording or structure.

Do not overteach

  • Do not require university-level terminology or theory.
  • Do not treat one interpretation as valid without evidence.
  • Do not replace close reading with plot summary or personal preference.

4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence

Step 1: Explicit Teaching & Modelling

Highlight one phrase, structure or image.

Students: Names what the text does.

Step 2: Guided Analysis

Place two versions side by side.

Students: Identifies what changes.

Step 3: Collaborative / Independent Practice

Model choice → context → effect.

Students: Builds a complete analytical sentence.

Step 4: Formative Assessment

Remove the worked example.

Students: Applies the frame independently.

Support, Core and Extend

Support

Use two short versions, colour-code choice and effect, and provide the sentence frame ‘The choice ___ makes the audience ___ because ___.’

Keep the concept: Keeps the same choice → effect reasoning with reduced text load.

Core

Annotate an extract and write a choice–context–effect explanation.

Keep the concept: Directly assesses the listed English concept.

Extend

Compare two audiences or mediums and evaluate which wording is more effective, citing evidence.

Keep the concept: Deepens evaluation without requiring later-year theory.
