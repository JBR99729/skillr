Success criteria

  • Identify a precise language or text choice.
  • Use the surrounding context as evidence.
  • Explain the effect on audience, relationship, purpose or reader response.

2. Subject-Specific Content & Key Examples

Choice → context → effect

Teacher model: Do not stop at naming a technique. Show the evidence, place it in context and explain the effect.

  1. Choice: Identify the exact word, structure or text feature connected to use comprehension strategies such as visualising, predicting, connecting, summarising, monitoring, questioning and inferring to interpret and evaluate ideas in texts.
  2. Context: Explain who is speaking or writing, for whom, and what the text is doing.
  3. Effect: Explain how the choice shapes meaning, relationship, purpose or reader response.

E1: Connect purpose to meaning and audience effect

This example focuses on reflecting on content by connecting and comparing information across and between texts. Students identify the relevant purpose, cite the exact evidence, and explain how it shapes meaning or audience response.

Teacher move: Highlight the exact wording that shows purpose. Ask students to connect that evidence to reflecting on content by connecting and comparing information across and between texts and then name the audience effect.

Ask: Which choice is evidence, and what effect does it create for the audience?

E2: Connect structure to meaning and audience effect

This example focuses on determining and applying criteria for evaluating the content of a website. Students identify the relevant structure, cite the exact evidence, and explain how it shapes meaning or audience response.

Teacher move: Highlight the exact wording that shows structure. Ask students to connect that evidence to determining and applying criteria for evaluating the content of a website and then name the audience effect.

Ask: Which choice is evidence, and what effect does it create for the audience?

E3: Connect evidence to meaning and audience effect

This example focuses on analysing the selection and composition of an image in a text and evaluating its effect on the credibility of the story. Students identify the relevant evidence, cite the exact evidence, and explain how it shapes meaning or audience response.

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • A feature name is enough to prove an interpretation: Use the frame: ‘The choice ___ suggests/positions ___ because ___.’
  • Effect means whether the student personally likes the text: Replace preference with an audience effect supported by the exact wording or structure.

Do not overteach

  • Do not require university-level terminology or theory.
  • Do not treat one interpretation as valid without evidence.
  • Do not replace close reading with plot summary or personal preference.
