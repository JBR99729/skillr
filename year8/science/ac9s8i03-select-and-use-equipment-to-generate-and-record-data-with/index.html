AC9S8I03 • Year 8 Science

Measure with Useful Precision

Instrument choice, scale reading, units and recording conventions determine the precision and usefulness of data.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain measure with useful precision using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect quantity, instrument range, resolution.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Equipment range and resolution must suit the quantity.
  • Measurements use correct scale-reading conventions.
  • Data tables record units and precision consistently.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not invent extra decimal places.
  • Do not use ‘accuracy’ and ‘precision’ as interchangeable terms.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Select: quantity

selecting and using equipment with required precision such as adjusting magnification to observe specific cell structures and recording this magnification and reading the bottom of the meniscus to determine the precise volume of liquid

Use using equipment with required precision to make the role of quantity visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E2: Record: instrument range

recording data with precision appropriate to the instrument such as rounding up or down with finer graduations or estimating an intermediate value with coarser graduations

Use data with precision appropriate to the instrument to make the role of instrument range visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E3: Use: resolution

using digital tools such as digital microscopes, simulations and video-recording devices when appropriate to observe, measure and record qualitative and quantitative data

Use resolution in context to make the role of resolution visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E4: Use: correct reading

using conventions related to dependent and independent variables with relevant units when constructing tables and spreadsheets

Use conventions related to dependent and independent variables with relevant to make the role of correct reading visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E5: Use: recorded value and unit

using appropriate positive and negative signs for standard units, number of decimal points and exponential notation where relevant when recording data

Use appropriate positive and negative signs for standard units, number to make the role of recorded value and unit visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

Common misconceptions
  • More decimal places always means more precision.
  • A digital display is automatically accurate.
