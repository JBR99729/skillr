AC9S8U01 • Year 8 Science

Inside Plant and Animal Cells

Cells are the smallest living units. Their internal structures perform jobs that keep the cell alive.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning how cell structures help plant and animal cells stay alive.

Success criteria

  • Identify the nucleus, cell membrane, cytoplasm, cell wall, chloroplasts and vacuole in a model.
  • Connect each cell structure to its function.
  • Compare plant and animal cells using evidence from diagrams or microscope images.
  • Explain one strength and one limitation of a cell model.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Cells are the basic units of living things.
  • The nucleus, cell membrane and cytoplasm are represented in both plant and animal cells.
  • Plant-cell models also show a cell wall, chloroplasts and a large vacuole, with functions linked to the cell's needs.
  • Plant and animal cells can be compared using visible evidence and scientifically useful models.
  • Microscopes extended human observation and changed scientific understanding of cells.

Prerequisites

  • Living things have observable features and needs.
  • A model represents selected features of something that may be difficult to observe directly.
  • Light microscopes magnify small specimens.

Do not overteach

  • Do not make detailed biochemical pathways, organelle ultrastructure, cell division or prokaryote–eukaryote classification the target.
  • Do not imply that every plant or animal cell has the same shape or that every plant cell contains visible chloroplasts.
  • Do not present a model's colours or proportions as literal microscope evidence.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Locate cells within a living organism
    Reveal the scale journey one level at a time.
    Students: Order organism, organ, tissue and cell.
  2. 2. Identify cell structures
    Build common structures first, then add plant-specific structures.
    Students: Label structures and match each to a function.
  3. 3. Separate microscope evidence from model conventions
    Compare a microscope field with a labelled model.
    Students: State what is directly visible and what has been simplified.
  4. 4. Compare plant and animal cells
    Sort shared and different structures using evidence.
    Students: Complete a compare model and justify one difference.
  5. 5. Evaluate models and scientific tools
    Contrast two models and trace how microscopes changed evidence.
    Students: Name a model strength, limitation and evidence-based scientific change.
Worked examples

From label to explanation

Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll that captures light energy for photosynthesis. They are shown in plant cells that photosynthesise.

Compare with evidence

Both models contain a cell membrane, cytoplasm and nucleus. The plant-cell model also contains a rigid cell wall and chloroplasts.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Zoom from a whole leaf to one cell

exploring an augmented or virtual reality tour of a plant or animal to ‘zoom in’ and understand the scale of cells

A living organism is made of organs, organs contain tissues, and tissues contain cells too small to see unaided.

E2: Match six structures to their functions

identifying the structure and function of organelles in cells including the nucleus, cell membrane, cell wall, cytoplasm, chloroplasts and vacuoles

Cell structures perform different jobs: control, boundary, reaction space, support, light capture and storage.

E3: Read microscope evidence carefully

examining a variety of cells, including single-celled organisms, using a light microscope, a digital microscope, simulations and photomicrographs

Microscope images are observations; diagrams and simulations organise or explain selected features.

E4: Compare plant and animal cells with evidence

comparing the similarities and differences of plant cells and animal cells visible with a light microscope and represented in a digital or physical model

Plant and animal cells share core structures, while the usual plant-cell model also includes a wall, chloroplasts and a large vacuole.

E5: Evaluate and improve a cell model

designing a physical or digital model of a cell and explaining how the representation models the cell

A useful model makes selected relationships clear but cannot copy every detail, colour, scale or process.

E6: Trace how tools changed cell knowledge

considering how the invention of the microscope has contributed to understanding of cell structure

Scientific knowledge of cells changed when microscopes made previously invisible evidence observable.

Common misconceptions

  • {"claim":"Every plant cell contains chloroplasts.","cause":"One textbook leaf-cell diagram is generalised to all plant tissues.","fix":"Compare a leaf cell with a root cell; chloroplasts occur mainly in photosynthetic tissues."}
  • {"claim":"Microscope images should look like brightly coloured diagrams.","cause":"Model conventions are confused with direct observations.","fix":"Place an uncoloured microscope field beside the diagram and label observation versus representation."}
  • {"claim":"A larger vacuole means the plant cell is always physically larger.","cause":"Diagram proportions are treated as measured scale.","fix":"State that models simplify relative size; use a scale bar or measurement before comparing actual cell size."}
