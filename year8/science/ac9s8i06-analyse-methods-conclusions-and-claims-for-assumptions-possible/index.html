AC9S8I06 • Year 8 Science

Test the Method and the Claim

Scientific evaluation checks assumptions, errors, conflicting evidence and unanswered questions before accepting a conclusion.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain test the method and the claim using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect method, assumption, source of error.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Evaluation links a specific limitation to its effect on evidence.
  • Conflicting results and assumptions affect confidence.
  • Conclusions stay within the data and identify useful next questions.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not use vague ‘more accurate’ improvements.
  • Do not reject a conclusion merely because uncertainty exists.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Restate the claim
    Use the shared model to foreground method.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving method.
  2. 2. Audit the method
    Use the shared model to foreground assumption.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving assumption.
  3. 3. Compare evidence
    Use the shared model to foreground source of error.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving source of error.
  4. 4. Set a justified confidence level
    Use the shared model to foreground evidence conflict.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving evidence conflict.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

A group claims light intensity alone caused higher solar output.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Identify: method

identifying assumptions then examining if extra variable controls are required and how these might affect the data and conclusion

Use assumptions then examining if extra variable controls are required to make the role of method visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E2: Identify: assumption

identifying sources of error in methods such as inconsistent variable control and inaccuracies in procedures or measurements, and explaining how the method could be improved

Use sources of error in methods to make the role of assumption visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E3: Compare: source of error

comparing results with other groups or secondary sources to examine consistency and describing where there may be conflicting results or conclusions

Use results with other groups or secondary sources to examine to make the role of source of error visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E4: Analyse: evidence conflict

analysing conclusions or claims to determine if there are further questions which should be explored to verify the conclusion or claim

Use conclusions or claims to determine if there are further to make the role of evidence conflict visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E5: Analyse: conclusion and next question

analysing conclusions to identify facts or premises that are taken for granted to be true, and discussing the reasonableness of those assumptions with others

Use conclusions to identify facts or premises that are taken to make the role of conclusion and next question visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

Common misconceptions
  • Human error explains every problem.
  • A result that matches the prediction proves the method valid.
