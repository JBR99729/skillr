Track Energy Through a System

Energy can be classified as kinetic or potential and tracked as it transfers and transforms.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain track energy through a system using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect energy store, kinetic, potential.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Kinetic energy involves movement; potential energy is associated with position or condition.
  • Energy transfers between objects and transforms between forms.
  • Heating is often a by-product and energy is conserved.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not introduce advanced energy equations as the lesson target.
  • Do not use arrows that imply energy disappears.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Define the system
    Use the shared model to foreground energy store.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving energy store.
  2. 2. Classify energy
    Use the shared model to foreground kinetic.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving kinetic.
  3. 3. Trace transfers
    Use the shared model to foreground potential.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving potential.
  4. 4. Account for heating
    Use the shared model to foreground transfer.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving transfer.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

A raised cart has gravitational potential energy.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Investigate: energy store

investigating relationships between kinetic and potential energy in a simple system such as a roller-coaster or Newton’s cradle

Use relationships between kinetic and potential energy in a simple to make the role of energy store visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E2: Explore: kinetic

classifying types of energy as either kinetic energy such as movement, heat and electricity or potential energy such as chemical, elastic and gravitational

Use classifying types of energy as either kinetic energy to make the role of kinetic visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E3: Explore: potential

critiquing and using representations such as flow diagrams to illustrate changes between different forms of energy in a system

Use critiquing and using representations to make the role of potential visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E4: Identify: transfer

identifying where heat energy is produced as a by-product of energy transfer, such as filament light globes, exercise, and battery charging and use

Use where heat energy is produced as a by-product to make the role of transfer visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E5: Use: transformation and heating

using electrical circuits and components to demonstrate electrical energy transfer and its transformation into heat, light and sound

Use electrical circuits and components to demonstrate electrical energy transfer to make the role of transformation and heating visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E6: Observe: energy store

observing a Rube Goldberg machine and identifying the energy transfers and transformations involved

Use a Rube Goldberg machine and identifying the energy transfers to make the role of energy store visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E7: Investigate: kinetic

investigating traditional fire-starting methods used by First Nations Australians and their understandings of the transformation of energy

Use traditional fire-starting methods used by First Nations Australians to make the role of kinetic visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

Common misconceptions
  • Energy is used up.
  • Electricity is stored inside wires.
