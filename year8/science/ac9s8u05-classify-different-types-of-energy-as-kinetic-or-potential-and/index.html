Track Energy Through a System

Classify energy as kinetic or potential, then trace how energy transfers and transforms through real systems without disappearing.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
What you need to know

Energy can be classified by how it is stored or expressed and tracked through a system. In this curriculum code, movement, thermal energy and electricity are treated as kinetic forms, while chemical, elastic and gravitational energy are treated as potential forms. The key skill is not memorising labels alone: identify the starting store, trace transfers, name useful outputs and account for heating or sound.

Success criteria

  • Classify common examples as kinetic or potential energy using the Year 8 curriculum framing.
  • Explain changes between gravitational potential and kinetic energy in systems such as a roller coaster.
  • Read and critique an energy-flow diagram.
  • Trace transformations in simple circuits and Rube Goldberg machines.
  • Explain why heating is often a by-product rather than saying energy is “lost”.
  • Complete simple numerical energy accounts using joules and, as an extension, simple useful-output percentages.
Energy cheat sheet

Kinetic

Energy associated with movement or moving particles.

Examples: moving bicycle, thermal energy, electrical energy in this curriculum classification.

Potential

Stored energy associated with position or condition.

Examples: gravitational, elastic and chemical energy.

Transfer

Energy moves from one object or part of a system to another.

Example: electrical transfer from a battery to a lamp.

Transformation

Energy changes from one form to another.

Example: gravitational potential → kinetic.

Quick check: classify a stretched band, moving skateboard, warm pan and charged battery. Then explain one transformation involving any two.
Important notes
  • Energy is conserved: it can be transferred and transformed, but it does not vanish.
  • Heating and sound still count: they are often less useful outputs, not “missing energy”.
  • Define the system: decide what is inside the system before identifying inputs and outputs.
  • Core calculations here are energy accounts: use supplied joule values to find missing outputs. Equations such as KE = ½mv², GPE = mgh and E = VIt are not the target of AC9S8U05.
Visual: roller coaster energy changes

In an ideal model, energy shifts between gravitational potential and kinetic. In a real coaster, some energy is continually transferred to the surroundings through friction, air resistance, heating and sound.

Energy flow diagrams

A strong flow diagram includes the starting store, the transfer pathway and all important outputs. If an input is 100 J, the outputs together must also account for 100 J.

Critique: Why is “chemical → light” an incomplete diagram for a battery torch?
Calculation workshop: energy accounting
Core Year 8 relationship: total input energy = useful output energy + other transferred energy.

Example 1 · Find the heating/sound output

A motor receives 120 J and produces 90 J of useful movement.

Calculation: 120 − 90 = 30 J.

Conclusion: 30 J is transferred to other outputs such as heating and sound.

Example 2 · Several outputs

A machine receives 400 J. It produces 260 J of movement and 90 J of heating.

Calculation: 400 − 260 − 90 = 50 J.

Conclusion: 50 J must be accounted for by another output such as sound.

Example 3 · Percentage extension

A device receives 250 J and produces 200 J of useful output.

Calculation: 200 ÷ 250 × 100 = 80%.

Conclusion: 80% of the supplied energy appears in the useful output. Treat percentage efficiency as extension practice, not the main curriculum target.

Circuits: what changes?
ComponentMain useful transformationOther common output
LampElectrical → lightHeating
Buzzer / speakerElectrical → soundHeating
MotorElectrical → kineticHeating and sound
HeaterElectrical → thermalOften the heating itself is the intended output

Do not say electricity is “stored in the wires”. The circuit provides a pathway for energy transfer from a source to components.

Rube Goldberg machines

Use a chain such as raised marble → rolling marble → falling dominoes → lever → bell. At each step, identify the current energy form and where energy transfers next. A useful model answer might trace gravitational potential → kinetic → kinetic transfer → sound + heating.

Challenge: Find two points where energy is transferred to the surroundings even though the machine continues working.
First Nations fire-starting knowledge

AC9S8U05 includes investigating traditional fire-starting methods used by First Nations Australians and understandings of energy transformation. In friction-based methods, repeated controlled motion transfers kinetic energy into thermal energy at contacting surfaces until suitable tinder can ignite. Teach this through culturally authoritative sources and local context where possible; do not reduce living cultural knowledge to a generic “primitive technology” example.

Worked examples: Observe → Connect → Conclude

1. Roller coaster

Observe: The car is highest at A and fastest near B.

Connect: At A it has a large gravitational potential store. As it descends, this transforms mainly into kinetic energy.

Conclude: The changing height and speed show energy transforming within the system while some is transferred to the surroundings in a real coaster.

2. Filament globe

Observe: The globe emits light and becomes hot.

Connect: Electrical transfer produces light but also transfers energy thermally.

Conclude: Heating is part of the energy account, not evidence that energy disappeared.

3. Motor calculation

Observe: Input = 300 J; useful movement = 210 J.

Connect: Conservation requires all 300 J to be accounted for.

Conclude: 90 J is transferred to other outputs such as heating and sound.

4. Flow-diagram critique

Observe: A diagram shows 200 J input but only 150 J output.

Connect: A complete energy model must account for the remaining 50 J.

Conclude: The diagram is incomplete unless it adds another output such as heating or sound.

Common misconceptions: test yourself

“Energy gets used up.”

False. It transfers and transforms; less useful heating and sound still contain energy.

“Electricity is stored inside wires.”

False. Wires provide a pathway for electrical energy transfer.

“Heat means a device failed.”

False. Heating is a common output even in working devices.

“Kinetic energy means only whole objects moving.”

False in this curriculum framing. Thermal and electrical energy are also classified as kinetic forms.

“An energy-flow arrow means energy disappeared from the previous form.”

False. The arrow represents transfer or transformation.

“If a machine is 70% useful, the other 30% is destroyed.”

False. The remainder is transferred to other outputs.

10 important questions to solve
  1. Classify a moving bicycle and a stretched spring as kinetic or potential.
  2. Explain the main energy change as a roller coaster moves downhill.
  3. Why does a Newton’s cradle eventually stop?
  4. Why should a torch flow diagram include heating?
  5. State the useful transformation in a motor.
  6. Trace the main energy sequence in a marble-and-domino Rube Goldberg step.
  7. Explain the energy transformation in a friction-based fire-starting method.
  8. A device receives 150 J and gives 110 J useful output. Find the other output.
  9. A system receives 500 J and outputs 320 J movement plus 120 J heating. Find the remaining output.
  10. Why must the total outputs in an energy diagram equal the total input?
Solutions with explanations
  1. Moving bicycle = kinetic; stretched spring = elastic potential.
  2. Gravitational potential energy decreases while kinetic energy increases.
  3. Energy is progressively transferred to the surroundings as heating and sound.
  4. Real lamps transfer part of the energy thermally, so light alone is an incomplete account.
  5. Electrical → kinetic, with heating/sound commonly also produced.
  6. Gravitational potential → kinetic → kinetic transfer through collisions.
  7. Kinetic → thermal through friction.
  8. 150 − 110 = 40 J.
  9. 500 − 320 − 120 = 60 J.
  10. Because energy is conserved; an incomplete total means an output or transfer has been omitted.
Australian Curriculum v9.0 and elaborations

AC9S8U05: classify different types of energy as kinetic or potential and investigate energy transfer and transformations in simple systems.

  • E1 — Roller coaster / Newton’s cradle: compare how gravitational potential and kinetic energy change through a simple mechanical system; explain why real systems also transfer energy as heating and sound.
  • E2 — Classify energy: sort movement, heat and electricity as kinetic forms and chemical, elastic and gravitational as potential forms using the curriculum framing.
  • E3 — Flow diagrams: construct and critique diagrams that show stores, pathways and outputs; identify missing outputs that would break the energy account.
  • E4 — Heating by-product: investigate examples such as filament globes, exercise and batteries and explain where thermal energy appears.
  • E5 — Circuits: use lamps, buzzers, heaters and motors to identify electrical transfer and transformations to light, sound, heat and movement.
  • E6 — Rube Goldberg: trace several linked transfers and transformations through a multi-step machine.
  • E7 — First Nations fire-starting: investigate culturally appropriate examples of traditional fire-starting and explain kinetic-to-thermal energy transformation.
Teacher toolkit: 50-minute lesson

0–5 min · Diagnostic

Sort six examples into kinetic/potential. Ask students to justify one choice.

5–15 min · Roller-coaster model

Use the visual above. Students trace height, speed and heating/sound.

15–25 min · Flow diagrams

Model a torch and motor. Require all outputs, not only the useful one.

25–35 min · Calculation practice

Use three energy-accounting problems with supplied joule values.

35–44 min · Circuit/Rube challenge

Trace energy through one simple circuit or multi-step machine.

44–50 min · Exit ticket

“A device receives 200 J and produces 140 J useful output. Where is the rest?” Require calculation + explanation.

Differentiation

Enabler

Provide pre-labelled input/output cards and the sentence frame “Energy starts as ___, transfers by ___ and becomes ___.”

Core

Require complete diagrams plus one missing-energy calculation.

Extension

Use percentage useful-output calculations and critique an intentionally incorrect energy diagram.
