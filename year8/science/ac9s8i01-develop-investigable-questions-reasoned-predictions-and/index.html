AC9S8I01 • Year 8 Science

Ask Questions Science Can Test

Investigable questions identify measurable variables; predictions and hypotheses give reasoned, testable expectations.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain ask questions science can test using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect observation, investigable question, independent and dependent variable.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Investigable questions can be answered with evidence.
  • Predictions state an expected pattern.
  • Hypotheses connect variables with scientific reasoning and can be tested.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not claim correlation automatically shows causation.
  • Do not require a hypothesis for purely descriptive investigations.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Observe a pattern
    Use the shared model to foreground observation.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving observation.
  2. 2. Name variables
    Use the shared model to foreground investigable question.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving investigable question.
  3. 3. Write a testable question
    Use the shared model to foreground independent and dependent variable.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving independent and dependent variable.
  4. 4. Reason a prediction
    Use the shared model to foreground prediction.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving prediction.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

Observation: brighter light appears to increase solar-cell output.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Discuss: observation

discussing what is meant by a causal relationship and examining how causation is different from correlation

Use what is meant by a causal relationship and examining to make the role of observation visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E2: Develop: investigable question

developing investigable questions to test causal relationships, such as: ‘How does the concentration of a salt solution affect plant cells? How does the amount of sunlight affect the amount of electricity produced by a solar cell?’

Use investigable questions to test causal relationships to make the role of investigable question visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E3: Develop: independent and dependent variable

developing investigable questions to explore scientific models, such as: ‘How do the shapes of the continents support the theory of plate tectonics?’

Use investigable questions to explore scientific models to make the role of independent and dependent variable visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E4: Predict: prediction

predicting what will happen when conditions change in a given scenario or phenomenon, such as: ‘When materials of less resistance are used to transfer electricity there will be less heat energy produced’

Use what will happen when conditions change in a given to make the role of prediction visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E5: Formulate: reasoned hypothesis

formulating hypotheses such as: ‘An earthquake of greater magnitude will cause more damage because there is more energy transferred’

Use hypotheses such as: ‘An earthquake of greater magnitude will to make the role of reasoned hypothesis visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

Common misconceptions
  • A topic is an investigable question.
  • A hypothesis is a guess.
