AC9S8U06 • Year 8 Science

Classify Matter with Particle Models

Particle types and arrangements distinguish elements, compounds and mixtures; symbols and formulas communicate composition.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain classify matter with particle models using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect element, compound, mixture.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Elements contain one type of atom; compounds contain chemically joined atom types in fixed ratios; mixtures contain substances together without chemical bonding.
  • Representations show different information and have limitations.
  • Symbols and formulas communicate composition efficiently.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not imply particles in diagrams are shown at true scale.
  • Do not teach bonding theory or electron configurations as the target.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Read the particles
    Use the shared model to foreground element.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving element.
  2. 2. Classify the sample
    Use the shared model to foreground compound.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving compound.
  3. 3. Translate representations
    Use the shared model to foreground mixture.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving mixture.
  4. 4. Evaluate the model
    Use the shared model to foreground particle model.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving particle model.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

A box contains identical particles, each made from one black and two white atoms.
