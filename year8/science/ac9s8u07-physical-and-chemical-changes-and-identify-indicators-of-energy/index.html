Has a New Substance Formed?

Evidence distinguishes physical change from chemical change, including observed energy changes.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain has a new substance formed? using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect physical change, chemical change, new substance evidence.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Physical changes do not create a new substance; chemical changes rearrange particles into new substances.
  • Indicators include gas, precipitate, persistent colour or temperature change.
  • Energy evidence must be interpreted with other observations.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not claim every bubble, colour change or temperature change proves a reaction.
  • Do not introduce full reaction-rate or equilibrium theory.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Observe before and after
    Use the shared model to foreground physical change.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving physical change.
  2. 2. Identify evidence

  3. 3. Classify the change

Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

Vinegar and bicarbonate produce bubbles and a temperature change.

Common misconceptions
  • Any colour change proves a reaction.
  • Melting is chemical because it needs energy.
