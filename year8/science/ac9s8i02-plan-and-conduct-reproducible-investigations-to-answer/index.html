AC9S8I02 • Year 8 Science

Plan a Reproducible Investigation

A reproducible plan controls variables, states assumptions and manages risks, ethics and cultural responsibilities.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain plan a reproducible investigation using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect question and variables, repeatable method, control and assumption.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Plans align variables, method and evidence with the question.
  • Assumptions and sources of risk are explicit.
  • Ethical and cultural protocols shape whether and how an investigation proceeds.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not treat cultural protocols as a generic permission checkbox.
  • Do not conduct investigations involving protected sites, materials or knowledge without authority.

Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

To test light intensity and solar-cell output, vary measured distance from one lamp.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Explore: question and variables

designing reproducible investigations that specifically test variables of the causal relationship and control the remaining variables

Use designing reproducible investigations that specifically test variables to make the role of question and variables visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E2: Identify: repeatable method

identifying assumptions related to testing a hypothesis using analogous models, such as using dialysis tubing to model the properties of plant cell walls or a shake table to model the effects of an earthquake on buildings

Use assumptions related to testing a hypothesis using analogous models to make the role of repeatable method visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E3: Explain: control and assumption

explaining why safety procedures address identified risks

Use safety procedures address identified risks to make the role of control and assumption visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E4: Consider: risk and ethics

considering ethical issues relating to the access to and use of biological material and secondary data

Use ethical issues relating to the access to and use to make the role of risk and ethics visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E5: Acknowledge: Country/Place protocol

acknowledging and recognising First Nations Australians’ artefacts and heritage sites of significance such as ceremonial grounds and traditional quarries, and ensuring they cause no harm to heritage sites and artefacts

Use recognising First Nations Australians’ artefacts and heritage sites to make the role of Country/Place protocol visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

Common misconceptions
  • Repeating trials makes an unfair test fair.
  • A risk list is a risk assessment.
