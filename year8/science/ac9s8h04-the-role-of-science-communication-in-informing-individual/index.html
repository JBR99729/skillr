AC9S8H04 • Year 8 Science

Science Communication Shapes Decisions

How scientific evidence is selected, framed and communicated can influence viewpoints, policy and regulation.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain science communication shapes decisions using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect evidence source, message, medium.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Purpose, audience and medium shape science communication.
  • Credibility depends on evidence and transparency, not popularity alone.
  • Communication can influence individual action and community policy.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not equate engagement with accuracy.
  • Do not teach persuasion techniques without checking evidence quality.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Check the evidence
    Use the shared model to foreground evidence source.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving evidence source.
  2. 2. Identify the audience
    Use the shared model to foreground message.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving message.
  3. 3. Analyse the message
    Use the shared model to foreground medium.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving medium.
  4. 4. Trace its influence
    Use the shared model to foreground audience.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving audience.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

A tsunami alert converts seismic and ocean data into an urgent public message.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Investigate: evidence source

investigating campaigns designed to increase rates of organ donation

Use campaigns designed to increase rates of organ donation to make the role of evidence source visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E2: Explore: message

exploring how seismic data is collected and shared between governments across the Asia-Pacific region and how governments use this data including for tsunami alerts

Use how seismic data is collected and shared between governments to make the role of message visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E3: Investigate: medium

investigating how promotion of biodegradable materials and the importance of using them has informed individual viewpoints

Use how promotion of biodegradable materials and the importance to make the role of medium visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E4: Research: audience

researching how science organisations and high-profile science communicators such as Professor Lisa Harvey-Smith or Dr Karl Kruszelnicki influence people’s attitudes to science

Use how science organisations and high-profile science communicators to make the role of audience visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

Common misconceptions
  • A famous communicator is automatically a reliable source.
  • Simplifying a message always makes it inaccurate.
