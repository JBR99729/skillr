AC9S8I05 • Year 8 Science

Find Patterns Without Ignoring Anomalies

Analysis describes patterns, trends and relationships while testing whether anomalies or alternative explanations matter.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain find patterns without ignoring anomalies using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect data set, pattern, trend.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Patterns and trends are described with direction and evidence.
  • Anomalies are identified and investigated.
  • Causal claims require more than correlation.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not hide inconvenient data.
  • Do not report a mean alone when the distribution or outliers matter.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Inspect the distribution
    Use the shared model to foreground data set.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving data set.
  2. 2. Describe the pattern
    Use the shared model to foreground pattern.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving pattern.
  3. 3. Locate anomalies
    Use the shared model to foreground trend.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving trend.
  4. 4. Test the explanation
    Use the shared model to foreground relationship.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving relationship.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

Solar output generally rises with light intensity, but one trial is much lower.

analysing changes in battery energy output following recharging over many cycles and relating to available chemical potential energy

Use changes in battery energy output following recharging over many to make the role of relationship visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E5: Compare: anomaly and explanation

comparing temperature differences obtained by reacting different proportions of the same chemicals to determine if there is a relationship

Use temperature differences obtained by reacting different proportions to make the role of anomaly and explanation visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

Common misconceptions
  • An anomaly should be deleted.
  • Two variables moving together proves causation.
