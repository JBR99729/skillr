AC9S8H03 • Year 8 Science

Evaluate Scientific Responses

A proposed science-based response can produce benefits, risks and trade-offs across ethical, environmental, social and economic dimensions.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain evaluate scientific responses using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect scientific response, benefit, risk.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Scientific knowledge informs responses but does not make value decisions alone.
  • Consequences can differ among stakeholders and over time.
  • Recommendations should acknowledge evidence, uncertainty and trade-offs.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not force a single moral answer where evidence supports several defensible choices.
  • Do not present stakeholder viewpoints without evaluating their evidence.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Define the issue
    Use the shared model to foreground scientific response.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving scientific response.
  2. 2. Explain the science
    Use the shared model to foreground benefit.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving benefit.
  3. 3. Map consequences
    Use the shared model to foreground risk.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving risk.
  4. 4. Justify a decision
    Use the shared model to foreground stakeholder.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving stakeholder.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

An earthquake early-warning system can give people seconds to act.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Research: scientific response

researching and discussing ethical issues that arise from organ transplantation

Use discussing ethical issues that arise from organ transplantation to make the role of scientific response visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E2: Discuss: benefit

discussing how scientists’ development of organoids has impacted on the ethical, environmental, social and economic issues that arise from using live animals in a laboratory to research diseases and treatments

Use how scientists’ development of organoids has impacted to make the role of benefit visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E3: Investigate: risk

investigating how scientific responses including new building materials, improved predictions and early warning systems have supported communities living in a country in the Asia-Pacific region located near plate boundaries, for example Japan, Indonesia or New Zealand

Use how scientific responses to make the role of risk visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E4: Examine: stakeholder

examining how the development of hybrid and solar, electric and hydrogen-powered vehicles are applications of contemporary science responses to the depletion of fossil fuels and exploring environmental considerations

Use how the development of hybrid and solar, electric to make the role of stakeholder visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E5: Explore: ethical, environmental, social and economic trade-off

exploring how the development of biodegradable materials has led to more sustainable packaging and reduction in landfill

Use how the development of biodegradable materials has led to make the role of ethical, environmental, social and economic trade-off visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

Common misconceptions
  • The scientifically possible option must be the best option.
  • Listing pros and cons is analysis.
