AC9S8U02 • Year 8 Science

From Cells to Organ Systems

Specialised cells form tissues and organs whose structures enable a whole system to keep an organism alive.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain from cells to organ systems using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect specialised cell, tissue, organ.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Biological organisation proceeds from specialised cells to tissues, organs and systems.
  • Structure at each level supports function.
  • A change at one level can affect survival of the whole organism.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not reduce every organ system to a simple machine analogy.
  • Do not teach detailed senior physiology or biochemical pathways as the target.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Locate the levels
    Use the shared model to foreground specialised cell.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving specialised cell.
  2. 2. Link structure to function
    Use the shared model to foreground tissue.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving tissue.
  3. 3. Trace the system
    Use the shared model to foreground organ.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving organ.
  4. 4. Test a disruption
    Use the shared model to foreground organ system.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving organ system.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

Alveoli have thin walls and a large surface area.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Compare: specialised cell

comparing 2-dimensional and 3-dimensional representations of organ systems to understand how organs are positioned within the body

Use 2-dimensional and 3-dimensional representations of organ systems to understand to make the role of specialised cell visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E2: Compare: tissue

comparing the structure and function of analogous systems in a plant and an animal

Use the structure and function of analogous systems to make the role of tissue visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E3: Examine: organ

examining the specialised cells and tissues involved in structure and function of particular organs in an organ system

Use the specialised cells and tissues involved in structure to make the role of organ visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E4: Explain: organ system

describing the structure of each organ in a system and relating its function to the overall function of the system

Use the structure of each organ in a system to make the role of organ system visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E5: Research: survival

researching how a disorder in cells or tissues can affect how an organ functions, such as how hardening of the arteries can lead to poor circulation or heart disease

Use how a disorder in cells or tissues can affect to make the role of survival visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E6: Investigate: specialised cell

investigating how an artificial organ mimics or augments the function or functions of a real organ

Use how an artificial organ mimics or augments the function to make the role of specialised cell visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

Common misconceptions
  • An organ works alone.
  • All cells in an organ are the same.
