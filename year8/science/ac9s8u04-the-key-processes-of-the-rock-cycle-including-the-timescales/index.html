Rocks Record Their Formation

Rock properties reflect processes in the rock cycle, which operate over very different timescales.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain rocks record their formation using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Igneous, sedimentary and metamorphic rocks form through different processes.
  • Observable properties provide evidence of formation.
  • Rock-cycle processes span short events to geological timescales and influence uses.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not present the rock cycle as a one-way loop.
  • Do not identify rocks from colour alone.

E1: Compare: igneous

Use weathering and melting in context to make the role of weathering and melting visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E5: Explain: time and properties

explaining the uses of different types of rocks with reference to their properties and formation

investigating how First Nations Australians have used quarrying to access rocks for use as or production of everyday objects such as grindstones, hammerstones, anvils and cutting tools

E8: Explore: metamorphic

Use how the mining of ores and minerals impacts to make the role of metamorphic visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

Common misconceptions
  • Every rock follows one fixed circular path.
  • All rock-cycle changes take the same time.
