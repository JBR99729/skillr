AC9S8H01 • Year 8 Science

Evidence Changes Scientific Ideas

Scientific explanations change when new tools, observations or perspectives produce stronger evidence.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain evidence changes scientific ideas using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect earlier explanation, new evidence, challenge.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Scientific knowledge is durable but open to revision.
  • New technologies and perspectives can reveal evidence unavailable earlier.
  • A revised explanation should account for existing and new evidence.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not present scientific change as a simple list of lone geniuses.
  • Do not imply every perspective has equal evidential support.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. State the earlier idea
    Use the shared model to foreground earlier explanation.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving earlier explanation.
  2. 2. Inspect the new evidence
    Use the shared model to foreground new evidence.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving new evidence.
  3. 3. Find the mismatch
    Use the shared model to foreground challenge.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving challenge.
  4. 4. Explain the revision
    Use the shared model to foreground revised model.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving revised model.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

Continental drift proposed moving continents but lacked a convincing mechanism.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Identify: earlier explanation

identifying how technological developments, such as those related to microscopes and medical imaging, have led to improved understanding of cells and organs

Use how technological developments to make the role of earlier explanation visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E2: Consider: new evidence

considering how advances in technologies have enabled the repair and replacement of organs using synthetic materials

Use how advances in technologies have enabled the repair to make the role of new evidence visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E3: Analyse: challenge

analysing how sustainability priorities such as efficiency and limiting environmental impact have led to innovative practices in mining and mine site regeneration

Use how sustainability priorities to make the role of challenge visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E4: Examine: revised model

examining the evidence that led to the acceptance of the theory of plate tectonics over the idea of continental drift

Use the evidence that led to the acceptance to make the role of revised model visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E5: Investigate: stronger explanation

investigating how advances in deep Earth imaging techniques have enabled identification of mineral, energy and water resources beneath surface sedimentary rock

Use how advances in deep Earth imaging techniques have enabled to make the role of stronger explanation visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E6: Discuss: earlier explanation

discussing the story of Sir Isaac Newton’s discovery of gravity or the questions that Albert Einstein asked which led him to developing a new theory

Use the story of Sir Isaac Newton’s discovery of gravity to make the role of earlier explanation visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E7: Research: new evidence

researching why Dmitri Mendeleev developed a different representation of the periodic table

Use Dmitri Mendeleev developed a different representation of the periodic to make the role of new evidence visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

Common misconceptions
  • Science changes because facts are unreliable.
  • One surprising result automatically overturns a theory.
