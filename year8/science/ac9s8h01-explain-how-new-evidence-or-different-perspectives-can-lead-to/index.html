What students learn in AC9S8H01

This unit helps students build a clear, usable understanding of Explain how new evidence or different perspectives can lead to.... The goal is not to memorise one answer pattern. Students should be able to explain the idea, recognise it in a new example and apply it in a short practice or worksheet task.

  • Start with the main idea: explain how new evidence or different perspectives can lead to changes in scientific knowledge.
  • Use concrete examples, pictures, oral explanation and short written responses before moving to independent practice.
  • identifying how technological developments, such as those related to microscopes and medical imaging, have led to improved understanding of cells and organs considering how advances in technologies have enabled the repair and replacement of organs using synthetic materials
  • Finish with mixed questions so students must choose the correct strategy rather than copy the last example.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Question banks for this unit should draw from the content description and the eligible elaborations below. First Nations-specific notes are kept out of generated question coverage unless they are part of the core concept.

  • Content description: explain how new evidence or different perspectives can lead to changes in scientific knowledge
  • E1: identifying how technological developments, such as those related to microscopes and medical imaging, have led to improved understanding of cells and organs
  • E2: considering how advances in technologies have enabled the repair and replacement of organs using synthetic materials
  • E3: analysing how sustainability priorities such as efficiency and limiting environmental impact have led to innovative practices in mining and mine site regeneration
  • E4: examining the evidence that led to the acceptance of the theory of plate tectonics over the idea of continental drift
  • E5: investigating how advances in deep Earth imaging techniques have enabled identification of mineral, energy and water resources beneath surface sedimentary rock
  • E6: discussing the story of Sir Isaac Newton’s discovery of gravity or the questions that Albert Einstein asked which led him to developing a new theory
  • E7: researching why Dmitri Mendeleev developed a different representation of the periodic table

How to use this unit

Read the topic guide, download the teacher slide for instruction, complete the printable worksheet, then use Practice and Test when the unit's checked 8-question bank is available.

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Describing what is seen without connecting it to a question, pattern or prediction.
  • Using everyday words only and missing simple scientific vocabulary.
  • Changing more than one thing in an investigation, which makes the comparison unfair.
