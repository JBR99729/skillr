Step 1 · Learn the idea

Efficient calculation depends on number structure, operation order and checking sign and magnitude.

Learning intention: Efficient calculation depends on number structure, operation order and checking sign and magnitude.

Success criteria

  • Explain the central relationship in operations with integers and rational numbers using correct mathematical language.
  • Use the worked methods for represent signed operations and choose an efficient strategy in unfamiliar examples.
  • Check the result using substitution, estimation, an inverse operation or another representation.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • use the 4 operations with integers and with rational numbers, choosing and using efficient strategies and digital tools where appropriate

Prerequisites

  • Relevant Year 7 number representations and vocabulary.

May support informally

  • Digital tools may test, visualise and verify after the mathematical structure is explicit.

Do not overteach

  • Do not introduce later-year formalism that is unnecessary for this content description.

Avoid this trap
  • Exact and approximate values are treated as interchangeable — Keep exact notation until the requested approximation step.
  • The operations with integers and rational numbers model is copied without interpretation — Connect every label in ‘Use patterns to assist in establishing the rules for the multiplication and division of integers.’ to its mathematical role.
  • The result is not verified — Use a second representation, boundary case, inverse operation or simulation check.

Important questions and answers

1. What is the central idea in Operations with Integers and Rational Numbers?

Answer: Efficient calculation depends on number structure, operation order and checking sign and magnitude.

2. How can you represent signed operations?

Answer: −6−(−9)=−6+9=3

3. Why is −3² different from (−3)²?

Answer: Exponentiation occurs before the leading negative: −3²=−9, while brackets make the base −3, so (−3)²=9.
