Position in Three Dimensions

A 3D coordinate system locates a point with an ordered triple referenced to three mutually perpendicular axes.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

A 3D coordinate system locates a point with an ordered triple referenced to three mutually perpendicular axes.

Learning intention: A 3D coordinate system locates a point with an ordered triple referenced to three mutually perpendicular axes.

Success criteria

  • Explain read an ordered triple using the approved model.
  • Apply navigate a digital 3d model to a new case.
  • Verify a conclusion with a second representation, estimate or test case.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • describe the position and location of objects in 3 dimensions in different ways, including using a three-dimensional coordinate system with the use of dynamic geometric software and other digital tools

Prerequisites

  • Relevant Year 7 space representations and vocabulary.

May support informally

  • Digital tools may test, visualise and verify after the mathematical structure is explicit.

Do not overteach

  • Do not introduce later-year formalism that is unnecessary for this content description.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • A diagram is trusted by appearance instead of stated conditions — Mark givens and name the theorem or transformation used.
  • The position in three dimensions model is copied without interpretation — Connect every label in ‘Locating aircraft/drones using latitude, longitude and altitude as a three-dimensional coordinate system.’ to its mathematical role.
  • The result is not verified — Use a second representation, boundary case, inverse operation or simulation check.
Support, core and extend

Support: Reduce numerical or representational load while retaining the same decision and explanation.

Core: Connect at least two representations, justify the method and verify independently.

Extend: Test a boundary case, counterexample or model limitation without introducing later-year procedures.

Quick check and mastery evidence

Students should explain the relationship in their own words, apply it in a new case and verify the result using another representation, estimate or check.
