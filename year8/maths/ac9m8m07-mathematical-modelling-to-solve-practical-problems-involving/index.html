Modelling Ratios and Rates

Ratio and rate models compare quantities multiplicatively and must preserve units, assumptions and context.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
What students learn

Ratio and rate models compare quantities multiplicatively and must preserve units, assumptions and context.

Learning intention: Ratio and rate models compare quantities multiplicatively and must preserve units, assumptions and context.

Success criteria

  • Explain scale a multi-part ratio using the approved model.
  • Apply review proportional assumptions to a new case.
  • Verify a conclusion with a second representation, estimate or test case.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • use mathematical modelling to solve practical problems involving ratios and rates, including financial contexts; formulate problems; interpret and communicate solutions in terms of the situation, reviewing the appropriateness of the model

Prerequisites

  • Relevant Year 7 measurement representations and vocabulary.

May support informally

  • Digital tools may test, visualise and verify after the mathematical structure is explicit.

Do not overteach

  • Do not introduce later-year formalism that is unnecessary for this content description.

Worked examples

Scale a multi-part ratio

Scales on maps and plans, the mixing of chemicals or ingredients, or calculating magnification factors applying relevant ratios and proportions.

Teacher prompt: For scale a multi-part ratio, which quantities must scale or compare by the same factor?

Model a direct rate

Model problems involving converting money amounts using different exchange rates and applying them when planning and budgeting for overseas travel.

Teacher prompt: For model a direct rate, which quantities must scale or compare by the same factor?

Compare financial rates

Income tax, using taxation rates on annual income, comparing different taxation brackets and rates of pay.

Teacher prompt: For compare financial rates, which quantities must scale or compare by the same factor?

Interpret a ratio-model solution

Model situations involving the use of ratios in radiocarbon dating methods, including the ratio of carbon-14 to carbon-12 isotopes in organisms.

Teacher prompt: For interpret a ratio-model solution, which quantities must scale or compare by the same factor?

Review proportional assumptions

Model situations involving ratio and its application in the making of string and cordage by First Nations Australians, including the ratio of length to the mass of a rope.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Dimensions, units or accuracy are mixed — Label every measure and convert before calculating.
  • The modelling ratios and rates model is copied without interpretation — Connect every label in ‘Scales on maps and plans, the mixing of chemicals or ingredients, or calculating magnification factors applying relevant ratios and proportions.’ to its mathematical role.
  • The result is not verified — Use a second representation, boundary case, inverse operation or simulation check.
Support, core and extend

Support: Reduce numerical or representational load while retaining the same decision and explanation.

Core: Connect at least two representations, justify the method and verify independently.

Extend: Test a boundary case, counterexample or model limitation without introducing later-year procedures.

Quick check and mastery evidence

Prompt: For scale a multi-part ratio, which quantities must scale or compare by the same factor?

Expected idea: Use the model evidence: Scales on maps and plans, the mixing of chemicals or ingredients, or calculating magnification factors applying relevant ratios and proportions. Then name the deciding relationship or limitation in a complete mathematical sentence.

Students should explain the relationship in their own words, apply it in a new case and verify the result using another representation, estimate or check.
