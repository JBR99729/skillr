Terminating and Recurring Decimals

A rational number's decimal either terminates or eventually repeats; fraction structure can predict which occurs.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
What students learn

A rational number's decimal either terminates or eventually repeats; fraction structure can predict which occurs.

Learning intention: A rational number's decimal either terminates or eventually repeats; fraction structure can predict which occurs.

Success criteria

  • Explain which fractions terminate? using the approved model.
  • Apply write recurring decimals clearly to a new case.
  • Verify a conclusion with a second representation, estimate or test case.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • recognise terminating and recurring decimals, using digital tools as appropriate

Prerequisites

  • Relevant Year 7 number representations and vocabulary.

May support informally

  • Digital tools may test, visualise and verify after the mathematical structure is explicit.

Do not overteach

  • Do not introduce later-year formalism that is unnecessary for this content description.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Which fractions terminate?

Curriculum: identifying when a fraction has a terminating decimal expansion from the prime factorisation of its denominator; for example, \frac7{24}\;=\;0.291\overset\_6 does not have a terminating decimal expansion, while \frac7{25}\;=\;0.28 does

Identify when a fraction has a terminating decimal expansion from the prime factorisation of its denominator.

Curriculum: identifying terminating, recurring and non-terminating decimals and choosing their appropriate representations such as \frac13 is represented as 0.\overset\_3

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Exact and approximate values are treated as interchangeable — Keep exact notation until the requested approximation step.
  • The terminating and recurring decimals model is copied without interpretation — Connect every label in ‘7/24 = 0.2916̅ does not have a terminating decimal expansion, while 7/25 = 0.28 does.’ to its mathematical role.
  • The result is not verified — Use a second representation, boundary case, inverse operation or simulation check.
Support, core and extend

Support: Reduce numerical or representational load while retaining the same decision and explanation.

Core: Connect at least two representations, justify the method and verify independently.

Extend: Test a boundary case, counterexample or model limitation without introducing later-year procedures.

Quick check and mastery evidence

Prompt: For which fractions terminate?, which algebraic step preserves equivalence, and why?

Expected idea: Use the model evidence: 7/24 = 0.2916̅ does not have a terminating decimal expansion, while 7/25 = 0.28 does. Then name the deciding relationship or limitation in a complete mathematical sentence.

Students should explain the relationship in their own words, apply it in a new case and verify the result using another representation, estimate or check.
