Terminating and Recurring Decimals — AC9M8N03

Recognise whether rational-number decimals terminate or repeat, use denominator structure to predict the behaviour, and distinguish recurring decimals from irrational decimals.

LearnTeacher SlidesHomeworkPractice SheetPracticeTest
1. Learning goals
  • Identify terminating and recurring decimal expansions.
  • Use recurring-bar notation accurately.
  • Predict termination from a simplified fraction's denominator.
  • Distinguish recurring rational decimals from non-recurring irrational decimals.

Australian Curriculum focus: recognise terminating and recurring decimals, using digital tools as appropriate

2. Prerequisite knowledge

Recall equivalent fractions, simplifying fractions, decimal place value, prime factors and long division.

3. Concept teaching

Every rational number has a decimal expansion that either terminates or eventually repeats. For example, 3/8=0.375 terminates, while 2/11=0.181818… repeats.

For a fraction in simplest form, a terminating decimal occurs exactly when the denominator has no prime factors other than 2 and/or 5. Powers of 10 contain only factors 2 and 5, which explains the rule rather than making it a fact to memorise.

A recurring decimal is still rational because its repeating pattern represents a fraction. Do not confuse non-terminating with irrational: the repeating/non-repeating distinction matters.

4. Worked/modelled examples

Predict without dividing

7/40 is in simplest form and 40=2³×5, so its decimal terminates. In fact, 7/40=0.175.

Recurring decimal

7/24 has denominator 24=2³×3. The factor 3 remains, so the decimal recurs: 0.291666…

Simplify first

6/15 simplifies to 2/5. Although 15 contains a factor 3, the simplified denominator is 5, so the decimal terminates: 0.4.

5. Common misconceptions and corrections
  • Any long decimal is irrational. Correction: a recurring decimal is rational, no matter how long its written form.
  • Check the denominator before simplifying. Correction: the prime-factor test applies to the denominator in simplest form.
  • A bar over one digit always means only that digit repeats. Correction: the bar must cover the full repeating block.
  • A denominator containing 2 or 5 must terminate. Correction: it terminates only if 2 and 5 are the only prime factors after simplification.
6. Guided practice
  1. Decide whether 3/20 terminates and explain using prime factors.
  2. Simplify 14/35, then predict its decimal behaviour.
  3. Write 0.272727… using recurring notation.
  4. Explain why 1/6 is rational even though its decimal does not terminate.
7. Independent practice
  1. Classify 7/8, 5/12, 9/25 and 11/30 as terminating or recurring.
  2. Predict whether 13/125 terminates without dividing.
  3. Simplify 21/28 and determine the decimal type.
  4. Write 0.16666… with correct recurring notation.
  5. Explain why a non-terminating recurring decimal is not irrational.
  6. Give one fraction whose decimal repeats with a two-digit block and verify using a digital tool.
8. Reasoning/problem-solving

A student says 18/45 must recur because 45 contains the prime factor 3. Determine whether the student is correct and explain the exact step they omitted.

9. Important questions and answers
  1. When does a fraction terminate as a decimal? In simplest form, its denominator has only prime factors 2 and/or 5.
  2. Is every non-terminating decimal irrational? No. Recurring decimals are non-terminating but rational.
  3. Why simplify the fraction first? Common factors can remove prime factors from the denominator and change the termination prediction.
  4. What does recurring notation communicate? It identifies the digit or block that repeats indefinitely.
10. Assessment-style questions + review hints
  1. Determine whether 17/60 terminates or recurs without calculating the full decimal.
    Factor the simplified denominator and identify any prime factor other than 2 or 5.
  2. A calculator shows 0.5833333333 for 7/12. Explain what the display hides.
    The display is finite; the exact decimal has a recurring 3.
  3. Find a fraction equivalent to 0.45 and use it to explain why the decimal terminates.
    Convert to 45/100, simplify, and inspect the denominator.
11. Exit ticket/mastery check
  • I identify terminating and recurring decimals.
  • I simplify before using the denominator test.
  • I use recurring notation correctly.
  • I distinguish recurring rational decimals from irrational decimals.

Exit ticket: Why does the fraction 3/15 terminate even though 15 has a factor 3?

12. Teacher + parent guidance

Teacher

Use fraction simplification and prime factorisation side by side. Digital tools should confirm patterns after students predict them, not replace the reasoning.

Parent/carer

Choose simple fractions and ask “terminate or repeat?” before using a calculator. Then use the calculator to check the prediction.
