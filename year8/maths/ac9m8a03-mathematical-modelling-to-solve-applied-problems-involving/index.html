Modelling with Linear Relations

A linear model represents constant change plus a starting value, within a stated and defensible domain.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

A linear model represents constant change plus a starting value, within a stated and defensible domain.

Learning intention: A linear model represents constant change plus a starting value, within a stated and defensible domain.

Success criteria

  • Explain build a linear model from a context using the approved model.
  • Apply test the model's assumptions to a new case.
  • Verify a conclusion with a second representation, estimate or test case.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • use mathematical modelling to solve applied problems involving linear relations, including financial contexts; formulate problems with linear functions, choosing a representation; interpret and communicate solutions in terms of the situation, reviewing the appropriateness of the model

Prerequisites

  • Relevant Year 7 algebra representations and vocabulary.

May support informally

  • Digital tools may test, visualise and verify after the mathematical structure is explicit.

Do not overteach

  • Do not introduce later-year formalism that is unnecessary for this content description.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • A rule is applied without preserving equivalence or its conditions — Return to the linked table, graph or expanded form and verify by substitution.
  • The modelling with linear relations model is copied without interpretation — Connect every label in ‘Model situations involving linear functions, including practical contexts such as taxi fares involving flag fall fees, motion in a straight line at a constant speed.’ to its mathematical role.
  • The result is not verified — Use a second representation, boundary case, inverse operation or simulation check.
Support, core and extend

Support: Reduce numerical or representational load while retaining the same decision and explanation.

Core: Connect at least two representations, justify the method and verify independently.

Extend: Test a boundary case, counterexample or model limitation without introducing later-year procedures.
