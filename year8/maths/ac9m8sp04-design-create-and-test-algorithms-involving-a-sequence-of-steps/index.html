Algorithms for Congruence and Similarity

A geometric decision algorithm needs ordered tests, explicit conditions and outputs that work for examples and counterexamples.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
What students learn

A geometric decision algorithm needs ordered tests, explicit conditions and outputs that work for examples and counterexamples.

Learning intention: A geometric decision algorithm needs ordered tests, explicit conditions and outputs that work for examples and counterexamples.

Success criteria

  • Explain design a congruence decision tree using the approved model.
  • Apply test and debug a geometric algorithm to a new case.
  • Verify a conclusion with a second representation, estimate or test case.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • design, create and test algorithms involving a sequence of steps and decisions that identify congruency or similarity of shapes, and describe how the algorithm works

Prerequisites

  • Relevant Year 7 space representations and vocabulary.

May support informally

  • Digital tools may test, visualise and verify after the mathematical structure is explicit.

Do not overteach

  • Do not introduce later-year formalism that is unnecessary for this content description.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • A diagram is trusted by appearance instead of stated conditions — Mark givens and name the theorem or transformation used.
  • The algorithms for congruence and similarity model is copied without interpretation — Connect every label in ‘Listing the properties or criteria necessary to determine if shapes are similar or congruent.’ to its mathematical role.
  • The result is not verified — Use a second representation, boundary case, inverse operation or simulation check.
Support, core and extend

Support: Reduce numerical or representational load while retaining the same decision and explanation.

Core: Connect at least two representations, justify the method and verify independently.

Extend: Test a boundary case, counterexample or model limitation without introducing later-year procedures.
