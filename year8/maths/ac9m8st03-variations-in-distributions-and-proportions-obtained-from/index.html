Sampling Variation and Sample Size

Random samples from one population vary; larger samples usually produce less variable estimates, not guaranteed identical results.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

Random samples from one population vary; larger samples usually produce less variable estimates, not guaranteed identical results.

Learning intention: Random samples from one population vary; larger samples usually produce less variable estimates, not guaranteed identical results.

Success criteria

  • Explain watch random samples vary using the approved model.
  • Apply explain uncertainty without guessing to a new case.
  • Verify a conclusion with a second representation, estimate or test case.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • compare variations in distributions and proportions obtained from random samples of the same size drawn from a population and recognise the effect of sample size on this variation

Prerequisites

  • Relevant Year 7 statistics representations and vocabulary.

May support informally

  • Digital tools may test, visualise and verify after the mathematical structure is explicit.

Do not overteach

  • Do not introduce later-year formalism that is unnecessary for this content description.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • A display or sample is treated as proof of a stronger claim — Link the inference to sampling, variation, context and uncertainty.
  • The sampling variation and sample size model is copied without interpretation — Connect every label in ‘Compare the proportion of students in favour of a proposal for a change in school uniform between different random samples of 50 students from the school population.’ to its mathematical role.
  • The result is not verified — Use a second representation, boundary case, inverse operation or simulation check.
Support, core and extend

Support: Reduce numerical or representational load while retaining the same decision and explanation.

Core: Connect at least two representations, justify the method and verify independently.

Extend: Test a boundary case, counterexample or model limitation without introducing later-year procedures.
