Linear Expressions

Equivalent expressions can look different while producing the same value for every permitted substitution.

What students learn

Learning intention: Equivalent expressions can look different while producing the same value for every permitted substitution.

Success criteria

  • Explain simplify expressions with algebra tiles using the approved model.
  • Apply explain why two expressions are equivalent to a new case.
  • Verify a conclusion with a second representation, estimate or test case.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • create, expand, factorise, rearrange and simplify linear expressions, applying the associative, commutative, identity, distributive and inverse properties

Prerequisites

  • Relevant Year 7 algebra representations and vocabulary.

May support informally

  • Digital tools may test, visualise and verify after the mathematical structure is explicit.

Do not overteach

  • Do not introduce later-year formalism that is unnecessary for this content description.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • A rule is applied without preserving equivalence or its conditions — Return to the linked table, graph or expanded form and verify by substitution.
  • The linear expressions model is copied without interpretation — Connect every label in ‘Using manipulatives to demonstrate that 2x+4 = 2(x+2), 3(a-b)=3a -3b, or 5(m+2n)+3m-4n = 5m+10n+3m-4n = 8m+6n.’ to its mathematical role.
  • The result is not verified — Use a second representation, boundary case, inverse operation or simulation check.
