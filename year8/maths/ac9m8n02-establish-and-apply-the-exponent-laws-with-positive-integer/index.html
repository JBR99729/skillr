Exponent Laws

Exponent laws describe repeated multiplication; they apply only when the base and operation meet the law's conditions.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
What students learn

Exponent laws describe repeated multiplication; they apply only when the base and operation meet the law's conditions.

Learning intention: Exponent laws describe repeated multiplication; they apply only when the base and operation meet the law's conditions.

Success criteria

  • Explain build the product-of-powers law using the approved model.
  • Apply why does a⁰ equal 1? to a new case.
  • Verify a conclusion with a second representation, estimate or test case.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • establish and apply the exponent laws with positive integer exponents and the zero-exponent, using exponent notation with numbers

Prerequisites

  • Relevant Year 7 number representations and vocabulary.

May support informally

  • Digital tools may test, visualise and verify after the mathematical structure is explicit.

Do not overteach

  • Do not introduce later-year formalism that is unnecessary for this content description.

Worked examples

Build the product-of-powers law

2³×2² can be represented as (2×2×2)×(2×2)=2^5 and connecting the result to the addition of exponents.

Teacher prompt: For build the product-of-powers law, which algebraic step preserves equivalence, and why?

Why does a⁰ equal 1?

(3^4)/(3^4) = 1, and 3^(4-4) = 3^0 to illustrate the necessity that for any non-zero natural number n, n^0 = 1.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Exact and approximate values are treated as interchangeable — Keep exact notation until the requested approximation step.
  • The exponent laws model is copied without interpretation — Connect every label in ‘2³×2² can be represented as (2×2×2)×(2×2)=2^5 and connecting the result to the addition of exponents.’ to its mathematical role.
  • The result is not verified — Use a second representation, boundary case, inverse operation or simulation check.
Support, core and extend

Support: Reduce numerical or representational load while retaining the same decision and explanation.

Core: Connect at least two representations, justify the method and verify independently.

Extend: Test a boundary case, counterexample or model limitation without introducing later-year procedures.
