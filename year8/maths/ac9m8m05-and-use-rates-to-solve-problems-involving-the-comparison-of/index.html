Rates and Unit Rates

A rate compares unlike units; a unit rate expresses the comparison per one unit and supports fair comparison.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
What students learn

A rate compares unlike units; a unit rate expresses the comparison per one unit and supports fair comparison.

Learning intention: A rate compares unlike units; a unit rate expresses the comparison per one unit and supports fair comparison.

Success criteria

  • Explain read a rate as unlike units using the approved model.
  • Apply interpret a rate in context to a new case.
  • Verify a conclusion with a second representation, estimate or test case.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • recognise and use rates to solve problems involving the comparison of 2 related quantities of different units of measure

Prerequisites

  • Relevant Year 7 measurement representations and vocabulary.

May support informally

  • Digital tools may test, visualise and verify after the mathematical structure is explicit.

Do not overteach

  • Do not introduce later-year formalism that is unnecessary for this content description.

Worked examples

Read a rate as unlike units

Identify examples of rates in the real world, including constant rates, rate of pay, cost per kilogram, recipes, simple interest and average rates.

Teacher prompt: For read a rate as unlike units, which quantities must scale or compare by the same factor?

Calculate a unit rate

Using a conversion rate to convert distances from miles into kilometres.

Teacher prompt: For calculate a unit rate, which quantities must scale or compare by the same factor?

Convert between compound units

Teacher prompt: For convert between compound units, which quantities must scale or compare by the same factor?

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Dimensions, units or accuracy are mixed — Label every measure and convert before calculating.
  • The rates and unit rates model is copied without interpretation — Connect every label in ‘Identify examples of rates in the real world, including constant rates, rate of pay, cost per kilogram, recipes, simple interest and average rates.’ to its mathematical role.
  • The result is not verified — Use a second representation, boundary case, inverse operation or simulation check.
Support, core and extend

Support: Reduce numerical or representational load while retaining the same decision and explanation.

Core: Connect at least two representations, justify the method and verify independently.

Extend: Test a boundary case, counterexample or model limitation without introducing later-year procedures.
