CODE: AC9M1N01
DESCRIPTOR: Recognise, represent and order numbers to at least 120 using physical and virtual materials, numerals, number lines and charts.
STATUS: AUTHORING-READY
============================================================

IXL MAPPINGS
- Number lines to 100 and 120; hundred/120 charts; spoken numbers to numerals; numerals to number words; ordinal numbers.
- Compare numbers to 100/120 using words, symbols, place-value models and number lines.
- Order numbers to 100/120, with and without number-line support.

KHAN ACADEMY MAPPINGS
- 1st grade maths > Place value > Numbers 0 to 120.
- Number grid; Missing numbers between 0 and 120; Numbers to 120 exercise.
- Supporting comparison work: Compare 2-digit numbers and place-value representations.

PRIOR-YEAR EVIDENCE REUSED
- Foundation evidence for one-to-one counting, recognising numerals, ordering small collections, before/after language and simple number-line direction is reusable.
- Year 1 extension researched: range to 120; crossing decade and hundred boundaries; number-chart structure; comparing and ordering mixed one-, two- and three-digit numerals.

AUTHENTIC EVIDENCE OBSERVED
- IXL practice used short horizontal number-line segments rather than always starting at zero. Students supplied an interior missing number and identified the number immediately before/after a target.
- Interacted across low and high ranges, including single digits, the 50s/60s and numbers above 100. This confirms transfer rather than memorising one fixed chart.
- IXL ordering presented three movable numeral cards and alternated smallest-to-largest language. Its worked example first compared number of digits, then compared digits from the highest place value.
- Khan exercise presented an ordered run near 120 with one missing entry. The response was typed rather than selected.
- Khan's step-by-step feedback used three small prompts: identify the count-by rule, ask what comes after the preceding number, then state the missing number. Related instruction links to a number-grid model and missing-number video.

REPRESENTATIONS REQUIRED
- Concrete collections grouped where useful; numeral cards; spoken number names; number words; short and full number lines; 100 and 120 charts; place-value models.
- Include forward and backward sequences, missing positions, before/between/after, comparison and ordering.

QUESTION TYPES AND DIFFICULTY PROGRESSION
- Accessible: name/read a numeral; count on by one; fill one gap; locate a number; identify before/after.
- Core: cross a decade; use a chart row/column; compare two numbers in words or symbols; translate words and numerals.
- Higher: order three or four mixed-length numerals; explain placement using tens/ones or relative location; diagnose a reversed comparison; complete sequences with gaps in different positions.
- Challenge/transfer: reason across 99/100 and 109/110; infer a hidden number from multiple clues; select or correct a representation.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS / DISTRACTORS
- Reversing before and after or left and right.
- Treating 100 as smaller than a two-digit number because it begins with 1.
- Comparing only the ones digit; reversing < and >; assuming chart movement down changes by 1 rather than 10.
- Dropping or inserting a zero in spoken/written numbers; stopping at 99; duplicating or skipping boundary numbers.

TEACHING SEQUENCE
1. Reconnect quantities, spoken names and numerals from Foundation.
2. Build the decade structure with bundles/ten frames and a 100/120 chart.
3. Count forward/backward and locate before, after and between.
4. Read and complete number lines and chart paths, including decade/hundred crossings.
5. Compare by number of digits, then tens and ones; connect words to symbols.
6. Order multiple numbers and justify the decision with place value or position.

RECOMMENDED SKILLRHUB COVERAGE
- Topic Page must teach all representations and explicitly model decade/hundred crossings, chart movement, comparison and ordering.
- Classroom View should use draggable numeral cards, a projected 120 chart, missing-number reveals and verbal justification prompts.
- Worksheet should progress from recognition and local gaps to chart/line interpretation, comparison and independent ordering.
- Practice bank needs balanced coverage of reading, representing, locating, sequencing, comparing, ordering and error analysis; avoid 48 cosmetic missing-number items.
- Test bank should be fully separate and sample every representation with less scaffolding and at least several justification/transfer items.

BOUNDARIES
- Do not extend to formal place-value algorithms or numbers far beyond 120.
- Roman numerals appear in IXL's broad alignment but are not stated in this AC v9 descriptor; they are optional enrichment, not required core coverage.

SOURCE REFERENCES
- IXL Australia Australian Curriculum v9 Year 1 alignment: AC9M1N01 mapping.
- IXL Year 1: Number lines up to 120; Put numbers up to 120 in order; Learn with an example.
- Khan Academy 1st grade maths: Place value > Numbers 0 to 120; interactive exercise and step-by-step solution.

RESEARCH DECISION
Enough authentic evidence and prior Foundation coverage exist to support commercial-grade original authoring. No further research is required for AC9M1N01.

CODE: AC9M1N02
DESCRIPTOR: Partition one- and two-digit numbers in different ways using physical and virtual materials, including partitioning two-digit numbers into tens and ones.
STATUS: AUTHORING-READY
============================================================

IXL MAPPINGS
- Make, partition and build teen numbers using words, models and addition sentences.
- Place-value models to 20; build multiples of ten and general two-digit numbers.
- Build and partition two-digit numbers with/without models; identify place-value models.
- Ways to make a number using tens and ones, including non-standard partitions.

KHAN ACADEMY MAPPINGS
- 1st grade maths > Place value > Ones and tens.
- Intro to place value; worked examples using 25 and 42.
- Groups of ten objects; Tens and ones; 2-digit place-value challenge.

PRIOR-YEAR EVIDENCE REUSED
- Foundation part-part-whole partitioning of small quantities and teen-number construction can be reused as entry knowledge.
- New Year 1 evidence focused on two-digit place value and flexible/non-canonical partitions, not only the standard tens-and-ones form.

AUTHENTIC EVIDENCE OBSERVED
- IXL first linked a numeral to a base-ten model and a spoken tens/ones description (for example, identify the number shown by several ten rods and unit cubes).
- Practice then reversed the direction: given a numeral and the same model, enter separate values for tens and ones.
- Difficulty increased from teen numbers and standard partitions to larger two-digit numbers.
- A higher IXL skill required interpreting a non-standard partition containing more than 9 ones, finding the total, and expressing an equivalent partition with one more or fewer tens.
- IXL's worked example explicitly taught the equivalence 1 ten = 10 ones, then regrouped one ten to create an alternative form.
- Khan organises the concept as concrete groups of ten -> tens and ones -> a two-digit challenge, supported by short place-value worked-example videos.
- Khan's exercise begins from a visual block collection and requests the total, connecting quantity to numeral before more abstract place-value work.

REPRESENTATIONS REQUIRED
- Loose objects bundled into tens; ten rods and ones cubes; ten frames; tens/ones tables; numerals; number words; expanded addition; arrow/partition diagrams.
- Standard and non-standard forms such as 4 tens 6 ones and 3 tens 16 ones must both occur.

QUESTION TYPES AND DIFFICULTY PROGRESSION
- Accessible: count tens/ones in a model; build a teen number; match numeral to model.
- Core: write tens and ones for any two-digit numeral; compose/decompose using words and expanded addition; handle zero ones or zero tens.
- Higher: generate multiple partitions; regroup 1 ten as 10 ones; solve missing-part statements; connect two different-looking models representing the same number.
- Challenge/transfer: diagnose a reversed digit/place-value claim; determine totals from non-standard partitions; justify equivalence without relying on appearance.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS / DISTRACTORS
- Counting rods as single ones; writing the count of blocks rather than their total value.
- Reversing tens and ones; reading 4 tens 5 ones as 54; treating a zero ones digit as absent or changing the number.
- Believing a number has only one valid partition; failing to exchange 1 ten for 10 ones.
- Adding the counts of tens and ones rather than their values; accepting non-equivalent regrouping.

TEACHING SEQUENCE
1. Bundle ten individual objects and establish 1 ten = 10 ones.
2. Compose teen numbers, then general two-digit numbers from rods/cubes.
3. Move between model, words, numeral and expanded addition.
4. Reverse the process by partitioning a given numeral.
5. Include zero cases and multiples of ten.
6. Exchange one ten for ten ones and create/compare multiple valid partitions.
7. Explain why different partitions preserve the same total.

RECOMMENDED SKILLRHUB COVERAGE
- Topic Page must explicitly teach both standard and flexible partitioning; flexible partitioning is the main Year 1 depth often omitted.
- Classroom View should include virtual bundling/exchange demonstrations and prompts asking for another way.
- Worksheet should move concrete -> pictorial -> symbolic and include matching, construction, decomposition and equivalence.
- Practice bank must vary direction and representation, include zero cases, missing values and non-standard partitions.
- Test bank should require independent conversion and justification, with no reused Practice prompts.

BOUNDARIES
- Keep numbers within the intended one- and two-digit range. Do not introduce formal column algorithms.
- Regrouping here is conceptual renaming of a quantity, not a written addition/subtraction algorithm.

SOURCE REFERENCES
- IXL Australia Australian Curriculum v9 Year 1 alignment: AC9M1N02 mapping.
- IXL Year 1: Build and partition two-digit numbers with models; Ways to make a number—tens and ones; Learn with an example.
- Khan Academy 1st grade maths: Place value > Ones and tens; related exercises and worked-example sequence.

RESEARCH DECISION
The interactive evidence establishes the complete progression from concrete standard partitions to flexible regrouping. AC9M1N02 is authoring-ready.

CODE: AC9M1N03
DESCRIPTOR: Quantify sets of objects, to at least 120, by partitioning collections into equal groups using number knowledge and skip counting.
STATUS: AUTHORING-READY
============================================================

RESEARCH SCOPE
- Reused existing Foundation and Year 1 number/place-value evidence for counting collections and cardinality.
- New live IXL Australia evidence focused on equal groups, skip counting by 2s/5s/10s, totals to at least 120, and the distinction between number of groups and group size.
- Khan Academy has no direct Grade 1 exercise covering this full Australian Curriculum descriptor; no weak mapping is claimed.

IXL MAPPINGS
- C.1–C.8 Learn/skip-count by twos, fives and tens.
- C.9 Count by twos, fives and tens.
- C.10 Skip-counting patterns with tables.
- C.11 Sequences: count up and down by 1, 2, 5 and 10.
- C.12 Count equal groups.
- C.13–C.17 repeated-addition/array extensions are useful selectively, but multiplication notation is not required by the descriptor.

LIVE QUESTION EVIDENCE
- Prompt form: “When counting by fives, what comes after 10?” then mixed-step variants such as counting by twos after a non-zero start.
- Equal-group form: two or more pictured groups followed by separate blanks for “number of groups” and “objects in each group”.
- Table form: constant group size in one column and cumulative total in the other; learner completes a later row.
- Worked examples explicitly count groups first, establish equal size, then skip count the total.
- Visual progression: concrete grouped objects → labelled group/size statements → skip-count sequence/table → total with a leftover.

TEACHING PROGRESSION
1. Verify that every full group is equal.
2. Identify group size and number of full groups separately.
3. Skip count once for each full group.
4. Count any leftover objects by ones.
5. State and check the complete total; reverse-check by recounting or regrouping.
6. Extend totals beyond 100 to meet “at least 120”.

MISCONCEPTIONS / DISTRACTORS
- Reporting the number of groups instead of total objects.
- Reporting group size instead of total objects.
- Skip-counting unequal groups as though they were equal.
- Losing, double-counting or absorbing leftovers incorrectly.
- Using the wrong skip (2, 5 or 10).
- Stopping at 100 despite objects remaining.
- Treating a visually larger gap between groups as a larger quantity.

AUTHORING COVERAGE REQUIREMENTS
- Include groups of 2, 5 and 10; totals below, at and above 100 through at least 120.
- Mix arrays, circles, bags, ten-frames, bundles, tables, sequences and short contexts.
- Include exact groups and groups plus leftovers.
- Ask both “How many groups/how many in each?” and “How many altogether?” without conflating them.
- Practice bank must move concrete → pictorial → symbolic → reasoning; test bank must be separate and less scaffolded.

RESEARCH DECISION
- Sufficient independent depth exists for Topic Page, Classroom View, worksheet/homework, 48 Practice and 16 separate Test questions.
- Do not collect more IXL examples unless authoring reveals a specific representation gap.

SOURCE REFERENCES
- IXL Australia Year 1 Maths: C.1–C.17, with live review of C.9, C.10 and C.12 on 2026-09-14.
- Australian Curriculum v9 descriptor retained from the current SkillrHub curriculum page.
- Khan Academy Grade 1 course reviewed; no direct full-descriptor mapping.

CODE: AC9M1N04
DESCRIPTOR: Add and subtract numbers within 20, using physical and virtual materials, part-part-whole knowledge to 10 and a variety of calculation strategies.
STATUS: AUTHORING-READY
============================================================

RESEARCH SCOPE
- Reused Foundation joining/separating, number bond and ten-frame evidence.
- New live IXL evidence covered models, number lines, counting on/back, doubles, related facts, make-ten and missing-part equations.
- Live Khan Academy Grade 1 evidence confirmed concise symbolic practice supported by strategy videos and related visual resources.

IXL MAPPINGS
- E.1–E.7 Understand addition with cubes, pictures, equations, number lines and words.
- F.1–F.4 addition strategies to 10 and making ten.
- I.1–I.8 Understand subtraction and zero/all cases.
- J.1–J.4 subtraction strategies and inverse relationships.
- O.1–O.12 addition strategies to 20: related facts, number lines, counting on, doubles, ten-frames and make-ten.
- P.1–P.17 addition within 20.
- R.1–R.9 subtraction strategies to 20.
- S.1–S.16 subtraction within 20.
- U.1–U.5 mixed addition/subtraction and fact families.

KHAN ACADEMY MAPPINGS
- Grade 1 Unit 2: Relate addition and subtraction.
- Add within 20; Adding 7 + 6; Adding 8 + 7.
- Add 3 numbers.
- Subtract within 20.
- Equal sign and missing-number exercises.
- Strategy support includes ten-frames, place-value blocks and worked examples.

LIVE QUESTION EVIDENCE
- IXL make-ten item asks the learner to choose the correct split/jumps on a number line, then calculate the total.
- Worked example for 9 + 9 decomposes the second 9 into 1 and 8: reach 10 first, then add the remainder.
- Khan exercise uses a clean one-step equation such as 4 + 5 = __, with related videos available rather than embedded answer cues.
- Across the mapped sets, representations progress from cubes/pictures to number lines, equations, missing parts and strategy choice.

TEACHING PROGRESSION
1. Model joining, separating and missing parts with objects.
2. Connect part-part-whole diagrams to equations.
3. Build fluency with bonds to 10.
4. Count on/back efficiently rather than recounting all.
5. Use doubles/near doubles and related facts.
6. Make ten, carry the leftover part, and explain the strategy.
7. Check subtraction using addition and vice versa.

MISCONCEPTIONS / DISTRACTORS
- Recounting all instead of counting on from the larger addend.
- Mixing the operation sign or reversing subtraction order.
- Making ten but forgetting the leftover.
- Counting number-line points instead of jumps.
- Assuming the equals sign means “the answer comes next”.
- Confusing a missing part with the total.
- Applying a strategy that changes the value of an addend.

AUTHORING COVERAGE REQUIREMENTS
- Stay within 20 and deliberately include boundary cases 0, 10 and 20.
- Balance addition and subtraction; include part-part-whole, ten-frame, number-line and equation formats.
- Cover count on/back, make ten, doubles/near doubles, related facts and inverse checking.
- Include unknowns in different positions, not only at the end.
- Practice and test questions must assess strategy understanding as well as final answers.

RESEARCH DECISION
- Authoring-ready; evidence is sufficient for all required deliverables and question-bank diversity.

SOURCE REFERENCES
- IXL Australia Year 1 Maths: E, F, I, J, O, P, R, S and U strands; live review of O.11 on 2026-09-14.
- Khan Academy 1st Grade Maths Unit 2, live review of Add within 20 on 2026-09-14.
- Australian Curriculum v9 descriptor retained from current SkillrHub curriculum mapping.

CODE: AC9M1N05
DESCRIPTOR: Use mathematical modelling to solve practical problems involving additive situations including simple money transactions; represent the situations with diagrams, physical and virtual materials, and use calculation strategies to solve the problem.
STATUS: AUTHORING-READY
============================================================

RESEARCH SCOPE
- Reused AC9M1N04 calculation-strategy evidence; this code adds modelling choices, practical contexts, money and interpretation.
- Live IXL evidence covered multiple additive story structures and Australian coin counting/comparison.
- Khan evidence contributed a graded sequence of addition/subtraction word problems, including more/fewer language.

IXL MAPPINGS
- H, L, N, Q, T and V word-problem families: joining, separating, result unknown, change unknown, start unknown and one-addend unknown.
- V includes matching situations to addition/subtraction sentences.
- BB.1 Coin values.
- BB.2 Count money with 5c and 10c coins.
- BB.3 Count money with all Australian coins.
- BB.4 Compare money amounts.
- Calculation strategies draw from AC9M1N04 mappings rather than being researched twice.

KHAN ACADEMY MAPPINGS
- Grade 1 Unit 2: Addition and subtraction word problems 1 and 2.
- Add and subtract within 20 word problems.
- Word problems with “more” and “fewer”.
- Missing-number practice supports unknown-part modelling.
- Khan has no Australia-specific coin work; IXL Australia is the direct money source.

LIVE QUESTION EVIDENCE
- IXL money item presents pictured Australian coins and asks “How much money is there?” with a dollar-entry field.
- Worked example totals by denomination: count dollar coins first, then count on by the value of each smaller coin.
- Word-problem sets vary what is unknown rather than relying on keyword spotting.
- Khan separates direct calculation from story interpretation and increases complexity through more/fewer and missing quantities.

TEACHING / MODELLING CYCLE
1. Read and retell the situation; identify what is known and unknown.
2. Choose a representation: objects, drawing, bar/part-whole model, number line or coins.
3. Decide whether quantities join, separate or compare.
4. Select and carry out a calculation strategy.
5. Label the answer with the correct unit, especially cents or dollars.
6. Check that the answer is reasonable in the original context.

MISCONCEPTIONS / DISTRACTORS
- Choosing an operation from one keyword without understanding the situation.
- Adding when the unknown is a missing part/change, or subtracting in the wrong order.
- Ignoring the question after calculating a visible total.
- Counting coins as objects rather than by value.
- Mixing cents and dollars or omitting the money unit.
- Treating coin size as value.
- Producing an equation that does not match the diagram/story.

AUTHORING COVERAGE REQUIREMENTS
- Balance join, separate, compare, missing-part, start-unknown and change-unknown contexts within 20.
- Include explicit modelling-choice questions, not calculations only.
- Use Australian coins and age-appropriate simple transactions; avoid unsupported price complexity.
- Include counting, comparing, paying exact amounts and simple change where calculations remain accessible.
- Require diagrams/materials/number lines across the practice progression.
- Test questions must use new contexts and require independent model/operation selection.

RESEARCH DECISION
- Authoring-ready; IXL covers the Australian money component that Khan lacks, while both support additive modelling depth.

CODE: AC9M1N06
DESCRIPTOR: Use mathematical modelling to solve practical problems involving equal sharing and grouping; represent the situations with diagrams, physical and virtual materials, and use calculation strategies to solve the problem.
STATUS: AUTHORING-READY
============================================================

RESEARCH SCOPE
- Reused equal-group recognition from AC9M1N03; this code adds practical modelling and distinguishes sharing from grouping.
- Live IXL evidence directly examined counting equal groups and dividing a total into a stated number of equal groups.
- Khan Grade 1 course has no direct equal-sharing/grouping exercise; no later-grade division content is imported.

IXL MAPPINGS
- C.12 Count equal groups.
- C.18 Divide by counting equal groups.
- C.13–C.17 repeated addition/arrays may support checking totals but should not replace practical sharing/grouping models.

LIVE QUESTION EVIDENCE
- IXL prompt shows 6 dots divided into 3 equal groups and asks for the number in each group.
- Worked example shows 9 dots in 3 equal groups, labels groups, then counts within one group to obtain 3.
- The paired C.12 structure asks separately for number of groups and number in each group.
- Representations are concrete/pictorial first and use short, explicit language suitable for Year 1.

TEACHING PROGRESSION
1. Act out fair sharing one item at a time among a known number of recipients.
2. Check that every recipient has the same amount.
3. Contrast with grouping: make groups of a stated size and count how many groups.
4. Record both number of groups and number in each group.
5. Use skip counting or repeated addition to verify the total.
6. Describe leftovers honestly; do not force an unequal object into a group.
7. Apply the model to familiar contexts such as plates, bags, teams or rows.

MISCONCEPTIONS / DISTRACTORS
- Confusing “how many groups?” with “how many in each group?”
- Sharing unevenly or stopping before all distributable items are used.
- Reporting the original total instead of the requested quotient-like result.
- Counting boundaries/spaces rather than objects.
- Treating equal sharing and equal grouping as the same question structure.
- Ignoring or hiding leftovers.
- Introducing formal division notation before the model is understood.

AUTHORING COVERAGE REQUIREMENTS
- Include both sharing (known number of groups) and grouping (known group size).
- Mix manipulatives, diagrams, arrays/rows, short stories and explanation questions.
- Ask learners to choose or critique a model, not only count it.
- Include fair/unfair examples and simple leftovers.
- Keep calculations concrete and Year 1 appropriate; formal division algorithms are out of scope.

RESEARCH DECISION
- Authoring-ready for Topic Page, Classroom View, worksheet/homework, 48 Practice and 16 separate Test questions.

CODE: AC9M1A01
DESCRIPTOR: Recognise, continue and create pattern sequences, with numbers, symbols, shapes and objects, formed by skip counting, initially by twos, fives and tens.
STATUS: AUTHORING-READY
============================================================

RESEARCH SCOPE
- Reused skip-counting number knowledge from AC9M1N03 without duplicating collection-quantification research.
- New emphasis is the algebraic structure of a repeating numerical rule across numbers, symbols, shapes and objects.
- Live IXL evidence covered sequence continuation, rule identification and table-based growing totals.
- Khan Grade 1 course lacks a direct pattern-sequence unit; no weak mapping is claimed.

IXL MAPPINGS
- C.1–C.8 learn and practise skip counting by 2s, 5s and 10s.
- C.9 Count by twos, fives and tens.
- C.10 Skip-counting patterns with tables.
- C.11 Sequences: count up and down by 1, 2, 5 and 10.
- Pattern representations elsewhere in IXL may support shapes/objects, but authoring must retain the descriptor’s skip-counting basis rather than simple AB repetition.

LIVE QUESTION EVIDENCE
- IXL table item states “Each pile has 2 T-shirts” and lists 1→2, 2→4, 3→6 … before asking for 6 piles.
- The worked example verbalises a constant rule (“count by tens”) and completes the table row by row.
- Mixed skip-counting prompts ask for the next number and vary 2, 5 and 10.
- The evidence supports moving from object groups to tables to abstract sequences.

TEACHING PROGRESSION
1. Build equal groups of 2, 5 or 10 with objects.
2. Say the running totals and connect each total to one additional group.
3. Represent the same rule with numerals, symbols or shapes.
4. Identify the constant change between adjacent terms.
5. Continue a sequence and fill internal missing terms.
6. Create a new sequence from a stated start and rule.
7. Explain why every step must follow the same rule; include backward continuation where suitable.

MISCONCEPTIONS / DISTRACTORS
- Counting by ones instead of the stated skip.
- Changing the rule partway through.
- Repeating an AB visual pattern rather than creating a skip-counting sequence.
- Looking only at the last digit and ignoring the constant difference.
- Starting from 0 when a different starting value is given.
- Losing alignment between group number and cumulative total in a table.
- Treating “next” as arbitrary rather than rule-governed.

AUTHORING COVERAGE REQUIREMENTS
- Balance skips of 2, 5 and 10.
- Include number, symbol, shape and object representations.
- Cover recognise rule, continue, fill gaps, find an error and create a sequence.
- Use varied starting points where age appropriate, not only 0.
- Include tables and grouped-object contexts that make the constant change visible.
- Keep AC9M1A01 distinct from AC9M1N03: assess sequence/rule structure, not merely total collection size.

RESEARCH DECISION
- Authoring-ready; sufficient depth exists for every required deliverable without further sampling.

PURPOSE AND SOURCE RULE
- This supplement strengthens the five-code batch with free, publicly accessible pedagogy from Australian government-supported and established nonprofit/university resources.
- Sources inform task architecture, representations, diagnostic prompts and misconceptions only. No source question wording should be copied.
- The Australian Curriculum v9 descriptor remains the authority for scope. Overseas resources are used only where the mathematics matches that descriptor and Year 1 developmental level.

AC9M1N03 — QUANTIFYING, COUNTING AND GROUPING COLLECTIONS
Additional evidence
- The Australian Mathematics Hub's Year 1 number-sequence planning guidance supports counting collections using structured skip-counting and recording the count, then noticing how the counted numbers form a pattern.
New authoring implications
- Include tasks in which students choose an efficient organisation before counting: loose objects, rows, pairs, fives or tens.
- Require the student to connect the physical grouping, the spoken count sequence and the recorded numeral.
- Add "Which arrangement is easiest to count, and why?" and error-analysis items, not only final-total items.
- Preserve Year 1 scope: flexible counting and grouping, without requiring formal multiplication notation.

AC9M1N04 — ADDITIVE STRATEGIES
Additional evidence
- Illustrative Mathematics Grade 1 Operations and Algebraic Thinking identifies a broad, coherent strategy set within 20: counting on, making ten, decomposing through ten, using addition/subtraction relationships and replacing a calculation with an equivalent easier one.
- The New Zealand curriculum's free "Addition and subtraction pick n mix" resource reinforces that students can use a range of strategies and that more than one strategy may be equally efficient.
New authoring implications
- Mix strategy-selection, representation and explanation questions with computation.
- Accept multiple mathematically valid methods where the prompt asks how to solve; assess whether the explanation matches the numbers.
- Include counter models, ten frames, number lines, part-part-whole diagrams and equations, then ask students to translate between them.
- Distractors should reflect real strategy errors: failing to compensate after making ten, counting the start twice, reversing a subtraction, or changing the total while regrouping.

AC9M1N05 — ADDITION AND SUBTRACTION IN CONTEXT
Additional evidence
- Illustrative Mathematics Grade 1 covers add-to, take-from, put-together, take-apart and compare situations, with the unknown in different positions and representations using objects, drawings and equations.
- Its free compare lesson demonstrates that the same difference-unknown story can be represented by both a subtraction equation and a missing-addend equation.
New authoring implications
- Do not let operation keywords determine every answer. Vary story structure and the location of the unknown.
- Include "Which equation(s) match this story?" items where more than one equation may be valid.
- Require students to explain what each number and the unknown represent in context.
- Include story-to-model, model-to-equation, equation-to-story and incorrect-model diagnosis.
- Benchmark conceptual interpretation as well as calculation accuracy.

AC9M1N06 — EQUAL SHARING AND GROUPING
Additional evidence
- Victorian Curriculum ARC's free Level 1 task has students share collections with counters and diagrams, name the equal groups and explain whether a sharing is equal or unequal.
- NRICH's free Share Bears task for ages 5–7 asks learners to test successive collection sizes, share them equally between two people, record results on a number line or number square, and explain the emerging pattern and leftovers.
New authoring implications
- Assess both division meanings informally: a known number of groups with unknown group size, and a known group size with unknown number of groups.
- Include fair/not-fair classification, construct-a-fair-share, identify leftovers, and justify equality using one-to-one matching.
- Add low-floor investigation items: test several totals, record which work, predict the next workable total and explain the pattern.
- Keep formal odd/even terminology optional enrichment; mastery is equal sharing/grouping with concrete or pictorial evidence.

AC9M1A01 — GROWING AND REPEATING PATTERNS
Additional evidence
- The Australian Mathematics Hub supports connecting skip-counting, recorded numbers and visible number-chart patterns.
- NRICH Share Bears shows a productive bridge from repeated equal sharing to marking successful totals and noticing a regular number pattern.
New authoring implications
- Go beyond "continue the pattern": ask students to state the repeat or growth rule, locate and repair an error, compare two patterns, and create a pattern satisfying a condition.
- Link concrete groups, rhythmic/skip counts, number lines and chart markings, while keeping the pattern itself—not computation speed—the assessment focus.
- Include non-example discrimination: a sequence can look regular locally but fail to follow the stated rule.
- Use explanation prompts such as "How do you know what comes next?" and "What stays the same; what changes?"

CROSS-CODE QUALITY IMPROVEMENTS
- Add explain/justify and error-analysis items across the bank, not only answer-selection.
- Deliberately vary representations and unknown positions so success cannot come from one surface template.
- Use low-floor/high-ceiling investigations in teaching and practice, while keeping scored items objectively markable.
- Treat multiple valid strategies as evidence of flexibility; mark the mathematical result and reasoning rather than enforcing one method unless the prompt explicitly targets that method.
- Maintain strict separation between practice mastery and benchmarking: these research additions do not change the product rule that only the first test attempt is recorded for grades.
