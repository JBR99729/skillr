Year 6 Maths Curriculum Australia: What Students Learn and How to Practise

Year 6 Maths consolidates primary-school number and measurement while preparing students for more formal secondary mathematics. Fractions, decimals, percentages, integer ideas, algebraic patterns, geometry, probability and data require increasingly flexible reasoning.

Connect representations

Students should move comfortably between fractions, decimals and percentages where appropriate, and between tables, diagrams, number lines and equations. Connections reduce the need to memorise isolated rules because students can reason from one representation to another.

Use the exact curriculum unit

The Year 6 Maths curriculum units help identify the specific skill. Each SkillrHub unit links its Topic Guide, Teacher Slides, Worksheet, Practice and Test, making it easier to target revision before secondary-school transition.

Demand explanation and checking

Ask students to estimate, justify operation choice and explain whether an answer is reasonable. When two methods are possible, compare efficiency. In probability and data, focus on interpretation as well as calculation.

Prepare for unfamiliar problems

Once routine examples are secure, vary the wording or representation. Students should recognise the underlying mathematics even when a problem does not resemble the worked example exactly.

Free-first learning

Try the free resources first

Before committing to another monthly or yearly learning subscription, use the free curriculum-linked resources available for the student’s current topic. Start with the Topic Guide, use Practice, review mistakes and then take the separate Test.

A larger paid question bank or learning program can be useful, but paying for more volume does not guarantee that a student will use it consistently. If the learner is already working through the free resources and still needs extra tutoring, marking, adaptive practice, reporting or more question volume, compare paid options for that specific need.

Free-first check: establish a consistent Learn → Practice → Test routine before deciding whether an additional paid service offers something the learner will genuinely use.
