Year 1 Maths Curriculum Australia: What Students Learn and How to Practise

Year 1 Maths extends early number sense into more deliberate strategies. Students begin working with larger quantities, partitioning numbers, skip counting, addition and subtraction, equal groups, measurement, time, shape, position, patterns and data.

What changes in Year 1 Maths?

Children still benefit from concrete materials, but they increasingly connect those models to diagrams and symbols. For example, an addition story can be acted out, shown with counters, represented as a part-part-whole model and finally written as a number sentence. Good practice keeps those representations connected.

Use the curriculum unit as your map

The Year 1 Maths curriculum units separate broad maths into precise learning goals. This avoids the common problem of finding a worksheet labelled “Year 1” that does not match what the child is currently learning. Choose the unit first, then use its topic guide, teacher slide, worksheet, Practice and Test.

Build fluency without losing understanding

Short recall practice is useful, but students should also explain strategies and solve unfamiliar examples. Ask questions such as “How did you know?”, “Can you show it another way?” and “What would change if the number were one more?” This develops flexible number thinking rather than dependence on one memorised procedure.

When to use worksheets

Printable sheets are useful for handwriting, drawing models and showing working. Keep the sheet tied to the current topic. A targeted addition worksheet after an addition lesson provides useful evidence; a random mixed sheet may make it difficult to tell whether an error comes from the new concept or an unrelated skill.

Free-first learning

Try the free resources first

Before committing to another monthly or yearly learning subscription, use the free curriculum-linked resources available for the student’s current topic. Start with the Topic Guide, use Practice, review mistakes and then take the separate Test.

A larger paid question bank or learning program can be useful, but paying for more volume does not guarantee that a student will use it consistently. If the learner is already working through the free resources and still needs extra tutoring, marking, adaptive practice, reporting or more question volume, compare paid options for that specific need.

Free-first check: establish a consistent Learn → Practice → Test routine before deciding whether an additional paid service offers something the learner will genuinely use.
