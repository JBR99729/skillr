Year 5 Maths Curriculum Australia: Topics, Worksheets and Practice

Year 5 Mathematics builds the bridge between primary number skills and the more formal reasoning students meet in later years. Learners work with larger whole numbers, factors and multiples, fractions and decimals, measurement, geometry, data and probability while becoming more independent in choosing and checking methods.

Match practice to the Australian Curriculum outcome

Year 5 Mathematics curriculum codes begin with AC9M5. If a teacher or parent already knows the code, go straight to the matching Year 5 Maths curriculum unit. Working from the exact outcome is more useful than assigning a generic worksheet because it keeps the explanation, examples and questions focused on the skill that actually needs improvement.

SkillrHub separates curriculum units so a learner can review one idea, practise it and then test it without mixing unrelated content. This is especially useful when a student appears to be struggling with a broad topic such as fractions but the real difficulty is narrower, such as comparing fractions, connecting fractions and decimals, or interpreting a word problem.

Number, operations, factors and multiples

Year 5 students need flexible strategies rather than one fixed written method for every calculation. Encourage them to estimate first, select an efficient strategy and then check the result. Factors and multiples should be connected to multiplication and division facts so they are understood as number relationships rather than isolated vocabulary.

When an answer is wrong, ask whether the problem came from place value, a fact that was not secure, the chosen operation or the calculation itself. Identifying the source of the error tells you what to practise next.

Fractions, decimals and percentages

Fractions and decimals become easier when students see them as different ways of representing quantity. Use number lines, area models and practical contexts before relying only on symbolic procedures. Students should compare values, explain equivalence and connect familiar fractions to decimal and percentage forms where appropriate.

Word problems are important here because they reveal whether the learner understands the quantity being represented. Ask students to estimate the size of the answer before calculating and to explain why the final value is reasonable.

Measurement and geometry

Measurement tasks should combine calculation with unit sense. Students need to decide which measurement is relevant, select suitable units and interpret diagrams carefully. Geometry work should include describing and reasoning about shapes and spatial relationships, not only naming them.

A practical checking routine is to label every diagram, write the unit with each measurement and compare the final answer with an estimate. This makes many avoidable errors visible before the work is submitted.

Statistics and probability

Data work should ask students to read, compare and explain information from tables and graphs. Probability tasks should use clear language about likelihood and outcomes. Encourage students to support conclusions with evidence from the data or the sample space rather than simply stating what looks likely.

A useful Year 5 Maths learning sequence

  • Find the skill: use the curriculum unit or code to identify the learning goal.
  • Review the idea: read the topic explanation and examples.
  • Practise one concept: use a focused worksheet or online Practice set.
  • Explain mistakes: identify why an incorrect answer occurred.
  • Return later: retest the same skill after a short gap to check retention.

This sequence is more effective than completing a large mixed worksheet when the student has not yet secured the underlying concept.

Free-first learning

Try the free resources first

Before committing to another monthly or yearly learning subscription, use the free curriculum-linked resources available for the student’s current topic. Start with the Topic Guide, use Practice, review mistakes and then take the separate Test.

A larger paid question bank or learning program can be useful, but paying for more volume does not guarantee that a student will use it consistently. If the learner is already working through the free resources and still needs extra tutoring, marking, adaptive practice, reporting or more question volume, compare paid options for that specific need.

Free-first check: establish a consistent Learn → Practice → Test routine before deciding whether an additional paid service offers something the learner will genuinely use.
