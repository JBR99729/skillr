Year 2 Science Curriculum Australia: What Students Learn and How to Practise

Year 2 Science builds on observation by asking students to identify relationships and patterns, compare evidence and describe changes using increasingly precise language.

What students learn

Topics can include living things and environments, materials and changes, Earth and space observations, forces in familiar situations and investigation skills. Rather than relying on a generic science workbook, use the Year 2 Science curriculum units to find the exact concept.

Connect facts with evidence

Encourage students to answer with both a claim and a reason. “This material is better because…” or “I noticed that…” starts the habit of evidence-based explanation. Simple tables, labelled drawings and comparisons help students record what they observe.

Use practice after the concept is visible

Introduce the idea with an example or demonstration, then use a worksheet to classify, label, predict or explain. Online Practice can present the same concept in new examples, while the Test provides a later checkpoint. This sequence reduces the chance of students memorising a single worksheet pattern without understanding the science.

Free-first learning

Try the free resources first

Before committing to another monthly or yearly learning subscription, use the free curriculum-linked resources available for the student’s current topic. Start with the Topic Guide, use Practice, review mistakes and then take the separate Test.

A larger paid question bank or learning program can be useful, but paying for more volume does not guarantee that a student will use it consistently. If the learner is already working through the free resources and still needs extra tutoring, marking, adaptive practice, reporting or more question volume, compare paid options for that specific need.

Free-first check: establish a consistent Learn → Practice → Test routine before deciding whether an additional paid service offers something the learner will genuinely use.
