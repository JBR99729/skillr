Year 1 English Curriculum Australia: Reading, Writing and Language

Year 1 English strengthens the move toward independent literacy. Students continue developing phonics and word knowledge while building reading fluency, vocabulary, comprehension, speaking and increasingly controlled sentence writing.

Reading and word knowledge

Children need regular opportunities to decode unfamiliar words, reread familiar text smoothly and discuss meaning. Phonics practice is most useful when it connects to actual reading and spelling rather than becoming an isolated worksheet routine.

Comprehension and discussion

Ask students to retell, identify key information, explain character actions and make simple inferences. Talking about a text before writing about it helps learners organise their ideas and use new vocabulary.

Writing in Year 1

Students work toward clearer sentences and short texts for different purposes. Encourage them to plan orally, write, reread and make one or two useful edits. The aim is not perfect adult writing; it is increasing control over meaning, sentence structure, spelling patterns and punctuation.

Free-first learning

Try the free resources first

Before committing to another monthly or yearly learning subscription, use the free curriculum-linked resources available for the student’s current topic. Start with the Topic Guide, use Practice, review mistakes and then take the separate Test.

A larger paid question bank or learning program can be useful, but paying for more volume does not guarantee that a student will use it consistently. If the learner is already working through the free resources and still needs extra tutoring, marking, adaptive practice, reporting or more question volume, compare paid options for that specific need.

Free-first check: establish a consistent Learn → Practice → Test routine before deciding whether an additional paid service offers something the learner will genuinely use.
