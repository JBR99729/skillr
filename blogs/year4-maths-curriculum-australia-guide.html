Year 4 Maths Curriculum Australia: What Students Learn and How to Practise

Year 4 Maths expects students to combine growing fluency with explanation. Whole-number operations, multiplication and division, fractions and decimals, measurement, time, angles, symmetry, probability and data are increasingly connected through problem solving.

What changes in Year 4?

Students should move beyond one preferred method and compare strategies. They may use place value to calculate, relate multiplication to division, connect fractions to number lines and interpret measurement or data in context. A correct answer matters, but the representation and reasoning behind it matter too.

Use the exact curriculum unit

The Year 4 Maths curriculum units identify the specific learning goal. SkillrHub links Topic Guide → Teacher Slides → Worksheet → Practice → Test so instruction and assessment stay aligned. This is more useful than assigning a broad worksheet simply because it carries a Year 4 label.

Mix fluency with unfamiliar applications

After students can complete standard examples, vary the context, ask for missing values, compare methods or require an estimate first. For fractions and measurement, diagrams and number lines can expose misconceptions that may stay hidden in symbolic calculations.

Review errors before adding more questions

A persistent mistake often points to one underlying concept: place value, operation choice, fraction meaning or interpretation of a diagram. Use the topic guide to reteach that idea, then return to targeted practice. This is more efficient than sending the learner through another long mixed sheet.

Free-first learning

Try the free resources first

Before committing to another monthly or yearly learning subscription, use the free curriculum-linked resources available for the student’s current topic. Start with the Topic Guide, use Practice, review mistakes and then take the separate Test.

A larger paid question bank or learning program can be useful, but paying for more volume does not guarantee that a student will use it consistently. If the learner is already working through the free resources and still needs extra tutoring, marking, adaptive practice, reporting or more question volume, compare paid options for that specific need.

Free-first check: establish a consistent Learn → Practice → Test routine before deciding whether an additional paid service offers something the learner will genuinely use.
