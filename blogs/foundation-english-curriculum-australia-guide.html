Foundation English Curriculum Australia: Reading, Writing and Language

Foundation English builds the bridge between spoken language and literacy. Children learn to listen, speak, hear sounds in words, connect letters and sounds, make meaning from texts and begin composing their own messages.

What does Foundation English focus on?

Important areas include oral language, phonological awareness, early phonics, print concepts, shared reading, vocabulary, comprehension and beginning writing. These skills work together. A child who can discuss a story, notice sounds and connect them to letters is building the foundations for independent reading and spelling.

Use the exact curriculum unit

The Foundation English curriculum units make it easier to choose a resource that matches the learning goal. Rather than downloading a broad literacy sheet, identify whether the child needs sound work, word reading, comprehension, sentence work or another specific skill.

Read, talk, then write

Conversation is an important literacy tool. Before asking for written answers, discuss the text, retell events, identify important words and ask why something happened. When children write, encourage them to say the sentence first, listen for words and sounds, then record what they can. Correctness matters, but so does willingness to communicate meaning.

Keep early literacy practice short and frequent

Five to fifteen focused minutes can be more useful than one long worksheet session. Revisit high-value sounds and words, reread familiar text for fluency, and use quick comprehension questions. If a learner struggles, move back to a smaller step rather than pushing through a long page.
