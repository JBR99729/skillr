Year 8 Maths Curriculum Australia: Topics, Worksheets and Practice

Year 8 Mathematics moves students from routine arithmetic into more formal reasoning. Learners work with irrational and rational numbers, exponent laws, linear expressions and equations, measurement, geometry, statistics and probability. The important shift is not simply doing longer calculations: students need to choose methods, explain relationships and apply familiar ideas when a question is presented in a new way.

Start from the exact curriculum skill

Australian Curriculum v9 Mathematics codes for Year 8 begin with AC9M8. When a teacher, parent or student has a specific code, the most efficient approach is to open the matching Year 8 Maths curriculum unit rather than assign a broad worksheet. Each SkillrHub unit links the curriculum outcome to a topic guide and then to focused student resources such as a worksheet, Practice and Test.

This matters because two Year 8 questions can look similar while testing different ideas. A student who makes an algebra error may need work on expanding and simplifying expressions, while another may understand the algebra but struggle to interpret a linear graph. Matching the practice to the exact outcome keeps revision useful.

Number and rational-number fluency

Year 8 number work includes irrational numbers in applied contexts, exponent laws, terminating and recurring decimals, operations with integers and rational numbers, and mathematical modelling involving percentages and financial situations. Students should practise estimation alongside exact calculation so they can judge whether an answer is sensible before accepting it.

A useful routine is: identify the number type, choose an efficient operation, calculate, then check the size and sign of the answer. For percentage and financial questions, students should also explain what the result means in the original situation rather than stopping at a number.

Algebra, equations and linear relationships

Algebra becomes a major Year 8 focus. Students create, expand, factorise, rearrange and simplify linear expressions, graph linear relationships, solve equations and inequalities, and verify solutions. Strong practice should move between words, tables, graphs and symbolic expressions so students learn that these are different representations of the same relationship.

For equations, substitution is an especially useful checking habit. For graphs, students should be able to describe what the gradient, intercept or change shown on the graph means in context. Short, varied questions are more valuable than repeating one worksheet pattern until the steps are memorised.

Measurement and geometry

Year 8 learners solve problems involving perimeter, area, volume, capacity and geometric relationships. Diagrams should be read carefully before any formula is chosen. Encourage students to label known measurements, identify what is being asked and write units throughout the working. This reduces common errors such as using a length when an area is required or applying a formula to the wrong shape.

Statistics and probability

Statistics and probability tasks should go beyond reading a graph. Students need to interpret data, compare representations, recognise variation and make reasoned statements about chance. Ask students to justify conclusions using the data rather than relying on a visual impression alone.

How to use Year 8 Maths worksheets effectively

Use a worksheet after the student has seen or discussed the underlying idea. Begin with a small set of direct questions, then include examples that change the representation or context. If several errors have the same cause, return to the matching topic guide rather than assigning more unrelated questions.

  • Diagnose: identify the exact concept or curriculum code causing difficulty.
  • Learn: review the explanation and worked examples in the topic guide.
  • Practise: complete a focused worksheet or online Practice set.
  • Check: review mistakes and explain why the correct method works.
  • Retest: use a fresh Test or daily drill after a gap.

Year 8 Maths revision by topic

For short revision sessions, the Year 8 Maths daily drills provide rotating practice. For a complete curriculum pathway, use the Year 8 Maths topic list, where individual outcomes are separated so students can work on one skill at a time.

If the learner is preparing for a test, prioritise weak outcomes first, then mix topics. Mixed practice is most useful after individual skills are secure because it requires the student to recognise which method to use without being told the topic in advance.
