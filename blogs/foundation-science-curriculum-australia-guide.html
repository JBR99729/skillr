Foundation Science Curriculum Australia: What Students Learn and How to Practise

Foundation Science begins with curiosity. Children observe living things, materials, movement and changes in the world around them, then learn to describe what they notice and ask useful questions.

What does Foundation Science focus on?

Early science should feel connected to everyday experience. Students may compare objects and materials, notice features of plants and animals, describe movement or change, and record observations with words, pictures or simple classifications. The goal is not heavy scientific vocabulary; it is learning to look carefully and connect a claim with something observed.

Build scientific thinking through talk

Ask children what they notice, what is the same, what is different and what they think might happen next. Encourage them to point to evidence: “I think this because…” can begin with something very simple. A short drawing with labels can be more valuable than a long page of disconnected questions.

Use simple investigations

Foundation investigation does not need complicated equipment. Sorting materials, observing shadows, comparing how objects move or tracking a plant over several days can build habits of observation. The important step is to connect the experience back to the curriculum concept and give the child language for describing it.
