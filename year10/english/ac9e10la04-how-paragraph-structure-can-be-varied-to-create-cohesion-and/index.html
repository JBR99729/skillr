Paragraph Cohesion and Image Integration

Paragraph length, sequencing, transitions and image placement can control pace, emphasis and the connections readers make across a text.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesWorksheetPracticeTest
What you need to know

AC9E10LA04: understand how paragraph structure can be varied to create cohesion, and paragraphs and images can be integrated for different purposes.

Analysis and writing toolkit

Paragraph purpose

Each paragraph should advance, qualify, illustrate or connect an idea.

Cohesion

Use reference chains, lexical links, transitions and logical sequencing—not only repeated connectives.

Variation

Single-sentence paragraphs can emphasise; longer paragraphs can develop complexity.

Image integration

Image + caption + nearby prose should add meaning, not duplicate each other.

10 important examples

1. Single-sentence emphasis

After a detailed paragraph on warnings, “No one listened.” creates a sharp tonal and rhythmic break.

2. Transition

“Yet the figures tell only part of the story” links back while signalling qualification.

3. Lexical cohesion

“heat”, “warming”, “temperature” maintain a semantic thread without clumsy repetition.

4. Reference chain

“The proposal… this plan… it…” keeps a referent clear across sentences.

5. Long paragraph

A sustained analytical paragraph can build claim, evidence, reasoning and qualification before moving on.

6. Image before prose

A striking image can establish emotional context before the article explains causes.

7. Image after prose

A diagram after explanation can consolidate a process the reader has just encountered.

8. Caption

A precise caption directs attention to the part of the image relevant to the argument.

9. Graphic novel sequencing

Panel size and transition control pace: a wide silent panel can slow reading and intensify reflection.

10. Website layout

Text beside an interactive map lets users connect local evidence with the written explanation.

Common mistakes
  • Using a new paragraph whenever a sentence becomes long rather than when the idea shifts.
  • Adding images as decoration with no interpretive function.
  • Overusing “however”, “therefore” and “furthermore” instead of building genuine semantic cohesion.
  • Assuming short paragraphs are always clearer; fragmentation can weaken development.
10 important questions to solve
  1. What effect can a one-sentence paragraph create?
  2. Rewrite a transition to signal contrast without using “however”.
  3. How does a reference chain create cohesion?
  4. Why might an author place a diagram after an explanation?
  5. What makes a caption analytical rather than decorative?
  6. How can panel size affect pace in a graphic narrative?
  7. Give one risk of over-fragmenting paragraphs.
  8. Explain how lexical cohesion differs from simple repetition.
  9. When might a long paragraph be more effective than several short ones?
  10. Design a text-image sequence for a short article explaining coastal erosion.
Indicative responses
  1. It can isolate and emphasise an idea, create a pause or mark a turning point.
  2. Example: “The apparent improvement disappears when the sample is examined more closely.”
  3. A chain such as “the policy / this proposal / it” lets readers track the same referent.
  4. The prose establishes the concept; the diagram then reinforces relationships visually.
  5. It tells the reader what evidence to notice and how it connects to purpose.
  6. Large panels can slow attention; rapid small panels can accelerate action.
  7. Readers may lose logical development because each paragraph feels disconnected.
  8. Lexical cohesion uses related vocabulary and semantic fields rather than merely repeating one word.
  9. When a complex claim needs sustained evidence, reasoning and qualification.
  10. Possible sequence: problem paragraph → labelled cross-section → wave-action explanation → before/after image with evidence caption → implication.

E1

Australian Curriculum elaboration: examining how authors vary paragraph structures for effect

In practice: Re-paragraph the same passage in two ways—one sustained paragraph and one with a one-sentence emphasis paragraph—then compare pace and focus.

E2

Australian Curriculum elaboration: examining the integration of paragraphs and images on websites for effect

In practice: Pair a short web paragraph with a diagram or photo and test whether image placement clarifies, extends or distracts from the written idea.

E3

Australian Curriculum elaboration: evaluating the effect of the integration of paragraphs and images in graphic novels

In practice: Analyse how a panel sequence combines image, caption and dialogue to control pace and cohesion.
