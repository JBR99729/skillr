Learning goals

Learning intention

We are learning to explain how deliberate language and text choices shape meaning for an audience or reader.

Australian Curriculum target: analyse how text structure, language features, literary devices and intertextual connections shape interpretations of texts

Success criteria

  • Identify a precise language or text choice.
  • Use the surrounding context as evidence.
  • Explain the effect on audience, relationship, purpose or reader response.
  • Transfer the explanation to a new example.
Prerequisite knowledge

Before this lesson

  • Read a short text closely.
  • Locate a phrase, structural choice or visual/text feature.
  • Use a complete sentence to explain an effect.
Key concept

Teach the core concept

In this topic, students move from naming a feature to explaining how the feature works in context.

  • analyse how text structure, language features, literary devices and intertextual connections shape interpretations of texts
  • choice → context → effect reasoning

Teacher model: Read a short example, underline one exact choice, identify who it is for, then explain what the choice makes the audience notice, feel, believe or question.

Worked examples

Worked examples

Choice → context → effect

Teacher model: Do not stop at naming a technique. Show the evidence, place it in context and explain the effect.

  1. Choice: Identify the exact word, structure or text feature connected to analyse how text structure, language features, literary devices and intertextual connections shape interpretations of texts.
  2. Context: Explain who is speaking or writing, for whom, and what the text is doing.
  3. Effect: Explain how the choice shapes meaning, relationship, purpose or reader response.

Questions and answers

Important questions and answers

  1. Q1: What exact language or text choice helps show connect voice to meaning and audience effect? A: Use a precise piece of evidence, explain its context, then link it to audience or reader effect.
  2. Q2: What exact language or text choice helps show connect representation to meaning and audience effect? A: Use a precise piece of evidence, explain its context, then link it to audience or reader effect.
  3. Q: Why is context important? A: The same choice can create a different effect when the audience, speaker, purpose or text type changes.
Common misconceptions

Common misconceptions

  • A feature name is enough to prove an interpretation.: Use the frame: ‘The choice ___ suggests/positions ___ because ___.’
  • Effect means whether the student personally likes the text.: Replace preference with an audience effect supported by the exact wording or structure.

Do not overteach

  • Do not require university-level terminology or theory.
  • Do not treat one interpretation as valid without evidence.
  • Do not replace close reading with plot summary or personal preference.

Independent practice

Independent practice

  1. Choose a short unseen text or extract.
  2. Highlight one deliberate language, structure or visual choice.
  3. Write one claim that links the choice to audience, purpose, relationship or reader response.
  4. Revise the sentence so it includes evidence, context and effect.
Reasoning and interpretation

Reasoning challenge

Explain how one deliberate choice shapes meaning in analyse how text structure, language features, literary devices and intertextual connections shape interpretations of texts.

Reasoning frame: The choice ___ matters because in this context it positions the audience to ___.

Practice and review

Assessment-style practice

  1. Analyse one exact word or phrase and explain how it positions the audience.
  2. Compare two versions of the same idea and evaluate which is more effective.
  3. Write a short analytical paragraph using claim, evidence and effect.

Review hint: A full-mark answer names the choice, quotes or describes evidence accurately, explains context, and links the effect to meaning.

Check understanding

Mastery check

Strong response: The response identifies a precise language or text choice, places it in context and explains its effect on audience, relationship, purpose or reader response.

Teacher and parent/carer guidance

Support, core and extend

Support

Use two short versions, colour-code choice and effect, and provide the sentence frame ‘The choice ___ makes the audience ___ because ___.’

Keep the concept: Keeps the same choice → effect reasoning with reduced text load.

Core

Annotate an extract and write a choice–context–effect explanation.

Keep the concept: Directly assesses the listed English concept.

Extend

Compare two audiences or mediums and evaluate which wording is more effective, citing evidence.

Keep the concept: Deepens evaluation without requiring later-year theory.

At home: Ask students to explain how one wording choice changes the way a reader responds, rather than only naming the technique.
