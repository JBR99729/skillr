Continuous Distributions and Boxplots

Boxplots and related displays compare centre, spread, shape and outliers while hiding or revealing different distribution details.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

Boxplots and related displays compare centre, spread, shape and outliers while hiding or revealing different distribution details.

Learning intention: Boxplots and related displays compare centre, spread, shape and outliers while hiding or revealing different distribution details.

Success criteria

  • Explain compare distributions with boxplots using the approved model.
  • Apply handle outliers responsibly to a new case.
  • Verify a conclusion with a second representation, estimate or test case.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • compare data distributions for continuous numerical variables using appropriate data displays including boxplots; discuss the shapes of these distributions in terms of centre, spread, shape and outliers in the context of the data

Prerequisites

  • Relevant Year 9 statistics representations and vocabulary.

May support informally

  • Digital tools may test, visualise and verify after the mathematical structure is explicit.

Do not overteach

  • Do not introduce later-year formalism that is unnecessary for this content description.

Compare distributions with boxplots

Teacher prompt: For compare shape across several displays, which labelled relationship justifies the conclusion?

Choose boxplot or histogram

Build and use a five-number summary

Teacher prompt: For build and use a five-number summary, what claim is supported, and what limitation remains?

Explore how the identification and appropriate handling of outliers is an important step in machine learning to ensure that they don't unduly influence the model.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • A display or sample is treated as proof of a stronger claim — Link the inference to sampling, variation, context and uncertainty.
  • The continuous distributions and boxplots model is copied without interpretation — Connect every label in ‘Construct and interpreting box plots and using them to compare data sets, understanding that box plots are an efficient and common way of representing and summarising data.’ to its mathematical role.
  • The result is not verified — Use a second representation, boundary case, inverse operation or simulation check.
Support, core and extend

Support: Reduce numerical or representational load while retaining the same decision and explanation.

Core: Connect at least two representations, justify the method and verify independently.

Extend: Test a boundary case, counterexample or model limitation without introducing later-year procedures.
