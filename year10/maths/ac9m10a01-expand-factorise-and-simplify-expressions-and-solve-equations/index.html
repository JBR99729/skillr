Expanding, Factorising, Exponent Laws and Algebraic Equations

Learn the essential Year 10 algebra skills through a revision cheat sheet, clear worked examples, visual scaffolding, common-error checks and a 10-question practice set with explained solutions.

What you need to know

This topic is about algebraic equivalence. Expansion, factorisation and exponent laws change the form of an expression without changing its value. When solving an equation, every valid step must preserve the same solution.

You should be able to expand and factorise expressions, connect factorised and expanded quadratic forms, complete the square, simplify powers with positive and negative integer exponents, and solve equations algebraically.

Year 10 algebra cheat sheet

Expansion

a(b + c) = ab + ac

(x + a)(x + b) = x² + (a + b)x + ab

Factorising

ab + ac = a(b + c)

For x² + bx + c, find p and q so that pq = c and p + q = b.

Completing the square

x² + bx + c = (x + b/2)² + c − b²/4

Half the coefficient of x, square it, then compensate for what you added.

Exponent laws

aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ

aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ (a ≠ 0)

(aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ

a⁰ = 1; a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ (a ≠ 0)

Fast checks: only combine like terms; exponent multiplication/division laws require the same base; expand a factorisation to verify it; substitute a solution into the original equation.

10 important worked examples

1. Expand one bracket

Question: Expand 4(3x − 2).

Step 1: multiply both terms by 4.

4 × 3x − 4 × 2 = 12x − 8.

Answer: 12x − 8.

2. Expand and collect like terms

Question: Simplify 3(2x − 5) − 2(x + 4).

Step 1: expand both brackets: 6x − 15 − 2x − 8.

Step 2: collect like terms: (6x − 2x) + (−15 − 8).

Answer: 4x − 23.

3. Expand two binomials

(x + 3)(x + 5) = x² + 5x + 3x + 15 = x² + 8x + 15.

Every term in the first bracket multiplies every term in the second.

4. Factorise a common factor

For 12x² − 18x, the greatest common factor is 6x.

12x² − 18x = 6x(2x − 3).

5. Factorise a quadratic

x² − x − 12 needs two numbers with product −12 and sum −1: −4 and 3.

x² − x − 12 = (x − 4)(x + 3).

Check: expanding gives x² + 3x − 4x − 12 = x² − x − 12.

6. Complete the square — with a visual model

Question: Rewrite x² + 8x + 7.

Step 1: half 8 to get 4; 4² = 16.

Step 2: x² + 8x + 7 = x² + 8x + 16 − 16 + 7.

Step 3: the first three terms form a perfect square: (x + 4)² − 9.

The four regions total x² + 4x + 4x + 16 = x² + 8x + 16, a square of side x + 4. Because the original constant was 7 rather than 16, subtract 9.

7. Product and quotient exponent laws

x³ × x⁵ = x⁸. Then x⁸ ÷ x² = x⁶, x ≠ 0.

Add exponents when multiplying the same base; subtract when dividing.

8. Negative exponents and a fraction

Question: Simplify (6x⁵y⁻²)/(3x²y).

Coefficients: 6/3 = 2. For x: x⁵/x² = x³. For y: y⁻²/y = y⁻³.

So 2x³y⁻³ = 2x³/y³, y ≠ 0.

9. Solve an equation with brackets

2(x − 3) + 5 = 3x − 4

2x − 6 + 5 = 3x − 4 → 2x − 1 = 3x − 4 → 3 = x.

Answer: x = 3. Substitute into the original equation to check.

10. Simplify powers before solving

If 2x³ × x² = 64x² and x ≠ 0, then 2x⁵ = 64x².

Divide by 2x²: x³ = 32.

Answer: x = ∛32. The key decision is to simplify the powers before isolating x.

Common mistakes
  • Missing a term when expanding: 3(x + 4) = 3x + 12, not 3x + 4.
  • Combining unlike terms: x² + x cannot become 2x³.
  • Using exponent laws for addition: x² + x² = 2x², not x⁴.
  • Reading x⁻³ as −x³: x⁻³ = 1/x³.
  • Wrong quadratic factor pair: check both product and sum, then expand your answer.
  • Changing only one side of an equation: equation-solving operations must preserve equality.
10 important questions to solve
  1. Expand 5(2x − 3).
  2. Simplify 4(3x + 1) − 2(x − 5).
  3. Expand (x − 4)(x + 7).
  4. Factorise 15x² − 10x.
  5. Factorise x² + 2x − 15.
  6. Write x² − 10x + 21 in completed-square form.
  7. Simplify a⁴ × a⁻⁷ using positive exponents.
  8. Simplify (12a⁶b⁻¹)/(3a²b²) using positive exponents.
  9. Solve 3(x + 2) − 4 = 2x + 9.
  10. Explain why x² + x³ cannot be simplified to x⁵.
Solutions with explanations
  1. 10x − 15. Distribute 5 to both terms.
  2. 10x + 14. 12x + 4 − 2x + 10 = 10x + 14.
  3. x² + 3x − 28. x² + 7x − 4x − 28.
  4. 5x(3x − 2). Remove the greatest common factor 5x.
  5. (x + 5)(x − 3). 5 × (−3) = −15 and 5 + (−3) = 2.
  6. (x − 5)² − 4. Half −10 to get −5; add and subtract 25.
  7. 1/a³. a⁴a⁻⁷ = a⁻³ = 1/a³, a ≠ 0.
  8. 4a⁴/b³. Divide coefficients and subtract exponents for matching bases.
  9. x = 7. 3x + 6 − 4 = 2x + 9 → 3x + 2 = 2x + 9 → x = 7.
  10. They are unlike terms. Exponent addition applies to multiplication: x² × x³ = x⁵; it does not apply to x² + x³.

AC9M10A01: expand, factorise and simplify expressions and solve equations algebraically, applying exponent laws involving products, quotients and powers of variables, and the distributive property.

  • E1: connect factorisation and expansion, including completed-square form for quadratic expressions. Examples 3, 5 and 6 teach these equivalent forms.
  • E2: apply exponent laws to algebraic terms, including positive and negative integral exponents, when simplifying expressions and solving equations. Examples 7–10 apply and check these rules.
