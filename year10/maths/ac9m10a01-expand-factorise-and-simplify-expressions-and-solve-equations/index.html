AC9M10A01 • Year 10 Maths • Algebra

Expanding, Factorising, Exponent Laws and Algebraic Equations

Learn the essential Year 10 algebra skills through rules you can revise quickly, fully worked examples, common-error checks and a 10-question practice set with solutions.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What you need to know

This topic is about algebraic equivalence. You may change the form of an expression by expanding, factorising or applying exponent laws, but its value must stay the same. When solving an equation, each valid step must preserve the same solution.

You should be able to expand and factorise expressions, connect factorised and expanded quadratic forms, complete the square, simplify algebraic powers with positive and negative integer exponents, and solve equations algebraically.

Cheat sheet

Expansion

a(b+c)=ab+ac

(x+a)(x+b)=x²+(a+b)x+ab

Factorising

ab+ac=a(b+c)

For x²+bx+c, find p,q with pq=c and p+q=b.

Completing the square

x²+bx+c=(x+b/2)²+c−(b/2)²

Exponent laws

aᵐaⁿ=aᵐ⁺ⁿ

aᵐ/aⁿ=aᵐ⁻ⁿ (a≠0)

(aᵐ)ⁿ=aᵐⁿ

a⁰=1 (a≠0)

a⁻ⁿ=1/aⁿ

Fast checks: only combine like terms; exponent laws for multiplication/division require the same base; expand a factorisation to verify it; substitute a solution into the original equation.

10 important worked examples

1. Expand one bracket

Expand 4(3x−2).

Step 1: multiply both terms by 4: 4×3x−4×2.

Answer: 12x−8.

2. Expand and collect like terms

3(2x−5)−2(x+4)=6x−15−2x−8=4x−23.

The minus sign belongs to the whole second bracket.

3. Expand two binomials

(x+3)(x+5)=x²+5x+3x+15=x²+8x+15.

Every term in the first bracket multiplies every term in the second.

4. Factorise a common factor

12x²−18x. The greatest common factor is 6x.

12x²−18x=6x(2x−3).

5. Factorise a quadratic

x²−x−12. Find two numbers with product −12 and sum −1: −4 and 3.

x²−x−12=(x−4)(x+3).

6. Complete the square

x²+8x+7. Half 8 to get 4; 4²=16.

x²+8x+7=x²+8x+16−16+7=(x+4)²−9.

7. Product and quotient exponent laws

x³×x⁵=x⁸. Then x⁸/x²=x⁶, for x≠0.

Add exponents when multiplying the same base; subtract when dividing.

8. Negative exponents

6x⁵y⁻²/(3x²y)=2x³y⁻³=2x³/y³, y≠0.

A negative exponent means reciprocal; it does not make the term negative.

9. Solve an equation with brackets

2(x−3)+5=3x−4

2x−6+5=3x−4 → 2x−1=3x−4 → 3=x.

Answer: x=3. Substitute into the original equation to check.

10. Solve after simplifying powers

If 2x³×x²=64x² and x≠0, then 2x⁵=64x².

Divide by 2x²: x³=32. Therefore x=∛32.

The important decision is to simplify the powers before isolating x.

Common mistakes
  • Missing a term when expanding: 3(x+4)=3x+12, not 3x+4.
  • Combining unlike terms: x²+x cannot become 2x³.
  • Using exponent laws for addition: x²+x²=2x², not x⁴.
  • Reading x⁻³ as −x³: x⁻³=1/x³.
  • Wrong quadratic factor pair: check both the product and the sum, then expand your answer.
  • Changing only one side of an equation: equation-solving operations must preserve equality.
10 important questions to solve

  2. Simplify 4(3x+1)−2(x−5).
  3. Expand (x−4)(x+7).
  4. Factorise 15x²−10x.

  6. Write x²−10x+21 in completed-square form.
  7. Simplify a⁴×a⁻⁷ using positive exponents.
  8. Simplify (12a⁶b⁻¹)/(3a²b²) using positive exponents.

Solutions with explanations
  1. 10x−15. Distribute 5 to both terms.
  2. 10x+14. 12x+4−2x+10=10x+14.
  3. x²+3x−28. x²+7x−4x−28.
  4. 5x(3x−2). Remove the greatest common factor 5x.
  5. (x+5)(x−3). 5×(−3)=−15 and 5+(−3)=2.
  6. (x−5)²−4. Half −10 to get −5; add and subtract 25: x²−10x+25−4.
  7. 1/a³. a⁴a⁻⁷=a⁻³=1/a³, a≠0.
  8. 4a⁴/b³. Divide coefficients; subtract exponents: a⁶/a²=a⁴ and b⁻¹/b²=b⁻³=1/b³.
  9. x=7. 3x+6−4=2x+9 → 3x+2=2x+9 → x=7.
  10. They are unlike terms. Exponent addition applies to multiplication: x²×x³=x⁵. Addition x²+x³ has no common like-term combination.
Australian Curriculum v9.0 and elaborations

AC9M10A01: expand, factorise and simplify expressions and solve equations algebraically, applying exponent laws involving products, quotients and powers of variables, and the distributive property.

  • E1: connect factorisation and expansion, including completed-square form for quadratic expressions. Examples 3, 5 and 6 show these equivalent forms directly.
  • E2: apply exponent laws to algebraic terms, including positive and negative integral exponents, when simplifying expressions and solving equations. Examples 7–10 apply these rules and distinguish valid from invalid uses.
