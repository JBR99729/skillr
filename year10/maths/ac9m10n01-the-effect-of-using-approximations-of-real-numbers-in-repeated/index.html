Exact Values, Surds, π and Rounding Error

Learn why exact forms such as √2, fractions and π should be kept through multi-step calculations, and how early rounding or truncation can change a final answer.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
What you need to know

An exact value represents a number without rounding. Examples include 1/3, √5 and 7π. An approximation replaces that exact number with a nearby decimal. Approximation is useful for measurement and communication, but if rounded values are reused in later steps, the small error can accumulate.

The central Year 10 habit is: calculate exactly for as long as possible, then round once at the end to the accuracy the context requires.

Exact-values cheat sheet

Exact forms

Fractions, π and surds such as √2 are exact.

0.333… = 1/3 exactly; 0.33 is only an approximation.

Surd rules

√(ab)=√a·√b for a,b≥0.

√(a+b) ≠ √a+√b in general.

Simplify perfect-square factors: √72=6√2.

Rounding rule

Keep guard digits in working; round only the final answer unless the problem explicitly asks otherwise.

Error language

Rounding chooses the nearest stated place.

Truncation cuts digits off without rounding.

Absolute error = |approx−exact|.

10 important worked examples

1. Exact versus approximate √2

Exact: √2. To 3 decimal places: 1.414.

If a later calculation uses (√2)², the exact route gives 2. Using 1.414² gives 1.999396. The approximation is close, but it is not exact.

2. A false square-root rule

√(9+16)=√25=5, while √9+√16=3+4=7.

This counterexample proves square roots do not distribute over addition.

3. Simplify before approximating

√72=√(36×2)=6√2.

Only if a decimal is requested: 6√2≈8.485.

4. Collect like surds exactly

2√45−√20+¼√80 = 2(3√5)−2√5+¼(4√5)=6√5−2√5+√5=5√5.

5. Circle area: keep π

A circle has radius 7 cm. Exact area: A=πr²=49π cm².

Using π≈3.14 too early gives 153.86 cm²; calculator π gives about 153.938 cm². Keep 49π until the final rounding instruction.

6. Repeated circumference calculations

A wheel of diameter 0.72 m rotates 250 times. Exact distance =250×0.72π=180π m.

Final decimal: 180π≈565.49 m. Using π=3.14 gives 565.2 m, already about 0.29 m different.

7. Compound-object volume

A cylinder has radius 2.5 cm and height 12 cm. Exact volume: V=πr²h=π(2.5)²(12)=75π cm³.

Do not round π after the first multiplication if this volume is then used in another calculation.

8. Truncation versus rounding

For x=7.9864, to 2 decimal places x≈7.99. Truncated to 2 decimal places it is 7.98.

The two approximations lie on opposite sides of the exact value and can affect repeated operations differently.

9. Repeated simple-interest cents

Interest on $1375 at 3.7% p.a. for 1 year is I=1375×0.037=$50.875.

If several annual amounts are accumulated, retaining $50.875 in calculation and rounding the final monetary result can differ from rounding each year to $50.88 first.

10. Compare two pathways

Calculate 7(√3)². Exact route: 7×3=21.

If √3≈1.73, then 7(1.73)²=20.9503. The discrepancy is entirely due to early approximation.

Common mistakes
  • Rounding after every calculator step: keep full calculator precision or exact notation until the end.
  • Treating ≈ as =: use = for exact equality and ≈ for an approximation.
  • Assuming √(a+b)=√a+√b: test 9 and 16 to see why this fails.
  • Combining unlike surds: √2+√3 cannot be simplified to √5.
  • Using 3.14 as if it were π: 3.14 is an approximation; π is exact notation.
10 important questions to solve
  1. State whether 5π is exact or approximate.
  2. Simplify √98 exactly.
  3. Show with numbers that √(a+b)=√a+√b is not a general rule.
  4. Simplify 3√8+√18.
  5. Find the exact area of a circle of radius 4 cm.
  6. Find the distance travelled by a wheel of diameter 0.5 m after 100 turns, exactly and then to 2 d.p.
  7. Round 12.4867 to 2 d.p. and truncate it to 2 d.p.
  8. Compare (1.41)² with (√2)².
  9. A cylinder has r=3 cm and h=10 cm. Give its exact volume.
  10. Explain why a multi-step calculation should normally be rounded only once.
Solutions with explanations
  1. Exact. π has not been replaced by a decimal.
  2. 7√2. √98=√(49×2).
  3. Example: √(9+16)=5 but √9+√16=7.
  4. 9√2. 3√8=6√2 and √18=3√2.
  5. 16π cm². A=πr².
  6. 50π m≈157.08 m. Distance=100×π×0.5.
  7. 12.49 rounded; 12.48 truncated.
  8. (1.41)²=1.9881; (√2)²=2 exactly.
  9. 90π cm³. π×3²×10.
  10. Each early approximation introduces error; later operations can magnify or accumulate it.
Australian Curriculum v9.0 and elaborations

AC9M10N01: recognise the effect of using approximations of real numbers in repeated calculations and compare the results when using exact representations.

  • E1: compare and contrast the effect of truncation or rounding on the final result when approximate real numbers are used instead of exact representations. Examples 1, 8 and 10 show where the error enters and how it changes the result.
  • E2: investigate approximation in multiple calculations involving compound shapes with circles, surface area and volume of compound objects, and repeated simple-interest calculations where answers are not exact cents. Examples 5–9 model these contexts.
