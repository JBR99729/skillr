Growth and Decay Models

Linear, quadratic and exponential models describe different change patterns and must be chosen, interpreted and critiqued in context.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

Linear, quadratic and exponential models describe different change patterns and must be chosen, interpreted and critiqued in context.

Learning intention: Linear, quadratic and exponential models describe different change patterns and must be chosen, interpreted and critiqued in context.

Success criteria

  • Explain choose linear, quadratic or exponential using the approved model.
  • Apply critique a population-growth model to a new case.
  • Verify a conclusion with a second representation, estimate or test case.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • use mathematical modelling to solve applied problems involving growth and decay, including financial contexts; formulate problems, choosing to apply linear, quadratic or exponential models; interpret solutions in terms of the situation; evaluate and modify models as necessary and report assumptions, methods and findings

Prerequisites

  • Relevant Year 9 algebra representations and vocabulary.

May support informally

  • Digital tools may test, visualise and verify after the mathematical structure is explicit.

Do not overteach

  • Do not introduce later-year formalism that is unnecessary for this content description.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • A rule is applied without preserving equivalence or its conditions — Return to the linked table, graph or expanded form and verify by substitution.
  • The growth and decay models model is copied without interpretation — Connect every label in ‘Model situations and choosing between linear, quadratic and exponential models by representing relationships in a table of values.’ to its mathematical role.
  • The result is not verified — Use a second representation, boundary case, inverse operation or simulation check.
Support, core and extend

Support: Reduce numerical or representational load while retaining the same decision and explanation.

Core: Connect at least two representations, justify the method and verify independently.

Extend: Test a boundary case, counterexample or model limitation without introducing later-year procedures.
