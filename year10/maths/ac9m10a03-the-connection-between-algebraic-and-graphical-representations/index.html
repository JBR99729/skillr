Exponential Relations

An exponential relationship multiplies by a constant ratio over equal input steps and appears as a curved graph.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

An exponential relationship multiplies by a constant ratio over equal input steps and appears as a curved graph.

Learning intention: An exponential relationship multiplies by a constant ratio over equal input steps and appears as a curved graph.

Success criteria

  • Explain spot a constant ratio using the approved model.
  • Apply model competing animal populations to a new case.
  • Verify a conclusion with a second representation, estimate or test case.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • recognise the connection between algebraic and graphical representations of exponential relations and solve related exponential equations, using digital tools where appropriate

Prerequisites

  • Relevant Year 9 algebra representations and vocabulary.

May support informally

  • Digital tools may test, visualise and verify after the mathematical structure is explicit.

Do not overteach

  • Do not introduce later-year formalism that is unnecessary for this content description.

Worked examples

Spot a constant ratio

Recognise that in a table of values, if the ratio between consecutive values of the dependent variable is constant, then it is an exponential relation.

Teacher prompt: For spot a constant ratio, which quantities must scale or compare by the same factor?

Connect exponential rules and graphs

Explore the links between algebraic and graphical representations of exponential functions using graphing software.

Teacher prompt: For connect exponential rules and graphs, which graph feature is direct evidence for the conclusion?

Explore exponential equations digitally

Use digital tools with symbolic manipulation functionality to systematically explore exponential relations.

Teacher prompt: For explore exponential equations digitally, which graph feature is direct evidence for the conclusion?

Model competing animal populations

Compare two population rules over equal time steps; identify whether each changes by a constant amount or constant ratio.

Teacher prompt: For model competing animal populations, which quantities must scale or compare by the same factor?

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • A rule is applied without preserving equivalence or its conditions — Return to the linked table, graph or expanded form and verify by substitution.
  • The exponential relations model is copied without interpretation — Connect every label in ‘Recognise that in a table of values, if the ratio between consecutive values of the dependent variable is constant, then it is an exponential relation.’ to its mathematical role.
  • The result is not verified — Use a second representation, boundary case, inverse operation or simulation check.
Support, core and extend

Support: Reduce numerical or representational load while retaining the same decision and explanation.

Core: Connect at least two representations, justify the method and verify independently.

Extend: Test a boundary case, counterexample or model limitation without introducing later-year procedures.
