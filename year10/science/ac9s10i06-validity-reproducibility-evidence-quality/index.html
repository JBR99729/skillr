AC9S10I06 • Year 10 Science

Evaluate Validity, Reproducibility and Evidence Quality

Evaluation asks whether a method tested the intended question, whether another investigator could reproduce it, and whether the evidence is accurate, precise, representative and sufficient for the conclusion being claimed.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
Core concepts
  • Validity: does the method actually test the intended relationship while controlling important alternatives?
  • Reproducibility: could another investigator repeat the procedure from the information provided?
  • Measurement quality: consider calibration, precision, accuracy, sample size, bias and uncertainty.
  • Evidence sufficiency: the strength and scope of a conclusion must match the evidence.
Worked examples

Validity

If both fertiliser dose and light exposure change in a plant-growth experiment, the effect of fertiliser cannot be isolated confidently.

Reproducibility

A method that says “heat gently until ready” is difficult to reproduce because both the heating condition and endpoint are undefined.

Precision is not accuracy

Measurements can cluster tightly around the wrong value when an instrument has a systematic calibration error.

Common mistakes
  • Assuming reliable results are automatically valid.
  • Assuming one successful replication proves reproducibility.
  • Treating uncertainty as a reason to ignore data rather than quantify or discuss it.
  • Writing only “human error” instead of naming the specific error mechanism and likely effect.
Australian and international curriculum mapping

Australian Curriculum v9.0: AC9S10I06 — assess validity and reproducibility of methods and evaluate conclusions and claims with reference to evidence quality.

Victoria: closest alignment is Levels 9–10 Science Inquiry skills involving evaluation of methods, data quality, uncertainty and evidence-based conclusions.

NSW: closest alignment is Stage 5 Science Working Scientifically outcomes involving evaluating investigations, reliability of evidence and validity of conclusions.

United States: closest alignment is NGSS Science and Engineering Practices involving Planning and Carrying Out Investigations, Analysing and Interpreting Data and Engaging in Argument from Evidence.

England: closest alignment is GCSE Working Scientifically, especially evaluating methods, uncertainty, reproducibility and conclusions.

New Zealand: closest alignment is the Nature of Science strand, particularly Investigating in Science and Understanding about Science.

Canada: closest alignment is provincial secondary Science inquiry expectations for evaluating methods, evidence quality and conclusions; exact wording varies by province.

These are planning comparisons, not exact one-to-one equivalences.
