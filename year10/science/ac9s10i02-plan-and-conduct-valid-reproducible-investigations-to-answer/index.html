What students learn in AC9S10I02

This unit helps students build a clear, usable understanding of Plan and conduct valid, reproducible investigations to answer questions and.... The goal is not to memorise one answer pattern. Students should be able to explain the idea, recognise it in a new example and apply it in a short practice or worksheet task.

  • Start with the main idea: plan and conduct valid, reproducible investigations to answer questions and test hypotheses, including identifying and controlling for possible sources of error and, as appropriate, developing and following risk assessments, considering ethical issues, and addressing key considerations regarding heritage sites and artefacts on Country/Place.
  • Use concrete examples, pictures, oral explanation and short written responses before moving to independent practice.
  • addressing possible sources of error through choice of equipment, variable control or further testing considering possible confounding variables or effects and ensuring these are controlled or accounted for in planned methods for data collection and analysis
  • Finish with mixed questions so students must choose the correct strategy rather than copy the last example.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Question banks for this unit should draw from the content description and the eligible elaborations below. First Nations-specific notes are kept out of generated question coverage unless they are part of the core concept.

  • Content description: plan and conduct valid, reproducible investigations to answer questions and test hypotheses, including identifying and controlling for possible sources of error and, as appropriate, developing and following risk assessments, considering ethical issues, and addressing key considerations regarding heritage sites and artefacts on Country/Place
  • E1: addressing possible sources of error through choice of equipment, variable control or further testing
  • E2: considering possible confounding variables or effects and ensuring these are controlled or accounted for in planned methods for data collection and analysis
  • E3: identifying safety risks and impacts on animal welfare and ensuring these are effectively managed within an investigation
  • E4: identifying the potential hazards of chemicals or biological materials and processes used in experimental investigations and how these should be addressed
  • E5: addressing ethical issues when collaborating with First Nations Australians to explore the development of a commercial product based on traditional ecological knowledges (not used for generated questions)
  • E6: modelling how to report the discovery of unregistered First Nations Australians artefacts and heritage or any unauthorised disturbance (not used for generated questions)
  • E7: considering the ethical and social issues and legal responsibilities involved in the care and use of animals for scientific purposes before starting an investigation involving animals

How to use this unit

Read the topic guide, download the teacher slide for instruction, complete the printable worksheet, then use Practice and Test when the unit's checked 8-question bank is available.

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Describing what is seen without connecting it to a question, pattern or prediction.
  • Using everyday words only and missing simple scientific vocabulary.
  • Changing more than one thing in an investigation, which makes the comparison unfair.
