Design Valid, Reproducible Investigations

A valid investigation tests the intended relationship while controlling important alternative causes. A reproducible method is transparent enough for another investigator to repeat, and it must also satisfy safety, ethical and cultural responsibilities.

What students learn — Core concepts and success criteria

A valid investigation tests the intended relationship while controlling important alternative causes. A reproducible method is transparent enough for another investigator to repeat, and it must also satisfy safety, ethical and cultural responsibilities.

What students need to know

  • A valid investigation tests the intended relationship while controlling important alternative causes. A reproducible method is transparent enough for another investigator to repeat, and it must also satisfy safety, ethical and cultural responsibilities.

Success looks like: students can apply the inquiry skill to a new context, justify methodological choices and match the strength of a conclusion to the evidence.

Worked examples

Control a confounding variable

To test light intensity and photosynthesis, keep plant species, temperature, carbon dioxide availability and measurement time consistent while varying light intensity.

Repeats do not fix bias

Repeating a miscalibrated sensor 50 times can produce a very consistent but systematically wrong result. Calibration must be corrected first.

Plan an ethical animal investigation

Before collecting animal data, minimise disturbance, justify the need for the observations and follow relevant animal-care requirements.

Respect Country/Place responsibilities

If an investigation involves First Nations heritage sites, artefacts or knowledge, appropriate authority and protocols must be established before access, recording or publication.

Common misconceptions and fixes
  • More repeats make any method valid: Repeats reduce random variation; they cannot repair a design that tests the wrong variable or contains systematic bias.
  • A detailed method is automatically reproducible: It must include operational details, equipment settings, units, sampling and decision rules.
  • Safety is added after the method: Hazards and controls can change the method itself and must be planned before data collection.
  • Cultural knowledge is freely usable data: Access, permissions, attribution and restrictions must be respected; some knowledge or places may not be appropriate to record or share.
Step-by-step inquiry method

Align question and variables

Make sure the variables or comparison groups answer the actual question.

Control alternative causes

Identify confounders and decide how each will be controlled, randomised, matched or measured.

Plan quality data

Choose suitable sampling, repeats, equipment, units and recording procedures.

Complete responsibility checks

Assess hazards, ethics, environmental impact and any Country/Place or heritage obligations before conducting the investigation.

Apply and transfer

A class wants to test whether screen brightness affects battery life. Design the key variables, controls, repeats and one validity check needed for a reproducible investigation.

Student check: answer using specific variables, evidence, method choices or limitations rather than generic phrases such as human error.
