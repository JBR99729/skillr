Earth’s Energy Balance and Global Climate Change

Climate emerges from energy transfer among atmosphere, hydrosphere, geosphere and biosphere. Changes to incoming, reflected, absorbed and outgoing energy alter long-term climate patterns.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
What you need to know

Solar radiation enters the Earth system; some is reflected and some absorbed. Earth emits infrared radiation. Greenhouse gases absorb and re-emit some infrared, influencing the energy balance. Climate refers to long-term patterns; individual weather events do not prove or disprove climate trends.

Climate-system cheat sheet

Energy flow

Incoming solar → reflection/absorption → transfer among spheres → outgoing infrared.

Greenhouse effect

Atmospheric gases interact with outgoing infrared; it is not a solid “blanket layer”.

Indicators

Atmospheric/ocean temperature, sea level, ice/permafrost, species distribution/biodiversity.

Model rule

Predictions depend on assumptions, initial conditions and represented processes; uncertainty must be reported.

Worked examples

Incoming solar energy

The Sun supplies most energy driving climate; land, oceans and atmosphere absorb different fractions and exchange energy.

Albedo feedback

Bright ice reflects more sunlight than dark ocean. Loss of ice can reduce reflection and increase absorption.

Greenhouse mechanism

Increasing greenhouse-gas concentration changes absorption and re-emission of outgoing infrared, shifting the energy balance.

Weather versus climate

A cold day is weather. A multi-decade temperature distribution or trend is climate evidence.

Multiple indicators

Ocean heat content, sea level and shifting species ranges provide evidence independent of one air-temperature dataset.

Deep ocean circulation

Density differences linked to temperature and salinity help drive deep circulation that redistributes heat.

Simulation

Changing one forcing or parameter and comparing outputs shows conditional consequences, not certainty detached from assumptions.

Mitigation and adaptation

Reducing greenhouse-gas emissions targets drivers; sea walls or heatwave plans reduce impacts.

Common misconceptions
  • Greenhouse effect is a physical lid.
  • Weather and climate are the same.
  • One data series is the whole evidence base.
  • Natural drivers mean human drivers cannot matter.
  • A model is worthless unless it predicts exactly.
Australian Curriculum v9.0 and elaborations

AC9S10U04: use models of energy flow between geosphere, biosphere, hydrosphere and atmosphere to explain patterns of global climate change.

  • E1: solar radiation and interactions with atmosphere, ocean and land.
  • E2: climate indicators including temperature, sea level, biodiversity, species distribution, permafrost and ice.
  • E3: climate change over time using visualisations and simulations.
  • E4: energy factors driving deep-ocean currents.
  • E5: advanced computing in weather and climate modelling.
  • E6: Earth-system predictions, mitigation and adaptation.
