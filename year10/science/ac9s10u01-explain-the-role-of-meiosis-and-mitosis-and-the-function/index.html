AC9S10U01 • Year 10 Science

Genes, Cell Division and Inheritance

DNA, genes and chromosomes carry hereditary information; meiosis and fertilisation generate inheritance patterns and variation.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain genes, cell division and inheritance using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect DNA and gene, chromosome, mitosis.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Genes are DNA sequences located on chromosomes.
  • Mitosis supports growth and repair; meiosis produces genetically varied gametes.
  • Mendelian models predict genotype and phenotype probabilities.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not imply one gene determines every characteristic.
  • Do not extend to complex linkage or molecular gene regulation as the target.

Use Mendelian inheritance to predict the ratio of offspring genotypes to make the role of mitosis visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

Common misconceptions
  • A gene and a chromosome are the same object.
  • A Punnett square predicts exact family outcomes.
