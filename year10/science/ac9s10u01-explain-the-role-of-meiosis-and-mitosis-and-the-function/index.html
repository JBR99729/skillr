DNA, Cell Division and Mendelian Inheritance

Genes are DNA sequences on chromosomes. Mitosis preserves chromosome information for growth and repair, while meiosis and fertilisation generate variation and predictable inheritance probabilities.

What you need to know

A chromosome is a long DNA molecule packaged with proteins; a gene is a section of DNA that contributes to a functional product/trait. Mitosis produces genetically similar daughter cells for growth and repair. Meiosis produces haploid gametes and reshuffles genetic information; fertilisation restores the diploid chromosome number. Mendelian models predict probabilities, not exact family outcomes.

Genetics cheat sheet

DNA → gene → chromosome

Genes are DNA sequences located on chromosomes.

Mitosis

1 parent cell → 2 genetically similar cells; chromosome number retained.

Meiosis

1 diploid cell → 4 genetically varied haploid gametes; chromosome number halved.

Mendelian terms

Genotype = allele combination; phenotype = observable outcome; dominant/recessive describe allele expression in a simple model.

10 important worked examples

1. Nest the structures

A cell nucleus contains chromosomes; chromosomes contain DNA; genes are defined DNA regions. Saying “a gene is a chromosome” collapses different levels of organisation.

2. Why mitosis matters

A skin cell divides by mitosis. DNA is copied, chromosomes are separated and two daughter cells receive the same chromosome set, supporting tissue growth/repair.

3. Why meiosis matters

Meiosis halves chromosome number so gametes can fuse at fertilisation without chromosome number doubling each generation.

4. Variation

Independent assortment and recombination during meiosis, plus random fertilisation, create new allele combinations in offspring.

5. Monohybrid cross Aa × Aa

Gametes from each parent: A or a. Punnett outcomes: AA, Aa, Aa, aa → genotype ratio 1:2:1; with A dominant, phenotype ratio 3:1.

6. Probability, not prediction of exact children

A 25% aa probability does not mean exactly one of every four children must be aa; each conception is a new probabilistic event.

7. Pedigree reasoning

If two unaffected parents have an affected child under a simple autosomal-recessive model, both parents must carry the recessive allele.

8. Mutation

UV radiation can damage DNA. A mutation in a body cell can contribute to cancer; a mutation must occur in a germ-line lineage to be inherited by offspring.

9. Genetic disorder

Cystic fibrosis can be modelled as autosomal recessive for introductory inheritance work, but real genetic conditions still involve molecular detail beyond a Punnett square.

10. Kinship and heredity context

First Nations Australian kinship/family structures are cultural knowledge systems with specific laws and responsibilities. When studying the curriculum elaboration, use authoritative community/context sources and do not reduce kinship to a Mendelian genetics analogy.

Common misconceptions
  • Gene = chromosome: a gene is a DNA region on a chromosome.
  • Mitosis creates variation: mitosis generally preserves genetic information; meiosis is the cell division central to gamete variation.
  • Dominant means common/stronger: dominance describes expression in heterozygotes, not population frequency or biological superiority.
  • Punnett square predicts exact family: it gives probabilities.
  • All mutations are inherited: inheritance depends on where the mutation occurs.
10 important questions to solve
  1. State relationship among DNA, genes and chromosomes.
  2. What is the main role of mitosis?
  3. Why must meiosis halve chromosome number?
  4. Name two sources of genetic variation associated with sexual reproduction.
  5. For Aa×Aa, give genotype ratio.
  6. For simple dominant A, give phenotype ratio.
  7. Why can 25% probability fail to appear in a family of four children?
  8. What can two unaffected parents with a recessive affected child imply?
  9. Distinguish somatic and heritable mutation.
  10. Why should First Nations kinship knowledge not be treated as a generic Punnett-square example?
Solutions with explanations
  1. Genes are DNA sequences located on chromosomes.
  2. Growth/repair by producing genetically similar daughter cells.
  3. So fertilisation restores rather than doubles the species chromosome number.
  4. Meiotic recombination/assortment and random fertilisation.
  5. 1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa.
  6. 3 dominant : 1 recessive.
  7. Probabilities describe repeated possibilities, not a fixed quota.
  8. Both parents are carriers under the simple autosomal-recessive model.
  9. Somatic mutations affect body-cell lineages; germ-line mutations can enter gametes and be inherited.
  10. Kinship is culturally specific knowledge/law and requires authoritative respectful context.
