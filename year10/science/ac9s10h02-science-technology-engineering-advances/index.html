YEAR 9 SCIENCE

Trace reciprocal advances in science and technology AC9S10H02

Learning intention: Investigate how improved technological capabilities enable science and how scientific questions and knowledge guide technological and engineering development.

More examples ↓Help with gaps ↓10-minute activity ↓

WORKED EXAMPLE

Penicillin development required connected questions, methods and tools

Historical accounts from the University of Oxford, the National Museum of Australia and the National Library of Australia describe complementary contributions to penicillin development. Investigating an active material required sensitive biological assays, extraction methods and usable preparations. Heatley contributed assay methods and purpose-designed apparatus; Florey, Chain and colleagues contributed coordinated biological and chemical investigation. Later, Hodgkin’s crystallographic work helped establish molecular structure. This is a reciprocal development story, not one observation or one inventor proving a finished medicine.

Learn the skill

1. Explain a tool enabling new evidence

Name the capability that changed: sensitivity, precision, scale, preparation or data analysis. Then identify the evidence it made possible. The penicillin case connects assays and extraction apparatus to investigation of active preparations; “better technology helped” alone leaves the causal link unexplained.

A student writes: “Heatley’s work helped because the apparatus was newer.”
Try it · Replace newness with a specific capability and scientific consequence.

For example: sensitive assays helped compare biological activity during isolation, while extraction methods and apparatus helped obtain usable preparations for further testing. Explain what the methods enabled, not just when they appeared.

2. Explain science guiding design

Scientific questions identify performance needs for engineering. An investigation may require comparable preparations, detection of small differences, reliable calibration or greater capacity. Engineers develop and test solutions under constraints; size or complexity alone does not establish scientific usefulness.

Fictional design brief: compare two weak activity signals repeatedly, using the same sample volume and a calibrated reference.
Try it · Which design criteria follow from this scientific need?

Adequate sensitivity, repeatable measurement, a suitable reference and controlled sample volume are relevant criteria. Greater capacity may help later, but it does not replace the ability to distinguish the signals reliably.

3. Close and evaluate the reciprocal loop

A question guides a tool or method; a changed capability enables evidence; interpretation refines explanations and further questions. Several people and disciplines can contribute. An unexpected signal might reveal a phenomenon or an instrument artefact, so calibration, controls and independent comparisons remain necessary.

A new detector shows a pattern absent from an older instrument, but has not yet been checked against a reference or independent method.
Try it · What should scientists and engineers investigate next?

Check calibration, settings, sample preparation and data processing, then compare appropriate independent measurements and model predictions. The pattern is a testable question; a newer instrument alone does not prove a new explanation.

Common mistakes

“The relationship runs only science → technology.” Tools can produce evidence that changes scientific understanding.

“One person alone explains the whole advance.” Specific complementary contributions and working conditions matter.

“A new instrument guarantees valid evidence.” Calibration, method and interpretation remain necessary.

10-MINUTE ACTIVITY

Draw a reciprocal development account

Use the historical table to connect question → design need → changed method → evidence → further question. Name two complementary contributions and one constraint. Then apply the structure to the fictional detector, distinguishing history from your proposal.

Adjust the challenge Support: use enabled and guided to connect ideas. Core: show both directions. Extend: explain how improving sensitivity could also expose a new measurement problem.

Use the historical text and paper models. Do not grow mould, culture microorganisms, handle antibiotics or treat this history as medical advice.

Three tricky questions

1. Does a timeline alone prove that a tool caused a discovery?

No. Explain the changed capability and evidence link, including other contributions and conditions.

2. Do science and engineering have identical aims?

No. Science develops evidence-based explanations; engineering designs and tests solutions under constraints. They often interact.

3. Must a technology always begin with a formal theory?

No. Practical design and experience can also lead to technologies and new scientific questions.

FAQ

Why was an initial inhibition observation insufficient?

It showed an effect under particular conditions; preparation and further biological, chemical and application evidence were still needed.

Why discuss constraints?

Sensitivity, capacity, reliability and resources affect what can be built, measured and inferred.

More teaching examples and curriculum coverage
Learning goals

New tools create new observations, while scientific understanding enables new technologies and engineering.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Technologies extend observation, measurement and analysis.
  • Scientific knowledge informs engineering design.
  • Science–technology relationships are iterative and include limitations.
Prerequisite knowledge

Students should distinguish science (building explanations), technology (tools and applications) and engineering (designing solutions), while recognising that real projects connect all three.

Key concept

Learning sequence: Identify the limitation → Trace the new tool → Explain the new evidence → Follow the application

Core teaching

  • Technologies extend observation, measurement and analysis.
  • Scientific knowledge informs engineering design.
  • Science–technology relationships are iterative and include limitations.

Key vocabulary and ideas

Worked examples

Worked reasoning model

  1. Fast computing makes large DNA data sets and radio-astronomy signals analysable.
  2. Algorithms convert raw signals into patterns scientists can test.
  3. New scientific questions then drive faster computing, sensors and data systems.
Common misconceptions
  • Technology is only applied science: Trace feedback in both directions between tool and knowledge.
  • A more advanced instrument removes uncertainty: Identify new sensitivity alongside calibration, sampling and interpretation limits.

Keep the Year 10 boundary: Do not describe technological progress as automatic or value-free. Do not claim bigger data alone produces better explanations.

Guided practice
  1. Choose DNA sequencing, climate modelling or astronomy.
  2. Trace tool → data → knowledge → improved tool in four steps.

Work through the sequence Identify the limitation → Trace the new tool → Explain the new evidence → Follow the application, explaining the evidence or mechanism at each step before moving on.

Independent practice
  1. Give one example of a technology that allowed scientists to collect evidence that was previously inaccessible.
  2. Explain one way scientific knowledge can lead to a new engineered technology.
  3. For medical imaging, identify a scientific idea, a technological tool and an engineering improvement.
  4. Explain why the relationship between science and technology is often two-way rather than a simple sequence.
Check your answers
  1. A sensitive telescope detector can record fainter objects than older equipment.
  2. Understanding how radio waves interact with materials can inform antenna design.
  3. In MRI, magnetic behaviour is the scientific principle, the scanner is the tool, and improved detector coils are an engineering change.
  4. Tools make new observations possible. The resulting questions and findings guide improvements to the tools.
Reasoning and problem-solving

Choose a technology such as medical imaging or telescopes. Trace one advance from technology to new scientific evidence and one scientific advance back into improved technology or engineering.

Reasoning standard: state the claim, use relevant evidence or a scientific mechanism, and explain why the evidence supports the conclusion without overclaiming.

Questions and answers
What is the central idea?
New tools create new observations, while scientific understanding enables new technologies and engineering.
What should a strong response do?
Use the sequence Identify the limitation → Trace the new tool → Explain the new evidence → Follow the application. Connect the evidence, mechanism or data to the claim.
What common trap should I avoid?
Technology is only applied science: Trace feedback in both directions between tool and knowledge.
Practice and review
  1. Choose a technology such as telescopes, gene sequencing or medical imaging. Analyse how an advance in the technology changed scientific knowledge and how later science improved the technology.
  2. Evaluate the statement: “Technology is only an application of science.” Use a specific example and evidence in your response.
  3. A new sensor is ten times more sensitive than the previous device. Explain two ways that improvement could change the questions scientists are able to investigate.

Review hint: Use precise scientific vocabulary, show the relevant mechanism or evidence, and state any limitation or uncertainty when the evidence does not justify a stronger claim.

Check understanding
  • I can explain and apply: Technologies extend observation, measurement and analysis.
  • I can explain and apply: Scientific knowledge informs engineering design.
  • I can explain and apply: Science–technology relationships are iterative and include limitations.

Exit ticket: Explain the core idea in 3–5 sentences and apply it to one new example without copying the worked model.

Teacher and parent guidance

For teachers

Require a two-way causal chain. “Science caused technology” is incomplete when the technology later changes what scientists can observe or measure.

For parents and carers

Pick an everyday technology and ask what science made it possible and what new scientific questions the technology can now help answer.
