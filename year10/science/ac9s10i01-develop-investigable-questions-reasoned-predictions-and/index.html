Develop Testable Explanatory Questions

What students learn — Core concepts and success criteria

Strong scientific questions identify a measurable relationship. A hypothesis proposes an evidence-based explanation, and a prediction states what should be observed if that explanation is supported.

What students need to know

  • Strong scientific questions identify a measurable relationship. A hypothesis proposes an evidence-based explanation, and a prediction states what should be observed if that explanation is supported.

Success looks like: students can apply the inquiry skill to a new context, justify methodological choices and match the strength of a conclusion to the evidence.

Worked examples

From observation to question

Observation: a trolley accelerates more when the pulling force increases. Question: How does net force affect acceleration when mass is kept constant?

Build a reasoned hypothesis

Increasing net force should increase acceleration because a larger unbalanced force produces a larger change in motion for the same mass.

Derive a prediction

If pulling force is doubled while mass stays constant, measured acceleration should increase.

Separate correlation from causation

If hotter days are associated with higher electricity use, the data show a relationship. A causal hypothesis still needs a plausible mechanism and a design that rules out alternatives.

Common misconceptions and fixes
  • Prediction = hypothesis: A prediction is an expected result; a hypothesis is the proposed explanation that produces the prediction.
  • Every question must be causal: Descriptive and correlational questions can be investigable when variables are measurable.
  • Vague terms are measurable: Replace words such as better or more effective with a measurable quantity.
  • One supported prediction proves the model: Support increases confidence; it does not prove that no alternative explanation exists.
Step-by-step inquiry method

Define the observation

State what is known or noticed without explaining it yet.

Name measurable variables

Identify what could change, what could be measured and what conditions matter.

Propose a mechanism

Use scientific knowledge to explain why a relationship may occur.

State a testable prediction

Describe the expected pattern and how evidence would support or challenge the hypothesis.

Apply and transfer

A student notices that reaction time appears slower after sleep restriction. Write an investigable question, a reasoned hypothesis and one prediction that could be tested ethically.

Student check: answer using specific variables, evidence, method choices or limitations rather than generic phrases such as human error.
