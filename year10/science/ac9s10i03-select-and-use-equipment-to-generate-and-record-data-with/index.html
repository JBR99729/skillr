Collect Precise, Replicable Data

Useful scientific data depend on appropriate equipment, calibration, resolution, consistent technique and a sample size that represents genuine independent evidence rather than repeated digital readings of the same event.

What students learn — Core concepts and success criteria

Useful scientific data depend on appropriate equipment, calibration, resolution, consistent technique and a sample size that represents genuine independent evidence rather than repeated digital readings of the same event.

What students need to know

  • Useful scientific data depend on appropriate equipment, calibration, resolution, consistent technique and a sample size that represents genuine independent evidence rather than repeated digital readings of the same event.

Success looks like: students can apply the inquiry skill to a new context, justify methodological choices and match the strength of a conclusion to the evidence.

Worked examples

Choose appropriate resolution

If temperature changes of about 0.2 °C matter, a thermometer marked only every 1 °C cannot resolve the required change reliably.

Check zero and calibration

A balance that reads 0.6 g when empty has a systematic offset. Zeroing or recalibrating it is required before interpreting measurements.

Sampling rate is not sample size

A motion sensor recording 100 values in one run gives many data points, but it is still one experimental run. Independent repeats provide stronger replicability evidence.

Record uncertainty honestly

If a ruler is marked in millimetres, reporting a length as 12.347826 cm implies precision the instrument did not provide.

Common misconceptions and fixes
  • Precise means accurate: Precision describes closeness or resolution; accuracy is closeness to the accepted or true value.
  • More decimal places mean better data: Reported precision must match the measuring instrument and method.
  • A large file means a large sample: Many readings from one run may not be independent observations.
  • Human error explains every anomaly: Name the specific mechanism: parallax, timing delay, inconsistent endpoint, calibration or another plausible source.
Step-by-step inquiry method

Match equipment to purpose

Select range, sensitivity and resolution appropriate to expected values.

Calibrate and standardise

Check zero/reference points and use the same measurement technique.

Collect independent evidence

Use an appropriate number of samples or repeated trials, not just a high digital sampling rate.

Record with units and limits

Store raw values, units, relevant uncertainty and enough metadata for replication.

Apply and transfer

Two groups measure the period of a pendulum. One times a single swing with a phone stopwatch; the other times 20 swings and divides by 20. Compare the precision and likely sources of uncertainty.

Student check: answer using specific variables, evidence, method choices or limitations rather than generic phrases such as human error.
