AC9S10H02 • Year 10 Science

Science and Technology Advance Together

New tools create new observations, while scientific understanding enables new technologies and engineering.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain science and technology advance together using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect scientific question, technology, new data.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Technologies extend observation, measurement and analysis.
  • Scientific knowledge informs engineering design.
  • Science–technology relationships are iterative and include limitations.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not describe technological progress as automatic or value-free.
  • Do not claim bigger data alone produces better explanations.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Identify the limitation
    Use the shared model to foreground scientific question.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving scientific question.
  2. 2. Trace the new tool
    Use the shared model to foreground technology.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving technology.
  3. 3. Explain the new evidence
    Use the shared model to foreground new data.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving new data.
  4. 4. Follow the application
    Use the shared model to foreground new explanation.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving new explanation.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

Fast computing makes large DNA data sets and radio-astronomy signals analysable.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Examine: scientific question

examining karyotypes and applications of gene technologies, such as gene therapy and genetic engineering and biotechnologies used to produce therapeutic proteins

Use karyotypes and applications of gene technologies to make the role of scientific question visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E2: Recognise: technology

recognising that the development of fast computers has made possible the analysis of DNA sequencing, radio astronomy signals and other data generated by major international science projects such as the Event Horizon Telescope, Large Hadron Collider, the Laser Interferometer Gravitational-Wave Observatory and the Square Kilometre Array

Use the development of fast computers has made possible to make the role of technology visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E3: Consider: new data

considering how computer modelling has improved knowledge and predictability of phenomena such as climate change and atmospheric pollution

Use how computer modelling has improved knowledge and predictability to make the role of new data visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E4: Investigate: new explanation

investigating how satellites generate global data including ocean temperatures, sea levels and forest and ice cover and examining how that data is used to evaluate the effects of climate change

Use how satellites generate global data to make the role of new explanation visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E5: Research: engineering application

researching how an understanding of the way DNA stores data has been applied to DNA bar coding to accelerate the pace of research in fields such as chemical engineering, materials science and nanotechnology

Use how an understanding of the way DNA stores data to make the role of engineering application visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E6: Examine: scientific question

examining how the recent use of female crash test dummies has shown women are at greater risk of injury in a car accident and considering implications for changing car safety features

Use how the recent use of female crash test dummies to make the role of scientific question visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E7: Explore: technology

exploring how the development of new materials and thin films has led to better computer chips and solar cells

Use how the development of new materials and thin films to make the role of technology visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E8: Investigate: new data

investigating how the development of superstrong, lighter alloys has enabled engineers to improve structural components in building, transportation and industry and to design products such as improved protective armour for police and soldiers

Use how the development of superstrong, lighter alloys has enabled to make the role of new data visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

Common misconceptions
  • Technology is only applied science.
  • A more advanced instrument removes uncertainty.
