AC9S10I07 • Year 10 Science

Argue from Multiple Lines of Evidence

Strong scientific arguments synthesise credible evidence, address alternatives and observe ethical and cultural protocols.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain argue from multiple lines of evidence using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect claim, evidence set, source credibility.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Arguments synthesise multiple credible evidence lines.
  • Reasoning addresses alternatives and limitations.
  • Secondary evidence is accessed and cited ethically, including cultural protocols.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not use culturally restricted information without permission.
  • Do not hide counter-evidence or exceed the evidence boundary.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Define the claim
    Use the shared model to foreground claim.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving claim.
  2. 2. Triangulate evidence
    Use the shared model to foreground evidence set.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving evidence set.
  3. 3. Address alternatives
    Use the shared model to foreground source credibility.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving source credibility.
  4. 4. State a bounded conclusion
    Use the shared model to foreground reasoning and rebuttal.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving reasoning and rebuttal.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

Claim: The universe has expanded from an early hot, dense state.
