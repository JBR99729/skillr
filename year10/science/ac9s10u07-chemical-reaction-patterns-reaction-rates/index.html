Chemical Reaction Patterns and Reaction Rates

Recognise recurring reaction patterns, represent reactions while conserving atoms, and explain rate changes using collision frequency and successful-collision conditions.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
What you need to know

Synthesis: simpler reactants combine. Decomposition: one compound breaks into simpler products. Displacement: an element replaces another in a compound when chemically favourable. Balanced equations conserve each type of atom. Reaction rate changes with temperature, concentration, surface area and catalysts because these alter collision frequency/effectiveness.

Chemistry cheat sheet

Patterns

A+B→AB; AB→A+B; A+BC→AC+B.

Balance

Change coefficients, never subscripts, to conserve atoms.

Faster rate

Higher temperature/concentration, larger surface area, suitable catalyst.

Catalyst

Provides lower-activation-energy pathway; changes rate, not overall amount predicted by limiting reactants.

Key worked examples

Synthesis

2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO. Two reactants form one compound type; coefficients conserve Mg and O atoms.

Decomposition

2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂. One compound produces simpler products.

Displacement

Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu: zinc displaces copper under suitable reactivity conditions.

Balancing

H₂ + O₂ → H₂O becomes 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O; changing subscripts would describe different substances.

Rate factors

Higher temperature, concentration and surface area generally increase successful collision frequency; a catalyst provides a lower-activation-energy pathway.

Cultural chemistry context

First Nations Australian chemical processes should be taught from specific authoritative cultural and scientific sources, with safe and appropriate detail.

Australian Curriculum v9.0 and elaborations

AC9S10U07: identify patterns in synthesis, decomposition and displacement reactions and investigate factors affecting reaction rates.

  • Represent reaction types using models, word equations and balanced symbolic equations.
  • Identify reaction type and predict products using chemical evidence.
  • Investigate temperature, concentration, surface area and catalysts.
  • Explore useful chemical processes, including culturally authoritative First Nations contexts.
