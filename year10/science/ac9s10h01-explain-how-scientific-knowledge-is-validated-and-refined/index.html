AC9S10H01 • Year 10 Science

How Science Is Validated and Refined

Publication, scrutiny, replication and converging evidence strengthen—or revise—scientific claims.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain how science is validated and refined using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect research question, method and data, publication.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Publication makes methods and findings available for scrutiny.
  • Peer review evaluates quality but does not guarantee correctness.
  • Replication and converging evidence validate, refine or challenge knowledge.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not present peer review as a truth certificate.
  • Do not equate one non-replication automatically with fraud.

Common misconceptions
  • Peer review proves a paper is true.
  • Consensus is a vote detached from evidence.
