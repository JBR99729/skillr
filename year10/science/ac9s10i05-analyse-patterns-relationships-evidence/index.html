AC9S10I05 • Year 10 Science

Analyse Patterns and Relationships in Evidence

Scientific analysis connects multiple sources of data and information to identify trends, patterns, relationships and anomalies. Conclusions should distinguish what the evidence shows from what is inferred and consider alternative explanations.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
Core concepts
  • Inspect units, variables, sample size and data quality before interpreting.
  • Describe trends, clusters, differences, relationships and anomalies before proposing causes.
  • Connect independent evidence sources where possible.
  • Correlation alone does not establish causation; consider mechanisms and confounding variables.
  • Investigate anomalies before excluding them.
Worked examples

Trend

If reaction rate rises consistently as temperature rises across repeated trials, first describe the direction and consistency of the pattern, then use collision theory to interpret it.

Anomaly

A point far from the trend may reflect measurement error, natural variation or a real additional factor. Check method and context before deciding whether it should remain.

Multiple lines of evidence

A climate conclusion is stronger when long-term temperature records, satellite observations and physical models support the same broad pattern.

Common mistakes
  • Treating every pattern as meaningful.
  • Deleting anomalies automatically.
  • Claiming causation from correlation alone.
  • Explaining a pattern before accurately describing the evidence.
Australian and international curriculum mapping

Australian Curriculum v9.0: AC9S10I05 — analyse and connect data and information to identify and explain patterns, trends, relationships and anomalies and draw conclusions.

Victoria: closest alignment is Levels 9–10 Science Inquiry skills involving analysis of patterns, relationships, anomalies and evidence-based conclusions.

NSW: closest alignment is Stage 5 Science Working Scientifically outcomes involving processing and analysing data and information and drawing evidence-based conclusions.

United States: closest alignment is NGSS Science and Engineering Practices, particularly Analysing and Interpreting Data and Constructing Explanations.

England: closest alignment is GCSE Working Scientifically, including presenting observations, identifying patterns and interpreting results.

New Zealand: closest alignment is the Nature of Science strand, especially Investigating in Science.

Canada: closest alignment is provincial secondary Science inquiry expectations for analysing evidence and identifying relationships; exact wording varies by province.

These are planning comparisons, not exact one-to-one equivalences.
