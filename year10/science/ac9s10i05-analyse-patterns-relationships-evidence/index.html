WORKED EXAMPLE

An overall rise can include shorter-term falls

These selected 1990–2005 records combine NOAA annual Mauna Loa CO₂ means and NASA annual global land–ocean temperature anomalies, using the stored 2026 source snapshot. Temperature anomalies are differences from the 1951–1980 reference average. Over the 15 elapsed years, CO₂ increases by 25.53 ppm and temperature anomaly by 0.23 °C. Yet temperature falls in some individual years while CO₂ rises. Matching calendar years makes comparison meaningful, but the station CO₂ measure and global temperature index remain different quantities and methods.

Learn the skill

1. Align the definitions before comparing

Check variable, unit, time window, place, baseline, sampling and processing. Join the same calendar year, and keep ppm separate from °C. Two agency names do not by themselves establish wholly independent experiments, and repeated displays of one dataset add no new observations. A temperature anomaly is a reference offset, not necessarily an erroneous point.

A student proposes averaging the CO₂ and temperature numbers into one “climate score” without defining a model or transformation.
Try it · Why is that inappropriate here?

The numbers represent different physical quantities and units. Compare their patterns using labelled representations and relevant scientific reasoning; an unexplained arithmetic average has no defined physical meaning.

2. Quantify first, then connect a mechanism

State direction, magnitude and time span before proposing an explanation. Greenhouse gases absorb and emit infrared radiation, influencing energy loss and storage under otherwise comparable conditions. Physical mechanisms and wider evidence support causal interpretation; these short annual series alone do not isolate all influences or estimate climate sensitivity.

Endpoint changes: +25.53 ppm and +0.23 °C from 1990 to 2005.
Try it · What are the endpoint rates, and what do they not establish?

25.53/15 = 1.702 ppm per year and 0.23/15 ≈ 0.0153 °C per year. They describe these endpoints, not fitted trends, future forecasts or a causal conversion factor between CO₂ and temperature.

3. Keep inconvenient values and investigate context

From 1991 to 1992, CO₂ rises 0.84 ppm while anomaly falls 0.19 °C; from 1998 to 1999, the changes are +1.70 ppm and −0.23 °C. Check definitions and records before exclusion. Historical volcanic aerosols and ocean–atmosphere variability are relevant context, but this table alone cannot assign the whole change to one cause.

A proposed graph removes 1992 and 1999 because they do not match an expected yearly rise.
Try it · How should the analysis be repaired?

Retain those records, explain the overall and shorter-term patterns, and investigate plausible influences and measurement context. NOAA’s reported uncertainty definition must not be transferred to temperature; this NASA table supplies no numerical annual uncertainty for invented error bars.

Common mistakes

“A positive relationship establishes its cause.” Use appropriate design, mechanism and wider evidence to examine causation.

“Anomaly always means measurement mistake.” In climate, anomaly commonly means difference from a reference average.

“A conflicting year should be deleted.” Investigate and explain it while preserving the raw record.

10-MINUTE ACTIVITY

Build a combined evidence explanation

Calculate the endpoint changes and one annual contrast. Write one sentence describing the observation, one explaining a relevant mechanism and one limiting the causal claim. Identify the units, baseline and spatial scope of both sources.

Adjust the challenge Support: align years and label units first. Core: distinguish a pattern from its explanation. Extend: explain why differently scaled dual axes can make visual agreement look stronger than it is.

Use the supplied dated data. No local weather observation, physical experiment or forecast is required; paper or a checked spreadsheet can support the comparison.

Three tricky questions

1. How many elapsed years separate 16 annual rows from 1990 through 2005?

15 years. The number of records and the elapsed interval are different.

2. Does 0.68 °C in 2005 mean the global absolute temperature was 0.68 °C?

No. It is an anomaly relative to the stated 1951–1980 reference average.

3. Do the cooler years alone refute greenhouse physics?

No. They show that annual temperature responds to multiple influences; evaluate the mechanism and broader evidence rather than expecting a one-variable yearly rule.

FAQ

Why use separate panels?

A shared year axis preserves timing while separate labelled vertical axes keep the different quantities and units clear.

What would justify excluding a value?

A documented, relevant reason such as confirmed transcription or measurement failure, with the original retained and the decision made transparent—not mere disagreement with a preferred pattern.

More teaching examples and curriculum coverage
Learning goals

Analysis explains patterns, relationships and anomalies by comparing data sources and scientific models.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Comparable data can reveal converging patterns or conflicts.
  • Anomalies and uncertainty affect interpretation.
  • Explanations connect data patterns to scientific mechanisms or models.
Prerequisite knowledge

Students should read graphs and tables, distinguish correlation from causation, calculate simple summary statistics and recognise an anomalous value.

Key concept

Learning sequence: Describe each data set → Align variables and scales → Find convergence or conflict → Explain with a model

Worked examples

Worked reasoning model

  1. Fossil sequence, homologous structures and DNA similarity show related patterns among species.
  2. No single line is complete, but convergence supports common ancestry.
  3. Conflicts prompt checks of dating, sampling or the evolutionary model rather than silent removal.
Common misconceptions
  • Different sources can be averaged immediately: Check units, definitions, sampling and comparability first.
  • An anomaly invalidates the whole data set: Test its cause and influence on the relationship.

Keep the Year 10 boundary: Do not combine incompatible data without transformation or caveat. Do not infer cause from pattern alone without design or mechanism.

Guided practice
  1. Triangulate three evolution or climate evidence cards.
  2. Build one conclusion and one uncertainty statement.

Work through the sequence Describe each data set → Align variables and scales → Find convergence or conflict → Explain with a model, explaining the evidence or mechanism at each step before moving on.

Three evidence cards from the accepted dated source records: (1) NOAA Mauna Loa annual CO₂ rises from 354.45 ppm in 1990 to 379.98 ppm in 2005. (2) NASA global temperature anomaly rises from +0.45 to +0.68 °C over those endpoints, relative to 1951–1980; 1991→1992 falls +0.41 to +0.22 while CO₂ rises 355.70→356.54 ppm. (3) The greenhouse explanation connects infrared absorption/emission to energy balance, while other influences affect individual years. Records use the November 2026 saved snapshot and may be revised.

Check the supplied case

Combine the overall increases with the greenhouse mechanism, but retain the annual counterexample and distinguish station CO₂ from global temperature. These cards provide different information, not three wholly independent experiments or full quantitative attribution. Preserve source/date/units/baseline, and do not invent temperature error bars.

Independent practice
  1. Distinguish a pattern, a trend, a relationship and an anomaly using a short example for each.
  2. A scatter plot shows a positive association. Explain why this does not by itself establish causation.
  3. Identify one plausible measurement cause and one genuine scientific cause of an anomalous data point.
  4. Explain when repeating a measurement is preferable to immediately excluding an anomalous value.
Reasoning and problem-solving

A dataset has an overall trend but one clear anomaly. Explain three possible responses: keep it, repeat the measurement, or exclude it, and state what evidence would justify each decision.

Reasoning standard: state the claim, use relevant evidence or a scientific mechanism, and explain why the evidence supports the conclusion without overclaiming.

Fictional seedling record, same labelled specimen and height reference: days 1–5 give 2, 4, 1, 8, 10 cm. No measurement fault or damage is yet documented.

Check the supplied case

Day 3 is unusual relative to the surrounding increase. Keep and flag it while checking specimen ID, height reference, transcription, measurement and possible real damage/change. Repeat a relevant measurement where possible. Exclude only for a documented reason while preserving the original and decision; do not simply replace 1 cm with a preferred value.

Questions and answers
What is the central idea?
Analysis explains patterns, relationships and anomalies by comparing data sources and scientific models.
What should a strong response do?
Use the sequence Describe each data set → Align variables and scales → Find convergence or conflict → Explain with a model. Connect the evidence, mechanism or data to the claim.
What common trap should I avoid?
Different sources can be averaged immediately: Check units, definitions, sampling and comparability first.
Practice and review
  1. A seedling’s heights on days 1–5 were 2, 4, 1, 8 and 10 cm. Analyse the trend and identify the anomaly. Describe the trend, quantify it where possible, investigate the anomaly and state a justified conclusion.
  2. Two datasets show similar averages but different spread. Explain how that difference could change the scientific interpretation.
  3. A media claim says one variable causes another because the two rise together. Evaluate the claim using the distinction between association, mechanism and controlled evidence.

Review hint: Use precise scientific vocabulary, show the relevant mechanism or evidence, and state any limitation or uncertainty when the evidence does not justify a stronger claim.

Teacher and parent guidance

For teachers

An anomaly is not permission to delete a point. Students need a reason linked to method, measurement or evidence before excluding data.

For parents and carers

Ask your child to point out the main pattern in a graph and then identify any value that does not fit, explaining what they would check next.
