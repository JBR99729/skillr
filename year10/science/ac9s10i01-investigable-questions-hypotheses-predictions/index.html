AC9S10I01 • Year 10 Science

Develop Investigable Questions, Hypotheses and Predictions

Strong scientific questions identify measurable relationships. A hypothesis proposes an evidence-based explanation, and a prediction states what should be observed if that explanation is supported.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
Core concept

Observation → investigable question → hypothesis → prediction → evidence.

  • A question identifies measurable variables or an observable relationship.
  • A hypothesis proposes a scientific explanation or mechanism.
  • A prediction states the expected result if the hypothesis is supported.
Worked examples

Force and acceleration

Observation: a trolley accelerates more when pulling force increases. Question: How does net force affect acceleration when mass is kept constant? Hypothesis: greater net force produces greater acceleration for the same mass. Prediction: doubling net force should increase measured acceleration.

Correlation and causation

If hotter days are associated with greater electricity use, the data show a relationship. A causal hypothesis still needs a plausible mechanism and a design that rules out alternatives.

Common misconceptions
  • A prediction is not the same as a hypothesis.
  • Not every investigable question must be causal; descriptive and correlational questions can also be scientific.
  • Vague words such as “better” or “effective” must be replaced with measurable quantities.
  • One supported prediction does not prove that no alternative explanation exists.
