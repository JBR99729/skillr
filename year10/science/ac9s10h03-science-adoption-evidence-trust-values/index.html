Why Society Adopts Scientific Knowledge and Practices

Evidence quality matters, but adoption also depends on perceived benefit and risk, trust, values, policy, access, cost and participation. Adoption itself does not prove scientific validity.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
Core idea

Ask two separate questions: How strong is the scientific evidence? and Why is the practice accepted or rejected? Adoption can vary with benefit, risk, trust, values, policy, access and cost.

Important examples
  • GM crops: evidence, economics, regulation, environmental concerns and market access.
  • Helmet evidence: compare injury rates fairly and consider exposure/confounding factors.
  • Genetic screening: benefits, privacy, discrimination, uncertainty and access.
  • Climate models: controversy can coexist with strong scientific evidence.
  • Citizen science: participation, local relevance and data-quality protocols.
  • First Nations ecological knowledges: collaboration requires governance, authority and cultural protocols.
