AC9S10U03 • Year 10 Science

Evidence for an Expanding Universe

The Big Bang model explains an evolving universe and is supported by converging astronomical evidence.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain evidence for an expanding universe using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect early hot dense universe, expansion, galaxy and star formation.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • The universe has changed from an early hot, dense state.
  • Expansion, cosmic microwave background radiation and light-element abundance support the model.
  • Models and technologies connect observations to cosmic history.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not describe expansion as ordinary motion from a centre through pre-existing space.
  • Do not present current unknowns such as dark matter as proof the whole theory fails.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Establish scale and time

  2. 2. Model expansion
    Use the shared model to foreground expansion.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving expansion.
  3. 3. Examine evidence
    Use the shared model to foreground galaxy and star formation.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving galaxy and star formation.
  4. 4. Evaluate the theory
    Use the shared model to foreground redshift.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving redshift.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

Light from many distant galaxies is redshifted.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Explain: early hot dense universe

describing the major components of the universe using appropriate scientific terminology and units including astronomical units, scientific notation and light-years

Use the major components of the universe using appropriate scientific to make the role of early hot dense universe visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E2: Build: expansion

constructing a timeline to show major changes in the universe which are thought to have occurred from the Big Bang until the formation of the major components such as stars and galaxies

Use a timeline to show major changes in the universe to make the role of expansion visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E3: Examine: galaxy and star formation

examining how stars’ light spectra and brightness is used to identify compositional elements of stars, their movements and their distances from Earth

Use how stars’ light spectra and brightness is used to make the role of galaxy and star formation visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E4: Explain: redshift

explaining how each different type of evidence, such as cosmic microwave background radiation, red or blue shift of galaxies, Edwin Hubble’s observations and proportion of matter in the universe, provides support for the acceptance of the big bang theory

Use how each different type of evidence to make the role of redshift visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E5: Research: cosmic microwave background

researching First Nations Australians’ knowledges of celestial bodies and explanations of the origin of the universe

Use First Nations Australians’ knowledges of celestial bodies and explanations to make the role of cosmic microwave background visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E6: Identify: early hot dense universe

identifying the different technologies used to collect astronomical data and the types of data collected

Use the different technologies used to collect astronomical data to make the role of early hot dense universe visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E7: Explore: expansion

exploring recent advances in astronomy, including the Australian Square Kilometre Array Pathfinder, and astrophysics, such as the discovery of gravitational waves, dark matter and dark energy; and identifying new knowledge which has emerged

Use recent advances in astronomy, to make the role of expansion visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

Common misconceptions
  • The Big Bang was an explosion at one location.
  • A scientific theory is an untested guess.
