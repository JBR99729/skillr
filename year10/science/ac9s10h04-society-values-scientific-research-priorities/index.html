AC9S10H04: How Society Shapes Scientific Research Priorities

Research agendas are shaped by needs, values, funding, ethics, infrastructure and power. Those influences affect which questions are investigated without automatically determining whether the resulting evidence is valid.

LearnTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
Learning goals: What you will learn

Needs, values, funding, ethics and power influence which scientific questions receive attention.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Societal needs and values influence research focus.
  • Funding and infrastructure enable some questions and constrain others.
  • Priority decisions have benefits, ethical dimensions and opportunity costs.
Prerequisite knowledge

Students should know that research has limited time, funding and people, and that societies make choices about which problems receive attention.

Concept teaching

Learning sequence: Identify the need → Locate influence → Trace the funding choice → Evaluate consequences

Core teaching

  • Societal needs and values influence research focus.
  • Funding and infrastructure enable some questions and constrain others.
  • Priority decisions have benefits, ethical dimensions and opportunity costs.

Key vocabulary and ideas

  • societal need
  • value or concern
  • funding and capacity
  • research question
  • benefit and opportunity cost
Worked and modelled examples

Worked reasoning model

  1. Climate risk can direct funding toward modelling, mitigation materials and adaptation.
  2. Government, community and commercial values influence targets and timeframes.
  3. Research choices should be examined for benefit, equity and neglected needs.
Common misconceptions and corrections
  • Scientists freely choose research without constraints: Map funding, infrastructure, ethics, regulation and community need.
  • Funded research is automatically biased or invalid: Separate influence on topic choice from evaluation of method and evidence.

Keep the Year 10 boundary: Do not imply all funding influence invalidates findings. Do not treat ‘society’ as one group with one value set.

Guided practice
  1. Compare two calls for research funding.
  2. Highlight whose needs are visible, whose are missing and what evidence guides priority.

Work through the sequence Identify the need → Locate influence → Trace the funding choice → Evaluate consequences, explaining the evidence or mechanism at each step before moving on.

Independent practice
  1. Give two examples of societal needs that could legitimately influence research priorities.
  2. Explain how limited funding creates trade-offs between research programs.
  3. Distinguish a research priority from a scientific conclusion.
  4. Explain how equity can be relevant when deciding which research questions receive attention.
Reasoning and problem-solving

A government can fund only one of three research programs. Build a transparent decision framework that considers scientific value, urgency, equity, cost and potential benefit.

Reasoning standard: state the claim, use relevant evidence or a scientific mechanism, and explain why the evidence supports the conclusion without overclaiming.

Important questions and answers
What is the central idea?
Needs, values, funding, ethics and power influence which scientific questions receive attention.
What should a strong response do?
Use the sequence Identify the need → Locate influence → Trace the funding choice → Evaluate consequences. Connect the evidence, mechanism or data to the claim.
What common trap should I avoid?
Scientists freely choose research without constraints: Map funding, infrastructure, ethics, regulation and community need.
Assessment-style questions and review hints
  1. A funding body must choose between research on a rare disease, renewable-energy storage and drought resilience. Construct and justify a transparent decision framework.
  2. Analyse how urgency, expected benefit, cost, public values and equity could influence the focus of scientific research.
  3. Evaluate the claim: “If society influences research priorities, the science cannot be objective.” Distinguish priority-setting from evidence evaluation.

Review hint: Use precise scientific vocabulary, show the relevant mechanism or evidence, and state any limitation or uncertainty when the evidence does not justify a stronger claim.

Exit ticket and mastery check
  • I can explain and apply: Societal needs and values influence research focus.
  • I can explain and apply: Funding and infrastructure enable some questions and constrain others.
  • I can explain and apply: Priority decisions have benefits, ethical dimensions and opportunity costs.

Exit ticket: Explain the core idea in 3–5 sentences and apply it to one new example without copying the worked model.

Teacher and parent guidance

For teachers

Do not frame social influence as automatically corrupting science. The curriculum target is to examine how legitimate needs, values and constraints shape research priorities.

For parents and carers

Ask your child why some diseases, technologies or environmental problems receive more research funding than others.
