AC9S10U07 • Year 10 Science

Reaction Patterns and Reaction Rate

Reaction types show recurring rearrangements, while collision conditions affect how quickly reactions occur.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain reaction patterns and reaction rate using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect synthesis, decomposition, displacement.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Synthesis, decomposition and displacement reactions have recognisable reactant–product patterns.
  • Balanced representations conserve atoms.
  • Rate depends on the frequency of successful collisions and can be changed by key factors.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not introduce equilibrium kinetics or rate laws as the target.
  • Do not confuse faster reaction with greater final yield.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Recognise the pattern
    Use the shared model to foreground synthesis.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving synthesis.
  2. 2. Represent the reaction
    Use the shared model to foreground decomposition.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving decomposition.
  3. 3. Model collisions
    Use the shared model to foreground displacement.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving displacement.
  4. 4. Test one rate factor
    Use the shared model to foreground collision model.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving collision model.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

Powdered calcium carbonate reacts faster than equal-mass chips with acid.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Define: synthesis

defining and representing synthesis, decomposition and displacement reactions using a variety of formats such as molecular models, diagrams, and word and balanced symbolic equations

Use representing synthesis, decomposition and displacement reactions using a variety to make the role of synthesis visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E2: Identify: decomposition

identifying reaction type and predicting the products

Use reaction type and predicting the products to make the role of decomposition visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E3: Investigate: displacement

investigating synthesis reactions such as reaction of metals with oxygen, formation of water and sodium chloride; decomposition reactions such as those used to extract metals; and displacement reactions such as metal and acid, neutralisation and precipitation

Use synthesis reactions to make the role of displacement visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E4: Investigate: collision model

investigating the effect of a range of factors, such as temperature, concentration, surface area and catalysts, on the rate of chemical reactions

Use the effect of a range of factors to make the role of collision model visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E5: Investigate: temperature, concentration, surface area and catalyst

investigating chemical reactions employed by First Nations Australians in the production of substances such as acids and ethanol

Use chemical reactions employed by First Nations Australians to make the role of temperature, concentration, surface area and catalyst visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E6: Investigate: synthesis

investigating some of the chemical reactions and methods employed by First Nations Australians to convert toxic plants into edible food products

Use some of the chemical reactions and methods employed to make the role of synthesis visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E7: Examine: decomposition

examining reactions that are used to produce a range of useful products

Use reactions that are used to produce a range to make the role of decomposition visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

Common misconceptions
  • A catalyst increases the amount of product.
  • Hotter particles become larger.
