AC9S10I08 • Year 10 Science

Create Effective Scientific Texts

Content, language, structure and digital features are selected to communicate evidence accurately to an identified audience.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain create effective scientific texts using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect purpose, audience, evidence selection.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Purpose and audience guide content and form.
  • Scientific representations and conventions remain accurate.
  • Editing checks evidence, uncertainty, accessibility and ethical attribution.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not simplify by removing essential uncertainty or units.
  • Do not use visual drama that exaggerates effects.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Define audience and purpose
    Use the shared model to foreground purpose.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving purpose.
  2. 2. Select the evidence
    Use the shared model to foreground audience.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving audience.
  3. 3. Design the text
    Use the shared model to foreground evidence selection.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving evidence selection.
  4. 4. Review accuracy and impact
    Use the shared model to foreground language and structure.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving language and structure.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

A policy brief on school-zone speed uses force and stopping-distance evidence, a concise graph and a qualified recommendation.
