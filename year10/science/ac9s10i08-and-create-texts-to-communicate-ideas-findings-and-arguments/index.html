Communicate Scientific Ideas, Findings and Arguments

What students learn — Core concepts and success criteria

Scientific communication selects language, structure, data displays and level of detail for a particular audience and purpose. It must distinguish evidence from interpretation, represent uncertainty honestly and acknowledge sources and limitations.

What students need to know

  • Scientific communication selects language, structure, data displays and level of detail for a particular audience and purpose. It must distinguish evidence from interpretation, represent uncertainty honestly and acknowledge sources and limitations.

Success looks like: students can apply the inquiry skill to a new context, justify methodological choices and match the strength of a conclusion to the evidence.

Worked examples

Adapt to audience without changing meaning

A technical report may use 95% confidence interval; a public summary can explain the same uncertainty in plain language without pretending the estimate is exact.

Integrate a figure with prose

Do not insert a graph without interpretation. State the pattern the figure supports, refer to the variables and explain why it matters to the argument.

Distinguish result from conclusion

Result: the treated group had a lower mean value. Conclusion: under these conditions, the treatment is associated with a reduction; further work is needed before generalising.

Cite evidence and limitations

A persuasive scientific text still identifies data sources, uncertainties, assumptions and competing explanations rather than hiding them.

Common misconceptions and fixes
  • Simplifying means removing uncertainty: Audience-friendly language must preserve the strength and limits of the evidence.
  • A graph speaks for itself: Every representation needs context, labels and interpretation.
  • Scientific writing must be passive and complicated: Clarity and precision matter more than unnecessarily complex wording.
  • Communication happens after reasoning is finished: Choosing what to show can expose gaps in evidence and improve the analysis itself.
Step-by-step inquiry method

Define audience and purpose

Decide what the audience needs to know and what background can be assumed.

Select evidence and representations

Choose relevant data, figures, models or examples and label them accurately.

Build a logical explanation

Structure claim, evidence, reasoning, limitations and implications so the argument can be followed.

Review for accuracy and accessibility

Check terminology, units, source attribution, uncertainty, visual integrity and whether simplification changed the scientific meaning.

Apply and transfer

Rewrite a technical finding about a 12% increase with a wide uncertainty range for a community newsletter. Preserve the uncertainty and avoid overstating the result.

Student check: answer using specific variables, evidence, method choices or limitations rather than generic phrases such as human error.
