Important questionsWith answers
What is the central idea?
Content, language, structure and digital features are selected to communicate evidence accurately to an identified audience.
What should a strong response do?
Use the sequence Define audience and purpose → Select the evidence → Design the text → Review accuracy and impact. Connect the evidence, mechanism or data to the claim.
What common trap should I avoid?
Audience adaptation permits changing the conclusion: Change explanation depth and form, not the supported meaning.
Assessment and reviewReady for practice
  • Audience adaptation permits changing the conclusion: Change explanation depth and form, not the supported meaning.
  • More technical vocabulary is more scientific: Prefer precise language the audience can interpret.

Keep the Year 10 boundary: Do not simplify by removing essential uncertainty or units. Do not use visual drama that exaggerates effects.

  1. Communicate the same scientific finding to a Year 10 science class and to a general community audience. Explain and justify at least four differences between the two versions.
  2. Create a concise evidence-based explanation of an unfamiliar dataset for a specified audience, selecting appropriate language, content and representation.
  3. Evaluate a science communication product that is accurate but poorly matched to its audience. Recommend changes while identifying information that must remain scientifically unchanged.

Review hint: Use precise scientific vocabulary, show the relevant mechanism or evidence, and state any limitation or uncertainty when the evidence does not justify a stronger claim.
