Newton’s Laws Quantify Motion

Net force changes motion, and force, mass and acceleration are linked quantitatively by F = ma.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain newton’s laws quantify motion using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect inertia, net force, action–reaction pair.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Newton’s laws relate force interactions to changes in motion.
  • Net force is the vector sum of forces on one object.
  • F = ma supports quantitative prediction and analysis.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not mix forces acting on different objects in one net-force calculation.
  • Do not treat speed and acceleration as interchangeable.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Describe the motion
    Use the shared model to foreground inertia.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving inertia.
  2. 2. Identify all forces
    Use the shared model to foreground net force.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving net force.
  3. 3. Find net force
    Use the shared model to foreground action–reaction pair.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving action–reaction pair.
  4. 4. Quantify acceleration
    Use the shared model to foreground F = ma.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving F = ma.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

A 1200 kg car experiences 3600 N net force forward.

Common misconceptions
  • A moving object needs a forward net force to keep moving.
  • Action and reaction forces cancel.
