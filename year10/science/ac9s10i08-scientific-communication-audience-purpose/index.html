AC9S10I08: Communicate Scientific Ideas, Findings and Arguments

Scientific communication selects language, structure, data displays and level of detail for a particular audience and purpose. It must distinguish evidence from interpretation, represent uncertainty honestly and acknowledge sources and limitations.

LearnClassroom ViewHomeworkPracticeTest
Learning goals: What you will learn

Content, language, structure and digital features are selected to communicate evidence accurately to an identified audience.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Purpose and audience guide content and form.
  • Scientific representations and conventions remain accurate.
  • Editing checks evidence, uncertainty, accessibility and ethical attribution.
Prerequisite knowledge

Students should structure a scientific explanation or argument, select relevant evidence and understand that purpose and audience determine language, detail and text features.

Concept teaching

Learning sequence: Define audience and purpose → Select the evidence → Design the text → Review accuracy and impact

Core teaching

Worked and modelled examples

Worked reasoning model

  1. A policy brief on school-zone speed uses force and stopping-distance evidence, a concise graph and a qualified recommendation.
  2. A technical appendix supplies method and uncertainty.
  3. Layered communication serves decision-makers without hiding the scientific basis.
Common misconceptions and corrections
  • Audience adaptation permits changing the conclusion: Change explanation depth and form, not the supported meaning.
  • More technical vocabulary is more scientific: Prefer precise language the audience can interpret.

Keep the Year 10 boundary: Do not simplify by removing essential uncertainty or units. Do not use visual drama that exaggerates effects.

Guided practice
  1. Draft a 60-word policy brief and choose one data visual.
  2. Peers audit precision, accessibility and overclaiming.

Work through the sequence Define audience and purpose → Select the evidence → Design the text → Review accuracy and impact, explaining the evidence or mechanism at each step before moving on.

Independent practice
  1. Explain how audience and purpose affect vocabulary, detail and text structure in scientific communication.
  2. Rewrite a technical scientific sentence for a general community audience without changing its scientific meaning.
  3. Choose an appropriate text or visual feature for communicating a trend in data and justify the choice.
  4. Identify two features that make a scientific argument clear and credible for a school assessment audience.
Reasoning and problem-solving

Communicate the same scientific finding to a Year 10 class and to a local community audience. Explain what information, vocabulary and visual/text features you would change and what must remain scientifically accurate.

Reasoning standard: state the claim, use relevant evidence or a scientific mechanism, and explain why the evidence supports the conclusion without overclaiming.

Important questions and answers
What is the central idea?
Content, language, structure and digital features are selected to communicate evidence accurately to an identified audience.
What should a strong response do?
Use the sequence Define audience and purpose → Select the evidence → Design the text → Review accuracy and impact. Connect the evidence, mechanism or data to the claim.
What common trap should I avoid?
Audience adaptation permits changing the conclusion: Change explanation depth and form, not the supported meaning.
Assessment-style questions and review hints
  1. Communicate the same scientific finding to a Year 10 science class and to a general community audience. Explain and justify at least four differences between the two versions.
  2. Create a concise evidence-based explanation of an unfamiliar dataset for a specified audience, selecting appropriate language, content and representation.
  3. Evaluate a science communication product that is accurate but poorly matched to its audience. Recommend changes while identifying information that must remain scientifically unchanged.

Review hint: Use precise scientific vocabulary, show the relevant mechanism or evidence, and state any limitation or uncertainty when the evidence does not justify a stronger claim.

Exit ticket and mastery check
  • I can explain and apply: Purpose and audience guide content and form.
  • I can explain and apply: Scientific representations and conventions remain accurate.
  • I can explain and apply: Editing checks evidence, uncertainty, accessibility and ethical attribution.

Exit ticket: Explain the core idea in 3–5 sentences and apply it to one new example without copying the worked model.

Teacher and parent guidance

For teachers

Assess communication choices as well as correctness. Students should justify why a representation, level of detail and vocabulary suit the intended audience and purpose.

For parents and carers

Ask your child to explain one science idea twice: first to you in plain language, then as a formal school response. Compare what changes and what scientific meaning must stay the same.
