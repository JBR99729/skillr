Evaluate Validity, Reproducibility and Evidence Quality

Evaluation asks whether a method actually tested the intended question, whether another investigator could reproduce it, and whether the evidence is accurate, precise, representative and sufficient for the conclusion being claimed.

What students learn — Core concepts and success criteria

Evaluation asks whether a method actually tested the intended question, whether another investigator could reproduce it, and whether the evidence is accurate, precise, representative and sufficient for the conclusion being claimed.

What students need to know

  • Evaluation asks whether a method actually tested the intended question, whether another investigator could reproduce it, and whether the evidence is accurate, precise, representative and sufficient for the conclusion being claimed.

Success looks like: students can apply the inquiry skill to a new context, justify methodological choices and match the strength of a conclusion to the evidence.

Worked examples

Validity check

If a plant-growth experiment changes both fertiliser dose and light exposure, the effect of fertiliser cannot be isolated confidently.

Reproducibility check

A method that says heat gently until ready is difficult to reproduce because the endpoint and heating conditions are undefined.

Accuracy versus precision

Measurements can cluster tightly around the wrong value if an instrument is systematically miscalibrated.

Evidence sufficiency

A conclusion about an entire population based on one convenient sample may be precise for that sample but not representative.

Common misconceptions and fixes
  • Reliable = valid: A procedure can give similar results repeatedly while consistently measuring the wrong thing.
  • One replication proves reproducibility: Reproducibility is supported by independent repetition under clearly reported conditions, not guaranteed by a single success.
  • Uncertainty makes data useless: Uncertainty is part of measurement and should be quantified or discussed, not hidden.
  • Evaluation means listing human error: Name specific sources, explain their likely direction or effect and propose realistic improvements.
Step-by-step inquiry method

Audit alignment

Does the method test the question and control the main competing variables?

Audit reproducibility

Could another investigator reproduce the procedure from the information given?

Audit measurement quality

Consider calibration, resolution, sample size, bias, random variation and uncertainty.

Judge the conclusion

Decide whether the strength and scope of the claim match the evidence and state the most important limitation.

Apply and transfer

A study reports a 3% difference between treatments, but measuring uncertainty is ±4%. Evaluate whether the evidence supports a meaningful difference and what should be improved.

Student check: answer using specific variables, evidence, method choices or limitations rather than generic phrases such as human error.
