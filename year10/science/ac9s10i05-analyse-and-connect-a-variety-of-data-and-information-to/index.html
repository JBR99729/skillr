AC9S10I05 • Year 10 Science

Connect Evidence Across Data Sets

Analysis explains patterns, relationships and anomalies by comparing data sources and scientific models.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain connect evidence across data sets using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect source A, source B, shared pattern.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Comparable data can reveal converging patterns or conflicts.
  • Anomalies and uncertainty affect interpretation.
  • Explanations connect data patterns to scientific mechanisms or models.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not combine incompatible data without transformation or caveat.
  • Do not infer cause from pattern alone without design or mechanism.
Teaching progression
  1. 1. Describe each data set
    Use the shared model to foreground source A.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving source A.
  2. 2. Align variables and scales
    Use the shared model to foreground source B.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving source B.
  3. 3. Find convergence or conflict
    Use the shared model to foreground shared pattern.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving shared pattern.
  4. 4. Explain with a model
    Use the shared model to foreground anomaly.
    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving anomaly.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

Fossil sequence, homologous structures and DNA similarity show related patterns among species.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Identify: source A

identifying similar trends and patterns in data from different sources such as homologous structures and fossil evidence

Use similar trends and patterns in data from different sources to make the role of source A visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E2: Analyse: source B

analysing data regarding the distribution of species in time and space to identify patterns and relationships between organisms

Use data regarding the distribution of species in time to make the role of source B visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E3: Explore: shared pattern

exploring relationships between variables using spreadsheets, databases, tables, charts, graphs and statistics to make reasoned predictions about global climate change

Use relationships between variables using spreadsheets, databases, tables, charts, graphs to make the role of shared pattern visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E4: Explore: anomaly

representing speed and acceleration data from investigations or simulations in tables and graphs and comparing how these facilitate the identification of relationships

Use representing speed and acceleration data from investigations or simulations to make the role of anomaly visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E5: Explore: model-based explanation

exploring how different interpretations can be made from data that is organised or processed in different ways, and the implications of this for data analysis

Use how different interpretations can be made from data that to make the role of model-based explanation visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

Common misconceptions
  • Different sources can be averaged immediately.
  • An anomaly invalidates the whole data set.
