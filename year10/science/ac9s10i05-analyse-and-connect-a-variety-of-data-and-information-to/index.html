AC9S10I05 • Year 10 Science

Analyse Patterns and Relationships in Evidence

Scientific analysis connects multiple sources of data and information to identify trends, patterns, relationships and anomalies. Conclusions should distinguish what the evidence shows from what is inferred and consider whether alternative explanations remain plausible.

What students learn — Core concepts and success criteria

Scientific analysis connects multiple sources of data and information to identify trends, patterns, relationships and anomalies. Conclusions should distinguish what the evidence shows from what is inferred and consider whether alternative explanations remain plausible.

What students need to know

  • Scientific analysis connects multiple sources of data and information to identify trends, patterns, relationships and anomalies. Conclusions should distinguish what the evidence shows from what is inferred and consider whether alternative explanations remain plausible.

Success looks like: students can apply the inquiry skill to a new context, justify methodological choices and match the strength of a conclusion to the evidence.

Worked examples

Identify a trend

If reaction rate rises consistently as temperature rises across repeated trials, describe the direction and strength of the pattern before explaining a mechanism.

Treat anomalies as evidence to investigate

One point far from the trend may reflect measurement error, natural variation or a real additional factor. Check method and context before excluding it.

Connect evidence sources

A climate conclusion is stronger when long-term temperature records, satellite observations and physical models support the same broad pattern.

Avoid causal overreach

A correlation between two variables does not by itself prove that one causes the other; confounders and mechanism must be considered.

Common misconceptions and fixes
  • Every pattern is meaningful: Patterns can arise by chance, bias or confounding, especially with small samples.
  • An anomaly should always be deleted: Investigate its cause and justify inclusion or exclusion transparently.
  • Correlation proves causation: Causal claims need design, mechanism and exclusion of credible alternatives.
  • Analysis begins with an explanation: Describe the evidence pattern first, then use scientific reasoning to interpret it.
Step-by-step inquiry method

Inspect the evidence

Check units, variables, sample size and data quality before calculating or interpreting.

Describe patterns precisely

Identify trends, clusters, differences, relationships and anomalies without adding unsupported causes.

Connect evidence and models

Use scientific knowledge to explain the pattern and compare evidence sources.

Test alternatives

Ask what other explanation, bias or limitation could produce the same evidence.

Apply and transfer

A dataset shows a strong negative relationship between exercise time and resting heart rate. State what can be concluded from the pattern alone and what extra evidence would be needed for a causal claim.

Student check: answer using specific variables, evidence, method choices or limitations rather than generic phrases such as human error.
