Construct Evidence-Based Scientific Arguments

A scientific argument links a clear claim to relevant evidence and scientific reasoning, evaluates competing explanations and acknowledges limitations. Strong arguments remain open to revision when better evidence becomes available.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
Core argument structure

Claim → Evidence → Reasoning → Competing explanations → Limitations → Qualified conclusion.

  • State a claim precisely and keep its scope within the evidence.
  • Select evidence that is relevant, independent and high quality.
  • Explain why the evidence supports the claim using scientific concepts or models.
  • Address credible counterevidence rather than ignoring it.
  • Use confidence language that matches uncertainty and limitations.
Worked examples

Claim–evidence–reasoning

Claim: increasing temperature increased reaction rate. Evidence: mean reaction time decreased across all tested temperatures. Reasoning: greater particle kinetic energy increases the frequency of successful collisions.

Competing explanations

If two mechanisms can explain the same pattern, identify what each predicts and what evidence would discriminate between them.

Counterevidence

Evidence that does not fit the preferred explanation should be investigated because it may reveal a limitation, another factor or a better model.

Common mistakes
  • Treating a scientific argument as an opinion.
  • Assuming more evidence automatically means stronger evidence.
  • Excluding counterevidence because it weakens a preferred explanation.
  • Using certainty language when the evidence supports only a tentative conclusion.
Australian and international curriculum mapping

Australian Curriculum v9.0: AC9S10I07 — construct arguments based on analysis of a variety of evidence to support conclusions or evaluate claims.

Victoria: closest alignment is Levels 9–10 Science Inquiry skills involving evidence-based explanations, argumentation and evaluation of competing claims.

NSW: closest alignment is Stage 5 Science Working Scientifically outcomes involving drawing conclusions, evaluating evidence and communicating evidence-based arguments.

United States: closest alignment is NGSS Science and Engineering Practice Engaging in Argument from Evidence, supported by Analysing and Interpreting Data and Constructing Explanations.

England: closest alignment is GCSE Working Scientifically, particularly interpreting evidence, evaluating claims and presenting reasoned conclusions.

New Zealand: closest alignment is the Nature of Science strand, including Communicating in Science and Understanding about Science.

Canada: closest alignment is provincial secondary Science inquiry expectations for evidence-based explanations, argument and evaluation; exact wording varies by province.

These are planning comparisons, not exact one-to-one equivalences.
