AC9S10I04 • Year 10 Science

Represent Data Without Distortion

Tables, graphs, statistics, models and equations should reveal the relevant relationship and communicate uncertainty honestly.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

We are learning to explain represent data without distortion using models and evidence.

Success criteria

  • Identify and connect data purpose, table or graph, descriptive statistic.
  • Use a model to explain rather than only describe the concept.
  • Apply the central idea to a curriculum example and justify the connection.
  • Evaluate one model limit, misconception or evidence boundary.
Concept boundary and prerequisites

Must teach

  • Representation choice is purpose- and data-dependent.
  • Conventions and scale affect interpretation.
  • Statistics and models summarise evidence but have limitations.

Prerequisites

  • Read a labelled scientific diagram or data display.
  • Distinguish an observation from an explanation.
  • Use evidence to support a short scientific statement.

Do not overteach

  • Do not use visual effects that distort magnitude.
  • Do not report statistics without units, context or distribution where relevant.
Teaching progression

  4. 4. Test interpretation

    Students: Annotate or explain the link involving model or equation.
Worked examples

Observe → connect → conclude

A force–acceleration graph tests F = ma for constant mass.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

E1: Use: data purpose

using spreadsheet software to carry out mathematical analyses of data

Use spreadsheet software to carry out mathematical analyses of data to make the role of data purpose visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E2: Explain: table or graph

describing sample properties such as mean, median, range and large gaps visible on a graph to make generalisations, acknowledging uncertainties and the effects of outliers

Use table or graph in context to make the role of table or graph visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E3: Consider: descriptive statistic

considering how data or information can be organised and represented to effectively communicate support for conclusions, including through visual or interactive models

Use how data or information can be organised and represented to make the role of descriptive statistic visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E4: Consider: model or equation

considering how the scales used for representing data affect interpretation of the data

Use how the scales used for representing data affect interpretation to make the role of model or equation visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E5: Evaluate: scale and limitation

evaluating the merits and limitations of time-lapse visual representations of changes in polar ice coverage with a mathematical representation

Use the merits and limitations of time-lapse visual representations to make the role of scale and limitation visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

E6: Compare: data purpose

comparing merits and limitations of patterns as represented by the periodic table with graphical representations of patterns such as melting point or boiling point, and with consideration of anomalies

Use merits and limitations of patterns as represented to make the role of data purpose visible and connect the evidence to the unit’s central explanation.

Common misconceptions
  • The representation with the most features is best.
  • Changing an axis only changes appearance.
