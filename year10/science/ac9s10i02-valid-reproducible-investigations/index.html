AC9S10I02 • Year 10 Science

Design Valid, Reproducible Investigations

A valid investigation tests the intended relationship while controlling important alternative causes. A reproducible method is transparent enough for another investigator to repeat, and it must also satisfy safety, ethical and cultural responsibilities.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesHomeworkPracticeTest
Core concepts

Valid investigations align question, variables and method. Reproducibility requires clear operational details, equipment settings, sampling and decision rules.

  • Control or account for confounding variables.
  • Use repeats to reduce random variation, not to hide systematic bias.
  • Plan hazards, ethics and environmental impacts before data collection.
  • Respect permissions and protocols when working with Country/Place, heritage sites or cultural knowledge.
Worked examples

Control a confounder

To test light intensity and photosynthesis, keep plant species, temperature, carbon dioxide availability and measurement time consistent while varying light intensity.

Repeats do not fix bias

Repeating a miscalibrated sensor 50 times can produce a consistent but systematically wrong result. Calibration must be corrected first.

Ethical and cultural responsibility

Animal investigations should minimise disturbance and follow animal-care requirements. Investigations involving First Nations heritage or knowledge require appropriate authority, permissions and protocols before access, recording or publication.
