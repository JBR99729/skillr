AC9M2N01

Numbers to 1000: Recognise, Represent and Order Numbers

This Year 2 mathematics unit develops students’ understanding of the number system by connecting spoken numbers, written numerals, number lines, hundreds charts and concrete place-value representations.

Students practise reading, writing, representing, comparing and ordering two-, three- and four-digit numbers. Activities include finding missing numbers, investigating number patterns and grouping real collections into ones, tens and hundreds.

Australian Curriculum achievement focus: recognise, represent and order numbers to at least 1000 using physical and virtual materials, numerals and number lines.

Year 2 Learning Goals

By the end of the unit, students should be able to:

Recognise numbers

Identify two-, three- and four-digit numerals, including numbers containing zero placeholders.

Represent numbers

Model numbers using physical materials, virtual manipulatives and groups of ones, tens and hundreds.

Order numbers

Arrange numbers from smallest to largest or largest to smallest using place-value knowledge.

Use number lines

Locate numbers, identify intervals and complete missing-number sequences.

Use hundreds charts

Position puzzle pieces and recognise horizontal and vertical number patterns.

Read and write numbers

Connect spoken number names with written numerals and compare similar-looking numbers.

How to Teach Numbers to 1000

Begin with physical materials before moving to drawings, numerals and abstract number-line tasks. Encourage students to explain how each representation shows the same number.

  1. Build the number. Use counters, bundling sticks, base-ten blocks, bottle tops or virtual place-value materials.
  2. Record the place value. Separate the collection into hundreds, tens and ones, then write the corresponding numeral.
  3. Locate the number. Place the number on a number line or identify its location in a hundreds chart.
  4. Compare and order. Compare the highest place value first before checking the remaining digits.
  5. Check understanding. Finish with a short worksheet question or exit ticket.

Concrete materials

Use ring pulls, bread tags, bottle tops, counters or blocks to create meaningful large collections.

Numbers containing zero

Explicitly compare numbers such as 608, 680 and 806 so students understand the role of zero.

Similar-looking numbers

Ask students to read and order numbers such as 808, 880, 818 and 881.

Explain the pattern

Require students to describe how they identified an interval or located a missing chart piece.

Assessment Opportunities

These activities can be used for diagnostic assessment, guided practice, independent work, homework or end-of-unit review.

Look for evidence that students can:

  • read and write numbers accurately;
  • identify the value of each digit;
  • complete number lines using the correct interval;
  • locate missing positions on hundreds charts;
  • represent numbers using hundreds, tens and ones;
  • compare and order numbers using place-value reasoning;
  • explain why one number is greater than another.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should Year 2 students know about numbers to 1000?

Students should recognise, represent, read, write, compare and order numbers to at least 1000 using numerals, number lines, hundreds charts and physical or virtual materials.

What curriculum code do these worksheets cover?

The resources align with Australian Curriculum Version 9.0 Mathematics code AC9M2N01 and elaborations AC9M2N01_E1, AC9M2N01_E2, AC9M2N01_E3 and AC9M2N01_E4.

Why are hundreds charts useful?

Hundreds charts help students see that numbers increase by one across each row and by ten down each column. This makes missing-number patterns easier to recognise.

How can the exit ticket be used?

Give the exit ticket during the final five minutes of a lesson to check whether students can identify missing numbers and understand number-line intervals.
