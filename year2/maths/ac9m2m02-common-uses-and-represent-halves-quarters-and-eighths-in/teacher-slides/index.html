CurriculumStart here

A fraction describes equal parts of one whole. Before naming a half, quarter or eighth, first decide what the whole is and check that it has been divided into equal parts.

Learning sequence: Whole → split equally → count equal parts → name the fraction → connect it to a real situation.

  • Half: 2 equal parts make one whole.
  • Quarter: 4 equal parts make one whole.
  • Eighth: 8 equal parts make one whole.

Fold and prove: fold paper into halves, then quarters, then eighths. Open it and check that all parts are equal.

Concept modelTeach from the board

1. Is this really one half?

A sandwich is cut into 2 pieces, but one piece is much larger. The pieces are not halves because halves must be equal in size.

2. Halving again

Cut one whole into 2 equal halves. Halve each half again: now there are 4 equal quarters. Halve every quarter again: now there are 8 equal eighths.

3. Measuring cup

If 2 half-cups fill one cup, each half-cup represents one half of the whole cup. Four quarter-cups fill the same whole cup.

4. Position

The halfway point of a race is a position that divides the full race distance into 2 equal lengths.

5. Time

Half an hour is half of 60 minutes, so it is 30 minutes. A quarter of an hour is one of 4 equal parts of 60 minutes, so it is 15 minutes.

Important questionsWith answers
  • Always identify the whole first.
  • Fraction parts must be equal.
  • 2 halves = 1 whole.
  • 4 quarters = 1 whole.
  • 8 eighths = 1 whole.
  • Half an hour = 30 minutes.
  • Quarter of an hour = 15 minutes.

Quick check

  1. How many quarters make one whole?
  2. Why are two unequal pieces not halves?
  3. Which is smaller for the same whole: one quarter or one eighth?
  4. How many minutes are in a quarter of an hour?
  5. Give one example of a fraction used to describe a position or event.
