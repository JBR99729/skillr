Learn the skill

Three connected steps through repeated halving

Start with equal halves, then halve again. Read the explanation, make the model, try an example and explain before moving on.

Words to use: whole, equal parts, half, quarter, eighth, halve. Have paper strips, counters, playdough and suitable measuring equipment ready for the practical activities.

1. Recognise, make and describe halves

Learning intention: I can make two equal parts and explain why one part is a half.

The whole is the complete object, length, collection or amount you start with. A half is one of two equal parts of that whole. Two pieces are halves only when each is the same amount.

Worked example — share a collection: The whole is 18 counters. Deal one counter at a time into two groups. Each group has 9 counters, so each is one-half of the original collection. Check: 9 and 9 make all 18, with none left over.

Make and check: Fold a paper rectangle so the ends meet, then match the two parts. For a length, compare the lengths; for a paper shape, compare the areas. Different shapes can have equal areas.

Try another material: Divide playdough into two equal masses and check with a balance. Share water into two equal volumes and compare in matching cups or with the same measure. Water height alone in differently shaped cups does not prove equality. An adult should set up and observe these practical tasks.

Watch for this: Two unequal pieces are not halves. Half a collection means half of the entire set, not two objects or half of every object. Half of 10 counters is 5; half of 18 is 9. Each answer depends on its own whole.

Try it: Make two equal groups from 14 counters. How many are in one-half? Show how you know. If 6 counters are half of a collection, how many are in the whole?

Check the answer

7 counters in each half: 7 and 7 make 14. A whole with a half of 6 has 12 counters: two matching groups of 6.

2. Make quarters by halving halves

Learning intention: I can halve both halves to make four equal parts of the same whole.

A quarter is one of four equal parts. Make halves first, then halve each half. A quarter is half of a half of the original whole.

Worked example — fold a strip:

  1. Fold a paper strip in half, matching the ends.
  2. Fold the folded strip in half again. Before opening it, predict how many equal parts you will see.
  3. Open it and check: four equal lengths cover the whole strip. Each is one-quarter, written 1/4.

Connect the parts: Two quarters make a half; four quarters rebuild the whole. Keep an unfolded matching strip beside your folds so the original whole stays clear.

Watch for this: Halving only one of the two halves leaves a half and two quarters. There are quarter-sized pieces, but the whole has not been divided into four equal parts. A quarter also does not always mean four objects: it means one of four equal shares of the stated whole.

Try it: Make quarters of a fresh strip using repeated halving. Show one-quarter, then put two quarters together. What fraction of the original strip do they make?

Check the answer

Halve the strip, then halve both halves. Each of the four equal parts is one-quarter. Two quarters make one-half of the original strip. Check the lengths by matching them.

3. Make eighths by halving quarters

Learning intention: I can halve each quarter to make eight equal parts and explain the connections.

An eighth is one of eight equal parts. Start with the same whole: make halves, halve both halves to make quarters, then halve every quarter. An eighth is half of a quarter of the original whole.

Worked example — halve again: Fold a strip into quarters. Fold once more before opening it. Predict eight equal lengths, then open and check every part. Name one part one-eighth, written 1/8. Two eighths cover one quarter; four eighths cover one half; eight eighths rebuild the whole.

Compare fairly: For matching wholes, one-eighth is smaller than one-quarter. The 8 names the number of equal parts, not the size of one part.

Watch for this: Eight unequal pieces do not show eighths. Halving just one quarter creates two eighth-sized pieces, but the remaining quarters are still larger. To divide the entire whole into eighths, halve all four quarters.

Try it: Take a whole strip. Show halves, quarters and eighths through repeated halving. Explain: half of a half is ___; half of a quarter is ___. How many eighths rebuild one quarter?

Check the answer

Half of a half is one-quarter; half of a quarter is one-eighth, always referring to the original whole. Two eighths rebuild one quarter. Check that the final strip has eight equal lengths with no gaps or unused part.

Exit check: Ask the learner to make a half of a collection, construct quarters and eighths, and explain why the parts are equal. If they can name a fraction but cannot make or justify it, return to folding, matching or equal sharing.

An adult can read directions aloud and accept spoken, signed, pointed, drawn or scribed explanations. Begin with pre-folded parts for support; progress to an unmarked whole. For a further challenge, ask the learner to rebuild the whole from one supplied quarter or eighth. Keep all activities within halves, quarters and eighths. Observe actual folding, balancing or pouring when checking practical skills.
