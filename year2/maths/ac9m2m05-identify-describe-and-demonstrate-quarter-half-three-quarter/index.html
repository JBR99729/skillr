Learn the skill

Small steps, clear examples

Open one focus at a time. Explain the model, try the question and check the reasoning.

1. Amount, direction and starting position

Keep a fixed centre and mark the start facing. One, two, three and four equal quarter-steps make a quarter, half, three-quarter and full turn. Clockwise follows clock hands; anticlockwise is the reverse. Trace the swept path as well as the finish.

Try it: Does finishing as you started prove a full turn?

Check the answer

No. You might have stayed still. A full turn needs evidence of one complete revolution.

2. Everyday objects and clock-hand turns

Mark one feature on a dial or toy, then show the start, directed arc and finish. Track just one clock hand as a turn model: starting at 3, a clockwise quarter turn reaches 6, a half turn reaches 9 and a full turn returns to 3.

Try it: From ↑, a clockwise three-quarter turn ends at ←. What anticlockwise turn has the same finish?

Check the answer

A quarter turn anticlockwise. The finish matches, but the amount and direction differ.

3. Demonstrate body turns and robot routes

Face a marked direction and perform each named turn. For a robot, a turn changes facing in place; forward moves in its current facing direction. Give a starting square and facing, then test a short instruction sequence. Use the mover’s perspective for left and right.

Try it: A robot faces right. It turns a quarter anticlockwise, then moves forward one square. What changes?

Check the answer

It faces up after the turn, then moves one square up. Turning alone does not move it to another square.

Try it together
  1. Body-turn check: stand facing one wall and demonstrate quarter, half, three-quarter and full turns.
  2. Clock connection: trace the minute hand from 12 to 3, 6, 9 and back to 12 and name each amount of turn.
  3. Arrow cards: draw a start arrow and predict the final direction before physically turning the card.
  4. Robot route: give a partner a short sequence of forward moves and turns and check the final facing direction.
  5. Everyday hunt: identify taps, handles, knobs and doors that rotate and describe the turn used.

Support: Use real objects, fewer choices and an adult read-aloud. Core: Explain and record the model independently. Extend: Create a new example or repair an error while staying within this skill.

Teaching tips and common mistakes
  • A full turn means moving forward. No: a turn changes orientation; a full turn finishes facing the start direction.
  • Quarter and three-quarter turns are interchangeable. They may sometimes end facing the same way if the turn direction changes, but they are different amounts of rotation.
  • Direction does not matter. For quarter and three-quarter turns, clockwise and anticlockwise usually produce different final directions.
  • Half turn means halfway across a path. Here “half” refers to half of a full rotation, not distance travelled.

Ask the learner to explain what each mark, unit or instruction means. Rebuild the model when an answer is correct by guessing. A written answer alone does not establish practical or digital performance.

Curriculum code and learning goal

AC9M2M05: identify, describe and demonstrate quarter, half, three-quarter and full measures of turn in everyday situations.

  • E1: identify turning objects and represent amount/direction of turn
  • E2: give and follow movement instructions using turns
  • E3: connect clock-hand movement with quarter, half and full turns and clockwise/anticlockwise language
  • E4: use turns in routes, grids and robot programming
