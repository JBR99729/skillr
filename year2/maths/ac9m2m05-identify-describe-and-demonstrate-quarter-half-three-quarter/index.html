Quarter, half, three-quarter and full turns

Describe rotation by how much an object turns and which direction it turns.

Step 1 · Learn the idea

A turn changes the direction an object faces. The amount of turn and the direction of turn are both important.

Learning sequence: Start direction → choose clockwise/anticlockwise → make the turn → name the amount → check final direction.

  • Quarter turn: 1 of 4 equal parts of a full turn.
  • Half turn: 2 quarter turns; faces the opposite direction.
  • Three-quarter turn: 3 quarter turns.
  • Full turn: 4 quarter turns; faces the starting direction again.
Essential turn model
Start ↑¼ clockwise →½ clockwise ↓¾ clockwise ←Full turn ↑

Clockwise follows the direction of clock hands. Anticlockwise goes the opposite way. A three-quarter turn one way reaches the same final direction as a quarter turn the other way, but the amount and direction of turn are different.

Worked examples

1. Quarter turn

Start facing north (↑). Make a quarter turn clockwise. You face east (→).

2. Half turn

Start facing east (→). A half turn ends facing west (←), regardless of whether the half turn is clockwise or anticlockwise.

3. Three-quarter turn

Start facing north (↑). Three quarter-turns clockwise lead to west (←).

4. Full turn

Start facing south (↓). After a full turn you face south again. You rotated; you did not move to a new position.

5. Robot instruction

A robot faces east. “Quarter turn anticlockwise” makes it face north. The instruction needs both the amount and the direction.

Guided activities
  1. Body-turn check: stand facing one wall and demonstrate quarter, half, three-quarter and full turns.
  2. Clock connection: trace the minute hand from 12 to 3, 6, 9 and back to 12 and name each amount of turn.
  3. Arrow cards: draw a start arrow and predict the final direction before physically turning the card.
  4. Robot route: give a partner a short sequence of forward moves and turns and check the final facing direction.
  5. Everyday hunt: identify taps, handles, knobs and doors that rotate and describe the turn used.
Common misconceptions
  • A full turn means moving forward. No: a turn changes orientation; a full turn finishes facing the start direction.
  • Quarter and three-quarter turns are interchangeable. They may sometimes end facing the same way if the turn direction changes, but they are different amounts of rotation.
  • Direction does not matter. For quarter and three-quarter turns, clockwise and anticlockwise usually produce different final directions.
  • Half turn means halfway across a path. Here “half” refers to half of a full rotation, not distance travelled.
Revision Notes
  • ¼ turn = one quarter-turn.
  • ½ turn = two quarter-turns.
  • ¾ turn = three quarter-turns.
  • Full turn = four quarter-turns.
  • Clockwise follows clock hands; anticlockwise is opposite.
  • Always identify the starting direction before deciding the final direction.

Quick check

  1. Facing north, where do you face after a quarter turn clockwise?
  2. What turn makes you face the opposite direction?
  3. Why does a full turn end facing the starting direction?
  4. Facing east, where do you face after a quarter turn anticlockwise?
Curriculum coverage and elaborations

AC9M2M05: identify, describe and demonstrate quarter, half, three-quarter and full measures of turn in everyday situations.

  • E1: identify turning objects and represent amount/direction of turn
  • E2: give and follow movement instructions using turns
  • E3: connect clock-hand movement with quarter, half and full turns and clockwise/anticlockwise language
  • E4: use turns in routes, grids and robot programming
