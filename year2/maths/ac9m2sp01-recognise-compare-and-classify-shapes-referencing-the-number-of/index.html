Compare and classify 2D shapes

Learning intention: Compare straight and curved boundaries, side counts, opposite sides and parallel sides.

Practice on app ↗Help with gaps ↓10-minute activity ↓

WORKED EXAMPLE

Worked example

The triangle has three straight sides; the circle has a curved boundary.

Learn the skill

1. Trace boundaries and classify by sides

Trace a closed boundary from a marked starting point. Count each straight side once. A triangle has three straight sides; a quadrilateral has four. Curved boundaries do not become straight sides because they resemble a familiar object.

Try it · A closed shape has five straight sides. What is it?

A pentagon.

2. Equal, opposite and parallel sides

Equal describes length. Opposite describes sides across from one another, rather than adjacent sides that share a corner. Parallel straight lines stay the same distance apart and would not meet if extended. Parallel pairs may be tilted; equal length alone does not establish parallelism.

Try it · Which sides of a rectangle are parallel?

The two opposite sides in each pair.

3. Create, move and change shapes

Sort cut-outs using a stated feature rule and explain alternatives. On a court sketch, distinguish outer straight sidelines from an internal centre circle. In a simple drawing tool, create a regular polygon, then move the whole shape and drag one vertex separately. Discuss which properties change. Regular means equal sides and equal corners; equal sides alone are not sufficient.

Try it · Does turning a triangle change its side count?

No. It still has three straight sides.

Common mistakes

Counting a corner as a side. Trace each straight edge between two corners and count each edge once.

10-MINUTE HOME ACTIVITY

Sort shapes by their sides

Draw or cut out triangles, squares, rectangles and pentagons in different orientations. Sort by side count, then find shapes with opposite sides that are parallel.

Adjust the challenge: Start with triangles and rectangles. Extend by drawing two different shapes with four sides.

FAQ

What do all quadrilaterals have in common?

They have four straight sides.

Learn the skill

Small steps, clear examples

Open one focus at a time. Explain the model, try the question and check the reasoning.

1. Trace boundaries and classify by sides

Trace a closed boundary from a marked starting point. Count each straight side once. A triangle has three straight sides; a quadrilateral has four. Curved boundaries do not become straight sides because they resemble a familiar object.

Try it: Which outlines can go in the “four straight sides” group?

Check the answer

The rectangle. The triangle has three straight sides; the circle has a curved boundary.

2. Equal, opposite and parallel sides

Equal describes length. Opposite describes sides across from one another, rather than adjacent sides that share a corner. Parallel straight lines stay the same distance apart and would not meet if extended. Parallel pairs may be tilted; equal length alone does not establish parallelism.

Try it: A square is turned onto a corner. Has it lost its four equal sides or parallel opposite pairs?

Check the answer

No. Turning the whole shape changes its position, not those features. It remains a square.

3. Create, move and change shapes

Sort cut-outs using a stated feature rule and explain alternatives. On a court sketch, distinguish outer straight sidelines from an internal centre circle. In a simple drawing tool, create a regular polygon, then move the whole shape and drag one vertex separately. Discuss which properties change. Regular means equal sides and equal corners; equal sides alone are not sufficient.

Try it: Does dragging one corner of a four-sided shape always change its side count?

Check the answer

No. It can remain a closed four-sided shape while lengths or parallel pairs change. Inspect the resulting boundary.

Teaching tips and common mistakes
  • Naming by appearance only — Count sides and describe features.
  • Rotated shapes look new — Turning a shape does not change its features.
  • Curved/straight mixed — Trace the boundary to check.

Ask the learner to explain what each mark, unit or instruction means. Rebuild the model when an answer is correct by guessing. A written answer alone does not establish practical or digital performance.

Curriculum code and learning goal

The content description and elaborations below show the curriculum ideas taught in this unit. Items marked as teaching context support lesson planning.

  • Content description: recognise, compare and classify shapes, referencing the number of sides and using spatial terms such as “opposite”, “parallel”, “curved” and “straight”
  • E1: sorting a collection of shapes in different ways based on their features such as number of sides, whether all sides are equal, whether pairs of opposite sides are parallel; for example, collections of triangles and other polygons
  • E2: manipulating shapes and recognising that different orientations do not change the shape; for example, cutting out pictures of various shapes, recognising they are they are still classified as the same shape even if they are upside down or on their side
  • E3: investigating the shapes of different sporting fields, describing and labelling their features including side lines, centre circles and goal squares; for example, labelling the lines on a basketball court and using spatial terms to describe them
  • E4: creating regular shapes using digital tools, describing and observing what happens when you manipulate them; for example, dragging or pushing vertices to produce irregular shapes, moving or rotating a shape
