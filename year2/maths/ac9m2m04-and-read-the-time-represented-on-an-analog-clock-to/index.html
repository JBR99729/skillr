Read analog clocks (AC9M2M04)

Read analogue clocks to the hour, half-hour and quarter-hour. At quarter past 3, the minute hand points to 3 and the hour hand has moved a little beyond 3.

Learn the skill

Small steps, clear examples

Open one focus at a time. Explain the model, try the question and check the reasoning.

1. Whole hours and half past

The long minute hand measures minutes; the short hour hand moves gradually between hour numbers. One full minute-hand cycle is an hour. At o’clock the long hand points to 12. At half past it points to 6 and the short hand is halfway to the next hour.

Try it: Name the time and explain the short hand.

Check the answer

Half past 4, or 4:30. The hour hand is halfway between 4 and 5 because half of that hour has passed.

2. Quarter past and quarter to

A quarter-cycle from 12 reaches 3: 15 minutes past. Three quarters of the cycle reaches 9: 45 minutes past, leaving a quarter until the next hour. Read both hands before naming the hour.

Try it: Is this quarter to 5 or quarter to 6?

Check the answer

Quarter to 6 (5:45). The short hand is approaching 6; it has not reached it. Quarter to 5 would be 4:45.

3. Build, represent and check a clock

Place 12, 3, 6 and 9 first on a paper clock; space the remaining numbers evenly and attach two distinct hands. Show 9:15, read it back as quarter past 9 and place the hour hand just past 9. Then show 11:45 with the short hand nearly at 12. Draw or set both hands, rather than changing only the minute hand.

Try it: Can a clock face by itself tell you whether 7 o’clock is morning or evening?

Check the answer

No. The event or other context must supply that information.

Try it together

Support: Use real objects, fewer choices and an adult read-aloud. Core: Explain and record the model independently. Extend: Create a new example or repair an error while staying within this skill.

Teaching tips and common mistakes
  • Reading only the short hand: always read the long minute hand first.
  • Calling 2:45 “quarter to 2”: quarter to names the next hour, so it is quarter to 3.
  • Putting the hour hand exactly on 2 at half past 2: it should be halfway towards 3.
  • Thinking 3 means 3 minutes: each clock number is 5 minutes for the minute hand, so 3 means 15 minutes.
  • Mixing quarter past and quarter to: minute hand on 3 = quarter past; on 9 = quarter to.

Ask the learner to explain what each mark, unit or instruction means. Rebuild the model when an answer is correct by guessing. A written answer alone does not establish practical or digital performance.

Curriculum code and learning goal
  • Content description: recognise and read the time represented on an analog clock to the hour, half-hour and quarter-hour.
  • E1: build an analog clock, place the numbers and two hands, and explain what each hand measures.
  • E2: connect movement of the hands with duration, including the halfway position for half past.
  • E3: divide the clock face into halves and quarters and connect these parts to quarter past, half past and quarter to.
