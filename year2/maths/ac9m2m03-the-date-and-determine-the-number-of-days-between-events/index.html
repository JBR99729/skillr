Learn the skill

Small steps, clear examples

Open one focus at a time. Explain the model, try the question and check the reasoning.

1. Read a date and use calendar patterns

A date includes day, month and year. Check whether the calendar starts on Monday or Sunday. Dates increase by one across a week and by seven down a weekday column. Months have different lengths; February has 28 days, or 29 in a leap year.

MonTueWedThuFriSatSun
1234567
891011121314
15161718192021

This is the opening of June 2026. Wednesday in the second row is 10 June 2026.

Try it: What date is two weeks after 3 June 2026?

Check the answer

17 June 2026, also a Wednesday: add 14 days.

2. Count days between events and cross boundaries

For elapsed days, count moves after the starting date. From 27 April to 2 May: 28, 29, 30 April, 1, 2 May = 5 days. If a task asks for attendance on every date including both endpoints, it asks a different question: 6 attendance dates.

Try it: How many days pass from 30 December to 2 January?

Check the answer

3 days: 31 December, 1 January, 2 January.

3. Create, update and use an event calendar

Choose a real month and year; place 1 under the correct weekday and allow six date rows if needed. Add meaningful class or family events, check for clashes and revise changed dates. For days remaining in a year, add the remaining part of this month and the lengths of all following months. Use a calendar, since months are unequal.

Try it: After 28 November 2026, how many days remain until 31 December 2026?

Check the answer

2 remaining November days + 31 December days = 33 days. The starting date is excluded.

Try it together
  1. Calendar detective: find today's date, yesterday's date and tomorrow's date.
  2. Week jumps: mark a date, then locate 1 week before, 1 week after and 2 weeks after.
  3. Event countdown: mark a class event and count the jumps from today.
  4. Cross-month challenge: count from the final few days of one month into the next.
  5. Class calendar: record birthdays, excursions and assemblies and compare which events are closer together.

Support: Use real objects, fewer choices and an adult read-aloud. Core: Explain and record the model independently. Extend: Create a new example or repair an error while staying within this skill.

Teaching tips and common mistakes
  • Counting both the start and finish date. For elapsed days, count the jumps after the start date.
  • Confusing weekday with date. “Tuesday” is a day; “Tuesday 14 May” is a date.
  • Thinking every month has the same number of days. Check the actual calendar.
  • Restarting at zero when the month changes. Time continues across the boundary.

Ask the learner to explain what each mark, unit or instruction means. Rebuild the model when an answer is correct by guessing. A written answer alone does not establish practical or digital performance.

Curriculum code and learning goal

AC9M2M03: identify the date and determine the number of days between events using calendars.

  • E1: locate dates and determine dates 2 weeks before or after
  • E2: create and use a class calendar for meaningful events
  • E3: use addition and calendar structure to determine days remaining in a month/year
  • E4: locate significant school, community and cultural dates
