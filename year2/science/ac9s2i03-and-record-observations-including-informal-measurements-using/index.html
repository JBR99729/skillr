What students learn in AC9S2I03

Scientific observations describe what is noticed, while measurements add a quantity. Records should be clear enough for another person to understand what happened.

Learning sequence: Observe → Measure → Record → Label → Review

  • Observe carefully
  • Use informal measurements
  • Choose suitable tools
  • Record in tables
  • Separate observation from explanation

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

The content description and elaborations below show the curriculum ideas taught in this unit. Items marked as teaching context support lesson planning.

  • Content description: make and record observations, including informal measurements, using digital tools as appropriate
  • E1: recording observations through text, drawing, counts, digital photography or video
  • E2: engaging in a guided discussion about how to measure something in a fair way
  • E3: using familiar units of measurement such as cups, handspans, walking paces, blocks or pencil lengths
  • E4: representing informal measurements with concrete objects, such as drawing chalk lines and using lengths of string to measure shadows
  • E5: exploring how digital tools can be used to make observations, such as simple clap-o-meter apps that measure sound volume, time lapse digital photography for observing apparent movement of celestial objects or slow-motion videos for observing a vibrating ruler

Common misconceptions
  • Guessing instead of observing — Record what can be seen or measured first.
  • Changing units — Use identical units in one comparison.
  • Record lacks labels — Include item, unit and observation clearly.
