YEAR 2 SCIENCE · AC9S2I03

Observe, measure, record clearly

Learning intention: Make and record observations, including informal measurements. Choose appropriate labels and digital tools when useful.

WORKED EXAMPLE

The number needs a unit

In a supplied record, strip A exactly spans six identical square tiles placed end to end. Strip B spans four of those same tiles. Record the object and unit, rather than writing only 6 or 4. The illustration is contextual; these measurements come from the table.

Strip A6 tile lengthsIdentical tiles, no gaps or overlaps

Artwork provides context. Read the supplied records for exact counts and measurements; do not measure the illustration.

Learn the skill

1. Separate noticing from explaining

Record what can be observed. A possible cause needs additional evidence.

ObservationPossible explanation
The dough has a flat sideSomeone may have pressed it
Try it · Does a flat side prove exactly who pressed the dough?

No. Record the flat side; the person and cause are not established by that alone.

2. Keep the units consistent

Align the first unit with the start. Use equal-sized units with no gaps or overlaps and name the unit.

Clear recordUnclear record
Strip A: 6 tile lengthsStrip A: 6
Try it · Can two strips both measure 6 units but have different lengths?

Yes, if different-sized units were used.

3. Make the record understandable

A dated table, labelled drawing, photograph, audio or video can preserve observations. Choose a format that shows the information needed.

Digital-record check

A photograph shows the strip and units; its caption names the object, date and measurement.

Try it · Can a photograph with no scale reveal an exact length?

Not by itself. Include a suitable unit or an actual measurement record.

Common mistakes

“A guess is a measurement.” If the measurement is missing, measure again or mark it as not recorded.

“A larger unit must give a larger count.” For the same length, larger units generally fit fewer times.

10-MINUTE ACTIVITY

Make a labelled measurement record

2 minutes: choose a safe paper strip and identical large tiles with an adult. 5 minutes: measure, draw and label the object and unit. 3 minutes: ask a partner to explain the record; add any missing information.

Adjust the challenge Support: use a strip that spans whole tiles. Extend: explain how a different-sized unit could change the count without changing the strip.

Use age-appropriate units and adult-approved devices. Keep photographs focused on objects and avoid other people’s private information.

Three tricky questions

1. A tile is missing in the middle of the measuring line. Is the count a reliable length?

No. Close the gap and measure again.

2. The object is partly hidden in a photo. Should we invent its hidden measurement?

No. Take a clearer record or measure directly.

3. A strip ends between unit marks. Must we pretend it is a whole number?

No. Describe the in-between position or use a more suitable unit with guidance.

FAQ

Does an observation have to be written?

No. Labelled drawings, dictated records and suitable digital records can also communicate it.

What is an informal unit?

An everyday repeated unit, such as an identical tile, rather than a standard unit such as a centimetre.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

The content description and elaborations below show the curriculum ideas taught in this unit. Items marked as teaching context support lesson planning.

  • Content description: make and record observations, including informal measurements, using digital tools as appropriate
  • E1: recording observations through text, drawing, counts, digital photography or video
  • E2: engaging in a guided discussion about how to measure something in a fair way
  • E3: using familiar units of measurement such as cups, handspans, walking paces, blocks or pencil lengths
  • E4: representing informal measurements with concrete objects, such as drawing chalk lines and using lengths of string to measure shadows
  • E5: exploring how digital tools can be used to make observations, such as simple clap-o-meter apps that measure sound volume, time lapse digital photography for observing apparent movement of celestial objects or slow-motion videos for observing a vibrating ruler

Quick questions

  • Give one informal measurement unit.
  • Why keep units the same?
  • What labels belong in a record?

If an answer is uncertain, return to the model and explain what each part represents before calculating or drawing a conclusion.

Common misconceptions
  • Guessing instead of observing — Record what can be seen or measured first.
  • Changing units — Use identical units in one comparison.
  • Record lacks labels — Include item, unit and observation clearly.
