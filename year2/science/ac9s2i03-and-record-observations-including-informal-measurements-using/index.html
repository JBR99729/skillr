What students learn in AC9S2I03

This unit helps students build a clear, usable understanding of Make and record observations, including informal measurements, using digital tools as appropriate. The goal is not to memorise one answer pattern. Students should be able to explain the idea, recognise it in a new example and apply it in a short practice or worksheet task.

  • Start with the main idea: make and record observations, including informal measurements, using digital tools as appropriate.
  • Use concrete examples, pictures, oral explanation and short written responses before moving to independent practice.
  • recording observations through text, drawing, counts, digital photography or video engaging in a guided discussion about how to measure something in a fair way
  • Finish with a mixed activity so students must choose the correct strategy rather than copy the last example.
Curriculum coverage and elaborations

The content description and elaborations below show the curriculum ideas taught in this unit. Items marked as teaching context support lesson planning.

  • Content description: make and record observations, including informal measurements, using digital tools as appropriate
  • E1: recording observations through text, drawing, counts, digital photography or video
  • E2: engaging in a guided discussion about how to measure something in a fair way
  • E3: using familiar units of measurement such as cups, handspans, walking paces, blocks or pencil lengths
  • E4: representing informal measurements with concrete objects, such as drawing chalk lines and using lengths of string to measure shadows
  • E5: exploring how digital tools can be used to make observations, such as simple clap-o-meter apps that measure sound volume, time lapse digital photography for observing apparent movement of celestial objects or slow-motion videos for observing a vibrating ruler

Common mistakes to watch for
  • Describing what is seen without connecting it to a question, pattern or prediction.
  • Using everyday words only and missing simple scientific vocabulary.
  • Changing more than one thing in an investigation, which makes the comparison unfair.
