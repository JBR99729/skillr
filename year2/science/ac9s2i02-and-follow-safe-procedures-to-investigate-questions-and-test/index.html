YEAR 2 SCIENCE · AC9S2I02

Plan the steps. Check the risks.

Learning intention: Suggest and follow safe procedures that answer a question and test a prediction. Explain why the steps are useful.

WORKED EXAMPLE

A plan for comparing two gentle drum taps

Question: does the same drum sound different after a very light tap and a slightly firmer, comfortable tap? Predict first. An adult checks the drum, mallet and listening level. Keep the drum, mallet and listener’s place the same, change the tap, and record the observation.

OrderProcedurePurpose
1Agree on the question and predictionKnow what is being tested
2Adult checks equipment, space and volumeMake the investigation safe
3Compare two gentle taps; record and pack awayCollect relevant observations

Artwork provides context. Read the supplied records for exact counts and measurements; do not measure the illustration.

Learn the skill

1. Match the steps to the question

Choose observations that answer the question. Looking only at the drum’s colour does not compare its sounds.

Relevant observation

Listen for a difference in loudness after each safe tap.

Try it · Would weighing the mallet alone answer which tap sounds louder?

No. The plan needs observations of the sounds.

2. Check hazards before starting

An adult checks for damage, loose parts and comfortable sound levels. Stop if the sound is uncomfortable or equipment breaks.

ProblemSafer response
Drum too close to an earMove it away before testing
Damaged band or malletStop and tell an adult
Try it · Should a child keep increasing the volume until a difference is obvious?

No. Keep the comparison comfortable; use supplied observations if a safe demonstration is unclear.

3. Follow and record in order

Change the intended action while keeping other important conditions similar. A partner should be able to follow the same written or drawn steps.

Keep similarChange
Drum, mallet, place and listener’s positionGentle tap strength
Try it · Could moving the listener much farther away confuse the comparison?

Yes. The listening position changed as well as the tap.

Common mistakes

“A safe plan must answer the question automatically.” It must be safe and collect relevant evidence.

“We can write a prediction after the test.” Make and keep the prediction before observing the result.

10-MINUTE ACTIVITY

Improve a procedure card

2 minutes: read the example plan. 5 minutes: arrange question → prediction → safety check → compare → record cards. 3 minutes: identify one unsafe or unhelpful change and improve it.

Adjust the challenge Support: use five picture cards. Extend: add an instruction that helps a second group repeat the comparison.

Use comfortable sounds well away from ears. An adult supervises any real demonstration. Do not use loud alarms or headphones for testing.

Three tricky questions

1. The equipment is undamaged but the sound hurts. Should testing continue?

No. Stop immediately and tell the adult.

2. A friend skips the prediction step. Can it be honestly added as a before-test prediction later?

No. Record that it was not made before the test.

3. Can a labelled drawing make a plan clearer?

Yes. It can show the setup and positions, alongside the ordered steps.

FAQ

What is a procedure?

An ordered set of steps that someone can follow.

Can a child suggest a better step?

Yes. Discuss it with the adult and check safety before trying it.

What students learn in AC9S2I02

A good investigation follows clear steps, manages risks and makes a fair comparison. Students change one factor, keep other important conditions similar and record what happens.

Learning sequence: Question → Plan → Safety check → Fair test → Record

Success looks like

  • Suggest procedures
  • Identify risks
  • Follow safety rules
  • Plan fair comparisons
  • Record steps

Quick questions

  • What comes before testing?
  • What makes a comparison fair?
  • Name one safety step.
  • Why record the procedure?

If an answer is uncertain, return to the model and explain what each part represents before calculating or drawing a conclusion.

Common misconceptions
  • Starting before planning — Agree on steps and safety first.
  • Changing several factors — Change one factor for a clearer comparison.
  • Ignoring clean-up — Safe procedures include clean-up and hand washing where needed.
