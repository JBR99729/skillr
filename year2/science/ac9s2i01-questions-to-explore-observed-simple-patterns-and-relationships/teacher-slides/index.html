Concept modelTeach from the board

Turn an observation into a testable question

Ice melts faster in sun→Which place melts ice faster?→I predict the sunny place

The question identifies what will be compared.

Write a prediction with a reason

I predict...because...based on...

A reason connects the prediction to previous evidence or experience.

Elaboration examplesCopied content

The content description and elaborations below show the curriculum ideas taught in this unit. Items marked as teaching context support lesson planning.

  • Content description: pose questions to explore observed simple patterns and relationships and make predictions based on experiences
  • E1: posing questions about how to make sounds with different instruments, such as: ‘If I do this, will it always produce a higher pitched sound?’
  • E2: posing questions about the appearance or position of celestial objects in space across time, such as: ‘I wonder if the moon will look the same tomorrow or next week, as it does today?’
  • E3: making predictions about what might occur when materials such as playdough or tissue paper are pulled with different strengths
  • E4: making predictions about the relationship between vibration and sound, such as: ‘I think that if a ruler is twanged harder, it will make a louder sound’
  • E5: making predictions about future appearances of phenomena in the sky at certain times of the week, month or year, such as the moon or satellites
Important questionsWith answers

Key idea: A useful scientific question can be explored by observing or testing. A prediction states what may happen and gives a reason based on experience or a pattern.

Remember

  • Notice patterns
  • Pose investigable questions
  • Make predictions
  • Give evidence-based reasons
  • Keep questions open

Quick questions

  • What makes a question investigable?
  • Write a prediction using because.
  • Turn one observation into a question.
  • Why is a prediction different from a result?

If an answer is uncertain, return to the model and explain what each part represents before calculating or drawing a conclusion.

Assessment and reviewReady for practice
  • Question cannot be explored — Ask about something observable or testable.
  • Prediction without reason — Use because to connect the prediction to evidence.
  • Question already gives the answer — Keep the question open until evidence is collected.
