AC9S2U02: Explore different actions to make sounds and how to make a variety of sounds, and recognise that sound energy causes objects to vibrate

What students learn in AC9S2U02

Sounds are produced when objects vibrate. Plucking, striking, shaking, blowing or rubbing can start vibrations, and changing the vibration can change the sound.

Learning sequence: Make sound → Observe vibration → Change action → Compare sound

Success looks like

  • Identify vibration
  • Name sound-making actions
  • Compare sounds
  • Change one feature
  • Use loud/soft and high/low correctly
Concept model and worked application

Connect a vibration to a sound

vibration → sound

A ruler, string, drum skin or elastic band moves back and forth while it makes sound.

Change the action and compare the sound

pluck gentlypluck stronglyshort stringlong string

Change one feature, listen carefully and describe what changed.

Guided learning activities

Elastic-band guitar

Pluck elastic bands safely and observe the movement before changing the tension.

vibration → sound

Sound action sort

Sort instruments or objects by whether they are struck, shaken, plucked, blown or rubbed.

drum: strikebell: shakestring: pluckwhistle: blow

One-change comparison

Change only one feature, such as string length, and describe how the sound changes.

same object→change length→compare sound
Curriculum coverage and elaborations

The content description and elaborations below show the curriculum ideas taught in this unit. Items marked as teaching context support lesson planning.

  • Content description: explore different actions to make sounds and how to make a variety of sounds, and recognise that sound energy causes objects to vibrate
  • E1: building vocabulary for describing sound, such as loudness and pitch, and comparing sounds made by musical instruments
  • E2: exploring different ways to produce sound using familiar objects and actions such as striking, blowing, scraping, plucking and shaking
  • E3: exploring how traditional musical instruments used by First Nations Australians produce their characteristic sounds (teaching context)
  • E4: exploring how voices have a unique sound by playing games such as guess the speaker
  • E5: observing vibrations produced by a twanged ruler held on a desk and experimenting with different ways of holding or positioning the ruler to produce observably different vibrations and sounds
  • E6: investigating how sound energy makes things vibrate such as when speaking, using tuning forks or observing music speakers
  • E7: investigating which materials best muffle sound
  • E8: designing and making instruments that produce different sounds, such as drums, rain makers, thongophones or box guitars
  • E9: discussing situations in which they have heard echoes and exploring how humans with vision impairment and other animals such as dolphins and bats use echolocation to locate objects in their environments

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Sound without vibration — Look or feel carefully: the sound-making object vibrates, even when the movement is small.
  • Changing many things at once — Change one feature so the comparison is useful.
  • Calling loud and high the same — Loud/soft describes volume; high/low describes pitch.
