What students learn in AC9S2I04

This unit helps students build a clear, usable understanding of And order data and information and represent patterns, including with.... The goal is not to memorise one answer pattern. Students should be able to explain the idea, recognise it in a new example and apply it in a short practice or worksheet task.

  • Start with the main idea: sort and order data and information and represent patterns, including with provided tables and visual or physical models.
  • Use concrete examples, pictures, oral explanation and short written responses before moving to independent practice.
  • adding labels to a drawing or digital photograph to indicate key features, such as properties of materials that stay the same when changed physically, or to indicate how sound is produced by an instrument ordering images of the changing appearance of the moon to show a monthly cycle
  • Finish with mixed questions so students must choose the correct strategy rather than copy the last example.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Question banks for this unit should draw from the content description and the eligible elaborations below. First Nations-specific notes are kept out of generated question coverage unless they are part of the core concept.

  • Content description: sort and order data and information and represent patterns, including with provided tables and visual or physical models
  • E1: adding labels to a drawing or digital photograph to indicate key features, such as properties of materials that stay the same when changed physically, or to indicate how sound is produced by an instrument
  • E2: ordering images of the changing appearance of the moon to show a monthly cycle
  • E3: using a graphic organiser to sort images of musical instruments and the actions used to produce their sound
  • E4: constructing simple column graphs and picture graphs with guidance to represent class investigations, such as recording objects that produce or do not produce sound
  • E5: completing a table to record the number of ways different materials can be changed physically

How to use this unit

Read the topic guide, download the teacher slide for instruction, complete the printable worksheet, then use Practice and Test when the unit's checked 8-question bank is available.

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Describing what is seen without connecting it to a question, pattern or prediction.
  • Using everyday words only and missing simple scientific vocabulary.
  • Changing more than one thing in an investigation, which makes the comparison unfair.
