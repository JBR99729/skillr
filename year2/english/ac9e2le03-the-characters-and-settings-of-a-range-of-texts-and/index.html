Learning goals

1. Outcome Overview & Core Concepts

Authors choose nouns, adjectives, verbs, dialogue and comparisons to shape how readers imagine characters and settings.

Learning target: discuss the characters and settings of a range of texts and identify how language is used to present these features in different ways

Big idea: Identify how language presents literary features in different ways

Learning routine: Find feature → Collect words → Infer impression → Compare texts

4-Step Instructional Sequence

4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence

Step 1: Explicit Teaching & Modelling

Name the target in child-friendly language and demonstrate one concrete example connected to context, language, character.

Step 2: Guided Analysis

Compare or practise a second example together. Students point to, say or show the clue.

Step 3: Collaborative / Independent Practice

Create or analyse one example together, then use a fresh familiar example independently.

Step 4: Formative Assessment

Use a one-minute check with a new example; require observable evidence rather than a guess.

Subject-Specific Content & Key Examples

2. Subject-Specific Content & Key Examples

Compare different presentations

brave vs recklesspeaceful vs lonelybright vs glaringwalked vs stomped

Small word changes can create a different impression.

Sort and notice

Highlight words that present a character or setting and label the impression created.

Guided practice

  1. Sort and notice — Highlight words that present a character or setting and label the impression created.
  2. Build or improve — Rewrite one setting using words that create a contrasting mood.
  3. Explain your choice — Use the words characterisation, setting, word choice to explain what you changed and why.
Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: comparing how similar characters or settings are described in texts from different contexts; for example, how the seasons are described
  • E2: identifying and comparing verb groups used to convey actions, emotions and dialogue in a range of literary texts
  • E3: identifying the language used to describe the landscape in First Nations Australians’ stories (teaching context)
Common Student Misconceptions

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • Listing adjectives only: Explain what impression the language creates.
  • Ignoring verbs and dialogue: Actions and speech also reveal character and setting.
  • Answer without evidence: Point to the word, sentence, image, sound pattern or text feature that supports the answer.
Practice Thought & Formative Assessment

Practice Thought & Formative Assessment

Use a fresh, code-aligned example. The student should show the exact word, sound, sentence, image feature, audience clue or text evidence that supports the answer.

Quick checks

  • What can a verb reveal?
  • How can setting words create mood?
  • Compare walked and stomped.
  • Why collect evidence?

Mastery evidence

  • Identify character language
  • Identify setting language
  • Infer impressions
  • Compare word choices
Revision Notes

Revision Notes

  • Remember: Identify how language presents literary features in different ways
  • Use the routine: Find feature → Collect words → Infer impression → Compare texts
  • Before answering: point to the clue in the spoken words, written sentence, image, page layout or text purpose.
  • Self-check: change the example slightly and see whether the same reasoning still works.
