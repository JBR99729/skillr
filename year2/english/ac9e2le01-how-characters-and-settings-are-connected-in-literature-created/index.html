AC9E2LE01: Discuss how characters and settings are connected in literature created by First Nations Australian, and wide-ranging Australian and world authors and illustrators

What students learn

1. Outcome Overview & Core Concepts

Settings influence what characters notice, value, fear, need and do. Readers discuss these connections while respecting the knowledge, place and perspective represented by each creator.

Learning target: discuss how characters and settings are connected in literature created by First Nations Australian, and wide-ranging Australian and world authors and illustrators

Big idea: Discuss how setting shapes characters in literature from varied creators

Learning routine: Identify character → Identify setting → Find connection → Respect context → Discuss

2. Subject-Specific Content & Key Examples

2. Subject-Specific Content & Key Examples

Guided practice

  1. Sort and notice — Create a character–setting evidence chart for an approved class text.
  2. Build or improve — Compare how two creators connect a character to place using words or images.
  3. Explain your choice — Use the words character, setting, context to explain what you changed and why.
3. Common Student Misconceptions

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • Treating cultures as one story: Discuss the specific creator and text; avoid broad assumptions.
  • Describing setting without connection: Explain how the place affects a character's actions or feelings.
  • Answer without evidence: Point to the word, sentence, image, sound pattern or text feature that supports the answer.
4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence

4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence

Step 1: Explicit Teaching & Modelling

Name the target in child-friendly language and demonstrate one concrete example connected to context, character, setting.

Step 2: Guided Analysis

Compare or practise a second example together. Students point to, say or show the clue.

Step 3: Collaborative / Independent Practice

Create or analyse one example together, then use a fresh familiar example independently.

Step 4: Formative Assessment

Use a one-minute check with a new example; require observable evidence rather than a guess.

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: discussing the depiction of characters in particular settings in well-known fairytales from wide-ranging world authors; for example, children in forests, and royalty in castles and towers
  • E2: recognising recurring characters in particular settings in texts by First Nations Australian authors (teaching context)
  • E3: exploring the way wide-ranging Australian authors and illustrators depict the Australian outback and the associated characters
