AC9E2LA04: Understand how texts are made cohesive by using personal and possessive pronouns and by omitting words that can be inferred

understand how texts are made cohesive by using personal and possessive pronouns and by omitting words that can be inferred

What students learn

1. Outcome Overview & Core Concepts

Pronouns help sentences connect without repeating the same noun. Readers must still be able to tell who or what each pronoun refers to.

Learning target: understand how texts are made cohesive by using personal and possessive pronouns and by omitting words that can be inferred

Big idea: Use personal and possessive pronouns to connect ideas clearly

Learning routine: Name person or thing → Replace carefully → Check reference → Remove repetition

2. Subject-Specific Content & Key Examples

2. Subject-Specific Content & Key Examples

Replace repeated nouns with clear pronouns

Mia found Mia's hat.Mia found her hat.She put it on.The ideas now connect.

Her and she refer clearly to Mia; it refers to the hat.

Omit words when the meaning stays clear

I chose red paintSam chose bluechosen noun can be inferredavoid awkward repetition

Only omit a word when the reader can easily supply it.

Sort and notice

Underline nouns, circle pronouns and draw arrows to their references.

Guided practice

  1. Sort and notice — Underline nouns, circle pronouns and draw arrows to their references.
  2. Build or improve — Rewrite a repetitive paragraph using clear personal and possessive pronouns.
  3. Explain your choice — Use the words pronoun, reference, cohesion to explain what you changed and why.
3. Common Student Misconceptions

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • Unclear pronoun reference: Repeat the noun if the reader may not know who she, he, they or it means.
  • Removing too many words: Omit words only when the missing meaning is obvious.
  • Answer without evidence: Point to the word, sentence, image, sound pattern or text feature that supports the answer.
4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence

4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence

Step 1: Explicit Teaching & Modelling

Name the target in child-friendly language and demonstrate one concrete example connected to language, pronouns, character.

Step 2: Guided Analysis

Compare or practise a second example together. Students point to, say or show the clue.

Step 3: Collaborative / Independent Practice

Create or analyse one example together, then use a fresh familiar example independently.

Step 4: Formative Assessment

Use a one-minute check with a new example; require observable evidence rather than a guess.

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: identifying language used to build information across a text; for example, through connecting similar and dissimilar things
  • E2: mapping examples of word associations in texts; for example, words that refer to the main character in a story: “Isy”, “she”, “I”, “sister”, “student”
  • E3: tracking how a person or thing is identified through a section of a text; for example, “eggs”, “they”, “them”
  • E4: identifying words left out that can be inferred from the surrounding text; for example, “Xanthe went to school. She had a lovely day.” (at school is inferred)
  • E5: using personal and possessive pronouns to link entities previously mentioned in the text
Practice Thought & Formative Assessment

Practice Thought & Formative Assessment

Revision Notes
