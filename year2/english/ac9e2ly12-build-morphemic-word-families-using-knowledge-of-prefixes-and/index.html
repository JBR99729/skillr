Build meaningful word families AC9E2LY12

Learning intention: I can add a prefix or suffix to a known base, explain the new meaning and use the family to spell a word.

An adult can read directions and model the examples. Use a short turn or return to a section later.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Keep the base and explain what changes

Cover means put something over. Un + cover makes uncover, meaning remove a covering or reveal something. Cover + ing makes covering, as in “I am covering the box.” The base cover connects the family through meaning and helps us keep its spelling. Simply sharing a rhyme is not enough.

Learn the skill

1. Add a prefix before the base

A prefix is a meaningful part added at the start of a base. Read the new word and check what the prefix changes. Use a conventional word that makes sense.

Re + play makes replay: play again. Un + kind makes unkind: not kind. In unzip, un means reverse the zipping action. Notice the meaning in its sentence rather than assuming un has exactly the same effect in every word.

Try it · Use re and tell to build a word meaning tell again. Write it in “Please ___ the story.”

Retell: re + tell. “Please retell the story.” The prefix re comes before tell and adds the idea again. Keep all the letters in the base tell, then reread the sentence.

2. Add a suffix after the base

A suffix is a meaningful ending added after a base. It can change meaning or the way a word is used. Keep track of both the new spelling and what the whole word means.

Help + er makes helper, someone who helps. Help + ful makes helpful, giving help. Friend + ly makes friendly, acting like a friend; friend + ship makes friendship, the relationship between friends. In each pair the familiar base helps with spelling.

Try it · Starting with help, write a word for someone who helps. Then write a different family word for giving help.

Helper and helpful. Both keep help. Er means someone who does the action in helper; ful contributes the helpful quality in helpful. Use each in a sensible sentence, such as “The helper carried the bag” and “That was a helpful idea.”

3. Check the family, then spell and use a new word

A meaningful family shares a base and connected meanings. Find the base, decide which added part matches your message, write the conventional word and reread it in a sentence.

Play, replay and player share the meaning of play. Day rhymes with play, but it is not built from the base play. Cover, covering and uncover share the base cover. Not every base accepts every ending, so check that your new word is used with that meaning.

Try it · You know friend. Write the word meaning the relationship between friends in “Their ___ grew as they played together.” Explain the parts.

Friendship: friend + ship. Keep friend, including its ie spelling, and add ship. “Their friendship grew as they played together.” The ending here names a relationship; it is not the separate boat meaning of ship.

Common mistakes

Grouping words only because they rhyme Play and day rhyme, but day is not built from play. Look for a shared meaningful base.

Treating matching first letters as a prefix Un in unkind changes kind to not kind. Uncle is not made from un plus a base cle with that meaning.

Adding any ending without checking meaning Build a conventional word and try it in a sentence. Different suffixes can produce different valid family words.

10-MINUTE ACTIVITY

Make a word family and use it

Use the supplied base and affix cards in More examples. Build several conventional words, put prefixes before and suffixes after the base, then explain each meaning in a sentence. Hide the completed models for the fresh spelling turn. The adult gives a meaning and the learner builds, writes and rereads a suitable family word.

Adjust the challenge Starter: choose between a prefix and suffix card for one modelled base. Core: build and explain two different words in a family. Stretch: combine un + help + ful to make unhelpful, explain how each part contributes and use it in a meaningful sentence.

Accept different conventional words when they satisfy an open prompt and the learner explains their meaning. Check actual spelling and sentence use. A correct card choice alone does not demonstrate independent writing.

Three tricky questions

Are play and day in the same morphemic family because they rhyme?

No. Day does not contain the base play or its meaning. Player and replay do.

In replay, where is the prefix and what does it add?

Re is before play. It adds again, so replay means play again.

Can friendly help you spell friendship?

Yes. Both use the base friend, keeping its ie spelling. Change the ending to match the new meaning: ly for friendly and ship for friendship.

FAQ

Does adding a suffix always leave the base spelling untouched?

No. These starter examples keep the base visible. Some other words change at the join; learn those patterns with an adult instead of assuming every word joins in the same way.

Is a word family the same as a list of synonyms?

No. Kind and nice can have related meanings without sharing a base. Kind, unkind and kindness form a meaningful family built around kind.

Complete base and affix cards

Bases: play, help, kind, cover, friend. Prefixes: re, un. Suffixes: er, ful, ing, ed, ness, ly, ship. Make conventional words and explain them in a sentence; do not assume every card joins to every base.

Sort the affix cards by position: re and un go before a base; these suffix cards go after. Use the actual meaning of each finished word to check your choice.

Family boundary check

Compare play, replay, player and day. Replay and player contain the meaningful base play; day shares a rhyme, not that base. Compare kind, unkind and nice: nice can have a similar meaning, but does not contain kind.

Check and explain

A morphemic family needs a shared meaningful base, not just matching sounds or a similar meaning. A valid new word must also have a conventional spelling and fit the sentence in which it is used.

Common Student Misconceptions

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • Any similar spelling is a family: Word-family members share a meaningful base, not just nearby letters.
  • Ignoring meaning changes: Explain what the prefix or suffix contributes.
  • Answer without evidence: Point to the word, sentence, image, sound pattern or text feature that supports the answer.

Quick answers and a new family

Check the earlier quick questions

A base is the meaningful part used to build the family. A prefix goes before it. Ful adds a quality in words such as helpful; it is spelled with one l. Valid play-family examples include player, played, playing and replay; explain their meanings, rather than accepting every letter combination.

New known base: pack. Read and write pack once, then cover it.

Fresh family task · answer after attempting

Write a word meaning pack again. Explain the prefix, use the new word in a sentence and reread it. Then build a word for someone who packs and explain which ending you added.

Check the family

Repack = re + pack, meaning pack again: “I will repack my bag.” Packer = pack + er, someone who packs: “The packer filled the box.” Both retain pack. Accept another conventional word only when it satisfies the precise meaning and uses the named base.
