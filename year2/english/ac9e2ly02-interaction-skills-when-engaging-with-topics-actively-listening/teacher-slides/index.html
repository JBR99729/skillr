1. Outcome Overview & Core Concepts

Effective interaction includes attentive listening, clear speaking, suitable volume and tone, relevant responses, respectful opinions and instructions in a logical order.

Learning target: use interaction skills when engaging with topics, actively listening to others, receiving instructions and extending own ideas, speaking appropriately, expressing and responding to opinions, making statements, and giving instructions

Big idea: Use interaction skills to exchange ideas and respond appropriately

Learning routine: Listen → Think → Respond → Extend → Check understanding

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: exploring ways to comment on what others say, including using sentence starters such as “I like the way you …”, “I agree that …”, “I have a different thought …”, “I’d like to say something different …”
  • E2: demonstrating appropriate listening behaviours, responding to and paraphrasing a partner’s contribution to a discussion; for example, in think pair share activities
  • E3: asking relevant questions and making connections with personal experiences and the contributions of others
  • E4: understanding how to disagree or respectfully offer an alternative

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • Waiting only for your turn: Listen for the other speaker's main idea before responding.
  • Instructions missing steps: Imagine the listener knows nothing and check the order.
  • Answer without evidence: Point to the word, sentence, image, sound pattern or text feature that supports the answer.

Practice Thought & Formative Assessment

Use a fresh, code-aligned example. The student should show the exact word, sound, sentence, image feature, audience clue or text evidence that supports the answer.
