YEAR 2 ENGLISH

Listen, respond and build ideas together AC9E2LY02

Learning intention: I can show understanding, add a connected idea and give or follow clear instructions.

An adult can read directions and model the examples. Use a short turn or return to a section later.

WORKED EXAMPLE

A connected reply shows what was understood

Original exchange: “The reading corner needs a lamp so we can see the books.” Reply: “You think a lamp would help us see. Where could we put it?” The reply restates the idea and asks about a missing detail. Listening is shown by the relevant response, not a compulsory gaze or posture.

Learn the skill

1. Listen, restate and clarify

Attend in a way that works for you. Say the main idea back without changing it, then ask a useful question about something unclear.

Speaker: “Let’s put the seed pots by the window because they need light.” Reply: “You want the pots near the window for light.”

Try it · Ask one relevant question that could help carry out the idea.

For example, ask which window, how many pots or where on the sill they should go. A useful question connects to the actual contribution. Do not insist on one memorised phrase.

2. Extend ideas and respond respectfully

A statement can add information; an opinion gives a view. You may agree or disagree while keeping your response relevant and respectful.

“I prefer a quiet reading game because I can hear the story.” Another speaker says, “I have a different thought: a word hunt would let us look for clues.”

Try it · Offer one connected reason or suggestion, then invite the other person’s view.

Accept different preferences with relevant reasons and respectful language. A suggestion should add useful detail, such as taking turns choosing games. Merely changing the topic does not extend the earlier idea.

3. Give, receive and repair instructions

State the goal and the steps in a usable order. Check the listener’s response and clarify missing objects or positions.

Paper task: draw a square; put a small circle inside it; draw a star above the square. No scissors are needed.

Try it · Give or follow those directions with a partner. How would you repair “Put it there”?

Name the object and position, for example “Put the small circle inside the square.” Compare the actual drawing with the directions, then swap roles. Naming good listening alone does not demonstrate taking part in the exchange.

Common mistakes

“Listening means waiting to speak.” Show understanding of the other person’s main idea.

“Disagreement must be rude.” Offer a respectful alternative and a relevant reason.

“A clear instruction needs no checking.” Check what the listener understood and repair uncertainty.

10-MINUTE ACTIVITY

Think, pair, respond and test

Use the complete idea, opinion and paper-direction cards in More examples. Restate a partner’s idea, give one connected response, follow directions and then lead them. Discuss one repair that helped.

Adjust the challenge Starter: adult models a restatement or question. Core: contribute relevantly and follow three directions. Stretch: combine two ideas or revise an instruction after a misunderstanding.

Accept spoken language, AAC and suitable access support; record prompts. Do not grade eye contact, accent or a fixed posture. Judge the meaning and usefulness of actual contributions.

Three tricky questions

1. Must I agree to show I listened?

No. An accurate restatement and relevant respectful alternative can show understanding.

2. Is “I like the way you…” always useful?

It needs a real, specific completion connected to the contribution.

3. Can a question improve an instruction?

Yes. Ask about a missing object, position, step or goal.

FAQ

What if I did not hear part of the message?

Ask for repetition or clarification; adjust the communication conditions where useful.

Can an adult act as the partner?

Yes. Preserve the learner’s own contribution and note the support used.

Actual exchange and paper-task directions

Idea card: “The reading corner needs a lamp so we can see the books.” First paraphrase that idea without changing it. Then ask a relevant question such as where the lamp would go, or add a reason or suggestion. Opinion cards: “I prefer a quiet reading game because I can hear the story.” “I prefer a word hunt because I enjoy finding clues.” Respond with a connected agreement or respectful alternative, not a criticism of the person. Paper task: draw a square; put a small circle inside it; draw a star above the square. Give or receive those directions in order, check the listener’s drawing, and repair “Put it there” by naming the shape and position. Discuss one statement, one opinion and one instruction. Partners swap roles. Observe actual contributions and clarification; eye contact, accent and one particular posture are not required evidence.

Common Student Misconceptions

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • Waiting only for your turn: Listen for the other speaker's main idea before responding.
  • Instructions missing steps: Imagine the listener knows nothing and check the order.
  • Answer without evidence: Point to the word, sentence, image, sound pattern or text feature that supports the answer.

Earlier worked model

Lina says, ‘The class garden needs more shade.’ Which reply shows active listening?

Curriculum alignment

AC9E2LY02: use interaction skills when engaging with topics, actively listening to others, receiving instructions and extending own ideas, speaking appropriately, expressing and responding to opinions, making statements, and giving instructions

  • exploring ways to comment on what others say, including using sentence starters such as “I like the way you …”, “I agree that …”, “I have a different thought …”, “I’d like to say something different …”
  • demonstrating appropriate listening behaviours, responding to and paraphrasing a partner’s contribution to a discussion; for example, in think pair share activities
  • asking relevant questions and making connections with personal experiences and the contributions of others
  • understanding how to disagree or respectfully offer an alternative
