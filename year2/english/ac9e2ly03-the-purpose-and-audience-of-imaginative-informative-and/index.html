AC9E2LY03: Identify the purpose and audience of imaginative, informative and persuasive texts

identify the purpose and audience of imaginative, informative and persuasive texts

Topic guideWorksheetPracticeTest

1. Outcome Overview & Core Concepts

Students learn to identify the purpose and audience of imaginative, informative and persuasive texts. Teach the target through short, familiar oral, visual and written examples. Useful lesson language includes audience, purpose, context. Students should demonstrate the skill in a fresh example, not only repeat a model.

Learning target: identify the purpose and audience of imaginative, informative and persuasive texts

2. Subject-Specific Content & Key Examples

Notice

Model one short example and identify the sound, word, sentence feature or text clue named in the outcome.

Compare

Choose which of two examples matches and explain the clue.

Make one together

Jointly create a sentence, oral response or text example using the feature.

Try a new example

Independently recognise or use the same feature in a fresh familiar context.

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • Naming without showing: Ask students to point to, say or demonstrate the exact feature.
  • One memorised example: Change the word, picture or sentence and check the same skill again.
  • Guessing: Ask: What did you hear or see that proves it?

4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence

Step 1: Explicit Teaching & Modelling

Name the target in child-friendly language and model one short example connected to audience, purpose, context.

Step 2: Guided Analysis

Compare two short examples. Students point to or say the clue.

Step 3: Collaborative / Independent Practice

Create one example together, then try a fresh familiar example independently.

Step 4: Formative Assessment

Use a one-minute check with a new example; require an observable clue rather than a guess.

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations
