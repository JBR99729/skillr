AC9E2LY11: Use knowledge of spelling patterns and morphemes to read and write words whose spelling is not completely predictable from their sounds, including high-frequency words

What students learn

1. Outcome Overview & Core Concepts

Spelling draws on phonics, common patterns, morphemes and visual memory. High-frequency words may contain a regular part and a less predictable part that must be remembered.

Learning target: use knowledge of spelling patterns and morphemes to read and write words whose spelling is not completely predictable from their sounds, including high-frequency words

Big idea: Use sound patterns, word parts and memory for less predictable spellings

Learning routine: Say word → Find known sounds → Find word parts → Notice tricky part → Practise

2. Subject-Specific Content & Key Examples

2. Subject-Specific Content & Key Examples

Analyse regular and tricky parts

saidsaidai has unusual sound

The word can be partly mapped by sound while the unexpected spelling is noticed explicitly.

Use meaning-bearing word parts

help + fulplay + edjump + ingbase word stays visible

Morphemes support both spelling and meaning.

Sort and notice

Underline predictable spelling parts and box the tricky part in common words.

Guided practice

  1. Sort and notice — Underline predictable spelling parts and box the tricky part in common words.
  2. Build or improve — Build word families from a base and explain what each added morpheme means.
  3. Explain your choice — Use the words pattern, morpheme, high-frequency to explain what you changed and why.
3. Common Student Misconceptions

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • Trying sound only: Use sound plus spelling pattern, word part and remembered tricky section.
  • Memorising every word as a whole shape: Analyse regular parts so memory work is smaller.
  • Answer without evidence: Point to the word, sentence, image, sound pattern or text feature that supports the answer.
4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence

4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence

Step 1: Explicit Teaching & Modelling

Name the target in child-friendly language and demonstrate one concrete example connected to context, spelling, morpheme.

Step 2: Guided Analysis

Compare or practise a second example together. Students point to, say or show the clue.

Step 3: Collaborative / Independent Practice

Create or analyse one example together, then use a fresh familiar example independently.

Step 4: Formative Assessment

Use a one-minute check with a new example; require observable evidence rather than a guess.

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: using known words and knowledge of spelling patterns and morphemes to write unknown words; for example, “one”, “once”, “only” and “lone”
  • E2: using context to read the correct word when an unknown word has more than one plausible pronunciation
Practice Thought & Formative Assessment

Practice Thought & Formative Assessment

Use a fresh, code-aligned example. The student should show the exact word, sound, sentence, image feature, audience clue or text evidence that supports the answer.

Quick checks
