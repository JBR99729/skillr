Guided practice

  1. Sort and notice — Sort words into general and precise vocabulary.
  2. Build or improve — Replace weak words in a short paragraph and explain each choice.
  3. Explain your choice — Use the words vocabulary, precise, topic-specific to explain what you changed and why.

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: selecting vocabulary appropriate to the topic to replace everyday language
  • E2: discussing new vocabulary encountered in learning area texts
  • E3: exploring language used to describe characters in narratives, including nouns; for example, “magician”, “wizard”, “sorcerer”, and adjectives such as “gentle”, “timid” or “frightened”
  • E4: identifying words from First Nations Australians’ languages relevant to a topic (teaching context)

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • Choosing the longest word: Choose the most accurate word, not the most complicated one.
  • Using topic words incorrectly: Check meaning and use the word in a sentence that makes sense.
  • Answer without evidence: Point to the word, sentence, image, sound pattern or text feature that supports the answer.

Practice Thought & Formative Assessment

Use a fresh, code-aligned example. The student should show the exact word, sound, sentence, image feature, audience clue or text evidence that supports the answer.

Quick checks

  • Give a precise verb for went.
  • What is topic-specific vocabulary?
  • Why can nice be unclear?
  • How do you check a word choice?

Mastery evidence

  • Identify general words
  • Choose precise verbs
