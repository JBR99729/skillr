YEAR 2 ENGLISH

Build useful noun groups and verb groups AC9E2LA07

Learning intention: I can add helpful noun details and find the whole verb group in a sentence.

An adult can read directions and model the examples. Use a short turn or return to a section later.

More examples ↓Help with gaps ↓10-minute activity ↓

WORKED EXAMPLE

Find both groups in the whole message

“The small brown dog was barking.” The noun group The small brown dog names the animal and gives useful detail. The verb group was barking tells about its action at that time. Was belongs with barking; brown describes the dog, so it is outside the verb group.

Learn the skill

1. Extend a noun with helpful words

A noun can name a person, place, object or idea. Articles such as a, an and the and adjectives can extend the noun group. Choose details that fit.

“A tree” can become “a tall tree”. “The dog” can become “the small brown dog”. In “Kindness matters”, kindness names an idea rather than a physical object.

Try it · Build a noun group around kite using an article and accurate describing words.

For example, “a bright red kite”. Accept another sensible group. The main noun remains kite, and the extra words identify or describe it; adding more adjectives is not automatically better.

2. Find the whole verb group

A verb can express an action or state. A verb group can include a main verb and helping words. Read the full sentence to see which words work together.

“The children are running.” The whole verb group is are running. “The bird has finished.” Has finished is the whole group. “The ball was touched.” Was touched works together.

Try it · Find the whole verb group in “The girl was painting”. What does the helper add?

Was painting is the group. Was helps place the ongoing painting in past time. Do not leave out the helper or include girl, which belongs to the noun group.

3. Compare what the added words do

Ask useful questions: how many, what kind, or what is it like? Keep noun details connected to the noun, and make verb forms fit the intended sentence.

“Two pairs of old walking shoes” specifies quantity, condition and type. “The parrots are calling” needs are for the plural subject; “The parrot is calling” uses is for one.

Try it · Improve “The bird flew” with a useful noun detail and a suitable two-word verb group.

For example, “The small bird was flying.” Accept another clear sentence and explain the meaning change. A switch from flew to was flying changes how the action is presented; it is not merely a longer copy.

Common mistakes

“Every nearby word is in the noun group.” Check what identifies or describes its noun.

“Only the action word is the verb group.” Include helpers such as was or are when they belong.

“Longer groups are always better.” Choose accurate details that help the reader.

10-MINUTE ACTIVITY

Build a sentence with two groups

Use the article, adjective and noun cards in More examples. Build one sensible noun group, add a suitable verb group and read the whole sentence. Swap one word and explain the changed meaning or grammar.

Adjust the challenge Starter: adult highlights a noun and a verb. Core: build a useful noun group and identify a whole verb group. Stretch: compare quantity or time changes in two clear sentences while keeping vocabulary familiar.

Use complete actual sentences. A child can point, colour, speak, use AAC or write to explain grouping. Do not score drawing or handwriting instead of grammatical meaning.

Three tricky questions

1. Is brown part of was barking?

No. Brown describes the dog; was barking is the verb group.

2. Must every verb be a physical action?

No. Verbs can also express states, as is does in “The child is happy”.

3. Does an always come before a word starting with a vowel letter?

Choose a or an by the next spoken sound, including any adjective. For example, an old tree and a small tree.

FAQ

Do noun groups need many adjectives?

No. Use only useful, accurate details.

Does a two-word verb group always mean now?

No. Compare is running, was running and will run in their complete contexts.

Actual word cards and sentences

Cards: articles a / an / the; adjectives small / brown / tall / noisy; nouns dog / tree / parrots. Build meaningful groups such as “the small brown dog” and “a tall tree”. Plain sentences: “The dog barked.” “A bird flew.” Add an accurate useful noun detail, then compare a suitable verb group: “The small dog was barking.” “The noisy bird is flying.” In “The small dog was barking”, the whole noun group is The small dog and the whole verb group is was barking. In “The ball was touched”, was touched is the verb group. Compare “two pairs of old walking shoes”: the extra words specify quantity, condition and type. Use familiar examples; longer is not automatically better.

Common Student Misconceptions

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • Adding random adjectives: Choose details that match the noun and purpose.
  • Calling every nearby word part of the noun group: Check which words actually describe or identify the noun.
  • Answer without evidence: Point to the word, sentence, image, sound pattern or text feature that supports the answer.

The green frog is hopping across the path now. Which two-word verb group tells what it is doing?

Answer: is hopping

Curriculum alignment

AC9E2LA07: understand that in sentences nouns may be extended into noun groups using articles and adjectives, and verbs may be expressed as verb groups

  • identifying nouns that refer to people, places, concrete objects and ideas in sentences in texts, and identifying the articles and adjectives that extend those nouns
  • building extended noun groups using articles and adjectives; for example, “the longest side”
  • building extended verb groups using verbs; for example, “was touched”
  • investigating how noun groups can be built up by asking questions about the noun such as “how many?”, “what’s it like?”, “what type?”; for example, “two pairs of old walking shoes”
