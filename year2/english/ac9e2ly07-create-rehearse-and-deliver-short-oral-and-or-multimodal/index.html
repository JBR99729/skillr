Learning goals

1. Outcome Overview & Core Concepts

Effective presentations have a clear structure, accurate topic words, useful visual support and delivery choices that help the audience follow the message.

Learning target: create, rehearse and deliver short oral and/or multimodal presentations for familiar audiences and purposes, using text structure appropriate to purpose and topic-specific vocabulary, and varying tone, volume and pace

Big idea: Present ideas clearly using structure, vocabulary, tone, volume and pace

Learning routine: Plan → Create support → Rehearse → Deliver → Reflect

4-Step Instructional Sequence

4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence

Step 1: Explicit Teaching & Modelling

Name the target in child-friendly language and demonstrate one concrete example connected to audience, purpose, language.

Step 2: Guided Analysis

Compare or practise a second example together. Students point to, say or show the clue.

Step 3: Collaborative / Independent Practice

Create or analyse one example together, then use a fresh familiar example independently.

Step 4: Formative Assessment

Use a one-minute check with a new example; require observable evidence rather than a guess.

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: preparing and delivering oral presentations using more formal language and specific vocabulary about content area topics
  • E2: sequencing ideas, points or events appropriately
  • E3: adjusting volume and tone when speaking in more formal situations
Common Student Misconceptions

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • Reading slides word for word: Use short prompts and speak to the audience.
  • Loud equals clear: Use suitable volume plus steady pace and precise articulation.
  • Answer without evidence: Point to the word, sentence, image, sound pattern or text feature that supports the answer.
Practice Thought & Formative Assessment

Practice Thought & Formative Assessment

Use a fresh, code-aligned example. The student should show the exact word, sound, sentence, image feature, audience clue or text evidence that supports the answer.

Quick checks

  • Why rehearse?
  • What should an opening do?
  • How can pace help?
  • When is a visual useful?

Mastery evidence

  • Plan presentation structure
  • Use topic vocabulary
  • Choose visual support
  • Vary delivery
Revision Notes

Revision Notes

  • Remember: Present ideas clearly using structure, vocabulary, tone, volume and pace
  • Use the routine: Plan → Create support → Rehearse → Deliver → Reflect
  • Before answering: point to the clue in the spoken words, written sentence, image, page layout or text purpose.
  • Self-check: change the example slightly and see whether the same reasoning still works.
