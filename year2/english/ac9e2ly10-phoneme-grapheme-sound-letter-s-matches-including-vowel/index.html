Step 1 · Learn the idea

1. Outcome Overview & Core Concepts

A phoneme may be represented by one or more letters. Readers recognise common and less common patterns, preserve sounds in clusters and notice silent letters and word parts.

Learning target: use phoneme-grapheme (sound-letter/s) matches, including vowel digraphs, less common long vowel patterns, consonant clusters and silent letters when reading and writing words of one or more syllables, including compound words

Big idea: Read and spell vowel patterns, clusters, silent letters and compound words

Learning routine: Say word → Identify sounds → Map graphemes → Notice pattern → Check meaning

2. Subject-Specific Content & Key Examples

2. Subject-Specific Content & Key Examples

Map sounds to graphemes

rainrainai = long /a/

The vowel digraph ai represents one vowel phoneme.

Notice clusters, silent letters and compounds

frog: fr clusterknee: silent ksunset: sun + setrobot: two syllables

Use both sound knowledge and known spelling patterns.

Sort and notice

Sort words by vowel digraph, consonant cluster, silent letter or compound word.

Guided practice

  1. Sort and notice — Sort words by vowel digraph, consonant cluster, silent letter or compound word.
  2. Build or improve — Map phonemes to graphemes in one- and two-syllable words.
  3. Explain your choice — Use the words phoneme, grapheme, pattern to explain what you changed and why.
3. Common Student Misconceptions

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • One letter equals one sound: Some sounds use digraphs, and some letters are silent.
  • Dropping a cluster sound: Stretch the word and preserve each consonant phoneme.
  • Answer without evidence: Point to the word, sentence, image, sound pattern or text feature that supports the answer.
4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence

4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence

Step 1: Explicit Teaching & Modelling

Name the target in child-friendly language and demonstrate one concrete example connected to phoneme, grapheme, reading.

Step 2: Guided Analysis

Compare or practise a second example together. Students point to, say or show the clue.

Step 3: Collaborative / Independent Practice

Create or analyse one example together, then use a fresh familiar example independently.

Step 4: Formative Assessment

Use a one-minute check with a new example; require observable evidence rather than a guess.

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: reading words with digraphs where one letter is not pronounced; for example, “knife” and “thumb”, when reading and using them in writing
  • E2: providing the sounds for less common letter-sound matches; for example, “ight”, and using them in writing
  • E3: reading words with vowel digraphs (“ee”, “oo”, “ai”, “ay”, “ea”)
Practice Thought & Formative Assessment

Practice Thought & Formative Assessment

Use a fresh, code-aligned example. The student should show the exact word, sound, sentence, image feature, audience clue or text evidence that supports the answer.

Quick checks

  • What sound does ai often represent?
  • Which letter is silent in knee?
  • What two words form sunset?
  • What is a grapheme?

Mastery evidence

  • Use vowel digraphs
  • Read less common long vowels
  • Preserve clusters
  • Recognise silent letters

Revision Notes
