1. Outcome Overview & Core Concepts

Fluent readers group words into meaningful phrases, use punctuation, maintain an appropriate pace and stop to reread when a word or idea does not make sense.

Learning target: read texts with phrasing and fluency, using phonic and word knowledge, and monitoring meaning by re-reading and self-correcting

Big idea: Read with phrasing, monitor meaning and repair errors

Learning routine: Decode → Group words → Read smoothly → Check meaning → Repair

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: using phonic (sound-letter) and morphemic knowledge, and knowledge of high-frequency words when decoding text
  • E2: monitoring own reading, self-correcting or reading back, re-reading when meaning does not make sense

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • Reading fast without meaning: Fluency includes accuracy, phrasing and understanding, not speed alone.
  • Guessing from the picture only: Check the printed letters, sounds and sentence meaning together.
  • Answer without evidence: Point to the word, sentence, image, sound pattern or text feature that supports the answer.

Practice Thought & Formative Assessment

Use a fresh, code-aligned example. The student should show the exact word, sound, sentence, image feature, audience clue or text evidence that supports the answer.
