AC9E2LY04: Read texts with phrasing and fluency, using phonic and word knowledge, and monitoring meaning by re-reading and self-correcting

read texts with phrasing and fluency, using phonic and word knowledge, and monitoring meaning by re-reading and self-correcting

Topic GuideClassroom ViewWorksheetPracticeTest
Learning goals

1. Outcome Overview & Core Concepts

Fluent readers group words into meaningful phrases, use punctuation, maintain an appropriate pace and stop to reread when a word or idea does not make sense.

Learning target: read texts with phrasing and fluency, using phonic and word knowledge, and monitoring meaning by re-reading and self-correcting

Big idea: Read with phrasing, monitor meaning and repair errors

Learning routine: Decode → Group words → Read smoothly → Check meaning → Repair

4-Step Instructional Sequence

4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence

Step 1: Explicit Teaching & Modelling

Name the target in child-friendly language and demonstrate one concrete example connected to reading.

Step 2: Guided Analysis

Compare or practise a second example together. Students point to, say or show the clue.

Step 3: Collaborative / Independent Practice

Create or analyse one example together, then use a fresh familiar example independently.

Step 4: Formative Assessment

Use a one-minute check with a new example; require observable evidence rather than a guess.

Subject-Specific Content & Key Examples

2. Subject-Specific Content & Key Examples

Read in meaningful phrases

Punctuation and meaning guide phrasing, not a pause after every word.

Use a repair strategy

stoprereadcheck letters and soundstry a word that fits meaning

Confirm the corrected word using both print and meaning.

Sort and notice

Mark phrase boundaries in a short passage and practise repeated reading.

Guided practice

  1. Sort and notice — Mark phrase boundaries in a short passage and practise repeated reading.
  2. Build or improve — Use a deliberate misread sentence to practise stopping and self-correcting.
  3. Explain your choice — Use the words fluency, phrasing, self-correct to explain what you changed and why.
Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: using phonic (sound-letter) and morphemic knowledge, and knowledge of high-frequency words when decoding text
  • E2: monitoring own reading, self-correcting or reading back, re-reading when meaning does not make sense
Common Student Misconceptions

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • Reading fast without meaning: Fluency includes accuracy, phrasing and understanding, not speed alone.
  • Guessing from the picture only: Check the printed letters, sounds and sentence meaning together.
  • Answer without evidence: Point to the word, sentence, image, sound pattern or text feature that supports the answer.
Practice Thought & Formative Assessment

Practice Thought & Formative Assessment

Use a fresh, code-aligned example. The student should show the exact word, sound, sentence, image feature, audience clue or text evidence that supports the answer.

Quick checks

  • What is phrasing?
  • When should you reread?
  • Is fastest always most fluent?
  • What clues help self-correction?

Mastery evidence

  • Decode accurately
  • Use punctuation
  • Read in phrases
  • Monitor meaning
Revision Notes

Revision Notes

  • Remember: Read with phrasing, monitor meaning and repair errors
  • Use the routine: Decode → Group words → Read smoothly → Check meaning → Repair
  • Before answering: point to the clue in the spoken words, written sentence, image, page layout or text purpose.
  • Self-check: change the example slightly and see whether the same reasoning still works.
