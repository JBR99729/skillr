Read accurately, phrase naturally and repair meaning AC9E2LY04

Learning intention: I can read the printed words, group ideas and reread to check or correct them.

An adult can read directions and model the examples. Use a short turn or return to a section later.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Check print before accepting a word

Printed sentence: “The children planted six seeds.” If it is read as “six seats”, stop and inspect the final letters and sounds. Seeds has d before its final s; seats has t. Read the actual printed word, then reread the whole sentence to check its meaning. A sensible guess alone is not enough.

Learn the skill

1. Use sound, word and meaningful-part knowledge

Check the full printed word. Blend known sound–letter patterns, recognise learnt words and use helpful bases, endings or compounds. Then check the sentence meaning.

Planted contains plant + ed; lunchbox contains lunch + box. In this word, the ending ed helps show a past action.

Try it · Read planted and lunchbox, then say how a useful part helped.

Listen to the actual reading and explanation. Plant plus the past ending supports planted; lunch and box form lunchbox. Do not model the assessed target aloud immediately before an independent reading attempt. Not every word is fully predictable from one simple pattern.

2. Read phrases for connected meaning

Group words in useful units and use punctuation to guide the voice. Fluency includes accuracy, natural phrasing and understanding, not racing.

“After lunch, the children carried a seed box to the garden.” One model groups After lunch / the children carried a seed box / to the garden.

Try it · Read the full passage in More examples. Try a natural phrase grouping, then reread smoothly.

Accept sensible alternative groupings that keep meaning and do not split words mechanically. Listen for accurate words and useful pauses; do not demand an identical accent, voice or fixed reading speed. Record adult modelling separately from independent reading.

3. Monitor and self-correct

Notice when print, grammar or meaning does not fit. Go back, inspect the word and reread the connected sentence after a repair.

Controlled adult slip: the child sees “The children planted six seeds” while the adult says “The children planted six seats.” The actual passage is supplied in More examples.

Try it · Which printed word needs checking? Repair it and reread. During your own reading, notice a mismatch if one occurs.

Seeds is printed, not seats. Check the letters and say the corrected sentence. A controlled detection task practises repair; record spontaneous self-correction separately. Do not create a fake learner mistake or treat a selection answer as fluent reading.

Common mistakes

“Fastest means most fluent.” Accuracy, meaningful phrasing and understanding matter.

“A picture can tell me which printed word to guess.” Decode the print; use meaning to check the result.

“Rereading is a failure.” Rereading can restore meaning and build confidence.

10-MINUTE ACTIVITY

Read, check, then reread

Use the actual passage and word cards in More examples. Read once, discuss one meaning question, then reread with useful phrasing. An adult records support, accurate word reading and any actual self-correction.

Adjust the challenge Starter: model a different short line before guided reading. Core: read the supplied connected passage. Stretch: transfer to another suitable decodable text and explain a real repair.

Give directions aloud if needed while keeping assessed text for the learner to read. Use appropriate access accommodations and note what competence was observed. A paper score cannot certify oral fluency.

Three tricky questions

1. Is one pause pattern compulsory?

No. Accept a natural grouping that preserves the sentence’s meaning.

2. Does context replace checking the letters?

No. A plausible word can still be different from the word printed.

3. Is a prompted repair independent self-correction?

No. Record the prompt and distinguish it from a spontaneous repair.

FAQ

Can we reread a familiar passage?

Yes. Also try a different suitable passage when checking transfer; do not confuse memorisation with new word reading.

Should every frequent word be memorised as irregular?

No. Use its actual sound–letter and word-part patterns, and teach any unusual part explicitly.

Actual reading passage and controlled repair

Passage: “After lunch, the children carried a seed box to the garden. A small bird landed on the fence. The children planted six seeds and went back inside.” Before independent reading, the adult may explain the task but should not read the target words or passage to the learner. Practise a different model first. Word cards: planted = plant + ed; seedlings = seed + ling + s; lunchbox = lunch + box. Use known sound–letter patterns, useful word parts and recognised words, checking all printed letters. The suffix ed in planted forms a past action; do not call every frequent word irregular. Phrase practice can group “After lunch” / “the children carried a seed box” / “to the garden”, with other sensible groupings accepted. Controlled adult slip: read “The children planted six seats” while the child sees the actual seeds sentence. Notice seats/seeds mismatch, inspect the letters, repair to seeds and reread for meaning. A prompted repair is recorded separately from a spontaneous self-correction during ordinary reading.

Common Student Misconceptions

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • Reading fast without meaning: Fluency includes accuracy, phrasing and understanding, not speed alone.
  • Guessing from the picture only: Check the printed letters, sounds and sentence meaning together.
  • Answer without evidence: Point to the word, sentence, image, sound pattern or text feature that supports the answer.

Revision Notes

  • Remember: Read with phrasing, monitor meaning and repair errors
  • Use the routine: Decode → Group words → Read smoothly → Check meaning → Repair
  • Before answering: point to the clue in the spoken words, written sentence, image, page layout or text purpose.
  • Self-check: change the example slightly and see whether the same reasoning still works.

Earlier worked model

Which reading sounds natural for the sentence ‘After lunch the class planted six seedlings’ ?

Answer: Pause briefly after lunch, then read the rest as one smooth idea.

A brief pause after the opening time phrase keeps the rest of the idea together.
