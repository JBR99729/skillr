Parent and teacher guide

Angles are easiest when students connect them to turns and corners. Begin with right angles, half turns and quarter turns, then compare angles as smaller or larger than a right angle. A common mistake is judging an angle by the length of its arms instead of the amount of turn. Use hinged strips, clock hands and shape corners to make the turn visible. Ask students to explain how they know an angle is acute, right or obtuse using mathematical language.

Common mistakes to watch for

Ask the student to explain their method before moving to a test. If they can only guess, return to one visual model and one worked example. Short practice with feedback usually works better than a long worksheet completed without review.
