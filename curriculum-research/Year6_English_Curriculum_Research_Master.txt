CODE: AC9E6LA02
DESCRIPTOR: Understand the uses of objective and subjective language, and identify bias.
STATUS: AUTHORING-READY
==================================================
Scope: fact, opinion, objective/subjective wording, evaluative language, connotation, bias, claim and evidence. IXL worked example checked: facts are verifiable; evaluative wording signals an opinion. Traps: objective tone can still select biased facts; plausible is not the same as verifiable; emotive wording is not evidence. Build original headline/review/report/social-post comparisons, connotation scales, neutral revision and reader-effect explanation; 48 practice/16 separate test. Boundary: bias is not merely any stated opinion.

CODE: AC9E6LA03
DESCRIPTOR: Explain how texts across the curriculum are typically organised into characteristic stages and phases depending on purposes, recognising how authors often adapt text structures and language features.
STATUS: AUTHORING-READY
==================================================
Scope: purpose, audience, stage/phase, structure and language features. IXL evidence: purpose, formality/tone, compare/contrast, cause/effect, problem/solution, text structures, figure-of-speech effect and genre comparison. Worked example: an informational health text was classified cause/effect, with relationship language represented in a cause/effect table. Traps: one connective mistaken for the whole-text relationship; rigid one-structure assumption; topic confused with purpose. Build annotated text, stage flow, comparison and revision tasks; 48 practice/16 separate test. Boundary: no fixed five-paragraph formula.

CODE: AC9E6LA04
DESCRIPTOR: Understand that cohesion can be created by the intentional use of repetition, and the use of word associations.
STATUS: AUTHORING-READY
==================================================
1. Intent: explain how repeated key terms, synonyms, pronouns and associated-word fields guide a reader through a text.
2. Knowledge: cohesion, repetition, lexical chain, synonym, antonym, association, reference, topic, paragraph link.
3. IXL mapping: Choose synonym, Find synonyms in context, Choose antonym, Find antonyms in context. Worked evidence opened: Find synonyms in context explains that synonyms have the same or near-same meaning and requires a context-fit choice rather than isolated word matching.
4. Misconceptions: repetition always makes writing poor; any two related words create cohesion; use of a synonym that changes the precise meaning.
5. Representations: colour-coded lexical chains, paragraph-link map, word-field sort, revised cohesive/non-cohesive versions.
6. Coverage: identify link words; trace chains; choose a context-accurate substitution; revise/reorder a paragraph; explain reader guidance. 48 practice/16 independent test.
7. Boundary: do not require advanced systemic-functional terminology.
8. Sources: IXL Australia Year 6 v9 mapping and R.2 interactive example; Khan supplementary only.
