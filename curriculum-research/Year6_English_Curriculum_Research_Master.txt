CODE: AC9E6LA01
DESCRIPTOR: Understand that language varies as levels of formality and social distance increase.
STATUS: AUTHORING-READY
==================================================
Scope: register, formality, social distance, audience, purpose, tone, modality and evidence-based explanation. IXL: formality, tone, fact/opinion, related-word difference and connotation. Khan: inference from textual clues, supplementary only. Traps: one-word judgements and calling informal varieties incorrect. Build original paired messages/dialogue/notices, revision and justification tasks; 48 practice/16 separate test. Boundary: no prescriptive dialect claims.

CODE: AC9E6LA02
DESCRIPTOR: Understand the uses of objective and subjective language, and identify bias.
STATUS: AUTHORING-READY
==================================================
Scope: fact, opinion, objective/subjective wording, evaluative language, connotation, bias, claim and evidence. IXL worked example checked: facts are verifiable; evaluative wording signals an opinion. Traps: objective tone can still select biased facts; plausible is not the same as verifiable; emotive wording is not evidence. Build original headline/review/report/social-post comparisons, connotation scales, neutral revision and reader-effect explanation; 48 practice/16 separate test. Boundary: bias is not merely any stated opinion.

CODE: AC9E6LA03
DESCRIPTOR: Explain how texts across the curriculum are typically organised into characteristic stages and phases depending on purposes, recognising how authors often adapt text structures and language features.
STATUS: AUTHORING-READY
==================================================
1. Intent: explain why a purpose-led text uses particular stages, structures and language features; recognise adaptation rather than applying a rigid formula.
2. Knowledge: purpose, audience, stage, phase, introduction, development, conclusion, sequence, cause/effect, compare/contrast, problem/solution, feature, cohesion.
3. IXL mapping: formality/tone/purpose; compare/contrast; cause/effect; problem/solution; identify text structures; figure-of-speech effect; compare genres.
4. Worked example checked: IXL “Identify text structures” uses an informational health passage. The explanation identifies cause/effect through relationship language and represents it in a cause/effect table. It contrasts the structural signals for cause/effect, compare/contrast, problem/solution and sequence.
5. Misconceptions: identify one connective then ignore the whole-text relationship; assume every text fits one pure structure; confuse topic with purpose.
6. Representations: annotated text, stage/phase flow, structure-signal table, two genre versions of one topic, paragraph reorder.
7. Coverage: identify/justify structure; match purpose to stages; compare adapted structures; revise for clearer staging; use language evidence. 48 practice and 16 independent test items must include literary, informative and persuasive texts.
8. Boundary: do not require a fixed five-paragraph formula or advanced discourse analysis.
9. Sources: IXL Australia Year 6 v9 mapping and E.4 worked example; Khan supplementary reading course.
