AC9E7LA03: Identify and describe how texts are structured differently depending on their purpose and how language features vary in texts

What students learn

1. Outcome Overview & Core Concepts

Students learn to identify and describe how texts are structured differently depending on their purpose and how language features vary in texts. They identify precise evidence and explain or evaluate how a language, structural, literary or multimodal choice shapes meaning, representation, audience response or purpose. Key concepts include purpose, context, language, text structure, literary, argument.

Learning target: identify and describe how texts are structured differently depending on their purpose and how language features vary in texts

2. Subject-Specific Content & Key Examples

2. Subject-Specific Content & Key Examples

Opinion plus reason

Move from “I liked the story” to “I found the ending effective because the repeated warning built suspense.” The second response gives a judgement and evidence.

Strength of judgement

Compare “might help”, “should help” and “will help”. The modal choice changes how certain or forceful the claim sounds.

Subjective and objective wording

“The brilliant new playground is exciting” evaluates; “the playground opened on Monday” reports a checkable fact. Discuss why a writer may choose either.

Transfer

Students underline one evaluative word in a fresh sentence, replace it with a weaker or stronger choice and explain the changed effect.

3. Common Student Misconceptions

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • Feature spotting: Explain how the exact feature shapes meaning, communication or response.
  • Retelling: Select precise evidence and analyse it rather than repeating content.
  • Automatic effect: Interpret choices through audience, purpose, context and surrounding text.
  • Vague explanation: Replace “this makes it better” with the exact feature, evidence and likely effect.
4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence

4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence

Step 1: Explicit Teaching & Modelling

Define the outcome-specific metalanguage (purpose, context, language) and model how to identify the feature, select evidence and explain or use it.

Step 2: Guided Analysis

Work through a second example together: What is the choice or feature? Where is the evidence? What does it do here?

Step 3: Collaborative / Independent Practice

Students annotate, compare, read, edit, speak or compose with a fresh example, then apply the concept independently.

Step 4: Formative Assessment

Use an unseen micro-text, oral task or creation task. Require precise evidence plus a justified explanation or deliberate crafting choice.

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: examining the structures of book or film reviews and how they might move from description of context to summary of the text and then to judgement of the text
  • E2: recognising the social purpose of a persuasive text and how the purpose is reflected in the text structure and by the language features; for example, analysing the structure and language features of advertising posters
  • E3: describing the structure and language features of literary texts, arguments, discussions, creative and analytical responses to literary texts, films or popular media, and discussing how the structure and language serves the purpose of the text
Practice Thought & Formative Assessment

Practice Thought & Formative Assessment

Use a fresh example that has not been modelled. Ask the student to show the exact clue, feature or language choice and explain or use it independently.
