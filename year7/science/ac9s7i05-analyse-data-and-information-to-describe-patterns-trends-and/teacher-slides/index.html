Year 7 Science · AC9S7I05

Analyse patterns, trends, relationships and anomalies

Move from reading values to making evidence-based statements about overall behaviour.

Learning intention & success

Students distinguish pattern, trend, relationship and anomaly; support descriptions with data; use mean/range where useful; investigate unusual results before exclusion; and avoid claiming causation from correlation alone.

Four analysis words
  • Pattern: repeated arrangement or regularity.
  • Trend: overall direction through ordered data.
  • Relationship: how one variable changes with another.
  • Anomaly: a value inconsistent with the overall pattern.
Model an evidence sentence

Masses 6 kg and 12 kg moving at the same speed have kinetic energies 12 J and 24 J.

Model: When speed is held constant, kinetic energy increases with mass; doubling mass from 6 to 12 kg corresponds to doubling kinetic energy from 12 to 24 J in these data.

Strong analysis names variables, states direction/form and quotes values.
Repeated data, mean & range

Means can summarise repeated trials, while range shows spread. Always inspect raw repeats: an average can hide variation or an anomaly.

One high value is not itself a trend. A flat mean does not prove every repeated measurement was identical.

Anomaly decision sequence
  1. Check transcription and units.
  2. Check instrument and method.
  3. Repeat or compare with other trials where possible.
  4. Consider whether the point could be a real rare event.
  5. Retain and flag it, or justify exclusion transparently.

Never delete an unusual value merely because it makes the graph look untidy.

Correlation ≠ causation

If two variables change together, describe an association unless the design and controls support a causal conclusion. Consider confounding variables and alternative explanations.

Teacher ask: Ice-cream sales and sunburn both rise in summer. What third factor can explain both?
