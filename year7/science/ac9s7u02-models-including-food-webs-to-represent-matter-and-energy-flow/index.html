What students learn in AC9S7U02

This unit helps students build a clear, usable understanding of Use models, including food webs, to represent matter and energy flow in ecosystems and predict the impact of changing abiotic and biotic factors on populations. The goal is not to memorise one answer pattern. Students should be able to explain the idea, recognise it in a new example and apply it in a short practice or homework task.

  • Start with the main idea: use models, including food webs, to represent matter and energy flow in ecosystems and predict the impact of changing abiotic and biotic factors on populations.
  • Use concrete examples, pictures, oral explanation and short written responses before moving to independent practice.
  • analysing food webs to show feeding relationships between organisms in an ecosystem and the role of microorganisms modelling how energy flows into and out of an ecosystem via the pathways of food webs
  • Finish with a mixed activity so students must choose the correct strategy rather than copy the last example.
Curriculum coverage and elaborations

The content description and elaborations below show the curriculum ideas taught in this unit. Items marked as teaching context support lesson planning.

  • Content description: use models, including food webs, to represent matter and energy flow in ecosystems and predict the impact of changing abiotic and biotic factors on populations
  • E1: analysing food webs to show feeding relationships between organisms in an ecosystem and the role of microorganisms
  • E2: modelling how energy flows into and out of an ecosystem via the pathways of food webs
  • E3: predicting the effects on local ecosystems when living things such pollinators or predators are removed from or die out in an area
  • E4: examining how events such as seasonal changes, destruction of habitat or introduction of a species impact abiotic and biotic factors and cause changes to populations
  • E5: investigating First Nations Australians’ responses to invasive species and their effect on food webs that many communities are a part of, and depend on, for produce and medicine (teaching context)
  • E6: considering how First Nations Australians’ fire management practices over tens of thousands of years have changed the distribution of flora and fauna in most regions of Australia (teaching context)

Common mistakes to watch for
  • Describing what is seen without connecting it to a question, pattern or prediction.
  • Using everyday words only and missing simple scientific vocabulary.
  • Changing more than one thing in an investigation, which makes the comparison unfair.
