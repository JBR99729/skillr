Circles, circumference and π

Learn how the centre, radius, diameter and circumference are connected, why the ratio C ÷ d is always π, and how to use C = πd = 2πr to solve Year 7 problems.

What this skill means

A circle is the set of points that are the same distance from a centre point. That fixed distance is the radius (r). The diameter (d) goes all the way across the circle through the centre, so d = 2r. The distance around the circle is its circumference (C).

For every circle, the circumference is a little more than 3 times the diameter. The exact constant ratio is called π (pi):

π = C ÷ d, so C = πd. Because d = 2r, we can also write C = 2πr.

  • Identify centre, radius, diameter and circumference on a diagram.
  • Use d = 2r and r = d ÷ 2.
  • Use C = πd or C = 2πr to find circumference.
  • Rearrange the relationship to find d = C ÷ π or r = C ÷ (2π).
  • Explain that C and d are proportional and that C ÷ d = π for every circle.
  • Use measurement to estimate π and judge whether an answer is reasonable.
Prerequisite knowledge
  • Multiply and divide decimals.
  • Substitute a value into a simple formula.
  • Round a decimal to an appropriate number of decimal places.
  • Understand ratios and proportional relationships at an introductory level.
Worked examples

Example 1: radius to diameter and circumference

A circle has radius 7 cm.

Diameter: d = 2r = 2 × 7 = 14 cm.

Circumference: C = 2πr = 2 × π × 7 = 14π ≈ 43.98 cm.

Example 2: diameter to circumference

A circular lid has diameter 12 cm. Use C = πd because the diameter is already known.

C = π × 12 = 12π ≈ 37.70 cm.

Example 3: circumference to radius

A circular track has circumference 31.4 m. Estimate its radius using π ≈ 3.14.

r = C ÷ (2π) = 31.4 ÷ 6.28 = 5 m.

Example 4: discovering π by measurement

Suppose a circular object has diameter 8.0 cm and measured circumference 25.2 cm.

C ÷ d = 25.2 ÷ 8.0 = 3.15. Measurement error means the result may not be exactly π, but it should be close to 3.14.

Why the formulas work

If you measure many circles and divide each circumference by its diameter, the quotient is always approximately 3.14. This constant is π. Therefore C ÷ d = π, which rearranges to C = πd. Since d = 2r, replacing d with 2r gives C = 2πr.

This also shows a proportional relationship: if a circle's diameter doubles, its circumference doubles; if the diameter triples, the circumference triples.

Choose the right strategy
  • Radius given? Use C = 2πr.
  • Diameter given? Use C = πd.
  • Circumference given? Use d = C ÷ π or r = C ÷ (2π).
  • Comparing circles? Think proportionally: circumference changes by the same scale factor as radius and diameter.
  • Physical measurement? Expect a small difference from π because string, rulers and circular objects are measured imperfectly.
Common mistakes and how to fix them
  • Mixing up radius and diameter: the diameter is twice the radius, not the other way around.
  • Using 2πd: use C = πd or C = 2πr; do not double the diameter again.
  • Using area units: circumference is a length, so write cm, m or km, not cm² or m².
  • Rounding too early: keep π on the calculator and round the final answer unless the question gives a value such as 3.14.
  • Assuming π changes: C ÷ d is the same constant for every circle. Differences in experiments come from measurement error.
Apply and transfer

A bicycle wheel has diameter 0.70 m. In one full rotation, the bike travels approximately one circumference of the wheel:

C = πd = π × 0.70 ≈ 2.20 m.

After 100 full rotations, the bike travels about 220 m. The important decision is recognising that one rotation corresponds to one circumference.

15 important questions to solve — with answers

These questions move from essential knowledge to reasoning and transfer. Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. A circle has radius 9 cm. What is its diameter?

    Answer

    18 cm, because d = 2r = 2 × 9.

  2. A circle has diameter 24 m. What is its radius?

    Answer

    12 m, because r = d ÷ 2.

  3. Which quantity is the distance around a circle: radius, diameter or circumference?

    Answer

    Circumference.

  4. A circle has diameter 10 cm. Find its circumference using π ≈ 3.14.

    Answer

    C = πd = 3.14 × 10 = 31.4 cm.

  5. A circle has radius 6 cm. Find its circumference to 1 decimal place.

    Answer

    C = 2πr = 12π ≈ 37.7 cm.

  6. A circular pond has circumference 62.8 m. Find its diameter using π ≈ 3.14.

    Answer

    d = C ÷ π = 62.8 ÷ 3.14 = 20 m.

  7. A circular sign has circumference 47.1 cm. Find its radius using π ≈ 3.14.

    Answer

    r = C ÷ (2π) = 47.1 ÷ 6.28 = 7.5 cm.

  8. Circle A has diameter 5 cm and Circle B has diameter 15 cm. How many times larger is B's circumference than A's?

    Answer

    3 times. The diameter is multiplied by 3, so circumference is also multiplied by 3.

  9. A student measures a diameter of 8.0 cm and circumference of 25.0 cm. Estimate C ÷ d. Is the result reasonable?

    Answer

    25.0 ÷ 8.0 = 3.125. Yes. It is close to π ≈ 3.14; the small difference can be explained by measurement error.

  10. Explain in one sentence what π represents.

    Answer

    π is the constant ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter: π = C ÷ d.

  11. A student writes C = 2πd for a circle with diameter 7 cm. Identify the error and calculate the correct circumference.

    Answer

    The factor 2 is used only with radius. Correctly, C = πd = 7π ≈ 22.0 cm.

  12. A wheel has radius 35 cm. How far does it travel in 20 complete rotations? Give your answer in metres to 1 decimal place.

    Answer

    One rotation: C = 2π(35) = 70π cm. For 20 rotations: 1400π cm ≈ 4398.2 cm = 43.982 m ≈ 44.0 m.

  13. Two students measure the same circle and obtain C ÷ d values of 3.09 and 3.16. Does this mean π has changed? Explain.

    Answer

    No. π is constant. The differing experimental values are caused by measurement uncertainty or error.

  14. A circular garden needs 50 m of edging. What radius should it have? Give your answer to 2 decimal places.

    Answer

    r = 50 ÷ (2π) ≈ 7.96 m.

  15. Enrichment: A circle's diameter increases by 25%. By what percentage does its circumference increase? Justify without choosing a particular diameter.

    Answer

    25%. Since C = πd, circumference is directly proportional to diameter. Multiplying d by 1.25 multiplies C by 1.25.

Australian Curriculum coverage and elaborations

AC9M7M03: describe the relationship between π and the features of circles including the circumference, radius and diameter.

  • E1: recognise and label centre, radius, diameter and circumference; use one measurement to determine related measurements, including d = 2r and r = C ÷ (2π).
  • E2: compare circumference with radius and diameter through practical measurement, such as drawing circles and using string to approximate circumference.
  • E3: investigate π as the constant proportional relationship between circumference and diameter, including historical approximations from Egypt, Babylon, Greece, India and China.
  • E4: investigate applications and significance of circles in everyday life of First Nations Australians, including examples such as basketry, symbols and architecture. Cultural meanings are diverse and should be taught using appropriate local and community-informed sources.
