Descriptive Statistics and Numerical Data

Acquire discrete and continuous numerical data, calculate measures of centre and spread, identify anomalies, compare distributions and choose statistics that represent the data fairly.

LearnHomeworkPracticeTest

What students need to master

Statistics is not just calculating an average. You need to know what type of data you have, calculate summaries accurately, recognise unusual values, compare centre and spread, and decide which summary is meaningful for the context.

The same mean or median can occur in very different distributions, so strong statistical reasoning uses more than one feature.

1. Discrete and continuous numerical data

Discrete data come from counts: number of pets, goals, students or books. Continuous data come from measurement: height, temperature, mass, time or length.

Recorded measurements may be rounded, but rounding does not make the underlying variable discrete. A reaction time written as 0.48 s is still continuous.

2. Mean, median, mode and range

Worked example: for 3,5,5,7,10: mean=6, median=5, mode=5 and range=7.

3. Outliers, anomalies and representative measures

An outlier or anomalous observation lies unusually far from the main data. It can strongly change the mean and range while having less effect on the median.

Example: 12,13,14,15,16,17,60 has median 15 but mean 21. The 60 pulls the mean upward. Median therefore better represents the central cluster.

Do not automatically delete an anomaly. Check measurement, recording, equipment and context. An unusual observation may be an error, or it may be genuine and important.

4. Skewness and distribution shape

A distribution with a long tail toward high values is right-skewed; a long tail toward low values is left-skewed. In many right-skewed distributions the mean is above the median because high values pull the average upward. The reverse is common for left skew.

This is a useful pattern, not an absolute rule that should replace looking at the actual data or display.

5. Same centre, different spread

Consider A: 8,10,12 and B: 1,10,19. Both have mean 10 and median 10. Their ranges are 4 and 18. Therefore the same measures of centre do not imply the same variability or distribution.

When comparing groups, discuss both typical value and spread. If outliers or skewness are present, explain how they affect your choice of statistic.

6. Digital tools, spreadsheets and AI contexts

Spreadsheets and digital tools can calculate statistics quickly, but the result still depends on correct data entry, cell selection, formulas, units and interpretation. Audit a digital mean by checking the data range and independently verifying a simple calculation.

Recommendation or AI systems may use descriptive statistics to summarise typical behaviour and identify anomalies. For viewing times 10,12,15,15,20,22,25,200, the mean is 39.875 while the median is 17.5. Median better represents typical viewing, but the 200-minute session should not automatically be deleted — it may be genuine behaviour worth investigating.

Common mistakes

  • Finding the median before ordering the data.
  • Calling range a measure of centre.
  • Assuming every data set has exactly one mode.
  • Using mean automatically when an extreme outlier dominates it.
  • Deleting anomalies without checking their cause.
  • Assuming equal means imply equal distributions.
  • Calling measured decimal data discrete just because values are rounded.
  • Trusting spreadsheet or AI output without checking inputs and context.

Answer guide

Mean=sum/count; median=middle ordered value; mode=most frequent; range=max−min. For 3,5,5,7,10: mean=6, median=5, mode=5, range=7. Ordering determines middle position. Multiple modes are possible. Range describes extreme-to-extreme spread. Mean is sensitive to outliers; median is resistant. Right skew has a longer high-value tail. Investigate anomalies for error or genuine variation. Same centre does not determine spread. Counts are discrete; measurements are continuous. Check digital formulas, cells, units and context. Robust summaries describe typical behaviour while anomaly systems flag unusual cases.
