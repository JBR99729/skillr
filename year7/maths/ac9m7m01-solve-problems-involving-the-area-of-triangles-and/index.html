Area of Triangles and Parallelograms

Derive the formulas, use perpendicular height correctly, investigate why area can stay unchanged as a shape slants or slides, and solve practical area problems.

Core ideas

Triangle

A = ½bh

A triangle is half a matching parallelogram or rectangle with the same base and perpendicular height.

Parallelogram

A = bh

Cutting and shifting a triangular end turns a parallelogram into a rectangle without changing area.

Perpendicular height

The height is the shortest distance from the base to the opposite side or vertex and meets the base at 90°.

Area invariance

If base and perpendicular height stay fixed, changing the slant or sliding a vertex parallel to the base does not change area.

Worked example

A triangle has base 18 cm and height 7 cm.

A = ½ × 18 × 7 = 63 cm²

If the top vertex slides sideways while remaining 7 cm above the base, the area is still 63 cm² because b and h have not changed.

Common mistakes

  • Using the slanted side instead of the perpendicular height.
  • Forgetting the ½ in the triangle formula.
  • Writing linear units instead of square units.
  • Assuming a shape with a larger perimeter must have a larger area.
  • Assuming any sideways movement changes area even when base and height are fixed.
