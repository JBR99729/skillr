Relationships Between Variables in Graphs

Read authentic graphs carefully: identify what changes, how fast it changes, when it stays constant, and what story the graph supports.

PracticeHomeworkTest

Core ideas

Positive slope

As x increases, y increases.

Negative slope

As x increases, y decreases.

Horizontal segment

The plotted y-value is constant.

Steepness

Shows rate of change when axes and scale are understood.

Changing slope

Means the rate of change itself is changing.

Story behind the graph

Interpret using context, but separate evidence from possible causes.

Worked interpretation

A distance-from-home graph rises steeply, stays horizontal, then falls gently to zero.

Interpretation: the traveller moves quickly away from home, stops, then returns home more slowly. The horizontal segment means distance from home did not change; the gentler return slope indicates lower speed.

Linear models and fitted lines

For a line such as y = 3x + 10, slope 3 means predicted y increases by 3 for each 1-unit increase in x. The intercept 10 is the predicted value when x = 0.

Important: a steeper slope does not mean a stronger relationship. Strength/model fit depends on how closely the data follow the pattern.

Common mistakes

  • Calling a horizontal segment “zero” instead of “constant”.
  • Ignoring axis labels, units or scale.
  • Assuming every graph pattern proves its cause.
  • Confusing slope size with strength of association.
  • Reading a rate graph as though it were a total-amount graph.

Curriculum connections

International connections: closely related to lower-secondary graph interpretation and rate-of-change work in England's Key Stage 3, US middle-school functions/data standards, and comparable secondary mathematics programs that interpret real-world relationships from graphs.
