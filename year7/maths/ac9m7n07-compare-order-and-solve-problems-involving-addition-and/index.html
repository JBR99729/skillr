Comparing, Adding and Subtracting Integers

Compare signed numbers, reason about magnitude and direction, and solve integer addition and subtraction problems using number lines and real contexts.

Key ideas

Compare and order

Numbers further right on a number line are greater. For negatives, the value closer to zero is greater.

−8 < −3 < 0 < 5

Sign and magnitude

The sign tells direction relative to zero. Magnitude is distance from zero.

|−12| = 12

Add integers

Positive movement goes right; negative movement goes left.

−7 + 10 = 3

Subtract integers

Subtracting a negative reverses direction.

4 − (−6) = 4 + 6 = 10

Worked examples

Compare: −11 < −4 because −11 lies further left.

Add: −8+5=−3. The magnitudes differ by 3 and the larger magnitude is negative.

Subtract: −7−(−12)=−7+12=5.

Context: a temperature of −6°C rises by 11°C, so the new temperature is 5°C.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking −12 is greater than −7 because 12 is larger than 7.
  • Calling magnitude “negative”; magnitude is a distance and is non-negative.
  • Forgetting that subtracting a negative becomes addition.
  • Confusing distance travelled with signed displacement.
  • Ignoring context words such as drop, rise, debt, deposit or below sea level.

High-yield

Important questions

  1. Order −14, 6, −2, 0 and −9 from least to greatest.
  2. Explain the difference between the sign and magnitude of −12.
  3. Why is −3 greater than −8?
  4. Evaluate −8+5 and explain the sign of the answer.
  5. Evaluate 7−(−6) and explain why subtracting a negative increases the value.
  6. Find the distance between −9 and 4.
  7. A temperature changes from −7°C to 5°C. What is the change?
  8. A balance is −$18 and a $25 deposit is made. What is the new balance?
  9. Evaluate −12+7−(−5)−9.
  10. A diver moves from −20 m to −5 m and then to −18 m. Distinguish total distance travelled from final displacement.
