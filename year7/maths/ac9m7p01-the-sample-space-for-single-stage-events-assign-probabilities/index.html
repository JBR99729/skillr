What students learn in AC9M7P01

This unit helps students build a clear, usable understanding of Identify the sample space for single-stage events; assign probabilities to the outcomes of these events and predict relative frequencies for related events. The goal is not to memorise one answer pattern. Students should be able to explain the idea, recognise it in a new example and apply it in a short practice or homework task.

  • Start with the main idea: identify the sample space for single-stage events; assign probabilities to the outcomes of these events and predict relative frequencies for related events.
  • Use concrete examples, pictures, oral explanation and short written responses before moving to independent practice.
  • discussing the meaning of probability terminology; for example, “probability”, “sample space”, “favourable outcome”, “trial”, “experiment” and “event” listing sample spaces for games involving throwing a coin or a dice, spinners, or lucky dip
  • Finish with a mixed activity so students must choose the correct strategy rather than copy the last example.
Curriculum coverage and elaborations

The content description and elaborations below show the curriculum ideas taught in this unit. Items marked as teaching context support lesson planning.

  • Content description: identify the sample space for single-stage events; assign probabilities to the outcomes of these events and predict relative frequencies for related events
  • E1: discussing the meaning of probability terminology; for example, “probability”, “sample space”, “favourable outcome”, “trial”, “experiment” and “event”
  • E2: listing sample spaces for games involving throwing a coin or a dice, spinners, or lucky dip
  • E3: exploring how relative frequencies can be used to make predictions by estimating the probability of an event; for example, in natural language processing (NLP), predictive text algorithms use the relative frequency of words in a set of texts to predict the next word in a sentence
  • E4: assigning the probability for throwing a 6 on a dice and using this to predict the number of times a 6 will occur when a dice is thrown multiple times
