AC9M7N02 • Year 7 Maths • Number

Prime Factorisation and Powers of Primes

Represent natural numbers as products of prime powers, recognise common prime-power sequences, and use prime structure to solve HCF and LCM problems.

Topic guideCheat sheetImportant questionsHomeworkPracticeTest

Key ideas

Prime factorisation

Break a natural number into prime factors and write repeated factors using exponents.

360 = 2³ × 3² × 5

Prime powers

Know common powers of 2, 3 and 5, such as 2⁷=128, 3⁵=243 and 5⁴=625.

HCF

Use the lowest common exponent of each shared prime.

LCM

Use the highest exponent required for every prime present.

Worked examples

Prime factorisation: 840 = 84×10 = (2²×3×7)(2×5) = 2³×3×5×7.

HCF: 48=2⁴×3 and 180=2²×3²×5, so HCF = 2²×3 = 12.

LCM: 24=2³×3 and 90=2×3²×5, so LCM = 2³×3²×5 = 360.

Common mistakes

  • Using composite factors such as 4 or 6 in the final prime factorisation.
  • Writing 2×2×2×3 as 2³+3 instead of 2³×3.
  • Taking the largest exponent for HCF instead of the smallest common exponent.
  • Forgetting a prime that appears in only one number when finding LCM.
  • Counting repeated prime factors as different distinct primes.

High-yield

Important questions

  1. Prime-factorise 360 and write the answer using exponent notation.
  2. Which number is represented by 2⁴×3²?
  3. Explain why 192 is not a power of 2.
  4. Find the HCF of 84 and 210 using prime factorisation.
  5. Find the LCM of 56 and 98 using prime factorisation.
  6. If A=2⁴×3 and B=2²×3³, find HCF(A,B) and explain your exponent choices.
  7. If A=2³×5² and B=2⁵×5, find LCM(A,B).
  8. 360=2³×3²×5. How many positive factors does 360 have?
  9. A bell rings every 18 minutes and another every 30 minutes. When will they next ring together?
  10. Explain in words why HCF uses minimum exponents while LCM uses maximum exponents.

  • E2: develop familiarity with powers of 2, 3 and 5.
  • E3: solve HCF/GCD and LCM problems by comparing prime factorisations.
