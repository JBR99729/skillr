AC9M7N08 • Year 7 Maths • Number

Recognise, represent and solve problems involving ratios

Understand ratios as relationships between quantities, connect them to fractions of a whole, share quantities proportionally and solve realistic mixture problems.

PracticeTestHomeworkTeacher slides

What students learn

E1 — Represent ratios

Use counters, diagrams and bar models to show relationships such as 1:4 and 1:1:2.

E2 — Connect ratios and fractions

Distinguish part-part from part-whole. In 2:3, the first group is 2/5 of the whole.

E3 — Share in a ratio

Add total parts, find one part, then scale each share.

E4 — Apply ratios

Use ratios in recipes, mixtures, money sharing and other realistic contexts.

Core method

For a:b with total T: total parts = a+b → one part = T/(a+b) → shares = a×one part and b×one part.

Example: Share $84 in the ratio 3:4. Total parts=7, one part=$12, so the shares are $36 and $48.

Part-whole connection: In 3:4, the first share is 3/7 of the whole and the second is 4/7.

Cheat sheet

Equivalent ratios

Multiply or divide every part by the same non-zero factor.

Simplest form

Divide all parts by their greatest common factor.

Fraction of whole

selected ratio part ÷ sum of all ratio parts.

Reverse ratio

Use a known quantity to find one part, then scale the unknown group.

Check

All shares must add back to the original total.

Common trap

1:4 does not mean 1/4 of the whole; it means 1 out of 5 total parts.

Important questions

  1. Explain the difference between a part-part ratio and a part-whole fraction.
  2. Simplify 18:30.
  3. Give two ratios equivalent to 3:5.
  4. In the ratio 2:7, what fraction of the whole is the first group?
  5. Share $132 in the ratio 5:6.
  6. Red:blue=4:7 and red=28. Find blue.
  7. A 900 mL drink has concentrate:water=2:7. Find both amounts.
  8. Explain why dividing a total by only one ratio number is usually wrong.
  9. Compare 1:2 and 2:5 concentrations. Which has the greater first-part proportion?
  10. Create a real context for 1:1:2 and represent it with a bar model.

Common mistakes

  • Treating a:b as the fraction a/b of the whole instead of a/(a+b).
  • Scaling only one side of a ratio.
  • Forgetting to add all parts in a three-part ratio.
  • Giving shares that do not add back to the total.
  • Reversing the order of labelled quantities.
