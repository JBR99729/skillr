Transformations in the Cartesian Plane

Describe translations, reflections and rotations using coordinates, investigate equivalent transformation sequences and create geometric patterns.

LearnHomeworkPracticeTest

What students need to master

A transformation maps every point of an original figure (the pre-image) to an image. In this unit, translations, reflections and rotations are rigid transformations: they preserve lengths, angle measures, area and congruence.

Descriptions must be precise. A translation needs a displacement; a reflection needs a mirror line; a rotation needs a centre, angle and direction.

1. Translations — E1

A translation slides every point by the same vector.

Rule: (x,y) → (x+a,y+b). Positive a moves right, negative a moves left; positive b moves up, negative b moves down.

Example: (2,4)→(7,9) has displacement (+5,+5).

Translations preserve orientation as well as size and shape. To check a translated polygon, confirm that every corresponding vertex has the same displacement vector.

2. Reflections — E1

A reflection flips a figure across a mirror line.

  • x-axis: (x,y)→(x,−y)
  • y-axis: (x,y)→(−x,y)

Points on the mirror line stay fixed. Corresponding points lie the same perpendicular distance from the mirror line. A reflection preserves size and shape but reverses orientation.

Example: reflecting (−4,7) in the y-axis gives (4,7).

3. Rotations — E1

A rotation turns points through a fixed angle about a centre. About the origin:

RotationCoordinate rule
90° anticlockwise(x,y)→(−y,x)
180°(x,y)→(−x,−y)
270° anticlockwise / 90° clockwise(x,y)→(y,−x)

Rotations preserve size, shape and orientation. Every point remains the same distance from the centre of rotation.

Example: (3,2) rotated 90° anticlockwise about the origin becomes (−2,3).

4. Equivalent transformations — E2

Different sequences can sometimes produce the same final mapping.

  • Two reflections in the same line give the identity transformation.
  • Two reflections in parallel lines give a translation. Its distance is twice the separation of the lines.
  • Two reflections in intersecting lines give a rotation about their intersection; the rotation angle is twice the angle between the lines.
  • Reflection in the x-axis followed by reflection in the y-axis gives a 180° rotation about the origin.

Example: reflect across x=1 then x=5. The mirror lines are 4 units apart, so the net effect is an 8-unit translation to the right.

5. Combining transformations — E2–E3

Order matters. For a multi-step transformation, use the image from one step as the input to the next.

Example: rotate (3,−4) 90° anticlockwise: (3,−4)→(4,3). Then reflect in the x-axis: (4,3)→(4,−3).

A reflection combined with a translation parallel to its mirror line forms a glide reflection. Repeated rotations can produce radial patterns; repeated translations can produce border or tessellation patterns.

6. Digital tools and pattern investigation — E1 & E3

Dynamic geometry and graphing tools let students apply transformations, drag points, record coordinates and test conjectures. They are especially useful for checking whether two different transformation sequences produce exactly the same image.

To establish equivalence, do not check only one point: corresponding vertices of the whole figure must finish at the same coordinates.

Common mistakes

  • Changing the wrong coordinate in an axis reflection.
  • Confusing 90° clockwise with 90° anticlockwise.
  • Omitting the centre or direction when describing a rotation.
  • Applying a rule to only one vertex of a polygon.
  • Performing a multi-step sequence in the wrong order.
  • Assuming reflection preserves orientation.
  • Claiming two transformation sequences are equivalent after checking only one point.

Australian Curriculum coverage

  • AC9M7SP03: describe transformations of sets of points using coordinates, translations, axis reflections and rotations about a given point.
  • E1: use digital tools to transform shapes and describe/record transformations.
  • E2: investigate patterns and different ways of producing the same transformation, including two successive reflections producing a translation.
  • E3: experiment with and recreate patterns using combinations of translations, reflections and rotations.

International curriculum connections

Comparable middle-years geometry internationally includes rigid transformations on coordinate grids, describing images and pre-images, composition of transformations, symmetry and pattern generation. Exact coordinate-rule expectations can vary by jurisdiction, so AC9M7SP03 remains the authoritative alignment for this resource.
