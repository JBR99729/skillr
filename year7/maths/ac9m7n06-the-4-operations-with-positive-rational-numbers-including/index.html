Operations with Rational Numbers: Fractions, Decimals and Percentages

Use all four operations with positive rational numbers and choose the representation that makes each calculation efficient.

What students learn in AC9M7N06

Students solve problems using addition, subtraction, multiplication and division with positive rational numbers, including fractions, decimals and percentages. They choose an efficient representation, explain their strategy and estimate to check reasonableness.

  • Recognise equivalent fraction, decimal and percentage representations.
  • Add and subtract fractions using equal-sized parts.
  • Multiply fractions and divide by the reciprocal.
  • Operate accurately with decimals and percentages.
  • Choose efficient strategies in practical problems.
  • Simplify exact answers and estimate to check.
Curriculum accuracy

Core AC9M7N06 Positive fractions, decimals and percentages using all four operations.

Extension only: negative rational numbers, additive inverses and signed multi-step calculations. AC9M7N06 is a Year 7 outcome; foundational rules are retrieval, while representation choice and efficiency are the required level.
What is a rational number?

A rational number can be written as a/b, where a and b are integers and b ≠ 0. Whole numbers, integers, fractions, terminating decimals and recurring decimals are rational.

Fraction

3/4

Decimal

0.75

Percentage

75%

Equivalent

3/4 = 0.75 = 75%

Division by zero is undefined, so a denominator cannot be zero.

Standard form

A fraction is in standard form when numerator and denominator have no common factor greater than 1 and the denominator is positive.

8/12 = (8 ÷ 4)/(12 ÷ 4) = 2/3

The greatest common factor is 4.

Choose the efficient representation
  • 12.5% of 96 → use 1/8 of 96 = 12.
  • 0.375 + 7/8 → use decimals because 7/8 = 0.875.
  • 3/5 of 45 → divide by 5, then multiply by 3.
  • 25% of 64 → use 1/4 of 64 = 16.
Choose form → calculate → simplify → estimate and check
Adding and subtracting fractions

Same denominator

Keep the denominator and operate on numerators.

5/7 + 3/7 = 8/7

7/9 − 2/9 = 5/9

Different denominators

Find the LCM and create equivalent fractions.

1/3 + 1/4 = 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12

Worked addition:

5/6 + 3/5 = 25/30 + 18/30 = 43/30

Worked subtraction:

5/6 − 3/5 = 25/30 − 18/30 = 7/30

Multiplying fractions

Multiply top by top and bottom by bottom. Cross-cancel common factors first when possible.

(a/b) × (c/d) = (a × c)/(b × d)

5/6 × 9/10 = 1/2 × 3/2 = 3/4

Dividing fractions

Keep the first fraction, change division to multiplication and replace the divisor with its reciprocal.

(a/b) ÷ (c/d) = (a/b) × (d/c), where c ≠ 0

9/2 ÷ 2/3 = 9/2 × 3/2 = 27/4

2/9 ÷ 3/2 = 2/9 × 2/3 = 4/27

Flip only the divisor. Division by zero is undefined.

Decimals and percentages
  • Align decimal points for addition and subtraction.
  • Estimate before multiplying decimals.
  • For decimal division, scale both values by the same power of 10.
  • Convert percentages to useful fractions or decimals.

4.8 ÷ 0.12 = 480 ÷ 12 = 40

35% of 240 = 84

Identity and inverse properties
  • Additive identity: r + 0 = r.
  • Multiplicative identity: r × 1 = r.
  • Multiplicative inverse: r × 1/r = 1 for r ≠ 0.
  • Reciprocal: the reciprocal of a/b is b/a when a ≠ 0.
Signed extension: r + (−r) = 0, so −r is the additive inverse of r.
Efficient properties and strategies
  • Use commutative and associative properties to regroup friendly values.
  • Partition percentages, for example 17.5% = 10% + 5% + 2.5%.
  • Cross-cancel before multiplying fractions.
  • Use compatible numbers and estimate before accepting an answer.
Worked classroom examples

5/7 + 3/7 = 8/7

5/6 + 3/5 = 43/30

2/8 × 5/6 = 1/4 × 5/6 = 5/24

9/2 ÷ 2/3 = 27/4

Signed extension:

2/8 × (−5/6) = −5/24

13/3 − (−24/9) + 17/6 = 59/6 = 9 5/6

Real-life scenarios
  • Recipes: scale fractional quantities across batches.
  • Shopping: calculate discounts, mark-ups and final prices.
  • Paint: use fractions or decimals of a tin and compare cost per litre.
  • Land use: calculate a fraction or percentage of an area.
  • Nutrition: total decimal amounts across meals.
  • Energy accounts: combine increases and decreases.

Students should state what each answer means and include units.

Suggested teaching sequence
  1. Retrieve equivalent fractions, decimals and percentages.
  2. Review standard form and common denominators.
  3. Teach addition and subtraction with models and symbols.
  4. Develop multiplication through “fraction of” contexts.
  5. Teach division through grouping and reciprocal reasoning.
  6. Connect decimal and percentage strategies.
  7. Compare correct methods for efficiency.
  8. Finish with mixed real-life problems where the operation is not named.
Curriculum coverage and elaborations

The original AC9M7N06 curriculum coverage remains intact.

  • Content description: use the 4 operations with positive rational numbers including fractions, decimals and percentages to solve problems using efficient calculation strategies.
  • E1: solve addition and subtraction problems involving fractions and decimals using arrays, algebra tiles, digital tools or informal jottings.
  • E2: choose an efficient representation such as 12.5%, 1/8, 0.125 or 125/1000.
  • E3: use properties, place value, patterns and facts for multiplication and division with fractions and decimals.
  • E4: solve multiplicative problems using models, calculators or informal jottings.
  • E5: use regrouping, partitioning and properties for additive problems.
  • E6: solve practical problems efficiently, including land use, nutrition and energy-account contexts.

Common mistakes
  • Adding or subtracting denominators.
  • Finding a common denominator for multiplication.
  • Flipping the first fraction or both fractions in division.
  • Forgetting that division by zero is undefined.
  • Not simplifying the final fraction.
  • Misaligning decimal place values.
  • Treating 12.5% as 12.5 instead of 0.125.
  • Choosing an inefficient representation.
  • Accepting calculator output without estimation.
  • Omitting units or context.
Lesson FAQs

Operations on rational numbers? Addition, subtraction, multiplication and division with numbers expressible as a/b, b ≠ 0.

Why common denominators? Addition and subtraction combine equal-sized fraction parts.

Multiplication rule? Multiply numerators and denominators, simplifying common factors.

Division rule? Multiply the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second.

Reciprocal? For non-zero a/b, it is b/a.

Identities? Zero is additive identity; one is multiplicative identity.

Efficient strategy? Choose the fraction, decimal or percentage form that creates compatible numbers.
