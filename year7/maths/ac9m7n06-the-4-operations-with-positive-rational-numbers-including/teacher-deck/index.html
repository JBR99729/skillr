AC9M7N06 • Year 7 Maths

Rational Number Operations

Fractions • decimals • percentages • efficient strategies

Teacher goal: develop flexible thinkers who choose a useful representation, calculate accurately and explain why their strategy is efficient.

Learning intentions

  • Use all four operations with positive rational numbers.
  • Move flexibly between fraction, decimal and percentage forms.
  • Simplify fractions and use common denominators correctly.
  • Choose efficient strategies for practical problems.
  • Estimate to check whether answers are reasonable.

Success criterion: “I can solve the problem and justify why my representation was useful.”

Curriculum lens

Core AC9M7N06: positive fractions, decimals and percentages.

Year 7 expectation: students choose representations and strategies, not merely repeat fraction procedures.

Extension: signed rational numbers and additive inverses may be explored, but should not replace the positive-rational core.

What is a rational number?

A rational number can be written as a/b, where a and b are integers and b ≠ 0.

Fraction

3/4

Decimal

0.75

Percentage

75%

Whole number

5 = 5/1

Ask students why 5/0 is not allowed. The reason is division by zero, not the size of the numerator.

Equivalent representations

1/8⇄0.125⇄12.5%

Equivalent forms are choices that can make a calculation easier.

12.5% of 96 = 1/8 of 96 = 12

Prompt: “Which form would you choose, and why?”

Standard form

A fraction is in standard form when numerator and denominator have no common factor greater than 1.

8/12÷42/3

Require students to name the common factor, not simply say “cancel”.

Add fractions with the same denominator

5/7 + 3/7 = (5 + 3)/7 = 8/7

Keep the denominator because the pieces are already the same size.

Misconception check: why is 8/14 wrong?

Add fractions with different denominators

  1. Find LCM(6, 5) = 30.
  2. Convert 5/6 to 25/30.
  3. Convert 3/5 to 18/30.
  4. Add: 25/30 + 18/30 = 43/30.

We are making equal-sized parts, not merely “matching bottom numbers”.

Subtract fractions

5/6 − 3/5 = 25/30 − 18/30 = 7/30

Use the same common-denominator reasoning as addition.

Estimate first: 0.83 − 0.6 is near 0.23, so 7/30 is sensible.

Multiply fractions

(a/b) × (c/d) = (a × c)/(b × d)

Cross-cancel before multiplying when possible.

5/6 × 9/10 = 1/2 × 3/2 = 3/4

A common denominator is not needed for multiplication.

Divide fractions

Keep→Change ÷ to ×→Flip the divisor

9/2 ÷ 2/3 = 9/2 × 3/2 = 27/4

Ask students to identify the divisor before flipping. Do not allow “flip everything” as a rule.

What is a reciprocal?

2/3

reciprocal = 3/2

5 = 5/1

reciprocal = 1/5

0

has no reciprocal

A non-zero number multiplied by its reciprocal equals 1.

Operate with decimals

  • Add/subtract: align decimal points.
  • Multiply: estimate, multiply as whole numbers, then place the decimal.
  • Divide: scale both numbers by the same power of 10.

4.8 ÷ 0.12 = 480 ÷ 12 = 40

Students should explain why multiplying both values by 100 preserves the quotient.

Percentages as useful fractions

10% = 1/10
12.5% = 1/8
25% = 1/4
37.5% = 3/8
50% = 1/2
75% = 3/4

37.5% of 80 = 3/8 of 80 = 30

Identity and inverse rules

  • Additive identity: r + 0 = r.
  • Multiplicative identity: r × 1 = r.
  • Multiplicative inverse: r × 1/r = 1, for r ≠ 0.

Signed extension: r + (−r) = 0, so −r is the additive inverse of r.

Efficient calculation strategies

  • Use commutative and associative properties to regroup friendly values.
  • Partition percentages into 10%, 5%, 2.5% and other useful parts.
  • Cross-cancel before multiplying fractions.
  • Choose fraction, decimal or percentage form strategically.
  • Estimate before and after calculating.

Compare two correct methods and ask which is more efficient for these numbers.

Worked example: mixed representations

Calculate 0.375 + 7/8.

  1. Recognise that 7/8 has a simple decimal form.
  2. Convert 7/8 = 0.875.
  3. Add 0.375 + 0.875.
  4. Answer: 1.25.

Worked example: mixed-number division

Calculate 1 1/2 ÷ 3/8.

  1. Convert 1 1/2 to 3/2.
  2. Replace division by multiplication by 8/3.
  3. 3/2 × 8/3 = 4.

Answer: 4

Signed-rational extension

Use only after positive-rational fluency is secure.

2/8 × (−5/6) = −10/48 = −5/24

13/3 − (−24/9) + 17/6 = 59/6 = 9 5/6

Make the sign reasoning explicit before the fraction arithmetic.

Real-life scenario: shopping

A jacket costs $85 and is discounted by 20%.

  1. 20% of 85 = 17.
  2. 85 − 17 = 68.

Sale price = $68

Ask what the $17 represents and why it is subtracted.

Real-life scenario: recipe and paint

Recipe

3/4 cup per batch for 3 batches.

3 × 3/4 = 2 1/4 cups

Paint

A 2.5 L tin is 3/4 full.

2.5 × 3/4 = 1.875 L

Students must include units and interpret the result.

Common mistakes

  • Adding denominators.
  • Finding a common denominator for multiplication.
  • Flipping the wrong fraction in division.
  • Forgetting to simplify.
  • Misaligning decimal place values.
  • Reading 12.5% as 12.5 rather than 0.125.
  • Using a calculator answer without checking reasonableness.

Guided practice

  1. Simplify 8/12. 2/3
  2. Calculate 1/3 + 1/4. 7/12
  3. Calculate 2/3 × 4/5. 8/15
  4. Calculate 2/9 ÷ 3/2. 4/27
  5. Find 35% of 240. 84

Ask different students to explain the operation, representation and reasonableness check.

Exit ticket

  1. Why must denominators match for addition?
  2. What is the reciprocal of 5/6?
  3. Calculate 5/6 − 3/5.
  4. Choose an efficient method for 12.5% of 96.
  5. Name one common error and correct it.

Key answers: equal-sized parts; 6/5; 7/30; 1/8 of 96 = 12; explanations vary.
