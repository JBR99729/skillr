1. Perfect squares and square roots are inverse ideas

A perfect square is a whole number multiplied by itself. A square root reverses that operation.

12 × 12 = 12² = 144 ⇄ √144 = 12

12 × 12 = 144 and √144 = 12

Try first: a tiled courtyard

A square courtyard contains 225 equal square tiles. Find its side length and perimeter.

Hint 1 — what operation finds the side?

Use the square root of the area: side = √225.

Hint 2 — reveal the side

√225 = 15, so each side is 15 tile lengths.

Reveal full solution

Perimeter = 4 × 15 = 60 tile lengths.

2. Square two-digit numbers efficiently

Split the number into tens and ones. A 43×43 area model contains 40×40, two 40×3 rectangles and 3×3.

Try: calculate 43² before revealing the steps

Write 43 as 40 + 3.

Reveal expansion

(40+3)² = 40² + 2(40)(3) + 3²

Reveal answer

1600 + 240 + 9 = 1849.

Key structure: (a+b)² = a² + 2ab + b².

Self-check

Which correctly expands 52²?

3. Bound non-perfect square roots

Find the neighbouring perfect squares. For √70, 64 < 70 < 81, so 8 < √70 < 9.

Try: locate √115

Find the perfect squares immediately below and above 115.

Reveal reasoning

10² = 100 and 11² = 121, so 10 < √115 < 11.

Self-check

Which bound is correct for √250?

4. Patterns in square numbers

1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, … have differences 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, … .

(n+1)² − n² = 2n + 1

The first differences rise by 2, so perfect squares have constant second difference 2. Squares can end in 0, 1, 4, 5, 6 or 9, but not 2, 3, 7 or 8.

Self-check

A 14×14 game board is enlarged to 15×15. How many new cells are added?

5. Where this appears in real life

Tiled floor

324 tiles in a square → side 18 → perimeter 72 tile lengths.

Square field

A 1600 m² square field has side √1600 = 40 m, so fencing the boundary needs 160 m.

Game board

A 20×20 board contains 400 cells. Increasing each side to 21 adds 21²−20² = 41 cells.

Square photograph

Tripling the side multiplies area by 3² = 9.

Common mistakes

  • √100 = 50: square root is not halving.
  • 12² = 24: n² means n×n, not 2n.
  • (a+b)² = a²+b²: the 2ab cross-term is missing.
