Perfect Squares and Square Roots

Build square numbers visually, reverse them with square roots, calculate two-digit squares efficiently, estimate non-perfect roots and use the ideas to solve real problems.

1. Perfect squares and square roots are inverse ideas

A perfect square is the product of a whole number multiplied by itself. A square root reverses that operation.

12 × 12 = 12² = 144   ⇄   √144 = 12

The geometry matters: 144 objects can form a 12-by-12 square. That is why the operation is called squaring.

12 × 12 = 144   and   √144 = 12

Move the slider. Notice that when the side changes by 1, the area does not change by 1.

Worked example: recover a side length

A square tiled floor contains 225 tiles. Because 225 = 15², each side has 15 tiles. Its perimeter is therefore 4 × 15 = 60 tile lengths.

2. Square two-digit numbers efficiently

Instead of long multiplication, split a number into tens and ones. An area model for 43 × 43 contains one 40×40 square, two 40×3 rectangles and one 3×3 square.

43² = (40 + 3)² = 40² + 2(40)(3) + 3² = 1600 + 240 + 9 = 1849

Key structure: (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b². The middle term is 2ab because there are two cross-rectangles.

Which is the correct expansion of 52²?

3. Estimate non-perfect square roots

If a number is not a perfect square, locate it between two neighbouring perfect squares.

Example: √70

8² = 64 and 9² = 81. Since 64 < 70 < 81, taking square roots gives 8 < √70 < 9.

Quick check

Between which consecutive integers does √115 lie?

4. Patterns hidden inside square numbers

Perfect squares begin 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, … . Their first differences are 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, … — consecutive odd numbers.

(n + 1)² − n² = 2n + 1

Because the first differences increase by 2 each time, the square-number sequence has a constant second difference of 2. This is a signature of quadratic growth.

Another useful observation: a perfect square can end in 0, 1, 4, 5, 6 or 9, but never 2, 3, 7 or 8. A valid final digit does not prove a number is square; an impossible final digit can rule it out.

Quick check

A 14×14 square grows to 15×15. How many tiles are added?

5. Apply squares and roots to problems

Area → perimeter

Area = 324 m² → side = √324 = 18 m → perimeter = 4×18 = 72 m.

Perimeter → area

Perimeter = 48 cm → side = 48÷4 = 12 cm → area = 12² = 144 cm².

Scale factor

If every side is tripled, area is multiplied by 3² = 9, not 3.

Common mistakes — and how to fix them

  • √100 = 50: square root does not mean divide by 2. Ask “what number times itself equals 100?”
  • 12² = 24: n² means n×n, not 2n.
  • (a+b)² = a²+b²: this misses the two cross-products; use a square area model to see 2ab.
  • √70 = 8 because 64 is nearby: √70 is not a whole number; state the correct bound 8 < √70 < 9.
  • Area and perimeter mixed up: area uses square units; perimeter measures distance around the boundary.
