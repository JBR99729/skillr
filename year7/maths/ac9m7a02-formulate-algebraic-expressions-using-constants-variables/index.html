Formulating Algebraic Expressions

Turn repeated arithmetic, verbal rules and changing relationships into clear algebra using variables, constants, operations and brackets.

PracticeHomeworkTest

Key concepts

Variables and constants

A variable such as x can change. A constant such as 5 stays fixed.

Implied multiplication

3x means 3 × x. The number multiplying the variable is the coefficient.

Brackets matter

3x+4 and 3(x+4) are different: the second multiplies the whole sum by 3.

Words → symbols

“Five less than seven times x” becomes 7x−5.

Worked examples

Generalise: x+x+x+x = 4x.

Translate: “double the sum of x and 8” = 2(x+8).

Simplify: n+(n+2)+(n+4)=3n+6.

Context: a fixed $12 charge plus $4 per person gives C=12+4p.

Important questions

  1. Explain the difference between 3x+4 and 3(x+4).
  2. Simplify x+(x+3)+(x+6).
  3. Translate “seven less than triple a number”.
  4. Write an expression for the product of a number and its successor.
  5. Explain why brackets are required in “half the sum of x and 9”.
  6. Expand 5(2x−3).
  7. Find and correct the error in 4(x+6)=4x+6.
  8. Create an expression with one fixed cost and one variable rate.
  9. Explain what a variable represents in a real context.
  10. Compare how x+4, 3x and x(x+1) change as x increases.

Common mistakes

  • Treating 3x as 3+x.
  • Ignoring brackets when an operation applies to a whole quantity.
  • Reversing “less than” phrases.
  • Combining unlike terms.
  • Using a variable without defining what it represents.

Curriculum connections

Australian Curriculum: AC9M7A02 E1 generalises arithmetic into algebraic expressions; E2 translates mathematical relationships from words to symbols; E3 treats variables as changing quantities and applies expressions in meaningful contexts, including Country/Place.

International connections: closely aligns with lower-secondary algebra expectations in England, New Zealand, Singapore and US middle-school standards: use variables, translate verbal descriptions, apply conventions and reason about equivalent expressions.
