Triangle and Polygon Interior Angles

Demonstrate that the interior angles of a triangle in the plane total 180°, use triangles to derive polygon angle sums, and solve unknown-angle problems with clear geometric reasoning.

Topic guideHomeworkPracticeTest
What you need to know first
  • An interior angle is the angle inside a shape at a vertex.
  • A straight angle measures 180°.
  • Angles can be represented algebraically, for example 2x°, 3x° and (x+20)°.
  • Polygons can be split into triangles by drawing diagonals that do not cross.
1. Why triangle angles add to 180°

The key fact is:

For every triangle in the Euclidean plane, A + B + C = 180°.

Concrete demonstration

Draw and cut out a paper triangle. Tear off its three corners and place the three angle corners next to each other. Their arms form a straight line, showing a total of 180°. Repeating this with different triangle shapes shows the result is not tied to one special triangle.

Worked example

A triangle has angles 50°, 60° and x°.

50 + 60 + x = 180
x = 180 − 110
x = 70°

Check: 50 + 60 + 70 = 180.

2. Algebraic triangle-angle problems

Translate the three angle expressions into one equation whose total is 180°.

Example: angles are 3x°, 4x° and 5x°.

3x + 4x + 5x = 180
12x = 180
x = 15

The actual angles are 45°, 60° and 75°. Do not stop at x unless the question asks only for x.

3. From triangles to polygon angle sums

Choose one vertex of a convex polygon and draw diagonals from that vertex to every non-adjacent vertex. An n-sided polygon is divided into n − 2 triangles.

Each triangle contributes 180°, so:

Interior angle sum = 180(n − 2) = 180n − 360

4. Unknown angles in polygons

Strategy: find the polygon's total angle sum, add the known angles, then subtract.

Example: a pentagon has angles 100°, 120°, 95°, 85° and x°.

Pentagon sum = 180(5−2) = 540°
Known total = 100+120+95+85 = 400°
x = 540−400
x = 140°

Check: 100+120+95+85+140 = 540.

5. Working backwards: find the number of sides

If the angle sum is known, solve 180(n−2)=S.

Example: interior angle sum = 1260°.

180(n−2)=1260
n−2=7
n=9

The shape is a nonagon. This reverse use of the formula is important because it shows understanding of the structure, not just substitution.

6. Regular polygons

A regular polygon has all sides equal and all interior angles equal. Once the total interior-angle sum is known, divide by n:

Each interior angle = 180(n−2) ÷ n

For a regular octagon: 1080÷8 = 135°.

Common mistakes to avoid
  • Using 360° as the triangle total. A triangle totals 180°.
  • Using 180n instead of 180(n−2).
  • Forgetting that the formula gives the total interior angle sum, not each angle.
  • Dividing by n for an irregular polygon. Equal division only works when the polygon is regular.
  • Stopping after finding x when the question asks for the actual angle sizes.
  • Trusting how a diagram looks instead of using the stated angle relationships and calculations.

Curriculum coverage

AC9M7M05: demonstrate that the interior angle sum of a triangle in the plane is 180° and apply this to determine the interior angle sum of other shapes and the size of unknown angles.

  • E1: use concrete materials, such as paper triangles, to demonstrate that the three interior angles form 180°.
  • E2: use decomposition and the triangle angle sum to generalise the interior-angle sum of an n-sided polygon as 180(n−2)=180n−360.
