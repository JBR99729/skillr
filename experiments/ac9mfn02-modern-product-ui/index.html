1 · Understand

What does “subitise” mean?

It means seeing how many without touching and counting each object. Adults often do this with dice. Foundation students build the same skill with collections up to 5.

See 5 as 4 and 1

4 + 1 = 5

Say: “I saw four together and one more. I knew it was five without counting each dot.”

Why this matters

Children stop treating every number as a long counting task and start seeing number structure.

5 can look different and still be 5.

That idea supports later addition, subtraction and part-part-whole thinking.

2 · Teach

Teach it in three moves

1

Flash

Show 1–5 counters or dots for about two seconds, then cover them.

2

Name

Ask, “How many?” Accept instant recognition; don’t turn it into a speed race.

3

Explain

Ask, “What did your eyes notice?” A child might say “2 and 2” or “4 and 1”.

3 · Compare

Different arrangement, same number

Move the same counters without adding or removing any. The amount stays the same even though the picture changes.

Quick check

Show 4 dots in a square. Cover them. Ask how many.

Then rearrange the same four dots into a line and ask again.

What mastery sounds like

“It is still 4. You moved them, but you didn’t add or take any away.”

4 · Fix misconceptions

What to do when a child gets stuck

Counts every dot
Rapid fix: use familiar 2-, 3- and 4-dot patterns. Flash briefly, cover, then ask what shape or parts they noticed.
Thinks rearranging changes the number
Rapid fix: move the same counters in front of the child and say, “Did I add any? Did I take any away?”
Guesses
Rapid fix: slow down and ask for evidence: “What smaller groups did your eyes notice?”
5 · Check

Ready for Practice?

RecogniseFlash 5 in a new arrangement. Can the child name it without one-by-one counting?
MatchCan the child match two different arrangements that both show 4?
ExplainCan the child say what they noticed: “3 and 2”, “4 and 1”, or the whole familiar pattern?
