# SkillrHub Science Topic Page Quality Standard

Applies to every Australian Curriculum v9.0 Science topic page from Foundation to Year 10.

## Purpose

A Science topic page must be a useful standalone mini-lesson, not a curriculum-code placeholder. A student should be able to learn the intended year-level concept from the page, complete supported Practice, and then demonstrate transfer on an independent Test.

## Curriculum alignment gate

For every Science code:

1. Treat the official content description as the required learning target.
2. Audit every official elaboration as a breadth check.
3. Include high-value elaboration concepts that materially improve understanding or are assessed in SkillrHub Practice/Test.
4. Do not claim elaborations are mandatory curriculum requirements; label them as curriculum teaching examples/breadth checks.
5. Never assess content that is absent from the topic page unless it is clearly labelled prerequisite knowledge.
6. Keep extension knowledge clearly separated from the required year-level target.
7. Where an elaboration includes First Nations Australians' knowledge, use respectful, place-aware wording; do not present First Nations knowledge as one uniform national system or as a decorative add-on.

## Required page structure

Every Science topic page should contain, in age-appropriate depth:

- Natural SEO title containing year level, topic phrase and curriculum code.
- Concise meta description describing the actual learning value.
- H1 with a readable topic name rather than the full raw curriculum sentence.
- Short learning summary.
- What students need to know / success criteria.
- Core concepts explained in teacher-authored language.
- Key vocabulary introduced in context.
- Worked examples or worked scientific reasoning.
- A model, diagram, table, comparison or concrete representation where it improves understanding.
- Common misconceptions specific to the code.
- Apply-and-transfer prompts using new contexts.
- Australian Curriculum content description and elaboration coverage.
- Direct links to Teacher Slides, the year-level written resource, Practice and Test.
- **Year 7 Science resource rule:** Homework is the sole written/printable resource. Do not expose a separate Worksheet link; legacy `/worksheet/` URLs may remain as redirects to Homework for compatibility.

## Year-level depth

### Foundation–Year 2
- Concrete observations, pictures and everyday examples.
- Very short explanations.
- Oral language and simple cause/effect or compare/sort reasoning.
- Avoid technical vocabulary unless the curriculum requires it.

### Years 3–6
- Explicit vocabulary and scientific descriptions.
- Simple models, fair comparisons, patterns and evidence.
- Short worked examples that move from observation to explanation.

### Years 7–8
- Complete concept explanation before assessment.
- Models, evidence, classification, mechanisms and limitations where relevant.
- Worked examples and misconception correction.
- Questions should progress from recognition to application and transfer.

### Years 9–10
- Connected explanations, evidence evaluation and multi-step reasoning.
- Model strengths/limitations and competing explanations where curriculum-relevant.
- Quantitative reasoning where required by the code.
- Avoid unnecessary senior-secondary depth.

## Practice and Test relationship

### Practice
- 20–24 questions where the bank size supports it.
- Build from confidence/basic understanding to medium application.
- May directly reinforce examples taught on the topic page.
- Target known misconceptions.
- Feedback should point to the exact concept needed.

### Test
- Use unseen scenarios and representations.
- Do not recycle Practice scenario skeletons with cosmetic wording changes.
- Test independent transfer of the curriculum skill.
- Distractors should be plausible misconceptions, not joke answers.
- Difficulty should come from reasoning, not obscure facts or trick wording.

## SEO standard

SEO is earned through useful content rather than keyword stuffing.

- Use the curriculum code once in title/H1 context and naturally in the page.
- Use a human search phrase in the title (for example, `Year 7 Biological Classification & Dichotomous Keys | AC9S7U01`).
- Keep the canonical URL unchanged unless a separate migration is intentionally planned.
- Use descriptive H2/H3 headings that match real student/teacher search intent.
- Include internal links to the curriculum hub, adjacent topics, teacher resources, Practice and Test.
- Avoid duplicated boilerplate such as generic `use the shared model to foreground...` text.
- Avoid pages whose main content is merely a restatement of the curriculum sentence.

## First Nations content standard

When curriculum content or an elaboration calls for First Nations Australian perspectives:

- State that knowledge systems are diverse and connected to particular Peoples, Countries/Places and languages.
- Prefer locally appropriate and community-approved sources when specific knowledge is used.
- Distinguish knowledge systems respectfully without ranking one as inherently superior.
- Respect cultural authority, permissions and restrictions around recording or sharing knowledge.
- Do not invent cultural examples to fill a template.

## QA sign-off

A Science topic page is PASS only if:

- the required content description is fully taught;
- no major assessed concept is missing;
- elaboration breadth has been reviewed;
- worked examples are scientifically correct;
- misconceptions are topic-specific;
- the page is readable at the intended year level;
- Practice teaches/reinforces the skill;
- Test checks transfer using new contexts;
- title/meta/headings are natural and useful for search;
- links and page resources function.

Use TUNE when the core teaching is sound but breadth, examples, wording or assessment alignment needs improvement. Use REBUILD when a student could complete the page/bank without learning or demonstrating the actual curriculum code.
