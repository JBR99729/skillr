# SkillrHub Mathematics Topic Page Quality Standard (Foundation–Year 10)

## Purpose
Every Mathematics topic page must teach the complete practical skill implied by the Australian Curriculum v9.0 content description before students are asked to demonstrate that skill in Practice or Test.

A page is not complete merely because the curriculum code, content description and elaborations appear on it. It must function as a concise, age-appropriate lesson.

## Core page structure
1. **What this skill means** — plain-language explanation of the mathematical idea.
2. **Prerequisite knowledge** — only what students genuinely need before beginning.
3. **Representations** — concrete, pictorial, numerical, algebraic, graphical or geometric representations as appropriate to the code.
4. **Worked examples** — fully authored examples showing the reasoning, not just answers.
5. **Why the method works** — short conceptual explanation where developmentally appropriate.
6. **Common misconceptions and errors** — code-specific, with corrections.
7. **Choose a strategy** — where multiple valid approaches exist, explain when each is useful.
8. **Apply and transfer** — at least one unfamiliar or contextual example that requires selecting the method rather than copying it.
9. **Australian Curriculum coverage** — content description plus official elaborations as a QA checklist; elaborations are examples, not separate mandatory outcomes.
10. **Practice / Test alignment** — Practice may scaffold; Test must use unseen situations and must not require content absent from the lesson.

## Worked-example minimum
For substantive Mathematics codes, normally include **2–4 genuinely worked examples**. Examples should progress from accessible to more demanding and should expose the decision-making step that students commonly miss.

## Depth progression
### Foundation–Year 2
- Concrete materials, pictures and familiar contexts.
- Strong number/shape/measurement sense before formal procedures.
- One-step reasoning and oral mathematical language.
- Minimal notation beyond the curriculum expectation.

### Years 3–5
- Connect concrete, pictorial and symbolic representations.
- Explain strategies, not only answers.
- Include simple word problems and comparison of methods where useful.
- Introduce mathematical vocabulary explicitly.

### Year 6 transition
- Increase formal notation and connected reasoning.
- Ask students to justify choices and interpret representations.
- Include multi-step examples where the curriculum supports them.
- Begin deliberate transfer to less familiar contexts.

### Years 7–8
- Balance conceptual understanding and procedural fluency.
- Connect tables, graphs, diagrams, equations and verbal descriptions where relevant.
- Include error analysis, method selection and unfamiliar applications.
- Practice should build confidence from basic to medium; Test should assess independent transfer.

### Years 9–10
- Multi-step reasoning, modelling, interpretation and justification.
- Explicit assumptions and limitations in modelling contexts.
- Compare equivalent representations and methods.
- Include error analysis and non-routine transfer without drifting into senior-secondary content unless clearly labelled extension.

## Authoring rules
- Do not restate the curriculum description as the lesson explanation.
- Avoid generic text such as “use the shared model to foreground the concept”.
- Do not create artificial real-world contexts that add reading load without mathematical value.
- Distractors and misconceptions should reflect plausible student errors.
- Do not teach a shortcut before conceptual meaning when that shortcut is likely to create misconceptions.
- Avoid forcing one method when the curriculum permits multiple mathematically valid strategies.
- Use exact notation consistently and ensure every displayed answer matches the worked reasoning.

## SEO standard
- Preserve existing canonical URLs unless there is a compelling migration reason.
- Title and H1 should contain the curriculum code, year level and a natural topic phrase.
- Meta descriptions should describe the actual learning value: concept explanation, worked examples, practice and test.
- Use descriptive H2/H3 headings based on genuine search intent (for example, “How to add fractions with unlike denominators”, not keyword lists).
- Internal links should connect the topic guide to Worksheet, Practice, Test, Teacher Slides and closely related Mathematics codes.
- Never keyword-stuff curriculum wording or create repetitive templated paragraphs solely for search engines.

## Assessment contract
**Lesson:** teaches everything needed for normal Year-level success.

**Practice:** basic → medium progression, targeted misconceptions, hints and feedback allowed.

**Test:** independent, unseen applications; no recycled scenario skeletons or unexplained content.

## Quality gate
A Mathematics topic page is production-ready only when an experienced teacher could answer **yes** to all of the following:
- Does the page teach the actual mathematical skill rather than merely name it?
- Does it cover the practical scope of the curriculum content description?
- Are the elaborations checked for important omitted teaching opportunities?
- Are worked examples correct, clear and developmentally appropriate?
- Are representations connected rather than shown in isolation?
- Are common misconceptions explicitly addressed?
- Could a student reasonably prepare for the linked Practice bank from this page?
- Does the Test assess transfer rather than recognition of rehearsed questions?
- Is the page useful to both a student and a teacher/parent without unnecessary bulk?
- Is the SEO value created by genuinely helpful content rather than repetition?
