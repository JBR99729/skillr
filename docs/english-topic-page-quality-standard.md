# SkillrHub English Topic Page Quality Standard — Foundation to Year 10

## Purpose
Every English curriculum topic page must be a useful, static, student-facing mini lesson for its exact Australian Curriculum Version 9.0 outcome. It must not be a curriculum-code placeholder, a paraphrase of the descriptor, or a generic literacy lesson.

This standard sits under `docs/static-curriculum-architecture-v2.md` and `docs/skillr-long-life-curriculum-content-standard.md`. Existing canonical URLs, analytics, resource links and useful authored content must be preserved.

## Core rule
**Outcome meaning → subject-specific examples/analysis → misconceptions → explicit teaching sequence → formative evidence → closest international English/ELA alignment.**

The content description is the required learning target. Official elaborations are a coverage and teaching-opportunity checklist. Every idea assessed in Practice/Test must be taught on the topic page or be reasonable prerequisite knowledge.

## Required five instructional sections

### 1. Outcome Overview & Core Concepts
- Explain the exact literacy/language/literature skill in direct, age-appropriate prose.
- Define the important English terminology students need for this outcome.
- Explain what successful reading, viewing, speaking, listening or writing looks like for the code.
- Do not use generic filler such as “The goal is not to memorise one answer pattern.”

### 2. Subject-Specific Content & Key Examples
- Include 3–4 concrete examples appropriate to the outcome and year level.
- Examples may include sentence models, short invented text extracts, rhetorical/literary devices, multimodal choices, editing decisions, structural comparisons or analysis moves.
- Explain the effect of the language/text choice; do not merely name a device.
- Use original short examples rather than copyrighted passages.

### 3. Common Student Misconceptions
- Include 3–4 errors specific to this exact outcome.
- Each misconception should have a concise correction or diagnostic cue.
- Never reuse generic “read the question carefully” advice as the main misconception content.

### 4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence
Use these four steps, adapted to the exact outcome:
1. **Explicit Teaching & Modelling** — teacher names the concept and models the thinking or crafting process.
2. **Guided Analysis** — teacher and students analyse examples together using evidence from language/text/mode.
3. **Collaborative / Independent Practice** — students apply the skill to a new example or create/edit their own text.
4. **Formative Assessment** — a short task reveals whether students can independently demonstrate the outcome and explain their choices/evidence.

### 5. Accurate International Curriculum Mapping
Mapping is **closest alignment, not exact equivalence**. English pages must map only to English/ELA/literacy/humanities standards. Never include Mathematics, Science or NGSS references on an English outcome page.

Required jurisdictions:
- Australia — Australian Curriculum v9.0 (exact Skillr code), Victorian Curriculum F–10 English, NSW English syllabus at the appropriate stage.
- United States — Common Core ELA/Literacy at the appropriate grade band (for example RL, RI, W, SL or L standards).
- United Kingdom — appropriate Key Stage / GCSE English Language and/or Literature.
- Canada — closest Grade-level English Language Arts alignment, identified as broad Ontario/BC-style alignment where a single national standard does not exist.
- New Zealand — appropriate NZC English level/phase alignment.
- India — closest NCERT/CBSE English Language and Literature alignment.

Do not invent a precise overseas standard code when the mapping has not been verified. A correct broad closest-alignment statement is preferable to a false exact equivalence.

## Curriculum elaborations
Keep a separate compact curriculum coverage section where useful. Elaborations should be taught through examples or instructional content, not merely pasted into a list. If an elaboration concerns First Nations Australian texts, voices, language or cultural knowledge, preserve the curriculum intent and use respectful, non-tokenistic wording.

## Depth progression
- **Foundation–Year 2:** oral language, phonological awareness, early reading/viewing, simple text purposes, sentence-level meaning and concrete examples. Keep explanations short and highly modelled.
- **Years 3–5:** connect text structures, language features, vocabulary, comprehension and composition. Students begin explaining how choices affect meaning.
- **Year 6:** transition to more explicit metalanguage, evidence-based interpretation, deliberate crafting and comparison.
- **Years 7–8:** analyse relationships among language, structure, audience, purpose, representation and context; require evidence and transfer to unfamiliar texts.
- **Years 9–10:** evaluate effects, compare interpretations, analyse nuanced language/structure/multimodality, justify claims with evidence, and deliberately craft/edit for audience and purpose.

## Static page and SEO requirements
- Teaching content must exist directly in static HTML and remain usable with JavaScript disabled.
- Preserve the existing canonical URL.
- Use a natural title/H1 containing curriculum code, year and a readable topic phrase.
- Meta descriptions should describe the actual skill taught, not just say “topic guide with worksheets”.
- Use descriptive H2/H3 headings and natural terminology students/teachers search for.
- Keep Worksheet, Practice, Test and Teacher Slides links intact.
- Search value must come from useful instructional content, not keyword repetition.

## Release gate / freeze criteria
An English topic page may be marked **FROZEN** only when all of the following pass:
1. Correct Australian Curriculum code, content description and year-level boundary.
2. Important elaboration concepts are explicitly taught or meaningfully represented.
3. No generic boilerplate or cross-subject contamination.
4. 3–4 useful, code-specific examples/analysis moves.
5. 3–4 code-specific misconceptions with corrections.
6. Practical four-step instructional sequence and formative check.
7. International mapping contains English/ELA equivalents only and is framed as closest alignment.
8. Static HTML, canonical URL, analytics and resource links remain intact.
9. Topic page content is consistent with linked Practice/Test expectations.
10. Final human/teacher QA finds no factual, linguistic, cultural or curriculum mismatch.

After freeze, edits should normally be limited to curriculum changes, factual/cultural corrections, broken links, strong teacher/user feedback, or deliberate product-wide standards changes.
