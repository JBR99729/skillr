## Original givens, answers and representation checks

| Code | Independent plan check |
| --- | --- |
| I01 | At the same net force, duration and initial rest state, the supplied mass/speed-change pairs give the same product: 1.5 × 6 = 3 × 3 = 9. A 6 kg prediction of 1.5 m/s is consistent with that ideal halving model. It is a conditional model prediction beyond the two supplied cases, not a collected result or a claim that mass alone fixes speed. No formal acceleration equation is required as core teaching. |
| I02 | Same total 200 g block and contact face, 40 cm in 4 s on a level path, horizontal pull and the central 20 cm reading segment define a reproducible steady-sliding comparison. Repeats, varied order, meter zero/range and raw newton records are explicit. A starting peak is not a steady reading; the repeated pulls do not become independent samples of all materials. The possible-artefact scenario correctly stops disturbance and separates walking approval from collection, recording and sharing authority. |
| I03 | A 0–5 N instrument with 0.1 N divisions is the more useful supplied choice for expected 2–4 N forces, provided the actual force remains within range. A 2.6 N pointer is exactly twenty-six intervals above zero. The original SVG must show all equal intervals and an unambiguous pointer; 2.600 N must not be invented as instrument precision. Repeated 50.4 g readings against a supplied 50.0 g reference suggest a common +0.4 g offset despite repeatability. Zero adjustment and calibration/reference checking are not synonyms. |
| I04 | The pairs (0 s, 0 m), (2 s, 0.4 m), (5 s, 1.0 m) agree with the specified ideal relationship d = 0.20t when t is in seconds and d in metres. Numeric spacing must preserve the 2 s then 3 s gaps. Connecting recorded positions does not establish every intermediate motion. The mean of 1.2, 1.4 and 1.3 s is 1.3 s; `=AVERAGE(B2:D2)` is valid if those cells contain exactly those same-unit raw trials. Categorical sighting events are neither an ordered continuous trend nor necessarily unique animals. |
| I05 | Moisture rises from 18% to 34% on the stated instrument scale as shade rises across the four sites: +16 percentage points. This is a positive association with site/context limitations, not proof that shade caused the difference. The separate repeated values 22, 23, 61 and 22 have mean 32%; 61 is a candidate anomaly to retain and check, not a known typo. The two distinct datasets must be labelled separately. |
| I06 | Report A gives mean growth 14 − 5 = 9 cm versus 9 − 5 = 4 cm but changes both temperature and light. Report B's matched light better isolates the temperature comparison; its 11 and 10 cm final means imply 6 and 5 cm mean growth from the supplied baseline. Six independent seedlings per group remain a limited sample; absent spread and exact temperatures preclude a significance claim or universal optimum. A top reading of 15 cm minus a soil baseline of 1 cm gives 14 cm length. Repetition does not remove a shared zero/baseline error. |
| I07 | The three independent cups per group give mean losses 18, 12 and 9 g. Two layers lose 3 g less on average than one layer in the supplied 24 h conditions. Assuming loss is water supports a bounded evaporation interpretation, while airflow/light mechanisms are not separately isolated. No universal agricultural recommendation follows. The argument must connect relevant values to the claim, consider limits/counterevidence, and distinguish source credit from permission to adapt or republish culturally governed material. |
| I08 | The 12 cm and 20 cm configurations have means 1.2 and 1.7 s: a 0.5 s difference. The stimulus supplies equal 1.5 m drop height, mass and release conditions. Three reset drops per configuration are repeated trials, not three independent rotor specimens. Family and peer versions must retain the same numbers, two-size scope and conditions while varying explanation and structure. The task requires actual composition and revision against criteria; recognition alone cannot demonstrate this competence. |
