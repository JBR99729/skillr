## Scope and checking

- LY11 includes direct reading, supported cover/write retrieval and some unseen adult dictation. The adult must hear the child read before supplying the target. It is not a complete decodable-book programme or reading-rate assessment.
- LY12 keeps both parts of the descriptor. One vowel letter can represent different sounds; two written vowels can represent one vowel sound. Each named syllable has a vowel sound. Ordinary accent variation is accepted, and no rigid universal letter-position rule is taught.
- LY13 keeps the screen hidden while an adult dictates. Copying a visible target does not demonstrate independent spelling. The paper response is checked against the complete standard spelling.
- LY14 uses supported read-cover-write retrieval, clearly distinguished from an unseen dictation test. Context helps distinguish familiar word meanings, but formal homophone classification is not required. Read-aloud assistance must not be mistaken for independent reading performance.
- LY15 treats word families as a shared meaningful base with grammatical endings. Rhyming groups are contrasting distractors. s can serve different grammatical jobs; ed and ing forms are selected or produced in sentence contexts. Formal terminology is not required from the child.

Some familiar words recur across phonics and spelling codes because the assessed action differs. The tasks do not manufacture variation with unrelated names or decorative situations. Word selection changes the sound/spelling pattern being practised. These are focused practice samples; 40 items do not exhaust the possible words for a skill.
