# Year 1 English literature review — batch 3

Reviewed 2026-09-06: LE01–LE05. Five original 24 Practice + 16 Test banks (200 items), preserving existing 8-question Practice and 12-question Test attempts. Four simple LE01 story-evidence SVGs replace generic hint cards; all other revised items are text-only. The English reference-review rollout is now 15/30 codes, 600 questions.

## Curriculum and assessment scope

Official source read: https://www.qcaa.qld.edu.au/downloads/aciqv9/english/curriculum/ac9_english_p-6_cd_sequence.pdf, literature on page 3.

| Code | Revised question focus | Assessment boundary |
|---|---|---|
| LE01 | How precise verbs, descriptive language, dialogue and depicted clues create character, setting and events | Original microtexts practise interpretation; they do not supply the required breadth of published First Nations Australian, Australian and world authors/illustrators. That literature selection remains necessary in teaching. No invented passage is presented as an authentic cultural text. |
| LE02 | Meaningful links between a supplied story moment and a supplied reader experience; similarities, differences and evidence | Options assess the relevance of a connection, not whether a child's personal feelings are correct. Learners need opportunities for their own discussion beyond this quiz. |
| LE03 | Characters, goals, settings, problems, event sequence, resolution, actions and dialogue | Short excerpts assess story understanding, not a complete reading-performance measure. |
| LE04 | Rhyme and alliteration, sound rather than spelling, imitation and sensible completion of sound patterns | Read-aloud remains available; choosing a pattern does not directly assess listening-only recognition, independent invention or performance. |
| LE05 | Preserve or deliberately adapt plot, roles, vocabulary, repeated language and structure; keep changes coherent | The source microstory is supplied in each relevant item. Choosing a retelling/adaptation is scaffolding, not evidence of independently retelling a familiar book orally or through role-play, writing, drawing or digital creation. |

## IXL evidence

Live mapping inspected at https://au.ixl.com/english/skill-plans/australian-curriculum-version-90-year-1. It lists tasks for LE03 and LE04; no direct tasks for LE01, LE02 or LE05. Their metadata records that absence rather than claiming invented direct equivalents.

- LE03: https://au.ixl.com/ela/year-1/use-actions-and-dialogue-to-understand-characters. The visible task uses a brief event and action to infer a likely feeling from two choices. Its worked example uses careful actions to infer a trait. Our original situations similarly require evidence but use different characters, events and wording. We avoid turning a single behaviour into an unsupported permanent label.
- LE04: https://au.ixl.com/ela/year-1/complete-the-rhyme. The visible task and worked example complete short text with a rhyming option. Our original lines additionally require sense, imitation and beginning-sound discrimination to cover the official descriptor. No IXL rhyme lines are reproduced.
- Related character-evidence demand also informs LE01 editorially, but this is not a direct IXL mapping or coverage of its published-literature requirement. LE02 and LE05 are curriculum-led.

Public representative tasks were available; login was unnecessary. We inspected displayed questions and worked explanations, not the full adaptive progression. This is editorial calibration, not measured cognitive-load equivalence, IXL endorsement or proof of equal learning outcomes.

## Defects repaired and design choices

Legacy banks contained many prompts such as asking for a character clue without supplying the story, and generic scripted answer distractors. Each revised item now provides its story, poem, situation or comparison. Feedback identifies the relevant clue or relationship. Distractors reverse an event, confuse role/place/action, omit a causal step, mistake a personal response for text evidence, or preserve sound while losing meaning.

Practice uses authored blocks of eight recognise, eight apply and eight reason items; Test uses separate examples. Runtime rotation is unchanged and is not an adaptive difficulty model. Four LE01 images depict a downturned expression beside a broken toy, footprints from a door to a cupboard, a small mouse beside a larger puddle, and a closed box with a protruding tail. Other image-related prompts describe the image explicitly; they do not all assess direct visual recognition. The diagrams contain evidence, not answer hints. Historical unused SVGs and QA logs remain archival and are not this review's evidence.

No topic guides, worksheets or teacher slides are changed. These banks supplement, rather than replace, authentic literature reading, conversation, personal response and creative performance.
