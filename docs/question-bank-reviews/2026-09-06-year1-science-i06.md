# Year 1 Science AC9S1I06 reference review

Reviewed 2026-09-06. Original 24 Practice + 16 Test items replace the legacy 56-item bank. Existing attempts remain 8 Practice and 12 Test, with answer shuffle and bank rotation. Six simple SVG evidence cards; no changes to topics, teacher slides or worksheets.

## Curriculum and reference scope

AC9S1I06: write and create texts to communicate observations, findings and ideas using everyday and scientific vocabulary. Official descriptor: https://www.qcaa.qld.edu.au/downloads/aciqv9/science/curriculum/ac9_science_p-6_cd_sequence.pdf

IXL Year 1 Australian Curriculum v9 mapping links I06 to magnet investigation and sunlight/shade: https://au.ixl.com/science/skill-plans/australian-curriculum-version-90-year-1

Reference https://au.ixl.com/science/year-1/what-objects-can-a-magnet-pull was revisited in the browser on this review. Its first question asks a child to connect an animated movement with an accurate sentence, distinguishing two force words. The animation had been inspected in the previous batch; this review reconfirmed the visible prompt and answer structure. No copied wording, media, characters or scene is used in this bank. The current visit displayed Sign in; public representative content was available, so no login was needed.

Previously inspected related reference https://au.ixl.com/science/year-1/investigate-sunlight-and-shade uses visible scene evidence to choose weather descriptions and label sun/shade. These examples support short evidence-to-language tasks, not independent writing assessment. No advanced adaptive stages or IXL writing tasks were inspected. Editorial comparison is not measured cognitive-load equivalence or an IXL endorsement.

## Content improvements

The old opening items included general questions about titles, labelling and presentation advice. The new bank requires children to communicate particular observations: matching captions and labels, using texture/force/absorption vocabulary, preserving time order and measurement units, making comparisons explicit, correcting reports against records, distinguishing observed facts from possible explanations, and supporting a design suggestion with evidence.

Practice 1–8 recognise accurate descriptions; 9–16 apply vocabulary and improve precision; 17–24 reason about evidence and limits of claims. Test uses separate authored contexts and combinations. This is an authoring progression; the runtime rotates questions and does not reproduce IXL adaptive delivery.

Distractors target reversed changes, omitted information, incorrect units/labels, confused predictions and results, and unsupported generalisations. Where questions ask for a more complete description, alternatives may be true but omit an explicitly requested detail. No reading-heavy science formalism or generic presentation etiquette is required.

## Assessment boundary

Selected responses assess recognising and improving scientific communication. They do not demonstrate that a child can independently write or create a text, drawing or oral report. The protected topic/worksheet journey remains available for that wider curriculum work; the quiz must not be represented as complete assessment of independent text creation.
