## M03: calendars — both mapped pages reviewed publicly

- days-of-the-week: full explanation orders seven weekdays and wraps back to Sunday; locates a given weekday then advances one day. Entry question asks for the day following Monday.
- read-a-calendar: worked solution identifies month length by finding its final numbered date; screenshot confirms the highlighted final date. Entry task asks for a date thirteen days later, using a displayed month grid.
- Authoring implications: explicit weekday headers; date versus weekday distinction; count day transitions rather than including the starting date; same-month and cross-month event intervals. The observed worked example itself did not explain elapsed-day counting.

## M04: clocks — all four mapped pages covered across this and M02 review

- match-analogue-clocks-and-times: worked whole-hour example separates short/hour and long/minute hands, uses minute hand at twelve for zero minutes, and writes minutes as two digits. Entry whole-hour matching question inspected using descriptive image alternatives.
- match-analogue-and-digital-clocks: worked half-hour example reads the hour already passed, counts minutes around from twelve, then matches the digits on either side of a colon. Screenshot confirms hour hand halfway between successive numerals. Entry whole-hour analogue-to-digital matching question read.
- read-clocks-and-write-times: worked half-hour example explains writing hour:minutes and choosing the earlier hour when the short hand is between numerals. Entry asks for the hour with :00 already supplied. Full opened explanation read.

- Authoring implications: correctly advanced hour hands; quarter-past and quarter-to, including next-hour naming and twelve boundary; no assumption that every IXL generic key illustration is in Year 2 scope (a 10:20 illustration appeared).

## M05: turns — mapped page reviewed publicly

- fractions-of-a-turn: opened explanation visually compares quarter, half, three-quarter and full turns; worked half-turn uses a straight-line configuration with a turn arc. Entry is a quarter-turn configuration, visually inspected. No answer submitted.
- Authoring implications: distinguish turn amount from final orientation; show starting direction and arc/direction; include everyday demonstrations and successive quarter-turns. Do not substitute formal angle terminology for the code's everyday movement requirement.

## SP01: shape properties — all ten mapped pages reviewed publicly

- count-sides-and-vertices: opened worked square counts and labels all four vertices, defines vertex as meeting sides; entry square vertex count visually inspected.
- compare-sides-and-vertices: full solution numbers square and hexagon corners then compares their counts. Entry compares sides of hexagon and square. Both visual models inspected.
- equal-sides: worked square versus non-square rectangle; solution highlights all four equal-length sides. Entry square versus trapezium. Both inspected.
- parallel-sides: definition uses constant separation and no intersection; worked and entry diagrams show crossing lines as non-examples. Opened solution inspected. Need original examples of opposite parallel sides in shapes, beyond these line non-examples.
- curved-parts: worked circle versus kite; solution highlights circular boundary. Entry circle versus pentagon. Opened diagrams inspected.
- attributes-of-polygons: worked comparison identifies five corners versus four and six, highlighting each angle; entry chooses six-corner irregular polygon from four/six/five-corner choices. Diagrams inspected. Keep SkillrHub emphasis on sides/spatial terms; formal angle classification is not the core SP01 descriptor.
- attributes-of-quadrilaterals: worked solution contrasts a trapezium without square corners with rotated rectangle and square; entry selects rotated square from other quadrilaterals. Opened diagrams inspected. Use orientation-invariant classification, but do not expand core content into next-year angle formalism.

- name-the-two-dimensional-shape: full opened square solution successively highlights sides, equal lengths, vertices and square corners. Entry is a circle. All worked diagram stages visually inspected. Four equal sides alone is not sufficient to define a square.
- select-two-dimensional-shapes: worked example examines each candidate, accepting three squares and rejecting a circle. Entry asks for all triangles among three triangles and a square. Full opened worked diagrams inspected. Original tasks should vary orientation, proportions and colour rather than rely on visual stereotypes.
- draw-polygons: worked definition requires closure, straight boundaries and non-crossing sides; shows a non-standard triangle as one valid three-vertex drawing on a dot grid. Entry asks the learner to draw a triangle. Opened explanation and final model inspected. Adult acceptance criteria should allow different valid drawings.

## SP02: locations — mapped page reviewed publicly

- location-in-a-grid: worked 3-by-3 grid labels top/middle/bottom and left/middle/right, highlights their intersection. Entry asks for bottom-left; screenshot identifies the star. Opened solution identifies top-right heart.
- Authoring gap: the observed task locates an object, but does not demonstrate following a pathway. Include familiar-space maps, a fixed viewer perspective, step-by-step moves, starting positions, destinations and route checking; avoid importing formal Cartesian coordinates.

## ST01 and ST02: all nine distinct mapped statistics pages reviewed publicly

The tally-selection and table-recording pages are shared by both codes.

- which-tally-chart-is-correct: worked solution counts each vegetable category separately, highlighting relevant objects, then matches every row of the tally chart. Includes a bundle of five plus a remainder. Entry drink categories contain a distractor swapping two category counts. Opened example inspected; detailed image alternatives supply all quantities.
- record-data-in-tables: worked solution isolates a requested biscuit category and inserts its count into the corresponding labelled row. Entry completes one vegetable count from an object collection. Opened solution inspected.
- interpret-tally-charts: worked solution reads two relevant category counts, then subtracts for a difference; entry is the same mathematical comparison with different names. Opened tally diagram and complete subtraction solution inspected.
- interpret-data-in-tables: worked solution combines two preferred excursion categories by addition, excluding the unrelated category. Entry asks for the difference between two class sizes. Complete opened explanation read.
- which-bar-graph-is-correct: worked comparison checks category-to-bar assignments; a distractor swaps category counts even though totals match. Entry distractor changes one bar by one unit. Both entry charts and worked solution inspected; vertical scale increases by one from zero.
- create-bar-graphs: worked example fills one missing day-category bar from written data; entry fills a missing country-category bar from a table. Opened diagram shows title, named axes, uniform scale from zero and bar height matching the provided quantity. The page uses miles and geopolitical context; those incidental contexts are not appropriate models for original Australian Year 2 content and were not independently fact-checked.
- interpret-bar-graphs: worked explanation follows the endpoints of two bars to the numerical scale before comparing; entry reads a single category value. Both graphs and complete opened solution inspected.
- create-picture-graphs: worked solution converts a tally count into a missing pictograph row using a one-picture-per-student key. Entry converts a table count into can symbols, also one-to-one. Entry diagram and opened worked solution inspected.
- interpret-picture-graphs: worked solution counts symbols in the requested category with a one-symbol-per-child key. Entry uses one symbol per page read. Both diagrams and complete opened solution inspected. No scaled or fractional-symbol worked example was observed.

ST01 authoring gaps: supplied collections and tables do not alone assess acquiring data. Include suitable categorical questions, observation, simple experiments, appropriate use of digital recording tools, relevant categories, one record per observation, checking omissions/duplicates, and creating labelled lists/tables.

ST02 authoring gaps: single-chart interpretation is insufficient. Include creating two representations of the same data, comparing their shared counts and different visual features, matching categories when their order changes, equal icon sizes/spacing, titles/labels/keys, and reasons one representation helps answer a particular question. Preserve one-to-one correspondence as the core Year 2 focus; include actual creation tasks with adult review where necessary.

## M01: public recheck — all six mapped pages reviewed

This supplements rather than replaces the earlier evidence.

- long-and-short: public worked example contrasts pen lengths; entry compares nails. Screenshots confirm that repeated image labels in the accessibility tree are construction fragments, not separate countable objects. Compare actual endpoints.
- tall-and-short: worked example labels two basketball hoops taller/shorter from a shared baseline; entry compares clock towers. Both visually inspected.
- light-and-heavy: worked example contrasts popcorn and a watermelon; entry contrasts a leaf and a peach. Opened worked diagram inspected. This is familiar-object comparison, not a measured balance experiment.
- holds-more-or-less: worked example contrasts same-type milk cartons of different sizes; entry contrasts water jugs. Opened diagram inspected. This does not establish a general rule that taller containers have greater capacity.
- compare-size-weight-and-capacity: observed worked example compares rose lengths; entry compares building heights. Both inspected. Do not claim this entry or example assessed all three quantities.
- measure-using-objects: complete opened solution aligns the first die with the start of a line, fills to the endpoint without gaps/overlap, and counts five equal-sized dice. Entry offers paper clips for a line; screenshot inspected, no manipulation or answer submitted.
- Authoring gaps: include equal-mass balance units, capacity measured with identical full scoops/cups, fair comparisons using the same unit, smaller units when needed, and misconceptions about apparent size. Earlier M01 draft restoration is still outstanding.
