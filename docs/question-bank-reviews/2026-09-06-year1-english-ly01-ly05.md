# Year 1 English literacy review — batch 4

Reviewed 2026-09-06: AC9E1LY01–LY05. Five original banks with 24 Practice and 16 Test items each (200 total). Existing attempts remain 8 Practice and 12 Test questions. All 200 revised questions are text-only; generic SVG hint cards are no longer referenced. The reference-review rollout reaches 20/30 Year 1 English codes (800 items).

## Curriculum and assessment scope

Official descriptors checked on page 4 of the QCAA Australian Curriculum v9 sequence: https://www.qcaa.qld.edu.au/downloads/aciqv9/english/curriculum/ac9_english_p-6_cd_sequence.pdf.

| Code | Revised focus | Assessment boundary |
|---|---|---|
| LY01 | Infer purposes from supplied requests, steps, facts, event details and organisation; compare texts sharing a topic; identify useful missing details | Selected responses support discussion of features and purposes, but do not assess a child's independent discussion. |
| LY02 | Relevant follow-up questions, turn-taking, adjusting speech, checking understanding, responding to an actual contribution and sharing ideas | These are judgement scenarios, not observations of real conversational performance. No mandatory eye contact or single body posture is used as proof of listening. |
| LY03 | Compare story events, informative statements and persuasive requests; distinguish realistic possibilities from fantasy without claiming all stories are impossible; recognise shared topics and combined purposes | Original microtexts support comparison. They do not replace reading and discussing complete texts. |
| LY04 | Attend to whole printed words, distinguish vowel/consonant changes, use short connected text, sentence grammar, pronouns, phrasing and punctuation to monitor meaning | Selected responses do not measure oral fluency, independent decoding accuracy or reading rate. Read-aloud support changes the task to supported comprehension. These passages are not labelled as a complete decodable reading scheme and do not replace matched decodable books and authentic reading. Context checks word reading; it is not a substitute for reading the printed letters. |
| LY05 | Literal retrieval, visualising descriptions, prediction, relevant connections, main idea, sequence, summary, questions, contextual vocabulary and cautious inference | Text-based reasoning is practised. Imagining a scene from words is not direct picture interpretation; choosing a connection or question is a scaffold for making one's own. |

## IXL references inspected

Live curriculum mapping: https://au.ixl.com/english/skill-plans/australian-curriculum-version-90-year-1. No direct tasks are listed for LY01 or LY02; their metadata records null references and a curriculum-led review.

- LY03: https://au.ixl.com/ela/year-1/which-could-happen-in-real-life. Displayed question and worked example contrast plausible everyday activity with personification/impossible activity using two picture choices. The visible descriptions were read; this is evidence of the task structure, not a claim to have inspected every adaptive level. Our original text contrasts retain the possibility distinction and extend to the informative/persuasive comparison required by the descriptor.
- LY04: https://au.ixl.com/ela/year-1/complete-the-sentence-with-the-correct-short-vowel-word. The displayed item and worked example choose among three short words using sentence meaning and grammar. The explanation connects sounding out with checking the completed sentence. Our items use original sentences and expand to whole-word attention, connected meaning and phrasing.
- LY05: https://au.ixl.com/ela/year-1/what-will-happen-next. The displayed task asks for a plausible next action from a picture; the worked example links preparation with a likely result. The displayed picture and choices were also inspected in a browser screenshot. Our predictions use different events and text evidence. Converting the evidence channel from pictures to text adds reading demand, so these are not claimed to have identical cognitive load. Existing read-aloud support is available.

Public examples were available without login. No IXL text, illustrations or answer sets are copied into the banks. Representative tasks inform editorial decisions; this is not empirical cognitive-load equivalence, coverage of the full adaptive progression, IXL endorsement or proof of equal outcomes.

## Quality changes

The old banks included underspecified prompts (for example, asking for text type without supplying a text), malformed feature substitutions, generic negative-instruction distractors and feedback that merely declared a match. Revised prompts supply the passage or conversation needed to answer. Explanations identify the relevant clue. Distractors include wrong referents, reversed sequence, similar printed words, mismatched grammatical forms, overconfident inference, topic-versus-purpose confusion and irrelevant replies.

Practice has eight recognise, eight apply and eight reason items. Test uses separate examples. These are authored progression labels, not a new adaptive engine. Answer positions are balanced within each bank. No topic guides, slides or worksheets are changed. Unused historical SVG assets and QA logs remain archival and are not evidence for this review.
