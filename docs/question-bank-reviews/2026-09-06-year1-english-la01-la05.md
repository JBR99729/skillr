## IXL comparison evidence and limits

The live curriculum mapping was inspected at https://au.ixl.com/english/skill-plans/australian-curriculum-version-90-year-1. It lists related tasks for LA01 and LA02, rhyme tasks for LA04, and no direct tasks for LA03 or LA05. Do not invent direct equivalents for the latter codes.

- LA01: https://au.ixl.com/ela/year-1/describe-the-difference-between-related-words. Actual visible question required distinguishing relative certainty between two related words. The example similarly contrasts a property within related vocabulary. This supports close semantic discrimination, but does not itself cover all interaction purposes or gestures. Our content is anchored in the official interaction descriptor rather than becoming a generic synonym bank.
- LA02: https://au.ixl.com/ela/year-1/compare-pictures-using-adjectives. Actual task asked learners to identify a pictured object with a named surface quality; a worked example contrasted length. The task structure and labels were inspected; visual answer correctness was not established from the ambiguous image alternative text. This is related descriptive vocabulary, not a complete assessment of explaining preferences. Our original preference situations explicitly connect a liked quality to a choice, without copying the pictured objects or prompt.
- LA04: https://au.ixl.com/ela/year-1/complete-the-poem-with-a-word-that-rhymes. Actual visible two-line poem completion used two word alternatives, requiring a matching end sound. Our original poems add checks of meaning, repeated phrases and explicit beat patterns to cover the full curriculum focus. No IXL poem lines or examples are reproduced.
- LA03 and LA05: official curriculum-led review; no direct IXL questions mapped. We do not label unrelated exercises as exact equivalents.

Public representative tasks were available, so login was not required. Only representative displayed questions/examples were inspected, not the complete adaptive progression. IXL-informed editorial judgement is not measured cognitive-load equivalence, IXL endorsement, or evidence of equal learning outcomes.

## Why replace the earlier items

The old banks contained 40 Practice and 16 Test items each. Examples of defects included missing stimulus text (a question asking for a reason without supplying its sentence), answer alternatives that overlapped (a polite request was contrasted with a polite question although both descriptions could apply), synthetic negative distractors, and generic SVG cards revealing a task hint rather than carrying evidence. Many explanations ended with the non-explanatory phrase 'This matches the task.'

The new questions supply their full context, use one defensible answer, and explain the actual language decision. Practice proceeds in authored blocks of eight recognising, eight applying and eight reasoning items; runtime bank rotation remains unchanged and is not adaptive IXL delivery. Separate Test examples assess the same intended skills. Some recurring task forms are deliberate; the contexts, language decisions and texts are authored, not generated by name substitutions.

## Assessment boundaries

Selected responses assess interpretation and language decisions. They do not directly assess a child's live interaction, independent writing, singing or physical use of navigation controls. Facial expressions, gestures and symbols are described in words in this batch, consistent with the requested removal of unwanted SVGs; that raises reading demand relative to picture-based tasks. Read-aloud remains available. Future image-dependent codes must retain necessary visual evidence rather than silently changing the assessed skill.
