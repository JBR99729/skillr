AC9M4N04 · Before you begin

Fractions are numbers that can be counted and located on a continuous number line. An improper fraction and a mixed numeral can represent the same point, such as 7/4 = 1 3/4.

Count, draw and explain fraction positions

Complete these tasks on paper or download the matching PDF homework. Show drawings and working to a parent or teacher for feedback.

  1. Count forwards by quarters from 1 3/4 to 2 3/4. Write every number.

  2. Count backwards by thirds from 3 to 1 2/3. Write every number.

  3. Draw a number line from 2 to 4 in halves. Mark 5/2 and 7/2 and write their mixed numerals.

  4. Draw three aligned lines from 0 to 2: one in halves, one in quarters and one in thirds. Label the point 1 with a fraction on each line.

  5. Draw a line from 1 to 2 in tenths. Mark 1 6/10 and give its improper fraction name.

  6. Draw a line from 0 to 2 in quarters. Start at 1 1/2 and make four quarter-jumps left. Label each landing.

  7. A line from 0 to 2 has ten equal intervals. What is one interval? Explain why it is not 1/10 of a whole unit.

  8. Place 10/3 on a number line between consecutive whole numbers. Write its mixed numeral and explain your position.

Answer and drawing checks

  1. 1 3/4, 2, 2 1/4, 2 1/2, 2 3/4. Accept 2 2/4 for 2 1/2.

  2. 3, 2 2/3, 2 1/3, 2, 1 2/3.

  3. 5/2 = 2 1/2 and 7/2 = 3 1/2. Check four equal half-intervals across the two-unit span.

  4. At 1 the labels are 2/2, 4/4 and 3/3. Each whole must have the stated equal intervals, and the three points must line up.

  5. 1 6/10 = 16/10. Mark the sixth tenth-interval after 1.

  6. 1 1/4, 1, 3/4, 1/2. Require four equal quarter-jumps to the left.

  7. Each interval is 1/5 of a whole unit. Ten intervals cover two wholes, so each whole contains five intervals.

  8. 10/3 = 3 1/3. It is one third after 3, between 3 and 4. Three wholes contain nine thirds.

Inspect the actual drawings and explanations. A correct label alone does not show that the whole, equal parts or number-line spacing are understood.
