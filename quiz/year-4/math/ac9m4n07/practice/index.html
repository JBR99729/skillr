Estimation, Rounding and Reasonableness

Read the quick preparation notes, then start when you are ready.

SkillrHub quiz practice is built for curriculum-aligned revision with answer feedback and a clear review path.

Quick preparation

  • An estimate is a useful nearby amount. Choose rounding or known facts that suit the calculation and the purpose.
  • For a budget, rounding costs up can help you allow enough money. Nearest-dollar rounding may round some costs down.
  • Explain whether your estimate is above or below the exact answer where you can. A close estimate supports reasonableness but does not prove an exact answer.

SkillrHub Practice guide

  • Worked example: Six tickets cost $4.60 each. The total is between 6 × $4 = $24 and 6 × $5 = $30. Allowing $30 covers the ticket cost because every price was rounded up.
  • Common mistake: Do not treat an estimate as the exact bill or change. In addition, equal opposite changes can cancel. In multiplication, rounding one factor up and the other down need not preserve the product.
