AC9M4P02 · Before you begin

Repeated experiments show variation: identical chance conditions can produce different short-run results. Larger combined samples often give a more stable picture of outcome relationships, but exact frequencies are not guaranteed.

Worked answers and adult review guide

  1. Each set totals 12 and agrees with its sequence. Report actual counts and similarities/differences; no forced next result follows. Adult checks actual work.

  2. All six faces appear as table labels, counts total 15 and match actual records. Ordering and ties follow the observed data, not expected equal frequencies.

  3. Total 18; mixed 6+4=10; matching 5+3=8. HT and TH identify which labelled coin showed heads. Require correct sums and explanation.

  4. Four counts total 18 and match real outcomes. Compare the category counts with the 18-trial example; equal trial totals make the counts directly comparable. Different observed counts can occur even with the same stated chances.

  5. Each suit and type table separately totals 8. Check actual card records and classification; consistent replacement restores the starting deck. Accept observed variation.

  6. Two starts each; four complete game records. A specific block or line-making move is valid. Few games and different choices/skill limit a claim about going first.

  7. Every move adds 1 or 2, ending at 20. At 18 add 2; at 19 add 1. Inspect both games and swapped starts; do not infer guaranteed advantage from two results.

  8. Both sets have actual 12-result records. Matching stated chances do not require matching sequences. Random sampling can supply varied examples, but accuracy and conclusions still require checking.
