Reading Scales, Partial Units and Digital Instruments

Read the quick preparation notes, then start when you are ready.

SkillrHub quiz practice is built for curriculum-aligned revision with answer feedback and a clear review path.

Quick preparation

  • Choose the attribute, instrument and unit: length, mass, capacity, duration or temperature. Read the unit on a digital display as well as the digits.
  • On a scale, find two labelled values and count the equal intervals between them. Divide their difference by the number of intervals to find each step.
  • Read whole and part units carefully. Estimate between marks only as precisely as the instrument allows, and explain how you checked the measurement.

SkillrHub Practice guide

Each attempt draws five questions from the full bank. Repeat an attempt to meet more questions and use the feedback to improve.

  • Worked example: A thermometer has five equal intervals from 20 °C to 30 °C. Each interval is 2 °C, so the third interval above 20 °C reads 26 °C. If an object's ends line up with 2 cm and 9 cm on a ruler, its length is 9 − 2 = 7 cm.
  • Common mistake: Count spaces between marks, not the marks themselves. A timer showing 02:35 in minutes:seconds means 2 minutes 35 seconds, not 2.35 minutes.
  • Materials and practical tasks: Some tasks need a ruler, a measuring jug, a timer or other named instrument. Prepare the equipment with an adult or complete the task in class. Record the actual measurement and unit; an adult checks practical work before awarding credit.
  • Review: Explain the value of one interval, the starting reading and the pointer position. For a made instrument, compare several readings with a trusted instrument.
