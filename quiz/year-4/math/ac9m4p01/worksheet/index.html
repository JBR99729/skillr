AC9M4P01 · Before you begin

Likelihood depends on the possible outcomes and conditions. Events are independent when one result does not change the chance of the next; they are dependent when the first event changes the available outcomes or conditions.

Worked answers and adult review guide

  1. Outcomes: red, blue, yellow. Least to most: yellow, blue, red. Red is most likely but not certain because other colours remain.

  2. Both bags are balanced between red and white, so chances are equal. Doubling both colours leaves the same balance; the total contents matter.

  3. Heads and tails remain equally likely on the next toss. The earlier outcomes do not change the fair coin; no outcome is owed.

  4. If red is drawn, 2 red and 1 blue remain; if blue, 3 red remain. Returning the drawn counter restores 3 red and 1 blue. Inspect actual recorded draw and correct diagram.

  5. Example: storm likely, so prepare an indoor backup and follow cancellation advice. The event depends on weather under the rule; likely is not guaranteed.

  6. Hearts/diamonds: red; clubs/spades: black. Red and club cannot coincide; red and heart can. Accept other correct pairs with explanations.

  7. Example: Wear your ... coat/shoes; c favours coat. Context ranks possibilities but cannot guarantee the writer’s intention. Check sentence fit and distinguishing letter.

  8. Label four sectors A, one B, one C. All sectors equal; A has more sectors, B and C each have one. Inspect the actual six-sector drawing and labels. Alternative valid counts must total six and satisfy the conditions.
