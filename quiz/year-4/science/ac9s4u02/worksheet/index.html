Worked answers and adult review guide

  1. Accept a creek, dam, puddle, soil water, ocean or groundwater. Require a valid labelled connection such as rain collecting in a creek or water infiltrating into the ground; groundwater is hidden and may be salty or fresh.

  2. Adult inspects two dated observations, not a completion tick. A smaller patch may support evaporation; infiltration or runoff may also remove water. Rain may add it. Accept no change with a truthful record; do not invent results.

  3. Evaporation carries liquid water into air as vapour; condensation forms liquid droplets; precipitation falls to land or ocean. Infiltration enters soil/groundwater. Check arrow directions and oral explanation. The drawing is a model, not the only route.

  4. Require a named local source and evidence link or provider name. Accept reservoir, bore, tank or other confirmed supply; treatment and delivery should be identified as applicable. Shower wastewater route is separately checked. Unverified steps must remain labelled uncertain.

  5. Adult checks actual observations and conditions. Drops on a cold surface can form from water vapour in surrounding air by condensation, not water passing through the container. No droplets is a valid observation under some conditions; do not force a result.

  6. Adult checks matching conditions, readings and losses. A smaller loss under the cover supports reduced evaporation in this comparison, not proof of zero evaporation. Accept unexpected results with honest records; consider spills, leaks, reading accuracy and repeat trials.

  7. The cover reduces water-surface exposure and slows evaporation. Connected waterways support people and ecosystems downstream. Identify this as a specific documented example; do not invent a story or describe every First Nations community as identical.

  8. Accept fixing leaks, avoiding unnecessary running taps and using appropriately treated recycled water for an approved use. Each action needs a reason. Irrigation or toilet-flushing approval does not by itself mean water is safe to drink.
