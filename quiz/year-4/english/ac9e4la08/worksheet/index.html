AC9E4LA08 • Year 4 English • Homework

Adverb Groups and Prepositional Phrases

Complete eight original reading and writing tasks. An adult checks your actual work and explanations; suitable alternative sentences are welcome.

Open practice

Eight sentence-detail tasks

Use the response space or separate paper. You may dictate your sentences using your usual communication support. Read and explain the actual words you choose.

Task 1

Read: “Before the concert, the performers waited very calmly beside the curtain.” Copy the two prepositional phrases and the adverb group. Explain what each adds to the waiting.

Task 2

Expand “The hedgehog rested” using a prepositional phrase for place and an adverb group of at least two words for manner. Underline or copy both groups and explain the added details.

Task 3

Compare “The children spoke politely” and “The children spoke with politeness”. Explain what is similar and how the forms differ. Write another pair about a different activity using an adverb and a prepositional manner phrase.

Task 4

Write two versions of “The players assembled” using “before the match” and “near the scoreboard”. Put time first in one sentence and place first in the other. Explain what changes and what stays the same.

Task 5

Read: “The student with a green bag wrote with great care.” Explain what each prepositional phrase describes. Rewrite the manner detail as a multiword adverb group while keeping the bag detail.

Task 6

Read: “Every Friday, the choir practises indoors.” Copy the time noun group and the place adverb. Rewrite the sentence to replace the place adverb with a prepositional place phrase, keeping the Friday routine.

Task 7

A note says, “Leah saw the actor with binoculars.” The intended meaning is that Leah used binoculars. Rewrite it to make this clear and explain the possible confusion.

Task 8

Create a two-sentence description of preparing a classroom display. Include one prepositional time phrase, one prepositional place phrase and one multiword manner adverb group. Copy the groups and explain their forms and jobs.

Examples and adult review guidance

Task 1

Example or expected evidence: “Before the concert” gives time; “beside the curtain” gives place. “Very calmly” is an adverb group giving manner.

Adult check: Require both complete prepositional phrases, the whole adverb group and accurate time/place/manner explanations. Words that only name the performers do not describe the circumstances.

Task 2

Example or expected evidence: The hedgehog rested quite peacefully beneath the hedge. “Quite peacefully” gives manner; “beneath the hedge” gives place.

Adult check: Require an actual complete sentence, a multiword adverb-headed manner group and a preposition plus its full object for place. Accept different sensible locations and manner choices.

Task 3

Example or expected evidence: Both describe manner. “Politely” is an adverb; “with politeness” is prepositional. Another pair: The group worked carefully. The group worked with care.

Adult check: Require shared manner meaning, accurate form distinction and an actual new meaning-matched pair. Do not require the exact model words.

Task 4

Example or expected evidence: Before the match, the players assembled near the scoreboard. Near the scoreboard, the players assembled before the match. The event, time and place stay the same; the opening detail changes.

Adult check: Require both circumstances in each complete sentence, the requested opening order and unchanged meaning. Accept appropriate comma placement without advanced grammar labels.

Task 5

Example or expected evidence: “With a green bag” identifies the student. “With great care” describes writing. The student with a green bag wrote very carefully.

Adult check: Require noun identification versus manner of activity, bag detail preserved and an adverb-headed group of more than one word such as “very carefully”.

Task 6

Example or expected evidence: “Every Friday” is a noun group giving time; “indoors” is an adverb giving place. Every Friday, the choir practises in the hall.

Adult check: Require correct original forms and an actual prepositional place replacement. Accept any suitable invented indoor setting; keep every Friday, choir and practice.

Task 7

Example or expected evidence: Leah used binoculars to see the actor. The original phrase could be understood as identifying an actor who had binoculars.

Adult check: Require unambiguous binocular use by Leah, preserved seeing/actor meaning and explanation of the possible attachment. A clear two-sentence rewrite is acceptable.

Task 8

Example or expected evidence: After lunch, we arranged pictures on the display board. We worked rather carefully. “After lunch” is prepositional/time; “on the display board” is prepositional/place; “rather carefully” is adverb-headed/manner.

Adult check: Require actual two-sentence text, all three complete requested groups, accurate form/function explanations and a coherent activity. Accept many original details; longer is not automatically better.
