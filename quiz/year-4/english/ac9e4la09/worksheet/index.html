Tense and Its Impact on Meaning worksheet

Complete eight original reading, editing and writing tasks. An adult checks the actual work and accepts suitable alternative answers.

Open practice

Eight tense and meaning tasks

Use the response space or separate paper. You may dictate through your usual communication support. Use imagined details where a task asks for your own writing.

Task 1

Write three sentences about a person feeding a pet: yesterday, a current daily routine and tomorrow. Keep the person and pet the same. Underline the verb groups and explain how the time changes.

Task 2

Read the information card: “The sports shed stores equipment. Teams borrow the balls. The caretaker checks the shelves.” List the three verbs and explain why present tense works here.

Task 3

Edit this recount of yesterday’s music lesson: “We listened to a song. We sing the chorus. We clapped the rhythm.” Rewrite the whole text and explain the one tense repair.

Task 4

Write a two-sentence mini-report comparing an invented playground last year and now. Use a past verb and a present verb. Explain what changed and why the tense shift helps.

Task 5

A timetable says, “The train leaves at ten tomorrow.” Rewrite it with “will”. Then explain why the original already refers to future time.

Task 6

Write a three-sentence recount of an imagined trip to a park last weekend. Use two different common irregular past verbs. Name their present/base forms afterwards.

Task 7

Compare “The children are making a model now” with “The children were making a model yesterday”. Copy each whole verb group and explain the difference. Then create another are/ were pair about a different activity.

Task 8

Write a short learning diary with three sentences: something you finished yesterday, something you are doing now and something you plan for tomorrow. Identify each verb group and explain why using different times is useful.

Examples and adult review guidance

Task 1

Example or expected evidence: Yesterday, Lina fed the rabbit. Lina feeds the rabbit each morning. Tomorrow, Lina will feed the rabbit. “Fed” is past, “feeds” describes the current habit and “will feed” presents a future action.

Adult check: Check three complete sentences, consistent participants, correct past/present-routine/future meanings and an explanation. Accept a different imagined pet/person and valid future forms. Require identification of all three complete verb groups by underlining or naming/listing; usual communication support is welcome.

Task 2

Example or expected evidence: stores; borrow; checks. The card describes the shed’s current function and routine activities.

Adult check: Require the three actual verbs and a current/general-information explanation. The words shed, teams and shelves are nouns, not the requested verbs.

Task 3

Example or expected evidence: We listened to a song. We sang the chorus. We clapped the rhythm. “Sang” places the singing in the same completed past lesson.

Adult check: Require all three actions in past time, sang rather than sing or singed, and an explanation based on the stated lesson time.

Task 4

Example or expected evidence: Last year, the playground had one shade sail. Now, it has two shade sails. “Had” and “has” help compare the past feature with the current one.

Adult check: Require two explicit time settings, grammatical verbs, a clear comparison and explanation. Invented details are welcome; no local inspection is required.

Task 5

Example or expected evidence: The train will leave at ten tomorrow. “Leaves” is a present form used for a schedule; “tomorrow” gives the future time.

Adult check: Check the same train, departure and time; will leave is grammatical. Explanation must not call the original wrong merely for lacking will.

Task 6

Example or expected evidence: We went to the park last weekend. We saw a bird beside the pond. We walked home before lunch. Went comes from go; saw comes from see.

Adult check: Require three connected past sentences and two correct irregular past/base pairs. Accept other familiar pairs such as took/take or found/find, not just the model.

Task 7

Example or expected evidence: Are making presents an activity in progress now; were making places it in the past. The children are drawing a map now. The children were drawing a map yesterday.

Adult check: Require both complete groups, current versus past explanation, and a grammatical new pair. The new activity can use usual writing or communication support.

Task 8

Example or expected evidence: Yesterday, I finished a puzzle. Now, I am reading a story. Tomorrow, I will draw a scene. Finished, am reading and will draw help the reader follow past, present and future.

Adult check: Require three clear time settings, complete appropriate verbs and a matching explanation. Accept imagined details and valid alternatives for future plans; different tenses are appropriate because the time changes. Require identification of all three complete verb groups by underlining or naming/listing; usual communication support is welcome.
