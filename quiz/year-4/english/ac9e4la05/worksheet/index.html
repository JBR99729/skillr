Navigation and Readability in Online Texts worksheet

Complete eight original reading, navigation, comparison and design tasks. Use the supplied static examples; no external website or account is needed. Write or draw on the page or separate paper. An adult can read prompts aloud and check your completed work.

Open practice

Worksheet tasks

For drawings, use the response space or a separate sheet. Adults: accept usual communication supports and assess the information and reasoning, not drawing neatness.

Task 1

Adult guidance and one possible response

Use Bring. Pack a towel and goggles. The subheading groups the equipment, separate from the time and meeting place.

Check the Bring label, both items, and an explanation of grouping. Accept equivalent wording; do not count the pool foyer as equipment.

Require all three destinations matched, distinct useful labels and one explanation tied to a reader purpose. Accept natural equivalent labels.

Task 5

Compare these online pages about the same puppet show. Page A: “WHEN: Friday, 5 pm. WHERE: Hall 2. TICKETS: $4.” Page B: “Our club began last year. The show is Friday at 5 pm in Hall 2. We make our own puppets. Tickets cost $4.” You need the place and cost quickly. Explain which layout helps and cite a feature from each.

Adult guidance and one possible response

A’s WHERE and TICKETS labels lead directly to Hall 2 and $4. B contains both details too, but they sit among club information in a paragraph.

Require the correct place and cost, A’s specific grouping and B’s mixed paragraph. Do not accept a claim that B omits the details or that layout proves accuracy.

Adult guidance and one possible response

A labelled Entrance and Pond joined by a path. Caption: Follow the path from the entrance to the pond. Put the map beside the matching directions.

Inspect the actual drawing or an accessible spatial description: both named places, a connecting route, relevant caption and placement beside directions. Artistic skill is not assessed; no real outdoor route is required.

Adult guidance and one possible response

Headline: Class seed swap. Visit details: Saturday, 11 am; school courtyard. Taking part: bring labelled seed packets; an adult helps at the table. Links: Seed packet labels; Courtyard map. Visit details groups time and place.

Require all four details, two meaningful groups, a relevant headline, two destination-predicting links and explanation. Accept other sensible layouts. Label it a fictional plan; no publication is required.

Adult guidance and one possible response

A’s sequence and matching diagrams help a beginner follow the method. B’s finished photos help with colour ideas. Put B’s instructions under a Making the fan heading, with steps separated, while keeping the photos in an Ideas section.
