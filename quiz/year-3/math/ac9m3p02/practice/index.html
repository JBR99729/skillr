Repeated Chance Experiments (AC9M3P02)

Practise reading results from repeated chance experiments and explaining variation. Each attempt uses a short shuffled set; feedback helps you review the reasoning.

Record and compare chance results

Before starting, name the possible outcomes, record every trial and compare what happened in each set. Different results do not automatically mean an experiment is unfair.

Worked example: two sets of spinner trials

Results for the same two-colour spinner
OutcomeSet ASet B
Red129
Blue811
Total trials2020

Check the totals: 12 + 8 = 20 and 9 + 11 = 20. Red happened 3 fewer times in Set B; blue happened 3 more times. Both sets had the same possible outcomes, but their results varied.

Common mistake: expecting two colours to appear equally often in every small set. Even equally likely outcomes can have different counts. A result of 12 red does not, by itself, tell us how the spinner is divided.

Try the reasoning: another set has 7 red and 13 blue. What stayed the same? What changed?

Check your explanation

The possible outcomes are still red and blue, and there are still 20 trials. The counts changed to 7 and 13. Describe the variation without claiming that every later set must match it.
