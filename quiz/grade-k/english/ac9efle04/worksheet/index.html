AC9EFLE04 — Topic Practice 1 Answer Key

Teacher copy • this sheet only

1. moon and spoon

Summary: Use this method: listen for the beat and repeated end sounds, then copy the pattern with voice and action; moon and spoon share the same spoken ending sound.

Hint: Say each pair aloud and listen to the end of both words.

2. clap

Summary: Use this method: listen for the beat and repeated end sounds, then copy the pattern with voice and action; the alternating action pattern restarts with clap.

Hint: Say the two-action unit clap, tap and repeat it once more.

3. beat → a steady pulse; rhyme → matching end sounds; chant → words spoken with a strong repeated rhythm

Summary: Use this method: listen for the beat and repeated end sounds, then copy the pattern with voice and action; each term is matched to the sound feature it names.

Hint: Start by matching beat with something you can tap steadily.

4. night

Summary: Use this method: listen for the beat and repeated end sounds, then copy the pattern with voice and action; night repeats the spoken ending sound in light.

Hint: Listen for a word ending with the same sound as light.

5. the pulse under the words

Summary: Use this method: listen for the beat and repeated end sounds, then copy the pattern with voice and action; a beat is the steady pulse that continues under the spoken line.

Hint: Think about what your hands keep doing evenly while the words are spoken.

AC9EFLE04 — Topic Practice 2 Answer Key

Teacher copy • this sheet only

6. A valid response keeps the spoken parts in order and assigns repeatable actions, such as tap, tap, clap-clap-freeze.

Summary: Use this method: listen for the beat and repeated end sounds, then copy the pattern with voice and action; the voice and body reproduce the same ordered sound-and-action pattern.

Hint: Choose one action for each spoken part and keep those actions the same on the repeat.

7. Their spoken end sounds match.

Summary: Use this method: listen for the beat and repeated end sounds, then copy the pattern with voice and action; rhyme is decided by listening to spoken ending sounds rather than judging spelling.

Hint: Close your eyes, say both words and compare what your ears hear at the end.

8. A valid line fits the spoken pulse and ends with a word such as see, tree, free or bee that rhymes with me.

Summary: Use this method: listen for the beat and repeated end sounds, then copy the pattern with voice and action; the new line preserves both the audible pulse and the target end-sound pattern.

Hint: Tap the beat first, then choose an ending word that sounds like me.

9. The final spoken order does not match clap-clap-freeze; change the words to hop-hop-stop or change the actions to match hop-stop-hop.

Summary: Use this method: listen for the beat and repeated end sounds, then copy the pattern with voice and action; the repaired voice and action sequences represent the same ordered pattern.

Hint: Compare the last three words with the last three actions one position at a time.
