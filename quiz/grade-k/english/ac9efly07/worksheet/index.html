AC9EFLY07 — Creating short spoken texts

Core Class Worksheet

Name: ____________________    Date: ____________

Print this page for the whole class. Enrichment is on a separate optional sheet.

1.

You are telling the class about a caterpillar you found. What should you talk about?

[A] A completely unrelated television show.[B] The caterpillar and what you noticed.[C] Every topic you can remember.
2.

Which opening suits a short report to classmates?

[A] Blue jump sandwich seven.[B] I will say nothing about my topic.[C] Yesterday I found a tiny shell at the beach.
3.

You are speaking to the whole class. Which volume is best?

[A] Clear enough for the group to hear without shouting.[B] A scream as loud as possible.[C] A whisper only the nearest person can hear.
4.

Which order makes a personal event easier to follow?

[A] Ending, random detail, beginning.[B] First what happened, then what happened next, then how it ended.[C] Unrelated facts in any order.
5.

How can picture cards help before speaking?

[A] They make listening unnecessary.[B] They replace every spoken word.[C] They can remind you of the topic and order of ideas.
6.

A child discovered a blue feather and wants to share it. Which sentence fits?

[A] I found this blue feather near the oval today.[B] I will tell three unrelated jokes.[C] My lunch was in a box yesterday.
7.

Which choice shares a personal response to a story?

[A] The book has a spine.[B] I liked the ending because the friends helped each other.[C] Our classroom has windows.
8.

During your report, you forget what comes next. What could help?

[A] Change to a random topic.[B] Stop listening to yourself.[C] Look at your next picture prompt.

9.Extension

Which ending suits a short report about planting a seed?

[A] Now I am waiting to see the first shoot grow.[B] Here is an unrelated riddle.[C] Anyway, my shoes are red.
10.Extension

What should a speaker do after practising a short report?

[A] Add as many unrelated details as possible.[B] Check that the ideas are clear, in order and loud enough for the audience.[C] Speak faster than anyone can understand.
