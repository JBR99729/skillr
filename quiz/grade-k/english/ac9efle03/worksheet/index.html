AC9EFLE03 — Literary text types and story features

Core Class Worksheet

Name: ____________________    Date: ____________

Print this page for the whole class. Enrichment is on a separate optional sheet.

1.

In a story, what is a character?

[A] A person or animal who takes part in the story.[B] The page number.[C] The full stop at the end.
2.

Which is an event in a story?

[A] The fox.[B] The book title.[C] The fox finds a key.
3.

Which opening sounds like a familiar fairy-tale beginning?

[A] Milk: keep refrigerated.[B] Once upon a time, there was a tiny castle.[C] Wash hands for 20 seconds.
4.

A story starts, ‘A girl called Amira lived beside the sea.’ What does the beginning introduce?

[A] A character and a setting.[B] A shopping list.[C] A set of instructions.
5.

Which sentence sounds like a story ending?

[A] First, open the packet.[B] This sign means stop.[C] At last, they were home safely.
6.

Which feature is common in many fairy tales?

[A] A list of ingredients only.[B] A problem that characters must face.[C] A table of bus times.
7.

A poem tells what happened to a bird across four short verses. Can it tell a story?

[A] Yes, a story can be told through poetry.[B] No, only long books can tell stories.[C] No, poems cannot have events.
8.

Which pair are both story features?

[A] price and barcode[B] button and password[C] characters and events

9.Extension

A story is told aloud by a storyteller rather than printed in a book. Is it still a literary text?

[A] No, spoken stories have no events.[B] Yes, stories can be told orally as well as in print or images.[C] No, stories must always be printed.
10.Extension

What should you look for to find how a story finishes?

[A] The final event or ending words.[B] The first letter of the title only.[C] The colour of the chair nearby.
