Words work together to make a message

Name: __________________________ Date: ______________

Show your thinking with objects, drawings or words. An adult may read the instructions aloud.

Use the first two questions in class and the last two for homework or independent practice.

1. Try the skill

Use these words to tell a message: bird / sings / The.

2. Try the skill

Change ‘The dog sleeps’ to tell about a cat. Which word changes?

3. Apply and explain

Say a sentence about someone jumping. Draw what your sentence means.

4. Apply and explain

A sentence says ‘The frog hops.’ Which words tell who it is about, and which word tells the action?

Check answers and explanations

Answer guide · AC9EFLA06

  1. The bird sings.
  2. Change dog to cat: The cat sleeps.
  3. For example, ‘The child jumps.’ Accept a meaningful spoken sentence matched by the drawing.
  4. ‘The frog’ tells who; ‘hops’ tells the action.

If help is needed: A row of familiar words does not automatically make a meaningful sentence. Say the complete message and check that the words work together.

AC9EFLA06 — Topic Practice 1 Answer Key

Teacher copy • this sheet only

1. The little dog

Summary: Those words belong together to name who performs the action because we find the who-or-what group, find the action-or-detail group and join their meanings.

Hint: Ask, Who ran to the gate?

2. For example: swims quickly

Summary: The added group tells what the fish does because we find the who-or-what group, find the action-or-detail group and join their meanings.

Hint: Add an action that a fish could do.

3. The yellow duck → splashed in the pond.; My warm socks → are in my boots.; A small bell → rang softly.

Summary: Each pair joins two compatible meaning groups into a sensible sentence because we find the who-or-what group, find the action-or-detail group and join their meanings.

Hint: Say each possible sentence aloud and listen for meaning.

4. Who group: The small bird; action group: sang loudly.

Summary: The split keeps the who group and action group intact because we find the who-or-what group, find the action-or-detail group and join their meanings.

Hint: Listen for all the words that name who, then all the words that tell what happened.

5. For example: My red hat is under the chair. The who-or-what group is ‘My red hat’ and the new detail group is ‘is under the chair’.

Summary: Changing the detail group changes the hat's location while keeping the who-or-what group because we find the who-or-what group, find the action-or-detail group and join their meanings.

Hint: Keep the first group exactly and add is plus a new place.

8.Challenge

A child puts the first break after ‘playful’ and the second break after ‘chased’ in ‘The playful kitten chased the ribbon’. Explain and improve the grouping.

Build a sentence with a who-or-what group, an action group and a where detail. Name each group after you say the sentence.

AC9EFLA06 • Answer Key

AC9EFLA06 — Topic Practice 2 Answer Key

Teacher copy • this sheet only

6. The tall tree → who or what?; sways in the wind → what happens?; beside the creek → where?

Summary: Each word group contributes a distinct part of the sentence's meaning because we find the who-or-what group, find the action-or-detail group and join their meanings.

Hint: Turn each right-side prompt into a question about the words.

7. For example: My brother whispered. The new action group is ‘whispered’, so the sentence says he spoke quietly instead of laughing.

Summary: A new action group changes what the sentence tells about the same person because we find the who-or-what group, find the action-or-detail group and join their meanings.

Hint: Keep My brother and choose one different action.

8. Keep ‘The playful kitten’ as the who group and ‘chased the ribbon’ as the action group because each group holds related words together.

Summary: The improved split keeps related words together and makes each group's contribution clear because we find the who-or-what group, find the action-or-detail group and join their meanings.

Hint: Ask who chased, then ask what the kitten did.

9. For example: The tiny lizard rested on the warm rock. ‘The tiny lizard’ is the who group, ‘rested’ is the action group and ‘on the warm rock’ is the where detail.

Summary: The three groups combine to name who, what happened and where because we find the who-or-what group, find the action-or-detail group and join their meanings.

Hint: Use this spoken frame: who or what; did what; where.
