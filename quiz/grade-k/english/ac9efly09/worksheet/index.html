2. Six sleepy seals.

Summary: This answer is right because six sleepy seals repeats one beginning speech sound; use listen, say or clap, then name the sound pattern.

Hint: Repeat each phrase aloud and listen for several nearby words sharing one beginning sound.

3. three

Summary: To check the answer, listen, say or clap, then name the sound pattern.

Hint: Let the child clap the spoken beats before counting; do not supply the count first.

4. Fog, log or another word with the same spoken ending as dog is correct.

Summary: This answer is right because a correct example shares the spoken ending heard in dog; use listen, say or clap, then name the sound pattern.

Hint: Stretch the ending of dog and test a new spoken word whose ending sounds the same.

5. Cake and lake → rhyme; helpful Henry → alliteration; three claps in banana → syllables.

Summary: This answer is right because each spoken example matches rhyme, alliteration or syllables; use listen, say or clap, then name the sound pattern.

Hint: Listen for matching endings, matching beginnings or separate word beats before making each match.

AC9EFLY09 — Topic Practice 2 Answer Key

Teacher copy • this sheet only

6. The soft blowing sound heard at the start of fish.

Summary: This answer is right because the selected description matches the beginning sound in fish; use listen, say or clap, then name the sound pattern.

Hint: Say fish slowly and choose the description of the speech sound you hear before the rest of the word.

7. A plausible spoken segmentation with the matching clap count is correct; many Australian speakers hear three beats in sun, flow, er.

Summary: This answer is right because the clap count follows the learner's natural spoken form of sunflower; use listen, say or clap, then name the sound pattern.

Hint: Clap sunflower as you naturally say it and report the claps for your spoken variety.

8. A line such as ‘Mia makes muffins’ uses three words beginning with the moon cue sound and shows alliteration.

Summary: This answer is right because the new line repeats the moon-cue beginning sound in nearby words; use listen, say or clap, then name the sound pattern.

Hint: Use three moon-cue beginning sounds in nearby words, then name the repeated-beginning feature.

9. Cake and lake share a spoken ending, while helpful Henry repeats the same beginning speech sound.

Summary: This answer is right because rhyme matches spoken endings while alliteration matches beginnings; use listen, say or clap, then name the sound pattern.

Hint: Compare where the sounds match in cake and lake with where they match in helpful Henry.
