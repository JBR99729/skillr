AC9EFLY11 — Topic Practice 1 Answer Key

Teacher copy • this sheet only

1. Upper-case A → lower-case a; upper-case D → lower-case d; upper-case G → lower-case g.

Summary: To check the answer, name the letter, match its case and use its cue word.

Hint: Say the shared name for each A, D and G pair instead of matching the different-looking shapes by appearance alone.

2. Ay.

Summary: This answer is right because ay is the alphabet name of printed upper-case A; use name the letter, match its case and use its cue word.

Hint: Look at printed upper-case A and answer with its alphabet name rather than the apple cue sound.

3. The short sound at the start of apple.

Summary: This answer is right because apple cues the common short vowel sound for the displayed letter form; use name the letter, match its case and use its cue word.

Hint: Say apple slowly and choose the description of the short vowel sound heard at its beginning.

4. Lower-case e → egg; lower-case i → insect; lower-case o → octopus.

Summary: To check the answer, name the letter, match its case and use its cue word.

Hint: Say each vowel letter's name, then use egg, insect and octopus as the three beginning-sound cue words.

5. The letter name is ay and its partner is printed lower-case a.

Summary: This answer is right because Ava begins with upper-case A and the matching form is its lower-case partner; use name the letter, match its case and use its cue word.

Hint: Look at the first printed form in Ava, give its alphabet name and then find the same letter in lower-case.

AC9EFLY11 — Topic Practice 2 Answer Key

Teacher copy • this sheet only

6. You.

Summary: This answer is right because you is the alphabet name requested for printed lower-case u; use name the letter, match its case and use its cue word.

Hint: The question asks for the letter name of lower-case u, so choose the spoken alphabet name rather than the umbrella cue.

7. Ay → lower-case a; ee → lower-case e; eye → lower-case i.

Summary: This answer is right because ay, ee and eye identify lower-case a, e and i; use name the letter, match its case and use its cue word.

Hint: Say ay, ee and eye aloud and pair each name with the printed lower-case vowel form it identifies.

8. Upper-case B matches lower-case b, upper-case D matches lower-case d, and upper-case G matches lower-case g because each pair shares one letter name.

Summary: To check the answer, name the letter, match its case and use its cue word.

Hint: Make three case pairs by shared names: bee, dee and gee, even though the upper-case and lower-case shapes differ.

9. Ay names the alphabet letter, while the apple cue supplies the short vowel sound that printed A and a can represent.

Summary: This answer is right because ay names the letter while apple cues one common sound it represents; use name the letter, match its case and use its cue word.

Hint: State what ay names, then describe the separate short vowel response supplied by the apple cue.
