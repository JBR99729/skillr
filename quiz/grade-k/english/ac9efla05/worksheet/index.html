AC9EFLA05 — Topic Practice 1 Answer Key

Teacher copy • this sheet only

1. The frog jumps.

Summary: The words work together to tell who and what happens because we read or hear the words, test the whole idea and mark the sentence boundary.

Hint: Say each choice aloud and ask what whole thought it tells.

2. For example: crawls

Summary: The added action completes the idea about the ant because we read or hear the words, test the whole idea and mark the sentence boundary.

Hint: Add words that tell what the ant does.

3. Birds fly. → whole idea; The red balloon → unfinished idea; under blue runs → does not make sense

Summary: Each group is classified by whether its meaning is whole and understandable because we read or hear the words, test the whole idea and mark the sentence boundary.

Hint: Check meaning, not how many words each group contains.

4. It communicates a whole idea.

Summary: Sentence completeness depends on meaning rather than length because we read or hear the words, test the whole idea and mark the sentence boundary.

Hint: Ask whether a listener understands one complete thought.

5. For example: Our class planted seeds.

Summary: The added words turn the beginning into an understandable complete sentence because we read or hear the words, test the whole idea and mark the sentence boundary.

Hint: Tell what the class does, has or is.

AC9EFLA05 • Answer Key

AC9EFLA05 — Topic Practice 2 Answer Key

Teacher copy • this sheet only

6. My red kite

Summary: The group names something but does not yet say the intended whole thought because we read or hear the words, test the whole idea and mark the sentence boundary.

Hint: Ask what happened to the kite or what it is like.

7. For example: The blue dog runs under the gate.

Summary: The repaired word order creates meaning and a clear sentence boundary because we read or hear the words, test the whole idea and mark the sentence boundary.

Hint: Choose who or what, add what happens, then read the result aloud.

8. The child must check whether the intended idea is complete; the group may need words such as ‘is resting’ before the full stop.

Summary: The response checks sentence meaning before relying on the ending mark because we read or hear the words, test the whole idea and mark the sentence boundary.

Hint: An ending mark shows a boundary, but ask whether the words express the whole thought.

9. For example: ‘The possum climbs.’ is complete, while ‘The small possum’ is unfinished because it does not yet tell the whole idea.

Summary: The comparison uses meaning to distinguish a sentence from an unfinished group because we read or hear the words, test the whole idea and mark the sentence boundary.

Hint: Keep the topic similar so the missing part is easy to notice.
