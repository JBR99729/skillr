Tier 1: Warm-Up

1.Warm-Up

Niko loses a boot in the rain. Which sentence is the reader's supported response?

Visual: Story event: Niko looks for one red boot while rain falls
[A] Niko has a boot.[B] I felt worried because Niko could not find the boot in the rain.[C] Rain is wet.
2.Warm-Up

A character gives the last piece of fruit to a hungry friend. Complete the thought.

I think the character is because they share the fruit.
3.Warm-Up

Match each story-response term to its example.

A. event

B. feeling

C. story clue

1. I felt relieved

2. the neighbour returned the boot

3. Niko smiled and held up the boot

Matches: __________________________

Tier 2: Core Practice

4.Core

Which response gives a thought about a character and a story clue?

[A] Hana is in the story.[B] Caves can be dark.[C] I think Hana is brave because she enters the dark cave to help her dog.
5.Core

Draw or describe the moment when Niko's boot is returned, then state how that moment makes you feel.

AC9EFLE02 — Topic Practice 1 Answer Key

Teacher copy • this sheet only

1. I felt worried because Niko could not find the boot in the rain.

Summary: Use this method: name the event or character, state your feeling or thought, then give a story clue; the reader names a feeling and links it to the lost-boot event.

Hint: Find the choice that tells how the reader feels and why.

2. caring

Summary: Use this method: name the event or character, state your feeling or thought, then give a story clue; the thought is supported by the character's sharing action.

Hint: Choose a word for someone who helps a hungry friend.

3. event → the neighbour returned the boot; feeling → I felt relieved; story clue → Niko smiled and held up the boot

Summary: Use this method: name the event or character, state your feeling or thought, then give a story clue; the happening, reader emotion and supporting evidence are kept separate.

Hint: An event is what happened, while a feeling tells an emotion.

4. I think Hana is brave because she enters the dark cave to help her dog.

Summary: Use this method: name the event or character, state your feeling or thought, then give a story clue; the thought brave is justified by Hana's story action.

Hint: Look for a thought followed by because and a character action.

5. A valid drawing or description shows the returned boot and gives a connected reader feeling such as happy or relieved.

Summary: Use this method: name the event or character, state your feeling or thought, then give a story clue; the chosen response mode represents the event and communicates the reader's feeling.

Hint: Show the returned boot first, then add a feeling face or feeling word.

Tier 2: Core Practice

6.Core

Mara is nervous before a race. Which sentence tells the reader's feeling rather than Mara's feeling?

[A] I felt hopeful that Mara would finish the race.[B] Mara felt nervous.[C] Mara stood at the start.
7.Core

The character starts at a new school. Share a connected experience or thought and point to the story event that prompted it.

Tier 3: Extension / Challenge

8.Challenge

Two readers feel differently when a fox escapes: one feels relieved and one feels worried. Explain how both responses could be supported.

9.Challenge

Choose a story moment and make a complete oral, drawn or written response that includes an event, a feeling or thought, and evidence.

AC9EFLE02 — Topic Practice 2 Answer Key

Teacher copy • this sheet only

6. I felt hopeful that Mara would finish the race.

Summary: Use this method: name the event or character, state your feeling or thought, then give a story clue; the words I felt identify the reader's own response.

Hint: Find the sentence that begins with what I, the reader, felt.

7. A valid response links the new-school event with a relevant personal experience or supported thought.

Summary: Use this method: name the event or character, state your feeling or thought, then give a story clue; the personal connection remains anchored to the character's new-school event.

Hint: Name the story event before adding what it reminds you of.

8. Both can be supported if each reader names a relevant clue, such as the fox becoming free or the fox entering danger.

Summary: Use this method: name the event or character, state your feeling or thought, then give a story clue; different reader responses are thoughtful when each one is tied to evidence from the event.

Hint: Give one fox-event clue for relieved and a different fox-event clue for worried.

9. A valid response identifies the selected event, communicates the reader's feeling or thought and supplies a relevant story clue.

Summary: Use this method: name the event or character, state your feeling or thought, then give a story clue; all three parts work together to make the personal response understandable and supported.

Hint: Plan three parts before you respond: what happened, what I felt or thought, and what clue supports me.
