1.

What does the word ‘dog’ do?

[A] It names a kind of animal and carries meaning.[B] It is only three unrelated marks.[C] It is punctuation.
2.

In ‘dogs’, what does the added s tell us?

[A] The dog is blue.[B] The dog is sleeping.[C] There is more than one dog.
3.

How many meaningful parts are in ‘dogs’ for this example?

[A] 1 because s never adds meaning[B] 2: dog + s[C] 4: d + o + g + s as four meanings
4.

Which word means more than one cat?

[A] cats[B] cat[C] catly
5.

Which pair shows a base word and a plural form?

[A] book / chair[B] book / blue[C] book / books
6.

In ‘hats’, which part names the object?

[A] at[B] hat[C] s
7.

In ‘hats’, what does s add?

[A] The meaning ‘more than one’.[B] The meaning ‘very small’.[C] The meaning ‘running’.
8.

Which statement about words is true?

[A] Words never have meaning.[B] Every letter is always a separate word part with its own meaning.[C] Words carry meaning and some can contain smaller meaningful parts.
