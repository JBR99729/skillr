AC9EFLA01 — Topic Practice 1 Answer Key

Teacher copy • this sheet only

1. May I please have help?

Summary: The polite request fits the classroom, the teacher–student relationship and the need for help because we notice the place, person and purpose before choosing respectful words.

Hint: Name who is listening and choose a clear, kind request.

2. help

Summary: The completed words clearly state the speaker's purpose because we notice the place, person and purpose before choosing respectful words.

Hint: Think about what the student needs from the teacher.

3. friend at play → Want to build with me?; teacher during class → Could you please show me again?; visiting adult → Would you like to see our class garden?

Summary: Each language choice is respectful and suited to its context because we notice the place, person and purpose before choosing respectful words.

Hint: Picture who is listening before you match each phrase.

4. Yes, you can use it after me.

Summary: The reply answers the friend's request kindly and clearly because we notice the place, person and purpose before choosing respectful words.

Hint: Choose the reply that responds to the friend and explains what can happen.

5. For example: Excuse me, may I please get past?

Summary: The revised request suits an unfamiliar adult and keeps the same purpose because we notice the place, person and purpose before choosing respectful words.

Hint: Begin with Excuse me or Please, then state what you need.

AC9EFLA01 — Topic Practice 2 Answer Key

Teacher copy • this sheet only

6. place

Summary: Place is one part of the context that guides how we speak because we notice the place, person and purpose before choosing respectful words.

Hint: Think about the difference between talking at home and in class.

7. greet → Good morning, Nan.; ask → Could I please borrow that pencil?; invite → Would you like to join our game?

Summary: Each phrase communicates the named purpose to another person because we notice the place, person and purpose before choosing respectful words.

Hint: Ask what job each phrase is doing.

8. For example: Friend—Want to see my tower? Visiting adult—Would you like to see the tower I built?

Summary: Both invitations keep the purpose while adapting the words to each relationship because we notice the place, person and purpose before choosing respectful words.

Hint: Keep the invitation idea the same, but picture each listener before speaking.

9. Different families, communities and schools may use different words; a language choice can be correct when it is kind, clear and fits the situation.

Summary: The explanation values language difference and checks whether words fit the situation because we notice the place, person and purpose before choosing respectful words.

Hint: Do not rank home and school language; explain how the situation guides the choice.
