AC9EFLE01 — Topic Practice 1 Answer Key

Teacher copy • this sheet only

1. My family sings together too, so that experience is similar to mine.

Summary: Use this method: notice the text experience, connect it with your own experience, then say whether it is similar or different; the response links the exact family-singing event with a relevant experience.

Hint: Find the answer that links something in the story with something from the reader's life.

2. different

Summary: Use this method: notice the text experience, connect it with your own experience, then say whether it is similar or different; walking and riding are not the same travel experience.

Hint: Ask whether the two children travel in the same way.

3. similar → alike in one clear way; different → not the same in one clear way; text clue → evidence in the words, event or image

Summary: Use this method: notice the text experience, connect it with your own experience, then say whether it is similar or different; each word is matched to the comparison or evidence job it performs.

Hint: Start with similar and look for the meaning that says alike.

4. In this story, the family leaves shoes at the door.

Summary: Use this method: notice the text experience, connect it with your own experience, then say whether it is similar or different; the comment describes evidence from this particular story without turning it into a rule about everyone.

Hint: Choose words that begin with what this story shows.

5. A valid response names the cricket event, a relevant personal experience and whether they are similar or different.

Summary: Use this method: notice the text experience, connect it with your own experience, then say whether it is similar or different; the response compares one clear story experience with one clear personal experience.

Hint: Use three parts: name the text event, name your experience, then say similar or different and give the reason.

AC9EFLE01 — Topic Practice 2 Answer Key

Teacher copy • this sheet only

6. I noticed the repeated journey image, and it reminded me of travelling with my family.

Summary: Use this method: notice the text experience, connect it with your own experience, then say whether it is similar or different; the response names a clue from the selected story and shares a personal connection without overgeneralising.

Hint: Look for the response about this named storyteller's work and this exact clue.

7. A valid response shares a relevant idea and points to the child, water container or dry path as evidence.

Summary: Use this method: notice the text experience, connect it with your own experience, then say whether it is similar or different; the idea is supported by an observable detail in the literature image.

Hint: Begin with I notice, then point to one exact part of the image.

8. The journeys are different because the story child travels a long way and the reader takes a short walk, and neither experience is labelled better.

Summary: Use this method: notice the text experience, connect it with your own experience, then say whether it is similar or different; the comparison identifies an exact difference and uses neutral, respectful language.

Hint: Describe the journey lengths and avoid words such as better, worse or normal.

9. A valid response gives one supported similarity, one supported difference and refers only to the selected story and the reader's own life.

Summary: Use this method: notice the text experience, connect it with your own experience, then say whether it is similar or different; the response can hold both a shared feature and a different feature at the same time.

Hint: Name one feature both experiences share, then name one feature that differs between the story and your life.
