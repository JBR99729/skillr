1. 4. Accept recognising the whole arrangement or seeing two groups of 2. Counting every dot gives the correct total but is not yet evidence of subitising.

Summary: 4. Accept recognising the whole arrangement or seeing two groups of 2. Counting every dot gives the correct total but is not yet evidence of subitising. Offer another look or a smaller familiar collection if needed; this is not a speed test.

Hint: Offer another look or a smaller familiar collection if needed; this is not a speed test.

2. 5. Recognising 3 and 2 as parts can help name the total.

Summary: 5. Recognising 3 and 2 as parts can help name the total. Use a real collection arranged in the stated parts, then cover it after a quick look.

Hint: Use a real collection arranged in the stated parts, then cover it after a quick look.

3. 3 → 2 and 1; 4 → 2 and 2; 5 → 3 and 2.

Summary: 3 → 2 and 1; 4 → 2 and 2; 5 → 3 and 2. Each pair of parts must make the named whole.

Hint: Each pair of parts must make the named whole.

4. Accept a familiar whole arrangement or recognised parts such as 4 and 1 or 3 and 2. The explanation must match the card shown.

Summary: Accept a familiar whole arrangement or recognised parts such as 4 and 1 or 3 and 2. The explanation must match the card shown. Adult: use an existing card, show it briefly and cover it before the response.

Hint: Adult: use an existing card, show it briefly and cover it before the response.

5. 3. Two and one make three.

Summary: 3. Two and one make three. Recognise the small parts before saying the whole.

Hint: Recognise the small parts before saying the whole.

AC9MFN02 — Topic Practice 2 Answer Key

Teacher copy • this sheet only

6. They show the same quantity. Rearranging the same five dots does not add or remove dots.

Summary: They show the same quantity. Rearranging the same five dots does not add or remove dots. Compare how many, rather than how much space the arrangement takes.

Hint: Compare how many, rather than how much space the arrangement takes.

7. Both drawings must contain exactly 4 dots. Record whether the child recognised the quantity or counted each dot.

Summary: Both drawings must contain exactly 4 dots. Record whether the child recognised the quantity or counted each dot. Try a familiar arrangement first, then move the same four dots.

Hint: Try a familiar arrangement first, then move the same four dots.

8. 5 and 5 → same; 5 and 4 → 5 has more; 3 and 4 → 3 has fewer.

Summary: 5 and 5 → same; 5 and 4 → 5 has more; 3 and 4 → 3 has fewer. Name each small quantity, then compare the totals.

Hint: Name each small quantity, then compare the totals.

9. Both totals are correct. The child who recognised five without counting each dot used subitising.

Summary: Both totals are correct. The child who recognised five without counting each dot used subitising. Explain the method used, not only whether the answer is right.

Hint: Explain the method used, not only whether the answer is right.
