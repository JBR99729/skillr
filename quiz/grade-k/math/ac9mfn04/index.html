About This Part-Part-Whole Quiz (AC9MFN04)

Part-part-whole understanding helps children see that a whole number can be made from smaller parts. For example, 5 can be seen as 4 and 1, 3 and 2, or 5 and 0.

This quiz supports Australian Curriculum Version 9.0 Foundation Mathematics AC9MFN04 , which develops flexible understanding of how collections up to 10 can be partitioned and recombined.

Australian Curriculum Alignment

  • Australian Curriculum Version 9.0 — AC9MFN04: partition and combine collections up to 10 using part-part-whole relationships and subitising to recognise and name the parts.

  • MAE-CSQ-01: related supporting outcome involving combining and separating collections to model addition and subtraction relationships.

Skills Practised

  • Part-part-whole relationships: recognise that a whole number can be made from two or more smaller parts.
  • Partitioning: break collections up to 10 into different combinations.
  • Combining: join two smaller collections to determine the whole.
  • Number bonds: recognise combinations such as 2 and 3 making 5 or 6 and 4 making 10.
  • Missing parts: identify an unknown part when the whole and one part are known.
  • Ten-frame recognition: use structured visual arrangements to recognise parts and wholes efficiently.
  • Subitising parts: recognise smaller groups within a larger collection without counting every object.
  • Multiple partitions: understand that one whole can be partitioned in several valid ways.
  • Commutative relationships: recognise that 3 and 2 make the same whole as 2 and 3.
