AC9SFH01 — Answer guide

SkillrHub · Adult copy. Read directions aloud if needed; accept spoken explanations grounded in the examples.

  1. The leaf has three brown spots. This describes something visible.

    Teaching hint: Separate observable detail from liking or hoping.

  2. magnifying glass. It can make small details easier to see.

    Teaching hint: Use the tool to answer an observation question.

  3. sense → sight; tool → magnifying glass; record → labelled drawing.

    Teaching hint: Notice the difference between observing and recording.

  4. questions. People ask questions to learn more from their observations.

    Teaching hint: Connect the question to something noticed.

  5. For example, the leaf is green and has a jagged edge; accept two actual visible details.

    Teaching hint: Use sight, or touch only when an adult says the object is safe.

  6. For example, “Will this seedling have more leaves next week?”

    Teaching hint: Keep the question connected to the observed seedling and leaves.

  7. notes; words or tables are also acceptable simple records.

    Teaching hint: A record should preserve what was actually observed.

  8. The bird has a long beak → observation; Why is its beak long? → question; a photo → record.

    Teaching hint: Identify what is noticed, asked and saved.

  9. The holes are the observation. An insect making them is a possible explanation that needs more evidence.

    Teaching hint: Do not turn a possible cause into something directly observed.

  10. For example: “This leaf has three spots.” Ask “Will more spots appear?” Make a dated drawing or photograph of the same leaf.

    Teaching hint: Accept any observation, linked question and suitable record; do not invent an outcome.
