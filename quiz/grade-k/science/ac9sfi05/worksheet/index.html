AC9SFI05 — Answer guide

SkillrHub · Adult copy. Read directions aloud if needed; accept spoken explanations grounded in the examples.

  1. describe what you observed in a discussion.

    Teaching hint: Share actual observations so others can understand them.

  2. drawing or poster. A visual can help explain the idea.

    Teaching hint: Speaking, pointing and models are also useful when suited to the evidence.

  3. question → what I want to find out; prediction → what I think may happen; observation → what actually happened.

    Teaching hint: Use separate wording for the three parts.

  4. poster; model or display is also acceptable.

    Teaching hint: Choose a way to communicate the actual idea or observation.

  5. For example, “The leaf has three holes.” Accept a specific observation from a real or supplied example.

    Teaching hint: Avoid replacing observed detail with a preference such as “It is nice”.

  6. For example, safely roll a ball along a clear surface and describe its movement to the listener.

    Teaching hint: A labelled sequence of drawings or a suitable recording can also show movement.

  7. respond. Listen and give a relevant response or question.

    Teaching hint: Refer to what the other person actually shared.

  8. poster → pictures and labels; model → clay animal showing features; discussion → talking in a circle group.

    Teaching hint: Match each method to how the information is shared.

  9. “I predicted three leaves, but I observed two.” Show a record with two leaves.

    Teaching hint: Keep the prediction and actual observation distinct when sharing.

  10. For example, use a labelled drawing of the observed leaf with its three holes. It lets the listener see the detail being described.

    Teaching hint: Accept a suitable drawing, model or display with a reason tied to the observation.
