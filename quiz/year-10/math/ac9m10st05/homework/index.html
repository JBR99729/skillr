Answer guide · AC9M10ST05

Other valid methods and well-supported explanations are welcome.

  1. Measure height and count leaves on each plant. For example, ‘Do taller plants in this garden tend to have more leaves?’
  2. The pairing is needed to investigate how the two measurements vary together.
  3. A positive, approximately linear association: taller plants in this sample tend to have more leaves.
  4. No. Age, species or growing conditions may influence both measurements; association alone does not establish causation.
  5. It is far from the main pattern and may strongly influence the fitted relation. Check recording, measurement and whether it belongs to the same population.
  6. It is extrapolation far beyond the observed range; the relationship may change.
  7. Sample plants reasonably across the intended population; define how height is measured and which leaves are counted. Restrict or record species and age where appropriate.
  8. The initial five plants show a positive association between height and leaf count, but the small sample and possible species or age differences limit generalisation.
