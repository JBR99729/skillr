Part A · Check the skill

Question 1

A group has 40 students: 20 walk to school, 12 play sport, and 8 both walk and play sport. Complete a two-way table.

Question 2

What proportion of walkers play sport?

Question 3

What proportion of non-walkers play sport?

Question 4

Compare those conditional proportions. What possible relationship do they suggest in this group?

Part B · Apply and reason

Question 5

A learner divides 8 by 40 to answer ‘What proportion of walkers play sport?’ Explain the denominator error.

Question 6

If there were 30 walkers and 10 non-walkers, why would raw sport counts alone be a poor comparison?

Question 7

Why does the observed association not prove walking makes students play sport?

Question 8

Name a limitation of using this one group to make a claim about all students.

Check answers and reasoning

Answer guide · AC9M10ST04

Other valid methods and well-supported explanations are welcome.

  1. Walk/sport 8; walk/no sport 12; no walk/sport 4; no walk/no sport 16. Row totals 20,20; column totals 12,28.
  2. 8/20 = 40%.
  3. 4/20 = 20%.
  4. Sport participation is more common among walkers in this group, 40% versus 20%. This suggests association, not causation.
  5. The question restricts the group to 20 walkers. 8/40 instead gives the joint proportion among all students.
  6. The group sizes differ; compare within-group proportions to account for the different denominators.
  7. Other factors may influence both activities, and this is an observational comparison.
  8. The group is small and may not be representative of the wider student population.
