Collect Precise, Replicable Data

Select and use suitable equipment to generate and record data with appropriate precision, units and methods so another group can replicate the data collection. Use precise science language and support longer responses with clear reasoning.

10
Short-answer questions
10
Long-answer questions
1
Research/application task

Part A

Short-answer questions

  1. What does precision mean when recording scientific data?

  2. Why should measurements include units?

  3. Give one example of equipment suitable for measuring temperature accurately.

  4. What is the difference between accuracy and precision?

  5. Why should data be recorded immediately during an investigation?

  6. What does replicable data collection mean?

  7. How can a digital sensor improve data collection?

  8. Why might a measuring cylinder be better than a beaker for measuring volume?

  9. What is one way to reduce parallax error?

  10. Correct this misconception: “More decimal places always means better data.”

Part B

Long-answer questions

  1. Choose suitable equipment to measure temperature, mass, time and length in a dissolving investigation. Justify each choice.

  2. Design a data table for repeated measurements of reaction time. Include headings, units and space for a mean.

  3. Explain how poor equipment choice can affect the quality of evidence in an investigation.

  4. Compare using a stopwatch, phone timer and light gate for measuring motion. Which gives more useful data and why?

  5. A group records volume as 20, 20.0 mL and 0.021 L. Explain why this is a problem and rewrite it consistently.

  6. Describe how to collect replicable data for an investigation into how ramp height affects trolley speed.

  7. Explain how repeated measurements and averaging can improve confidence in a result.

  8. Identify three possible measurement errors in a heating experiment and explain how to reduce them.

  9. Evaluate whether data collected with no units, no repeats and vague equipment can support a reliable conclusion.

  10. Write a full data-collection plan for one Year 9 investigation, including equipment, precision, units, table and repeat strategy.

Part C

Research and understanding task

Research one scientific instrument, such as a data logger, thermometer probe, pH meter, microscope, seismometer or light gate. Explain what it measures, its units, its precision and why it improves evidence quality.
