Year 9 Science • AC9S9H04 • Authored homework

How Society Shapes Research Priorities

Examine how society’s values, needs, risks, funding choices and ethical concerns influence which scientific questions are investigated and how research is used. Use precise science language and support longer responses with clear reasoning.

10
Short-answer questions
10
Long-answer questions
1
Research/application task

Part A

Short-answer questions

  1. What is a research priority?

  2. Give one example of a social need that could influence science research.

  3. How can public health concerns shape research funding?

  4. Why might governments fund some science projects more than others?

  5. How can environmental values influence research priorities?

  6. What is one ethical concern that could affect whether research should proceed?

  7. Why might industry fund scientific research?

  8. Explain the difference between basic research and applied research.

  9. How can community pressure influence scientific investigation?

  10. Correct this misconception: “Scientists choose research topics without any influence from society.”

Part B

Long-answer questions

  1. Explain how a society facing drought might influence scientific research priorities in water management, agriculture or climate science.

  2. Compare two possible research priorities: developing new medicines and developing renewable energy. What social needs might support each one?

  3. Analyse how funding can affect which scientific questions are answered first.

  4. Describe how ethical concerns could shape research involving genetics, animals, artificial intelligence or medical testing.

  5. A student says, “The most scientifically interesting question should always be funded first.” Evaluate this claim using society’s needs and values.

  6. Explain how a major event such as a pandemic, bushfire, flood or food shortage can redirect scientific research.

  7. Use a cause-and-effect chain to show how public concern can lead to policy change, funding and scientific investigation.

  8. Compare how government, university, industry and community groups might have different research priorities.

  9. Create a decision table for choosing between three research projects, using evidence, cost, benefit, risk and fairness.

  10. Write a recommendation about which science research area should be prioritised in your community and justify it with evidence-based reasoning.

Part C

Research and understanding task

Research one real example where society’s values or needs shaped science research, such as vaccine development, renewable energy, bushfire science, water security, medical research or food production. Explain the social need, the research response and one ethical or economic consideration.
