Answer guide · AC9M9ST05

Other valid methods and well-supported explanations are welcome.

  1. Travel mode is categorical; journey time is numerical. Define the school population and a consistent journey/time reference.
  2. It does not represent both travel groups or the wider student population fairly.
  3. Walking median 12, range 8. Cycling median 9, range 13; cyclists have a lower sample median but greater spread.
  4. No. Small observational samples, differing distances and other variables prevent that causal or universal claim.
  5. For example, distance travelled, route, traffic, age or weather.
  6. The populations differ. Collection methods, locations, definitions and dates may also differ.
  7. In these samples the cycling median is 3 minutes lower, but times overlap and the samples are small. The result may not generalise.
  8. For example, collect a larger reasonably representative sample and compare similar travel distances; this reduces some sampling or confounding problems.
