Real numbers, exact values and inequalities

Work with real numbers by comparing rational and irrational values, using exact forms such as π and square roots, estimating when useful, and representing inequalities correctly. Show working, use correct notation and check reasonableness.

Research/application task

Part A

Short-answer questions

  1. Classify 7/8, √25, √30, 0.125, π and -4 as rational or irrational where appropriate.

  2. Write an exact expression for the circumference of a circle with radius 6 cm, then give a decimal approximation.

  3. Estimate √50 to one decimal place and explain why it is between 7 and 8.

  4. Place √2, 1.5, 3/2 and π/2 in ascending order.

  5. Explain the difference between an exact value and an approximation.

  6. Solve x + 4 < 11 and show the solution on a number line.

  7. Write an inequality for “n is at least 12”.

  8. Round 7π to two decimal places and explain why the exact form may be better.

  9. Correct this misconception: “Every decimal is irrational.”

  10. Give a real-world situation where an inequality is more useful than a single number.

Part B

Long-answer questions

  1. A circular table has diameter 1.2 m. Find the exact circumference in terms of π and an approximate circumference to the nearest centimetre.

  2. Compare √45 and 6.7 without using a calculator as your only explanation. Show reasoning.

  3. A container must hold at least 2.5 L but less than 3 L. Write and graph an inequality for the capacity c.

  4. Solve 3x - 5 ≥ 16, then test two values to check your solution set.

  5. Explain why √49 is rational but √50 is irrational, using definitions and examples.

  6. A student says 22/7 and π are the same because calculators give close answers. Evaluate the claim.

  7. Use a number line to show an interval of possible values for a measurement rounded to 8.4 cm to the nearest tenth.

  8. Create and solve a practical problem that needs an exact value first and a rounded answer at the end.

  9. Write a full solution to a problem involving real numbers, inequalities and approximation, including a reasonableness check.

  10. Explain how exact values, approximations and inequalities can all be useful in measurement or design.

Part C

Research and understanding task

Find one real situation involving a measurement limit, tolerance, design size or circular measurement. Create a problem using exact values or inequalities, solve it, and explain the meaning of the answer.
