Answer guide · AC9M9ST04

Other valid methods and well-supported explanations are welcome.

  1. A bar chart compares categorical counts; separate bars preserve the categories.
  2. A scatterplot, with one paired point per person and labelled measurement axes.
  3. A time-series line graph with months in order and rainfall units labelled.
  4. It shows parts of a whole, not paired values of two numerical variables.
  5. The truncated axis exaggerates the visual difference; a zero baseline is important when bar lengths represent counts.
  6. Dots at the listed values, with two at 12. Most cluster at 10–18 while 35 is unusually high; the mean obscures that structure.
  7. Different bin widths or scales can create misleading apparent differences in shape or spread.
  8. Specify whether sales means item count or money, and label the time scale and period. Other relevant missing context may also be identified.
