Year 7 Science • AC9S7I04 • Homework

Select and construct appropriate scientific representations

Use tables, graphs, models, dichotomous keys and mathematical relationships to organise and interpret scientific data. Explain why each representation suits the data.

5
2-mark questions
5
5-mark questions
1
enriched investigation

Part A

5 × 2-mark questions

1. Classify each dataset as discrete or continuous: (a) number of insects found under a log; (b) temperature recorded every hour.

2. Explain why a line graph is more suitable than a column graph for showing plant growth over 6 weeks.

3. In a food web, what does the direction of an arrow represent? Give one example.

4. Name two ways spreadsheets help scientists organise or analyse data.

5. State two features of a well-constructed dichotomous key.

Part B

5 × 5-mark questions

6. Tide representation. Tide heights are: Mon 1.2 m, Tue 1.5 m, Wed 1.8 m, Thu 1.4 m, Fri 1.1 m. Identify the data type, select and justify a suitable graph, and describe one pattern.

7. Food web analysis. A food web contains grass, grasshoppers, frogs, snakes and decomposers. Identify a producer, primary consumer and secondary consumer; explain a likely effect if snake numbers decrease; and explain the role of decomposers.

8. Dichotomous key. Construct a key for four leaves with different shape, edge, colour or size. Use at least three branching steps, mutually exclusive choices and observable features.

9. Mathematical relationship. Secondary data show high tides recurring roughly every 12.4 hours. Explain how a graph could demonstrate the periodic pattern and identify one limitation of using secondary tidal data.

10. First Nations astronomy. Explain how a specific First Nations Australian astronomical observation can be analysed as a seasonal pattern. Include the importance of acknowledging the source/community and compare what the observation and a modern astronomical dataset can each contribute.

Part C • Enriched

11. Scientific investigation using representations

Design a small investigation into how temperature relates to insect activity in a local environment. Include: a clear aim and hypothesis; a method collecting both continuous and discrete data; a raw-data table; two appropriate graphs with labelled axes; a simple food-web model; one source of uncertainty; and a respectful plan for comparing your findings with appropriately sourced First Nations seasonal observations if such local knowledge is available for use.

Extension worksheet

Representation construction practice

A. For each dataset, choose a representation and justify it: number of kangaroos in four regions; temperature over 24 hours; plant height over 6 weeks; relationship between soil moisture and plant height.

B. Create a food web using five organisms from a local ecosystem. Label producer/consumers/decomposer and explain one consequence if a species is removed.

C. Design a spreadsheet layout for a 10-day dataset. Include headings with units and identify one formula and one graph you would use.
