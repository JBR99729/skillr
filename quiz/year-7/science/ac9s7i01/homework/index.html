Year 7 Science • AC9S7I01 • Homework

Investigable Questions, Predictions & Hypotheses

Develop questions that can be answered through systematic evidence, identify variables, use scientific models, make reasoned predictions and construct testable hypotheses.

Includes: 40 short-answer mastery questions • 5 × 5-mark questions • 1 × 10-mark enriched investigation

Part A

40 short-answer mastery questions

  1. Explain what makes a question investigable.
  2. Give one example of an investigable question and explain why it is testable.
  3. Give one example of a non-investigable question and explain why it cannot be answered scientifically as written.
  4. Rewrite “Why do plants grow?” as an investigable question with measurable variables.
  5. Identify one factor that can limit whether a question is feasible in a school investigation.
  6. Explain why “Is water healthy?” is too broad or ambiguous to be an investigable question as written.
  7. Rewrite “How do chemicals affect the environment?” into one clear, testable question.
  8. Explain why repeatability is useful when developing an investigable question.
  9. Describe one way a reliable web-based resource can help refine an investigable question.
  10. Explain the difference between a measurable question and an opinion-based question.
  11. Describe what First Nations ecological knowledge can include.
  12. Explain how long-term, place-based observations can help refine scientific questions.
  13. Give an example of a scientific question that could be refined through consultation with relevant First Nations knowledge holders.
  14. Describe one natural pattern that may be understood through long-term ecological observation, such as tides, seasons, flowering or animal behaviour.
  15. Explain why First Nations knowledges should remain connected to the relevant community, place and context when used in science learning.
  16. Define a scientific model.
  17. Explain how the particle model can help develop investigable questions about states of matter.
  18. Write one investigable question that uses the particle model.
  19. Explain why scientific models are useful even though they are simplified representations.
  20. Give one example of a scientific model and state what it helps explain or predict.
  21. Define independent variable.
  22. Define dependent variable.
  23. Define controlled variable.
  24. Identify the IV and DV in “How does water temperature affect sugar dissolving time?”
  25. Rewrite “How does light affect plants?” so the variables are clear and measurable.
  26. Explain why relevant controlled variables are important in a fair test.
  27. Write an investigable question that tests a relationship between two variables.
  28. Explain how measuring tide height at different points in a lunar cycle can test for a pattern.
  29. Describe one relationship that can be tested using heating or cooling.
  30. Explain why “How does heating affect balloon volume?” is a testable relationship question.
  31. Define a reasoned prediction.
  32. Write a reasoned prediction for “How does temperature affect balloon volume?”
  33. Explain why a scientific prediction should be based on scientific knowledge rather than opinion.
  34. Write a prediction using the particle model for dissolving sugar in warmer water.
  35. Explain how predictions help scientists plan and interpret investigations.
  36. Define a hypothesis.
  37. Write a hypothesis for balloon heating using: If… then… because…
  38. Write a hypothesis for “How does parachute surface area affect descent time?”
  39. Explain why a hypothesis must include scientific reasoning and be testable.
  40. Convert “I think plants will grow taller with more light” into a full testable hypothesis with a scientific reason.

Part B

Five 5-mark questions

  1. Investigable vs non-investigable questions (5 marks). Explain the difference between the two types of question, give one reason a question may not be investigable, then rewrite “Why do plants grow?” using measurable variables.
  2. First Nations ecological knowledge (5 marks). Describe how consultation with relevant First Nations Australians and long-term ecological knowledge can refine questions about environmental change. Use an example involving tides, seasons or invasive species.
  3. Scientific models (5 marks). Explain how the particle model can guide the development of an investigable question about states of matter. Include a description of the model and one testable question.
  4. Variables (5 marks). For “How does temperature affect the dissolving time of sugar?”, identify the IV, DV and two controlled variables, then explain why controls matter.
  5. Hypothesis (5 marks). Write a full hypothesis for “How does parachute surface area affect descent time?” using If… then… because… and correct reasoning about air resistance.

Part C

Enriched investigation — 10 marks

Design a scientific investigation for: “How does heating affect the volume of a balloon?”

  1. Write a clear investigable question with variables.
  2. Write a reasoned prediction using the particle model.
  3. Write a full hypothesis.
  4. Identify the IV, DV and three controlled variables.
  5. Explain how the particle model supports your reasoning.
  6. Explain one way relevant long-term environmental knowledge or observations could help refine a related real-world question, while keeping the knowledge connected to its source and context.
