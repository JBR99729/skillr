Explain Earth, Sun and Moon cycles

Use relative position, illumination and gravity to explain the event in the question. Keep seasons, lunar phases, eclipses and tides distinct rather than choosing an answer from a familiar keyword.

Worked example: a new moon without an eclipse

The Moon is between Earth and the Sun at new moon. That position alone does not guarantee a solar eclipse. The Moon's orbit is tilted relative to Earth's orbital plane, so the Moon's shadow usually passes above or below Earth. A solar eclipse needs sufficiently close alignment for the shadow to fall on Earth.

Seasons: axial tilt changes sunlight angle and day length through the year. Earth–Sun distance does not explain opposite seasons in the two hemispheres.

Phases: we see different portions of the Moon's sunlit half. Earth's shadow is involved in a lunar eclipse, not ordinary lunar phases.

Tides: the relative positions of the Sun and Moon affect tidal range. A spring tide is not named after the season.

Model limitation: a small model may show order and alignment while distorting sizes and distances.

Check your reasoning: explain why a full moon does not always bring a lunar eclipse.

Check the explanation

Full moon places Earth between the Sun and Moon, but the tilted lunar orbit usually carries the Moon above or below Earth's shadow. A lunar eclipse also requires close alignment.
