Year 7 Science • AC9S7I03 • Homework

Equipment, precision and scientific data

Use correct scientific vocabulary, units and reasoning. Justify equipment choices using range, capacity and resolution.

Part A — 5 × 2-mark questions

1. You need to measure 12 mL of acid accurately. Name the most appropriate equipment and explain why.
2. Explain one reason a digital stopwatch may provide more precise timing data than a classroom clock.
3. Convert 0.45 L to millilitres and include the correct unit.
4. In an investigation measuring temperature every minute for 10 minutes, identify the independent variable.
5. Name one abiotic factor measurable with a digital sensor and state an appropriate unit.

Part B — Word problems

6. A reaction is expected to rise from 22°C to 65°C. Choose between thermometers 0–50°C, –10–110°C and 50–150°C. Justify your choice using range and resolution.
7. You need 8 mL of acid. Compare a 250 mL beaker, a 100 mL cylinder with 1 mL divisions and a 10 mL cylinder with 0.2 mL divisions. Which is best and why?
8. A soil-moisture sensor gives 34%, 52% and 41% within one minute. Give two possible causes and describe one way to improve reliability.
9. A plant identification app suggests a species from one image. Explain how you would check the identification before recording it as confirmed.
10. Design column headings for a cooling experiment recording temperature every 30 seconds for 10 minutes.

Part C — 5 × 5-mark questions

11. A student must measure 2.36 g of powder. Compare balances with 1 g, 0.1 g and 0.01 g resolution. Select the best and explain why the other two are less suitable.
12. Compare a digital temperature probe and a glass thermometer for measuring water cooling from 80°C to 25°C. Include at least two advantages and one limitation of digital tools.
13. Design a data table for light intensity (lux) and temperature (°C) measured every minute for 20 minutes. Include appropriate headings and explain which variable belongs in the first column.
14. Convert 0.124 m into centimetres and millimetres, then explain why consistent units matter when comparing datasets.
15. Explain three reasons a sensor may give variable readings and one method for improving the reliability of the recorded data.

Part D — Hard multi-step investigations

16. Cooling curve: Plan how to measure water cooling from 80°C to room temperature. Include equipment, sampling interval, digital tool, table headings, units, controlled variables and a precision justification.
17. Soil moisture vs plant growth: Choose sensors/tools, measurement frequency, spreadsheet columns, unit conventions and two likely measurement errors.
18. Reaction time: Design a precise comparison of reaction time in quiet and noisy conditions. Include timing method, repeats, averaging and unit conversion between seconds and milliseconds.
19. Greenhouse: Plan simultaneous measurement of light intensity and air temperature for one hour. Include sensors, sampling interval, table structure and two sensor limitations.
20. Dissolving rate: Design a measurement system for 5.00 g sugar dissolving at different temperatures. Include mass, temperature, timing and volume equipment plus one way to standardise stirring.

Part E — Enriched investigation

21. 10 marks: Design a complete investigation to test whether temperature affects sugar dissolving rate. Include equipment choices with resolution, digital tools, a data table with units, three controlled variables, one likely source of error and how to reduce it. Justify every measurement choice.
