Year 7 Science • AC9S7H03 • Homework

Scientific responses: evidence, impacts and trade-offs

Use evidence and reasoning to evaluate scientific responses through ethical, environmental, social and economic lenses.

10
2-mark questions
8
extended responses
7
curriculum case studies

Part A

Focused 2-mark questions

  1. Explain one environmental benefit of creating larger ecological reserves and one economic challenge that may arise. (2 marks)

  2. Describe one way laboratory-grown meat could reduce environmental impact and one social reason some people may not accept it. (2 marks)

  3. Identify one social benefit of desalination plants and one environmental risk they pose to marine ecosystems. (2 marks)

  4. Explain one ethical issue that arises when non-Indigenous groups use First Nations medicinal plant knowledge. (2 marks)

  5. Describe one environmental advantage of cultural burning and one ethical reason cross-cultural partnerships must respect First Nations leadership. (2 marks)

  6. Explain how scientific knowledge of lift and drag has improved aircraft design, and identify one environmental concern linked to modern air travel. (2 marks)

  7. State one ethical reason chemical weapons were restricted internationally and one social impact gas warfare had on people in the First World War. (2 marks)

  8. Choose one scientific response from this topic. Explain one benefit and one drawback of that response. (2 marks)

  9. Explain why ethical considerations are important when applying scientific knowledge to solve real-world problems. (2 marks)

  10. Describe how one scientific solution from AC9S7H03 can have both a positive and a negative impact on society. (2 marks)

Part B

Extended-response questions

  1. Scientists argue that larger ecological reserves can better maintain ecosystem function. Explain why, then evaluate the ethical, environmental, social and economic impacts of creating a large reserve in an area currently used for farming, recreation or development. (5 marks)

  2. Describe how laboratory-grown meat is produced and why it is proposed as a response to environmental problems. Evaluate possible ethical, environmental, social and economic impacts if it becomes widely consumed. (5 marks)

  3. Explain how desalination turns seawater into fresh water. Analyse how desalination can help society while also affecting marine ecosystems, considering environmental, social, ethical and economic factors. (5 marks)

  4. Describe how First Nations Australians’ knowledges can contribute to identifying medicinal properties of endemic plants. Evaluate the implications when others use this knowledge, including consent, cultural authority, environmental protection and fair benefit-sharing. (5 marks)

  5. Explain what cross-cultural land-management partnerships are, using cultural burning as an example. Evaluate environmental, ethical, social and economic implications, including First Nations leadership and cultural authority. (5 marks)

  6. Describe how understanding lift, drag, thrust and weight has influenced aircraft design. Analyse ethical, environmental, social and economic considerations linked to modern aviation. (5 marks)

  7. Explain how scientific knowledge about gases and toxic chemicals was used in First World War chemical warfare. Discuss how the harms contributed to later international controls such as the Geneva Protocol and Chemical Weapons Convention. (5 marks)

  8. Choose two responses from ecological reserves, laboratory-grown meat, desalination or cross-cultural land management. Compare their ethical, environmental, social and economic implications and argue which response is more responsible overall in the scenario you describe. (5 marks)

Answer strategy

For extended responses

Use this structure: issue → scientific response → evidence → benefit → risk → ethical/environmental/social/economic impacts → balanced judgement. Name who or what experiences each impact rather than only listing category labels.
