Year 7 Science • AC9S7I02

Homework & Investigation Worksheet

Plan reproducible investigations by matching methods to hypotheses, identifying variables and assumptions, managing risks and ethics, and respecting heritage responsibilities and local protocols on Country/Place.

Part A • Core worksheet

Planning an investigation

  1. Write a testable hypothesis for an investigation of your choice.
  2. For “Increasing the amount of fertiliser will increase plant height”, identify the independent variable, dependent variable and three controlled variables.
  3. Explain why controlled variables matter in a fair test.
  4. Identify two assumptions when measuring water temperature with a digital thermometer and explain how one incorrect assumption could affect results.
  5. Write a reproducible four-step method to test “Sugar increases the rate of yeast fermentation.” Include measurements and repeats.
  6. Identify two hazards in a heating investigation and give one control for each.
  7. Explain one ethical issue when investigating living organisms and how the method should minimise harm.
  8. Explain what you should do if you notice a possible First Nations artefact or heritage site during fieldwork.
  9. Explain one benefit of collaborating with a local First Nations community or organisation in an ecosystem investigation and one protocol that should be agreed before fieldwork.
  10. For “Does water temperature affect the dissolving rate of sugar?”, identify IV, DV, three controls, one assumption, one risk/control pair, and two reproducible method steps.

Part B • Extended response

10 deeper questions

  1. Explain why a scientific investigation must be reproducible.
  2. Describe independent, dependent and controlled variables using a temperature-versus-dissolving example.
  3. Discuss why assumptions must be identified and how an incorrect assumption can affect validity.
  4. Explain four ways a vague method can be improved to increase reproducibility.
  5. Describe the importance of risk assessment and give two hazard-control examples.
  6. Explain one ethical issue involving living things and how it should be managed.
  7. Discuss why First Nations heritage sites and artefacts must be protected during field investigations.
  8. Explain the value of collaborating with First Nations communities in ecosystem investigations.
  9. Evaluate how uncontrolled variables can affect the validity of an investigation.
  10. Explain how peer review of another student’s method can improve investigation design.

Part C • 5-mark questions

Five exam-style responses

  1. 5 marks: Explain why identifying independent, dependent and controlled variables is essential for a valid investigation.
  2. 5 marks: Describe the importance of recognising assumptions and explain how incorrect assumptions can affect results.
  3. 5 marks: Explain how risk identification and management contribute to safe and effective investigations.
  4. 5 marks: Discuss why reproducibility matters and describe two ways to improve reproducibility in a student-designed method.
  5. 5 marks: Explain why First Nations heritage sites and artefacts must be protected during scientific investigations on Country/Place.

Part D • Enriched

10-mark investigation question

Evaluate how collaboration with First Nations Australians can improve the design, ethical considerations and outcomes of ecosystem investigations. In your answer, discuss co-design, local ecological knowledge, permissions/protocols, heritage protection, how knowledge will be used, and how outcomes can be mutually beneficial.
