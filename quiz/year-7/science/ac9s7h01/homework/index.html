Year 7 Science • AC9S7H01

Homework — How Scientific Knowledge Changes

Core idea: scientific knowledge is evidence-based and revisable. New evidence, improved technology or different perspectives can strengthen, refine or replace an explanation.

Part A — Core understanding

1. Explain why the statement “science changes, therefore science is unreliable” is incorrect.
2. Distinguish between new evidence and a different perspective. Explain how either can influence scientific knowledge.
3. Why is repeatable, independently checked evidence stronger than one surprising observation?
4. Explain why a scientific model may be useful even if scientists later revise it.

Part B — Curriculum case studies

5. Classification: Organisms A and B look very similar, but DNA evidence shows B is genetically much closer to C. Explain what scientists should reconsider and why.
6. Cultural burning: Explain how long-term First Nations observations and cultural burning practices can inform contemporary biodiversity and fire management. Avoid claiming that one knowledge system simply “replaces” another.
7. Sustainable harvesting: Explain how practices such as protecting breeding areas, avoiding pregnant animals and taking only what is needed can support sustainable populations.
8. Astronomy: Al-Battani used careful astronomical measurements to refine eclipse predictions and estimates of the solar year. Explain how this illustrates the development of scientific knowledge across cultures and time.
9. Aircraft: Engineers find that wing-tip vortices increase drag. Explain how evidence about airflow could lead to a design change and a measurable benefit.

Part C — Evidence analysis

Fire-management dataset: After a land-management program incorporating cultural burning, monitoring records 40% fewer intense fires, 25% greater native plant diversity and a 60% reduction in fuel load.
10. Identify two results that support the claim that the management approach may benefit the ecosystem.
11. Why would scientists still need information about the study design, comparison sites and repeated observations before making a strong causal conclusion?
12. Write an evidence-based conclusion that is appropriately cautious.

Part D — Higher-order application

13. A new explanation accounts for everything an older model explained and also correctly predicts observations the older model could not. Describe the steps scientists should take before accepting the new explanation.
14. Evaluate: “The newest scientific idea is always the best scientific idea.” Use evidence quality and testing in your response.
15. Choose classification, cultural burning, sustainable harvesting, historical astronomy or aircraft design. Write a structured response using: previous understanding → new evidence/perspective → revised understanding → why the revision is stronger.

Extension

Find one credible example of scientific knowledge that has changed. Record the source, identify the evidence that caused the change, and explain why the newer explanation was accepted. Do not simply state that scientists “changed their minds”.
