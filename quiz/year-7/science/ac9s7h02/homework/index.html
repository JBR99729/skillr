Year 7 Science • AC9S7H02 • Homework

Cultural perspectives and world views in science

Explain how culture, values and world views can influence scientific questions, knowledge and decisions while keeping evidence central to scientific claims.

10
Short-answer questions
6
Long-answer questions
1
Application task

Part A

Short-answer questions

  1. In 2–3 sentences, explain the main idea of AC9S7H02. Include the words evidence and perspective.

  2. Give two ways a First Nations classification system may organise living things differently from contemporary biological taxonomy.

  3. Why is it inaccurate to say that all First Nations Australians use one identical classification system?

  4. Explain how protecting a breeding season or breeding area can support sustainable harvesting.

  5. Why should cultural protocols and the source community be respected when using First Nations ecological knowledge?

  6. Explain one way a scientist’s personal values can influence their work without changing the scientific evidence.

  7. What do the curriculum examples of Richard Levins and Joseph Rotblat show about science as a human endeavour?

  8. Why might farmers, ecologists and First Nations communities have different perspectives on a proposed biological control?

  9. How did Wang Zhenyi contribute to scientific knowledge about astronomy?

  10. Explain how David Unaipon’s knowledge of boomerang aerodynamics contributed to his 1914 vertical-lift flying-machine concept.

Part B

Long-answer and reasoning questions

  1. Compare a First Nations classification system with contemporary biological taxonomy. Explain how different criteria can be useful for different contexts and purposes without ranking one system as simply “better”.

  2. A community harvests a species only after its breeding season. Over 12 years, surveys show stable adult numbers and regular numbers of young animals entering the population. Explain what this evidence suggests and one limitation of the conclusion.

  3. A scientist chooses to study a disease that mostly affects remote communities because they believe the problem has been neglected. Explain which part of the scientific process is influenced by the scientist’s values and which parts must still be evidence-based.

  4. A proposed biological control is likely to reduce an invasive pest but may also affect a native species important to local communities. Write a balanced recommendation explaining what scientific evidence and community perspectives should be considered before a decision is made.

  5. Explain why Wang Zhenyi is more than just a “famous scientist” example for this code. Link her cultural and historical context, her eclipse investigations and the idea that scientific knowledge develops through contributions from diverse people.

  6. Evaluate the claim: “Because science is based on evidence, culture has no influence on science.” State whether you agree, partly agree or disagree, and support your answer with at least two AC9S7H02 examples.

Part C

Application task — biological control decision

A region is considering releasing a biological control to reduce an invasive species. Create a decision table with at least four perspectives, such as ecologists, farmers, First Nations communities and local residents. For each perspective, identify one possible benefit, one possible concern and one piece of evidence they would need. Then write a 5–7 sentence recommendation explaining how evidence and perspectives should be combined in the final decision.
