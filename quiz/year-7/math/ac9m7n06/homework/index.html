Operations with Positive Rational Numbers

Choose representations strategically. Full marks require correct arithmetic and a sensible reason for choosing fractions, decimals or percentages.

Quick reference: common denominator for fraction ± • multiply across after simplifying • divide by multiplying by the reciprocal • convert representations when it makes the arithmetic easier • estimate to check

Part A • 10 marks

Five 2-mark questions

  1. Calculate 3/4 + 0.6. Show the conversion you use. [2]

  2. Evaluate 1.25 − 2/5. [2]

  3. Write 12.5% as a decimal and a fraction in simplest form. State which form is most efficient for calculating 12.5% of 64. [2]

  4. Calculate 0.3 × 5/6. Give the exact answer. [2]

  5. Evaluate 0.9 ÷ 3/10. Show one conversion or reciprocal step. [2]

Part B • 25 marks

Five 5-mark questions

  1. Calculate 5/8 + 0.375. [5]

    (a) Represent the two quantities using a common model such as an array, fraction bar or number line. (b) Convert to a common representation. (c) Add. (d) Explain why your representation is efficient. (e) Describe one alternative method and compare it.

  2. Calculate 12.5% of 96 using the most efficient representation. [5]

    (a) Rewrite 12.5% in a simpler form. (b) Calculate the value. (c) Explain why the chosen form is efficient. (d) Show a correct decimal method. (e) Compare the amount of work in the two methods.

  3. Calculate 2/3 × 3/4. [5]

    (a) Use the commutative property to rewrite the factors if useful. (b) Simplify before multiplying where possible. (c) Calculate and simplify. (d) Represent the product with an area/array model. (e) Explain why cancelling common factors improves efficiency.

  4. A nutrition plan records 32.5 g on Day 1, 45/2 g on Day 2, and 28% of 100 g on Day 3. [5]

    (a) Convert all three values to one representation. (b) Find the total. (c) Explain why your representation is efficient. (d) Express the total in one alternative representation where meaningful. (e) Explain how an estimate can check your total.

  5. Calculate 0.48 ÷ 3/5. [5]

    (a) Rewrite 0.48 as a fraction. (b) Apply the reciprocal rule. (c) Simplify and calculate. (d) Explain the division as a grouping problem or model. (e) Compare this fraction method with a decimal-only strategy.

Part C • Enrichment

Land-use planning investigation

A council allocates a 960-hectare area as follows: 18% parkland, 0.32 retail, and 1/5 manufacturing. The remainder is reserved for housing and community facilities.

Task: Choose an efficient representation for each allocation, calculate the hectares in each named category and the remaining area, justify your strategy choices, and show at least one place where commutative or associative reasoning makes a calculation easier. Finish by explaining how using inconsistent or inefficient representations could increase the risk of a planning error.
