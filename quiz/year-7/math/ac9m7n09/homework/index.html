Part A • 10 marks

Five 2-mark questions

  1. A lift starts at floor 0, goes up 9 floors, then down 14 floors. Write a signed-number model and state the final floor. [2]

  2. 55% of a Year 7 cohort of 320 students attends an event. Write a proportion model and calculate the number attending. [2]

  3. A fundraiser earns $480 and costs $525. Model the situation and determine the profit or loss. [2]

  4. A bank account starts at $150. A debit of $45 and a credit of $20 occur. Write a signed-number model and find the final balance. [2]

  5. 12% of a 900-student school votes in a survey. Model and calculate the number of voters. [2]

Part B • 25 marks

Five 5-mark questions

  1. A submarine begins at −240 m. It rises 85 m, drops 120 m, rises 60 m and then drops 30 m. [5]

    (a) Represent the movements with signed numbers. (b) Compute the final depth. (c) Describe a number-line model. (d) Explain why a reference level and signed values are useful. (e) Interpret the final result.

  2. 23% of a school population of 1200 students votes yes to a proposal. [5]

    (a) Write a proportion equation. (b) Calculate the number of yes votes. (c) Show a second representation such as 0.23 or 23/100. (d) Explain which representation is most efficient. (e) Interpret the result in context.

  3. A sausage sizzle has revenue of $1050 and total costs of $780. [5]

    (a) Write a profit equation. (b) Calculate the profit. (c) Calculate percentage profit based on cost. (d) Explain why percentage profit helps compare events of different sizes. (e) Write a concluding statement.

  4. A bank account starts at $600. Transactions are debit $120, credit $75, debit $200 and credit $40. [5]

    (a) Represent the transactions using signed numbers. (b) Find the final balance. (c) Organise the transactions in a two-column table. (d) Explain why the signed model is useful. (e) Check your answer using a second method.

  5. A school site is 18,000 m² and 35% is green space. [5]

    (a) Write a proportion model. (b) Calculate the green-space area. (c) Show an alternative fraction or decimal representation. (d) Explain why your representation is efficient. (e) State one reasonableness check for the result.

Part C • Enrichment

Comparing two land-use proportions

A hypothetical 2.4 million km² region is modelled with 18% in Category A and 27% in Category B. This task is designed to practise the AC9M7N09 land-use modelling elaboration without treating these percentages as claims about actual Australian land-use data.

Task: (1) model both areas with percentage equations; (2) calculate both areas; (3) compare them using a ratio, percentage-point difference and relative percentage increase; (4) explain why proportional representations help compare land-use categories; (5) state at least one assumption or limitation of the model; and (6) explain how you would verify real-world data before making a cultural, agricultural or geographical conclusion.
