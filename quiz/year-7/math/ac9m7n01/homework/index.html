Perfect Squares and Square Roots

Complete Part A first, then show full working in Part B. Use square and square-root notation correctly and explain your reasoning where requested.

Useful reminders: n² = n × n • √a is the positive number whose square is a • (a+b)² = a² + 2ab + b² • square perimeter = 4 × side

Part A

Core understanding

  1. Write the first 12 positive perfect square numbers, from 1² to 12². Then state √81 and √144.

  2. A square garden has side length 18 m. Find its area. Then write the related square-root statement.

  3. Without using a decimal approximation, determine between which two consecutive whole numbers √70 lies. Show the two perfect squares you used.

  4. Calculate 43² using 43 = 40 + 3 and the distributive/area-model method. Show all terms, including the middle term.

  5. A student says 15² = 30 because “squared means multiply by 2”. Explain the mistake and give the correct value of 15².

Part B

Reasoning and application

  1. A square tiled floor contains 576 tiles arranged in a square. Find the number of tiles along one side and the perimeter in tile lengths.

  2. The square numbers 12² and 13² differ by 25. Use this example to explain why consecutive square numbers differ by 2n + 1.

  3. A student expands (50 + 6)² as 2500 + 300 + 36. Identify the exact error, correct the expansion and calculate 56².

  4. A square photograph is enlarged so that each side becomes 3 times as long. A student claims the area also becomes 3 times as large. Explain why this is incorrect and state the correct area scale factor.

  5. A square has perimeter 84 cm. Find its side length and area. Then check your result by taking the square root of the area.

Part C

Real-world design task

A school wants to create a square outdoor chess-and-games area using exactly 900 square paving tiles. Each tile is 30 cm by 30 cm.

  1. How many tiles will be along each side?
  2. What is the side length of the completed square in metres?
  3. What is its perimeter in metres?
  4. If a 2 m-wide entrance is left unfenced, how many metres of fencing are required?
  5. Explain how square roots helped you solve the problem.
