Use sentence openings to guide the reader

Name: __________________________ Date: ______________

Explain your choices using evidence from the example. Give reasons where requested.

Use the first two questions in class and the last two for homework or independent practice.

1. Try the skill

A paragraph describes changes to a garden. Which opening links more directly to it: ‘In the garden, ...’ or ‘My cousin, ...’? Explain.

2. Try the skill

Rewrite ‘A quiet reading corner stands behind the library desk’ to give prominence to its location.

3. Apply and explain

Write two sentences about a playground. Begin the second with a place mentioned in the first to guide the reader.

4. Apply and explain

Explain why your second sentence connects to the first.

Check answers and explanations

Answer guide · AC9E5LA04

  1. ‘In the garden, ...’ directly maintains the location as the focus, unless the paragraph deliberately shifts to the cousin.
  2. Behind the library desk stands a quiet reading corner. Other grammatical rewrites with that location first are acceptable.
  3. For example: ‘A new shade sail covers the sandpit. Under the sail, younger children build castles.’
  4. It picks up a previously introduced place or feature and uses it as the starting point for new information.

If help is needed: A different sentence opening is not automatically better. It should connect ideas and give prominence to the intended message.
