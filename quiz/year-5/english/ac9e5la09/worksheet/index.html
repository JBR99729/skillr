AC9E5LA09 · Before you begin

Use commas to separate an introductory prepositional phrase when it helps the reader see where the main clause begins, and use apostrophes carefully to show ownership when more than one person or thing possesses something.

Check: identify the whole prepositional phrase, find its object, then decide whether a comma improves the boundary. For possession, first decide whether the owners act together or own things separately before placing the apostrophe.

Use this printable homework sheet to practise phrase boundaries, comma decisions and possessive apostrophes in complete Year 5 sentences.

What to check

  • Select the complete prepositional phrase, not only the preposition.
  • Do not add an apostrophe simply because a noun is plural.
  • For several owners, work out whether ownership is shared or separate before punctuating.
  • Read the whole sentence again after editing to check that the punctuation clarifies meaning.
