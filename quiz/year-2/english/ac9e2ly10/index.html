AC9E2LY10 • Year 2 English

Advanced Sound–Letter Patterns

use phoneme-grapheme (sound-letter/s) matches, including vowel digraphs, less common long vowel patterns, consonant clusters and silent letters when reading and writing words of one or more syllables, including compound words

Choose a learning activity. Practice rotates 8 questions from a 24-question bank; Test draws 12 questions from a separate 16-question bank.

Unit focus

  • A phoneme may be represented by one or more letters. Readers recognise common and less common patterns, preserve sounds in clusters and notice silent letters and word parts.
  • Use this routine: Say word → Identify sounds → Map graphemes → Notice pattern → Check meaning.
  • Watch for one letter equals one sound: Some sounds use digraphs, and some letters are silent.
