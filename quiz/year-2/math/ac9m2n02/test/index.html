Regroup and Rename Numbers (AC9M2N02)

Practise partitioning, equal exchanges and zero. Some tasks ask you to build, draw or explain; a grown-up checks those responses.

Before you begin

A hundred flat is worth 100. A ten rod is worth 10. A one cube is worth 1. H means hundreds, T means tens and O means ones.

  • Partition means split a number into parts. Rearrange means move the whole parts into a different order. Keep each unit name with its number.
  • The usual grouping has fewer than 10 pieces in each place. Other equal groupings may have 10 or more tens or ones.
  • 10 ones have the same value as 1 ten. 10 tens have the same value as 1 hundred. You can exchange in either direction.
  • Complete tens include the tens inside hundreds. Leftover ones are the ones left after making complete groups of ten.
  • A zero records an empty place in the usual numeral. No separate ten rods does not mean there are no tens inside the hundreds.
  • Use blocks, drawings or words for the tasks that ask you to show or explain. You may speak, sign or have someone write your explanation. These tasks wait for a grown-up’s check.
Worked examples and learning support

41 = 4 tens + 1 one = 3 tens + 11 ones. All three names have the same value.

165 = 1 hundred + 6 tens + 5 ones = 16 tens + 5 ones.

203 = 2 hundreds + 0 tens + 3 ones = 20 tens + 3 ones.

Open the hundred into 10 tens. Keep the 6 tens and 5 ones already there. Both models make 165: 16 complete tens and 5 leftover ones.
