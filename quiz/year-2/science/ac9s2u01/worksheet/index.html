AC9S2U01 — Topic Practice 1 Answer Key

Checked teacher copy

  1. B. Earth is a planet.Earth travels around the Sun and is one of the eight planets.
  2. Sun → B (star); Moon → C (Earth's natural satellite); Earth → A (planet).
  3. Third.
  4. Accept any two supported observations, for example: Earth has a curved/round outline; large areas of water are visible; land is visible; cloud cover is visible.
  5. Accept: a camera on a satellite or spacecraft, or a camera used by an astronaut/space mission.
  6. Example: the model helps show relationships such as Earth being smaller than the Sun and travelling around it; it is not exact because real sizes and distances are enormously different and cannot both be shown accurately on a desk.
  7. Mainly day: Sun. Mainly night: most stars. Sometimes day or night: Moon.
  8. Example: stars are still present and shining during the day, but the bright daytime sky makes most too faint for our eyes to see.
  9. 12:00 pm. The shadow is shortest then (65 cm), consistent with the Sun appearing highest.
  10. Prediction: the shadow will usually become longer again. Reason: later in the afternoon the Sun appears lower in the sky.

AC9S2U01 — Topic Practice 2 Answer Key

Checked teacher copy

  1. Repeated observations show change over time, allowing a pattern to be identified and a sensible prediction to be made. One drawing shows only one time.
  2. Thin curved lit part → B (crescent); almost fully lit → C (full Moon); difficult to see because lit side mostly faces away → A (new Moon).
  3. C. About 29–30 days.
  4. Example: the Moon remains approximately spherical. As it moves around Earth, we see different amounts of its sunlit half, so its visible illuminated portion appears to change.
  5. Earth's rotation on its axis.
  6. Within a day: shadow changing morning to midday. About a month: Moon phase cycle. Across seasons: different star patterns visible.
  7. D. Quarter Moon. Blue Moon, blood Moon and supermoon are special named events, not separate regular phase shapes.
  8. Accept: measure or mark the same object's shadow at different times; use teacher-provided safe images/time-lapse; use approved specialist equipment under appropriate supervision. Do not accept staring at the Sun or ordinary binoculars/magnifiers.
  9. Example: First Nations Australian sky knowledges are diverse and place-based. Naming the specific People/community respects the source and avoids presenting one tradition as if it belongs to all First Nations Peoples.
  10. Accept a workable repeated-observation plan. It must name the target observation, include repeated times/dates, and state a record such as a table, dated drawing, measurement or labelled notes. Safety must be appropriate if the Sun is involved.
