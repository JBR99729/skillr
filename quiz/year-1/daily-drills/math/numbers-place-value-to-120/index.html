Number and Place Value to 120

Use tens and ones to understand two- and three-digit numbers. Compare numbers from the greatest place value first.

Key ideas

  • read and write numbers
  • tens and ones
  • before, after and between
  • order numbers
  • skip-count patterns

Worked example: 47 = 4 tens + 7 ones.

Common trap: 47 is not 4 + 7.

Key words

tens — groups of ten
ones — single units
partition — split into place-value parts
greater — larger value
less — smaller value
How to use this drill: review the short notes when learning, then close them on later attempts and practise from memory. The rotating bank delays a full set repeat for 30 attempts on the same browser.

What this Year 1 / Grade 1 maths drill practises

Read, order and partition numbers to at least 120. This daily drill is designed as short retrieval practice rather than a complete course. It can be used for school revision, homework, warm-ups or independent practice.

  • read and write numbers
  • tens and ones
  • before, after and between
  • order numbers
  • skip-count patterns

International use: the topic is organised by broadly useful school-level progression. Exact curriculum timing and terminology can vary between countries, states and schools.
