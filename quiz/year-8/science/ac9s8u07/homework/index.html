Physical and Chemical Changes

Compare physical and chemical changes using observations, particle ideas, energy and evidence of new substances. Answer in full sentences where useful, and use evidence or examples in longer responses.

10
Short-answer questions
10
Long-answer questions
1
Research/application task

Part A

Short-answer questions

  1. 1. Define physical change.

  2. 2. Define chemical change.

  3. 3. State one sign that a chemical change may have occurred.

  4. 4. Why is melting ice a physical change?

  5. 5. Why is burning paper a chemical change?

  6. 6. Explain why a colour change alone is not always enough evidence of a chemical reaction.

  7. 7. What does gas production suggest during a reaction?

  8. 8. Classify dissolving sugar in water as physical or chemical and justify your answer.

  9. 9. What is meant by a new substance forming?

  10. 10. Give one example of an exothermic or endothermic change.

Part B

Long-answer questions

  1. 1. Compare physical and chemical changes using particle arrangement, reversibility and formation of new substances.

  2. 2. A student mixes two clear liquids and bubbles form. Explain what this evidence could suggest and what extra evidence would strengthen the conclusion.

  3. 3. Explain why rusting iron is a chemical change but cutting iron into smaller pieces is a physical change.

  4. 4. Describe a simple investigation that could test whether a change produced a new substance.

  5. 5. Evaluate the claim, “If a change can be reversed, it must be physical.” Include an example or limitation.

  6. 6. Explain the difference between dissolving, melting and reacting using particle ideas.

  7. 7. Use observations to classify three changes: evaporating water, burning fuel and mixing sand with salt.

  8. 8. Describe how temperature change can be evidence in chemical reactions, but also why it must be interpreted carefully.

  9. 9. Write a table comparing at least four examples of physical and chemical changes with evidence for each classification.

  10. 10. Write a full explanation of how scientists use multiple observations to decide whether a chemical reaction has occurred.

Part C

Research and understanding task

Research one useful chemical change in everyday life, industry, cooking, medicine or the environment. Explain the reactants, products if known, evidence of change and why the reaction matters.
