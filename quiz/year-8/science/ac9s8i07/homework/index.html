Year 8 Science • AC9S8I07 • Authored homework

Communicate Science Clearly

Communicate scientific ideas and findings for a purpose and audience using evidence, correct vocabulary and clear structure. Show reasoning in full sentences where useful, and check calculations or conclusions before finishing.

10
Short-answer questions
10
Long-answer questions
1
Research/application task

Part A

Short-answer questions

  1. 1. Why does audience matter in science communication?

  2. 2. Write a clearer version of: “The stuff changed because it reacted.”

  3. 3. What is one feature of a good scientific explanation?

  4. 4. Why should evidence be included in a conclusion?

  5. 5. Name two ways to communicate scientific findings besides a written report.

  6. 6. What is the role of a labelled diagram?

  7. 7. How can headings improve a science report?

  8. 8. Why should scientific vocabulary be defined for younger readers?

  9. 9. What is one difference between a poster and a lab report?

  10. 10. Write one sentence that links evidence to a claim.

Part B

Long-answer questions

  1. 1. Write a short explanation for Year 6 students about the difference between physical and chemical change.

  2. 2. Turn a set of investigation results into a clear claim-evidence-reasoning paragraph.

  3. 3. Design a one-page poster plan explaining why cells are the basic units of living things.

  4. 4. Explain how you would communicate plate tectonics differently to a teacher, a Year 5 student and a community group.

  5. 5. Rewrite a confusing conclusion so it includes the claim, evidence and reasoning.

  6. 6. Create a labelled flow chart explaining an energy transformation in an everyday device.

  7. 7. Compare a table, graph and paragraph for communicating results. Which is best for which audience?

  8. 8. Write a short speech explaining a science issue and include one piece of evidence.

  9. 9. Evaluate a science infographic: what makes it clear, and what might mislead readers?

  10. 10. Prepare a communication plan for a science investigation, including purpose, audience, format and key evidence.

Part C

Research and understanding task

Find a science communication example such as an infographic, museum panel, video or article. Explain who it is for, what it communicates well and how it could be improved.
