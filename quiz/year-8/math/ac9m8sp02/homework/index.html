Answer guide · AC9M8SP02

Other valid methods and well-supported explanations are welcome.

  1. For example, opposite sides are parallel and equal; opposite angles are equal.
  2. 75°, since the interior angles total 360°.
  3. It has two pairs of parallel opposite sides; its right angles are additional properties.
  4. A rhombus has four equal sides but need not have right angles. A square is a special rhombus.
  5. 112°, 68°, 112°; adjacent angles are supplementary and opposite angles equal.
  6. Alternate angles at the parallel sides match and AC is shared, giving congruent triangles by ASA; corresponding sides AB and CD are equal.
  7. No. A non-rectangular parallelogram can have unequal diagonals that still bisect each other.
  8. One right angle suffices: adjacent angles are supplementary and opposite angles equal, so all four become right angles.
