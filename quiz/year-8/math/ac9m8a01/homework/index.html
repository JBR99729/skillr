Answer guide · AC9M8A01

Other valid methods and well-supported explanations are welcome.

  1. 6x + 15.
  2. 4(2x + 3).
  3. 2a + 12; combine like variable terms and constants separately.
  4. 2(x + 3) = 2x + 6; multiply both terms by 2.
  5. 10x - 5 - 3x - 12 = 7x - 17. Distribute, then combine like terms.
  6. For example, 3(2x + 3) and 2(3x + 4) + 1; expansion gives 6x + 9 in each case.
  7. 4(2x + 3) = 8x + 12 square units.
  8. No. Both give 2 at x = 0, but give 3 and 5 at x = 1. An algebraic identity must hold for every permitted value.
