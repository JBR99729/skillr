Parent and teacher guide

Chance language helps students reason about everyday uncertainty. Begin with clear events that are impossible, certain, likely or unlikely. A common mistake is using personal preference instead of evidence, such as saying rain is likely because they want it to rain. Use simple spinners, bags of counters and everyday examples so students can justify their answer. Ask what outcomes are possible and whether each outcome has a fair or unfair chance. The goal is careful reasoning, not formal fractions yet.

Common mistakes to watch for

Ask the student to explain their method before moving to a test. If they can only guess, return to one visual model and one worked example. Short practice with feedback usually works better than a long worksheet completed without review.
