Parent and teacher guide

Students often know time words from daily life but need practice comparing them mathematically. Begin with familiar units such as days in a week, months in a year and minutes in an hour. Then build questions that ask students to choose the most sensible unit for a situation. A common mistake is treating all time units like base-ten place value, for example thinking 100 minutes equals 1 hour. Keep anchor facts visible: 60 minutes in an hour, 24 hours in a day and 7 days in a week. Short word problems help students see why the unit matters.

Common mistakes to watch for

Ask the student to explain their method before moving to a test. If they can only guess, return to one visual model and one worked example. Short practice with feedback usually works better than a long worksheet completed without review.
