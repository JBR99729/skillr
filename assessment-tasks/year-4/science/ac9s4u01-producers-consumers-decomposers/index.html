AC9S4U01 • Year 4 Science assessment task

Producers, consumers, decomposers and food chains assessment

A practical assessment for checking whether students can classify organism roles, construct food chains and explain how feeding relationships operate in a habitat.

Assessment stimulus

Use a simple habitat containing grass, grasshoppers, frogs, snakes, fungi and bacteria. Students may work from a labelled image, organism cards or a short written habitat description.

Assessment questions

  1. Classify each organism as a producer, consumer or decomposer.
  2. Construct one valid food chain from the habitat.
  3. Explain what the arrows in the food chain show.
  4. Predict one likely effect if the producer population decreases.
  5. Explain why decomposers are important in the habitat.
  6. Compare a food chain with a food web.

What strong evidence looks like

  • correctly identifies producer, consumer and decomposer roles;
  • draws arrows from food source to the organism receiving energy;
  • uses cause-and-effect reasoning when predicting a population change;
  • explains decomposition as part of nutrient cycling rather than simply “cleaning up”;
  • recognises that real habitats contain interconnected food chains.

Teacher use

This task can be used as an exit assessment, short written task, oral conference or small-group investigation. It is aligned to Australian Curriculum AC9S4U01 and closely related to Victorian Curriculum VC2S4U03.
