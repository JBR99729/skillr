AC9M8N01 — IRRATIONAL NUMBERS IN APPLIED CONTEXTS
STATUS: MORE-RESEARCH-REQUIRED

CURRICULUM DESCRIPTION
Recognise irrational numbers in applied contexts, including square roots and π.

CURRICULUM BOUNDARY
Define rational numbers as ratios of integers and recognise irrational numbers as non-terminating, non-recurring decimals. Include non-perfect square roots and π in measurement/design contexts, locate approximate values on number lines, and distinguish exact forms from decimal approximations. Formal irrationality proofs and operations with surds are not required.

IXL MAPPING AND EVIDENCE
Direct mappings:
- D.1 “Identify rational and irrational numbers”: https://au.ixl.com/maths/year-8/identify-rational-and-irrational-numbers
- F.13 “Square roots of perfect squares”
- F.14 “Estimate square roots”: https://au.ixl.com/maths/year-8/estimate-square-roots
- F.15 “Relationship between squares and square roots”
IXL’s progression begins with classifying integers, fractions, terminating/repeating decimals, π and radicals; it then brackets a non-perfect square root between consecutive perfect squares and estimates its position. For √50, use 7²=49 and 8²=64, so √50 lies just above 7. This worked-method pattern should transfer to visual number-line questions rather than calculator-only recognition.
Applied contexts: square diagonals, circular circumference/diameter, exact Pythagorean lengths and design measurements.
Misconceptions: every square root is irrational; every non-terminating decimal is irrational; π=3.14 exactly; a calculator display is the complete value; √a lies halfway between neighbouring integers; negative numbers have ordinary real square roots; confusing √49 with ±7.

KHAN / TRUSTED EVIDENCE
Khan “Intro to rational & irrational numbers” defines rational numbers as fractions of integers and uses π and non-perfect roots as irrational examples:
https://www.khanacademy.org/math/algebra/x2f8bb11595b61c86%3Airrational-numbers/x2f8bb11595b61c86%3Airrational-numbers-intro/v/introduction-to-rational-and-irrational-numbers
Khan classification evidence notes √8 and π cannot be written as ratios of integers:
https://www.khanacademy.org/math/algebra-1-illustrative-math/x6418b49dfbc9d0c9%3Aquadratic-equations-part1/x6418b49dfbc9d0c9%3Arational-irrational-solutions/v/recognizing-irrational-numbers
AC v9/QCAA provide the applied-context emphasis.

AUTHORING TRANSFER
Topic Page: number-system hierarchy; terminating/repeating versus non-recurring decimals; perfect-square filter; bracket-and-estimate method; exact versus approximate notation; square diagonal and circle examples.
Classroom View: card sort, construct √2 from a unit square, place roots on a number line, compare π with 3.14, calculator display discussion and error-analysis exit ticket.
Homework: classify representations, estimate roots, order mixed rational/irrational values, interpret exact lengths and critique false claims.
Feedback: identify the representation, test whether the root is perfect or decimal terminates/repeats, bracket using perfect squares, then use ≈ only for rounded values.

P01–P08 rational prerequisites; P09–P16 classify numbers; P17–P24 perfect/non-perfect roots; P25–P32 estimate/order on number lines; P33–P40 π and measurement contexts; P41–P48 exact/approximate reasoning and error analysis.

AC9M8N02 — EXPONENT LAWS AND ZERO EXPONENT
STATUS: MORE-RESEARCH-REQUIRED

CURRICULUM DESCRIPTION
Establish and apply the exponent laws with positive integer exponents and the zero exponent, using exponent notation with numbers.

CURRICULUM BOUNDARY
Derive laws from repeated factors before applying them: same-base product, same-base quotient where expressions remain defined, power of a power, power of a product/quotient and a⁰=1 for a≠0. Numerical bases are the stated endpoint; negative exponents, fractional exponents and broad symbolic algebra belong later.

IXL MAPPING AND EVIDENCE
Direct mappings:
- F.6 “Multiplication with exponents”
- F.7 “Division with exponents”
- F.8 “Multiplication and division with exponents”
- F.9 “Power rule”
- F.10 “Evaluate expressions using properties of exponents”
- F.11/F.12 “Identify equivalent expressions involving exponents I/II”
IXL’s worked-method progression expands powers to expose repeated factors, compresses the result, and only then applies the law. Example structure: 3²×3⁴ has 2+4 factors of 3, so it is 3⁶; (5³)² has 3×2 factors of 5, so it is 5⁶. For a⁴÷a⁴=1, subtracting exponents gives a⁰, establishing a⁰=1 when a≠0.
Misconceptions: multiplying bases when bases are equal; multiplying exponents in a product; subtracting exponents when bases differ; (a+b)²=a²+b²; a⁰=0; treating −3² and (−3)² as identical; applying quotient laws when the base is zero; combining unlike bases without justification.

KHAN / TRUSTED EVIDENCE
Khan’s integer-exponent review states aⁿaᵐ=aⁿ⁺ᵐ and aⁿ/aᵐ=aⁿ⁻ᵐ for non-zero denominator:
https://www.khanacademy.org/math/algebra/x2f8bb11595b61c86%3Arational-exponents-radicals/x2f8bb11595b61c86%3Aexponent-properties-review/v/multiplying-and-dividing-powers-with-integer-exponents
Khan’s zeroth-power lesson distinguishes a⁰=1 for non-zero a from 0 raised to positive powers:
https://www.khanacademy.org/v/the-zeroth-power

AUTHORING TRANSFER
Topic Page: repeated-factor derivations; law table with conditions and non-examples; parentheses/negative-base contrast; zero exponent derivation; mixed-law worked examples verified by evaluation.
Classroom View: factor-string manipulation, students predict laws, law-or-not sorting, mistake clinic and mini-proof of zero exponent.
Homework: expand/compress, apply one law, combine laws, evaluate numerical expressions, identify equivalent forms and explain invalid moves.
Feedback: check same base and operation, expand one step if uncertain, apply only the matching law, retain parentheses and verify numerically.

48-PRACTICE BLUEPRINT
P01–P08 exponent meaning/evaluation; P09–P16 product law; P17–P24 quotient/zero exponent; P25–P32 power of power/product/quotient; P33–P40 mixed evaluation/equivalence; P41–P48 conditions, proofs and error analysis.
16-TEST BLUEPRINT
T01–T04 foundations; T05–T08 single laws; T09–T12 independent mixed-law evaluation; T13–T16 derivation and error analysis.

AC9M8N03 — TERMINATING AND RECURRING DECIMALS
STATUS: MORE-RESEARCH-REQUIRED

CURRICULUM DESCRIPTION
Recognise terminating and recurring decimals, using digital tools as appropriate.

CURRICULUM BOUNDARY
Recognise, generate and distinguish terminating and recurring decimals from fractions; use bar/dot notation consistently; use long division and calculators/spreadsheets to investigate patterns. In lowest terms, denominators with only prime factors 2 and/or 5 terminate; other denominators recur. Formal algebraic conversion of recurring decimals to fractions may be an extension but is not the descriptor’s central requirement.

IXL MAPPING AND EVIDENCE
Direct mappings:
- D.5 “Convert between decimals and fractions or mixed numbers”
- D.6 “Repeating decimals”: https://au.ixl.com/maths/year-8/repeating-decimals
- A.4 prime factorisation as a supporting denominator test
IXL question progression distinguishes finite decimal strings from repeating patterns, translates recurring notation, and converts common fractions through division. Example method: 1÷6=0.1666…; the 6 repeats, so write 0.1 with a bar over 6. For 3/40, 40=2³×5, hence the decimal terminates; for 2/15, the simplified denominator includes 3, hence it recurs.
Digital-tool evidence must include awareness that a calculator display is truncated/rounded and may hide a long repeating cycle.
Misconceptions: “recurring” means the final digit repeats once; all non-terminating decimals recur; recurring decimals are irrational; writing the bar over non-repeating digits; deciding from an unsimplified denominator; assuming every calculator display terminates; confusing 0.3 with 0.333….

KHAN / TRUSTED EVIDENCE
Khan rational-number classification explains that terminating and repeating decimals are rational, while non-terminating non-repeating decimals are irrational:
https://www.khanacademy.org/v/categorizing-numbers
Khan fraction/decimal resources support long division and recurring-pattern recognition. Exact full-code digital investigation is not packaged as one lesson: SOURCE-NOT-MAPPED at full-code level.
NRICH fraction-decimal investigations provide additional pattern reasoning: https://nrich.maths.org/primary/geometry-and-measure/fractions-decimals-percentages

AUTHORING TRANSFER
Topic Page: decimal-type flowchart; long division examples; recurring notation; simplified-denominator prime-factor test; calculator limitation panel; cycle-length investigation.
Classroom View: predict before calculating, group fractions by denominator, spreadsheet expansion, uncover hidden cycles, notation checks and justify-from-denominator exit ticket.
Homework: classify decimals, continue patterns, write recurring notation, predict from denominators, verify digitally and diagnose truncated-display claims.
Feedback: simplify the fraction, factor the denominator, identify whether only 2/5 remain, then confirm by division and mark the full recurring block.

48-PRACTICE BLUEPRINT
P01–P08 decimal/fraction prerequisites; P09–P16 recognise terminating/recurring; P17–P24 notation and patterns; P25–P32 fraction-to-decimal division; P33–P40 denominator-factor prediction; P41–P48 digital-tool interpretation and reasoning.
16-TEST BLUEPRINT
T01–T04 recognition/notation; T05–T08 conversion; T09–T12 independent denominator reasoning; T13–T16 calculator evidence and misconception analysis.

AC9M8N04 — FOUR OPERATIONS WITH INTEGERS AND RATIONAL NUMBERS
STATUS: MORE-RESEARCH-REQUIRED

CURRICULUM DESCRIPTION
Use the 4 operations with integers and with rational numbers, choosing and using efficient strategies and digital tools where appropriate.

CURRICULUM BOUNDARY
Combine signed integers, fractions, decimals and mixed forms using all four operations and correct order of operations. Require efficient strategy selection, estimation and exact/technology-supported checking. Rational operations should include negative fractions/decimals and multi-step expressions; modelling belongs mainly in N05.

IXL MAPPING AND EVIDENCE
Direct mappings:
- C.1–C.15 operations with integers, especially “Add, subtract, multiply and divide integers” and “Evaluate numerical expressions involving integers”
- E.2–E.9 operations with rational numbers, especially https://au.ixl.com/maths/year-8/evaluate-numerical-expressions-involving-rational-numbers
IXL progression moves from sign rules and number-line/counter models to mixed operations and expressions. High-value worked-method patterns: rewrite subtraction as addition of the opposite; use parity of negative factors for product sign; use a common denominator for fraction addition; multiply by the reciprocal for fraction division; preserve brackets and apply multiplication/division before addition/subtraction.
Efficiency evidence: 17−(−23) is recognised as 17+23; 4.8×25 can use 25=100÷4; fractions may be simplified before multiplying. Digital tools should confirm difficult arithmetic after the expression and estimate have been set up.
Misconceptions: two negatives always make a positive, even in addition; subtracting denominators; inverting both fractions; applying order left-to-right without precedence; losing a negative sign outside brackets; converting exact fractions to rounded decimals too early; calculator entry not matching written structure.

KHAN / TRUSTED EVIDENCE
Khan arithmetic materials support negative-number operations, fraction operations and order of operations:
https://www.khanacademy.org/math/arithmetic-home/negative-numbers
https://www.khanacademy.org/math/arithmetic/fraction-arithmetic
Khan is useful for visual sign/fraction models; AC v9/QCAA add explicit efficient-strategy and digital-tool choice.

AUTHORING TRANSFER
Topic Page: sign reasoning models and rules; fraction/decimal strategies; operation decision table; order-of-operations examples; estimate–calculate–check cycle; calculator-entry audit.
Classroom View: number-line integer warm-up, strategy comparison, mixed-representation relay, bracket/error clinic and “mental/written/digital?” justification.
Homework: signed operations, rational conversions, mixed expressions, wordless numerical problems, efficient-method choices and calculator-output checks.
Feedback: identify operation and signs, choose a common form, state the strategy, preserve brackets, estimate the sign/size, calculate and compare.

48-PRACTICE BLUEPRINT
P01–P08 integer addition/subtraction; P09–P16 integer multiplication/division; P17–P24 fraction operations; P25–P32 decimal/mixed rational operations; P33–P40 order of operations; P41–P48 efficiency, technology and error analysis.
16-TEST BLUEPRINT
T01–T04 integer fluency; T05–T08 rational operations; T09–T12 independent mixed expressions; T13–T16 strategy justification and technology audit.

AC9M8N05 — MATHEMATICAL MODELLING WITH RATIONAL NUMBERS AND PERCENTAGES
STATUS: MORE-RESEARCH-REQUIRED

CURRICULUM DESCRIPTION
Use mathematical modelling to solve practical problems involving rational numbers and percentages, including financial contexts; formulate problems, choosing efficient calculation strategies and using digital tools where appropriate; interpret and communicate solutions in terms of the situation, reviewing the appropriateness of the model.

CURRICULUM BOUNDARY
Complete the modelling cycle with rational/percentage contexts: formulate variables, assumptions and constraints; select mental, written or digital strategies; calculate; interpret; communicate; and review. Include discounts, mark-ups, GST, profit/loss, percentage change, budgets and signed changes. Use current financial data only when sourced and dated; the mathematics must not depend on unstable tax thresholds.

IXL MAPPING AND EVIDENCE
Direct mappings:
- I.5/I.6 “Find what percent one number is of another”
- I.8/I.9 “Percents of numbers and money amounts” / word problems
- I.12–I.14 percent change and reverse-original-amount problems
- J.2–J.7 unit prices, GST, discount, mark-up, sale price and multi-step percent problems
Key worked-method progression: identify whole/base and part/change; convert percent to a multiplier; apply discount/tax in the correct order; preserve full precision; interpret money to cents; compare alternatives. A 20% discount uses multiplier 0.80, not subtracting 20; a later 10% rise acts on the new amount, so opposite percentages do not generally cancel.
Modelling extension beyond IXL routines: state assumptions, choose a representation/technology, test reasonableness and review limitations such as fees, rounding, eligibility or changing rates.
Misconceptions: wrong reference whole; adding the percent sign as a number; 20% off then 20% up returns to original; treating GST-inclusive and GST-exclusive prices identically; adding successive percentages instead of multiplying; rounding each intermediate step; assuming calculator output is a complete contextual answer; silently using outdated tax rates.

KHAN / TRUSTED EVIDENCE
Khan “Percent word problems: tax and discount” models identifying the original amount, percent and resulting total:
https://www.khanacademy.org/math/cc-seventh-grade-math/cc-7th-fractions-decimals/cc-7th-percent-word-problems/v/tax-discount-and-tip-examples
IXL supplies richer Australian consumer-maths mappings; AC v9/QCAA control the full modelling/review cycle.
Australian Taxation Office is the authoritative source for GST and any dated tax-table contexts:
https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst

AUTHORING TRANSFER
Topic Page: modelling-cycle panel; identify base/change/result; percentage multipliers; GST-inclusive/exclusive worked examples; repeated change; spreadsheet formula example; assumptions/limitations checklist and contextual communication model.
Classroom View: compare two sale offers, build a budget/spreadsheet, predict then calculate repeated changes, peer-audit assumptions and finish with a recommendation backed by evidence.
Homework: discounts/mark-ups/GST, percentage change, reverse original amounts, budgets and multi-step comparison models with written conclusions.
Feedback: name the reference whole, convert to a multiplier, show sequence/order, retain precision, round once for money and review whether the model omitted any relevant condition.

48-PRACTICE BLUEPRINT
P01–P08 percent/rational foundations; P09–P16 discounts and mark-ups; P17–P24 GST and money; P25–P32 percentage change/reverse amounts; P33–P40 budgets and repeated change; P41–P48 complete models, digital-tool outputs, assumptions and review.
16-TEST BLUEPRINT
T01–T04 foundations; T05–T08 financial calculations; T09–T12 independent multi-step/repeated-change models; T13–T16 formulate, communicate and review. Do not reuse Practice contexts.
