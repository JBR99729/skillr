Addition and Subtraction within 20

What students learn

Concept model and worked application

For subtraction, think about the missing part or count back carefully.

Use ten as a stepping stone

Explain an addition or subtraction strategy using parts within 20.

Worked example

Work out 8 + 5 without counting all thirteen objects from one.

  1. Eight needs two more to make ten. Split the five into two and three.
  2. Add the two to eight: 8 + 2 = 10. Three of the five are still left.
  3. 10 + 3 = 13, so 8 + 5 = 13. Check by removing five from thirteen.

Watch for this mistake: Do not use the whole five again after making ten. The two used and the three left must still total five.

Try it, then explain

  1. Use ten to solve 9 + 4. Show how you split 4.

    Hint

    Nine needs one more.

    Answer and explanation

    Split 4 into 1 and 3: 9 + 1 + 3 = 13.

  2. Find 14 - 6 by going back through ten.

    Hint

    First remove enough to reach ten.

    Answer and explanation

    14 - 4 = 10; two more must be removed, so 10 - 2 = 8.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

These curriculum examples extend the central model into varied contexts. They are learning contexts, not extra definitions to memorise.

E1

using drawings, physical and virtual materials and number combinations within 10 to add and subtract collections to 20

Try and explain: Draw 15 counters and cross out six. What remains, and which number sentence matches?

Answer and teaching point

Nine remain. 15 − 6 = 9; the crossed-out counters show the amount taken away.

E2

adding and subtracting numbers within 20, using a variety of representations and strategies such as counting on, counting back, partitioning and part-part-whole knowledge of numbers to 10; for example, using partitioning and combining 7+5=7+3+2=10+2=12

Try and explain: Use making ten to calculate 9 + 5. Explain how you split five.

Answer and teaching point

Split five into one and four: 9 + 1 + 4 = 10 + 4 = 14.

E3

developing and using strategies for one-digit addition and subtraction based on part-part-whole relationships for each of the numbers to 10 and subitising with physical and virtual materials; for example, 8 and 6 is the same as 8 and 2 and 4

Try and explain: You know 6 + 6 = 12. How does that help find 6 + 7?

Answer and teaching point

Seven is one more than six, so the total is one more than 12: 13.

E4

representing story problems involving addition and subtraction of numbers within 20 using a Think Board, recognising and using + and - symbols and the equal sign to represent the operations of addition and subtraction; showing and explaining the connections between any materials used using the language of plus and minus, and the numbers within the story problem

Try and explain: Ten birds sit on a fence. Four more arrive. Draw the story and write an equation that matches.

Answer and teaching point

10 + 4 = 14. The drawing should join a group of ten and a group of four; fourteen is the whole.

E5

creating and performing addition and subtraction stories told through First Nations Australians’ dances

Cultural safety: Use a community-approved source, name the specific Nation or community where the source does, and avoid presenting diverse First Nations knowledges as one generic tradition. Follow local cultural and intellectual property protocols before inviting community participation.

Common misconceptions and traps
  • Counting all every time — Count on from the larger number when possible.
  • Mixing add and subtract — Read the story: are parts joining or is a part being removed/found?
  • Stopping at 10 — After making ten, remember to add the leftover part.
