Learn the skill

Small steps, clear examples

Open the focus you need for today’s worksheet. Read, try and explain before moving on.

1. Share fairly

Share twelve counters among three plates, one at a time. Each plate gets four. Equal sharing asks how many each receives.

Try it: Share ten counters between two plates.

See answer and explanation

Five on each. Check both amounts are equal and all ten are used.

2. Make groups of a given size

Grouping asks how many groups can be made. Put twelve counters into groups of four: there are three groups.

Try it: Make groups of three from twelve.

See answer and explanation

Four equal groups. The answer is the number of groups.

3. Name the answer clearly

Twelve shared among three gives four in each group. Twelve grouped in fours gives three groups. Say which question you answered.

Try it: What does “three” mean in twelve grouped in fours?

See answer and explanation

Three groups, each containing four counters.

4. Check with repeated totals

Three groups of four make 4 + 4 + 4 = 12. Count the objects to check a sharing or grouping model.

Try it: Check five groups of two.

See answer and explanation

2, 4, 6, 8, 10: ten objects altogether.

5. Show any leftovers

Ten counters shared among three plates gives three each and one left over. Do not hide it or make one share larger.

Try it: Share fourteen counters among four plates without breaking counters.

See answer and explanation

Three each and two left over. All shares stay equal.

Try a worked example together

Sharing and grouping ask different questions

Work through the example

  1. The model contains twelve counters, arranged as three groups of four.
  2. Sharing: twelve counters shared equally among three people gives four each.
  3. Grouping: twelve counters put into groups of four makes three groups.

Watch for this: Say whether the answer tells how many in each group or how many groups.

Try it together

Share eight counters between two people. Then make groups of two from eight counters.

Answer and teaching guidance

Sharing gives four each. Grouping makes four groups of two.

An adult can read the directions, model the idea and accept an oral, pointed or drawn explanation where appropriate. For reading, spelling, handwriting or speaking tasks, observe the child doing the stated skill.

Common mistakes and quick fixes
  • Unequal sharing — Check every group has the same amount.
  • Confusing group size and number of groups — Ask: how many groups, or how many in each group?
  • Stopping before leftovers — If there are leftovers, state them clearly.
