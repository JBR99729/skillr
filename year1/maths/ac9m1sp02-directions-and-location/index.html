Learn the skill

Small steps, clear examples

Open the focus you need for today’s worksheet. Read, try and explain before moving on.

1. Choose a start and facing direction

Directions need a known start. Left and right depend on the traveller's facing direction; point along that direction first.

Try it: Why might two people face different left directions?

See answer and explanation

They may face different ways. State the traveller's viewpoint.

2. Describe where objects are

Beside, between, in front of and behind help locate objects. Use a named reference object.

Try it: A bag is between a chair and a desk. What are its two reference objects?

See answer and explanation

The chair and desk. The location is described relative to them.

3. Give one clear movement at a time

Separate a turn from a move. “Turn right, then take two steps forward” gives an order and a distance.

Try it: Does turning right mean moving one step right?

See answer and explanation

No. Turning changes which way you face; a move changes position.

4. Follow and check the route

Use an adult-cleared space. Follow the stated steps in order, then check the destination against the instructions.

Try it: What should you do if a direction is unclear?

See answer and explanation

Stop and ask for the start, viewpoint, distance or next step; do not guess.

5. Create another route

Different safe routes can reach the same place. Give enough steps for someone else to follow and test your directions.

Try it: How can a partner check your route?

See answer and explanation

Follow it from the stated start and facing direction, then confirm the destination.

Try a worked example together

Use a clear start and facing direction

Work through the example

  1. The start arrow points upwards. Forward means the way that arrow faces.
  2. Move forward two squares. Turn right on the spot.
  3. Move forward one square in the new direction. Follow the instructions in order.

Watch for this: A turn changes where you face; it is not an extra sideways step.

Try it together

At the same start, move forward one square, turn right, then move forward two squares.

Answer and teaching guidance

Finish two squares to the right and one square above the start, facing right.

An adult can read the directions, model the idea and accept an oral, pointed or drawn explanation where appropriate. For reading, spelling, handwriting or speaking tasks, observe the child doing the stated skill.

Common mistakes and quick fixes
  • No starting point — Always state where to start.
  • Left/right confusion — Face the same direction before using left and right.
  • Too vague — Use clear movement and position words, not just over there.
