Learn the skill

Small steps, clear examples

Open the focus you need for today’s worksheet. Read, try and explain before moving on.

1. Find what the story asks

Read for the known amounts and the unknown. A game needs 15 counters; nine are ready. The question asks for the missing part.

Try it: How many more counters are needed?

See answer and explanation

Six. 9 + 6 = 15, or 15 − 9 = 6.

2. Draw before calculating

Draw two parts inside a whole. For eight red beads and five blue beads, the unknown whole is 8 + 5 = 13.

Try it: Draw a model for 12 beads with five removed.

See answer and explanation

Start with 12; mark five removed and seven remaining. 12 − 5 = 7.

3. Choose from the situation

A bus has ten passengers. Three leave, then two board. Follow each change: 10 − 3 = 7; 7 + 2 = 9.

Try it: A box holds six pencils. Four are added and two removed. How many remain?

See answer and explanation

Eight: 6 + 4 − 2 = 8. Explain each change.

4. Solve whole-dollar money problems

A toy costs $8 and you pay $10 in play money. Find the missing amount: $8 + $2 = $10. The change is $2.

Try it: A $6 item is paid for with $10. Find the change.

See answer and explanation

$4. Check that $6 + $4 = $10. State dollars with the answer.

5. Explain and check the answer

The calculation must answer the story. If Jo has $14 and receives $15, the new total is $29; these are whole-dollar amounts.

Try it: How can you check the total of $29?

See answer and explanation

Use $29 − $15 = $14, or combine the two amounts again. Check the question and money unit.

Try a worked example together

Build the story before choosing the operation

Work through the example

  1. A child has 7 dollars and receives 5 more dollars.
  2. The two amounts join. The parts are 7 dollars and 5 dollars.
  3. 7 + 5 = 12. The child now has 12 dollars. Check that the answer fits the story.

Watch for this: Read what happens. The same numbers can belong to a joining or a taking-away story.

Try it together

You have 12 counters and give away 5. How many remain?

Answer and teaching guidance

7 counters. Here the whole is 12 and the removed part is 5: 12 − 5 = 7.

An adult can read the directions, model the idea and accept an oral, pointed or drawn explanation where appropriate. For reading, spelling, handwriting or speaking tasks, observe the child doing the stated skill.

Common mistakes and quick fixes
  • Number grabbing — Read the story before choosing an operation.
  • Money answer without units — Use cents or dollars in the answer.
  • Ignoring missing part problems — Sometimes subtraction is solved by thinking addition: 6 + ? = 10.
