Learning journey

What you’ll learn, strand by strand

This topic is taught through 6 focused strands. Each strand builds one part of the skill before learners move into independent practice.

  1. 1

    Blend sounds

    Blend phonemes and graphemes smoothly to decode unfamiliar words rather than guessing.

  2. 2

    Read phrases

    Move from single-word decoding into short phrases while keeping meaning and phrasing together.

  3. 3

    Use punctuation

    Notice punctuation cues so reading sounds natural and meaning is clearer.

  4. 4

    Check the letters

    Look back at the graphemes when a word does not sound right and correct the decoding.

  5. 5

    Check grammar and meaning

    Ask whether the sentence sounds right and makes sense, then self-correct when needed.

  6. 6

    Reread for fluency

    Read the sentence or passage again with greater accuracy, smoothness and expression.

How to use this roadmap: learn the idea, study examples, explain your thinking, then practise and check mastery.

Key concept

Learning goal: I can read with phonics, phrasing and meaning, and I can notice when I need to self-correct.

  • Recognise many high-frequency words while reading.
  • Use phonics, phrasing, context and grammar together to keep meaning.
  • Self-correct or ask for help when the text stops making sense.

See the idea: Check the letters when a word does not fit

Check the letters when a word does not fit

Work through the example

  1. In “The frog hops”, look through the letters in frog and blend the sounds in order.
  2. If you read flag, the meaning may alert you to a problem. Return to the letters and re-blend.
  3. Read the phrase again smoothly and check that it makes sense. A picture can support meaning, but it does not replace decoding.

Watch for this: Guessing a word from a picture is not the same as reading its spelling.

Try it together

Read “The frog hops” aloud. Point to the letters while checking frog.

Answer and teaching guidance

Hear the child read the word and sentence. Supply help with the taught letter–sound correspondences when needed.

An adult can read the directions, model the idea and accept an oral, pointed or drawn explanation where appropriate. For reading, spelling, handwriting or speaking tasks, observe the child doing the stated skill.

Teach each strand: model, practise and check

Choose a short text with letter–sound patterns the child has been taught. Explain unfamiliar vocabulary before reading. A decodable text practises those patterns; an authentic text has a real purpose, such as a classroom notice or a book. An adult may support unfamiliar words in an authentic text.

1. Blend sounds in order

Point to shop. Say /sh/ /o/ /p/, then blend the sounds into the whole word. The two letters sh work together to represent one sound.

Try: Read fish. Which two letters work together?

Answer and teaching guidance

sh represents one sound. Blend /f/ /i/ /sh/. If the child names letters without blending, model the sounds and slide them together.

2. Read meaningful phrases

Read The little dog / ran home. The slash shows a useful phrase group for practice; it is not printed punctuation. First solve unfamiliar words, then reread the words together so the sentence sounds like speech.

Check: Who ran home? Listen for the child keeping The little dog together. Answer: the little dog.

3. Let punctuation guide your voice

Read Can the dog jump? as a question. Read Stop! with an urgent voice, without shouting. A full stop marks the end of a sentence; take a natural pause before the next sentence.

Try: Read The dog can jump. and Can the dog jump? aloud. Listen for the child distinguishing a statement from a question.

4. Check all the letters

The printed word is frog. If you say flag, compare the middle letters: r and o. Read /f/ /r/ /o/ /g/, then blend. Check the beginning, middle and end; a first letter alone does not identify a word.

5. Check grammar and meaning after decoding

The printed sentence is The dog runs. A reader says The dog run. Return to runs, notice the final s, and reread. The spelling tells you the word; the sentence helps you check that it sounds right and makes sense.

Try: You read The dog jumps. as The dog bumps. Both words can be words, but the printed word starts with j. Return to the letters instead of choosing a word that merely seems plausible.

6. Reread for fluency

Read The fish swims. First read accurately, then reread in a meaningful phrase. Explain what the fish does. Fluency includes accuracy, suitable pace and expression; racing through a sentence is not the goal.

Adult check: Listen to both readings. Notice whether the child keeps the words correct, groups them smoothly and explains that the fish swims. Give brief help, then invite another reading.

High-frequency words and asking for help

Practise familiar words such as the and is in short sentences. Use the known letter–sound parts and explicitly teach any unusual part. If blending and rereading still do not resolve a word, the child can ask an adult for help, then read the sentence again.

Support, core and extend

  • Support: model one word, blend together and echo-read a short phrase.
  • Core: read a short taught-pattern text, correct a mismatch and reread with meaning.
  • Extend: reread a slightly longer familiar text or an adult-supported classroom notice, keeping accurate words, phrasing and meaning.

Exit check

Listen to the child read The fish swims. Ask them to explain what the fish does, check the letters after an error, and reread the sentence smoothly. Expect /f/ /i/ /sh/, the correct printed words and an explanation of swimming. Reteach the particular sound or word that caused difficulty before trying again.

Common misconceptions
  • Only guessing from context — Look at the letters and sounds.
  • Sounding without meaning — After decoding, check the sentence makes sense.
  • Robot reading — Practise phrase groups, not one word at a time forever.
Try these
  1. Blend and check — Read a decodable word, then use it in a sentence.
    ship → The ship sails.
  2. Phrase scoop — Mark small phrase groups and re-read smoothly.
    The little dog / ran home.
  3. Meaning monitor — Stop when reading sounds wrong and fix it.
    Does it make sense?
Explain and apply

Blend and check meaning

Read: The frog sat on a log. Which two words rhyme? Read their letters first, then explain how the sentence helps you check the meaning.

Answer and teaching guidance

Frog and log rhyme. Read their printed letters and blend first; the sentence then helps you check the meaning. A rhyming guess alone does not identify a printed word.

Use phonics first

You see the word shop. Which sounds help you read it?

Answer and teaching guidance

The digraph sh and the sounds /o/ and /p/ help the reader blend the word.

Read in phrases

Where is a good pause: The little dog / ran home or The / little dog ran home?

Answer and teaching guidance

The little dog / ran home keeps words in meaningful phrases and supports fluency.

Self-correct

The printed sentence is “The frog hops.” You accidentally read “The flag hops.” What should you do?

Answer and teaching guidance

Stop and return to the printed letters. Blend the sounds to check the word, then reread and check meaning; do not guess the word from the picture.

Check your understanding

Quick checks

  • What should you do if a word does not make sense?
  • Blend: sh-o-p.
  • Why re-read a sentence?
  • What is phrasing?

I am ready when I can

  • I can recognise many high-frequency words while reading.
  • I can use phonics, phrasing, context and grammar together to keep meaning.
  • I can self-correct or ask for help when the text stops making sense.
