Learning journey

What you’ll learn, strand by strand

This topic is taught through 5 focused strands. Each strand builds one part of the skill before learners move into independent practice.

  1. 1

    Hear which sound changes

    Listen closely to the starting, middle and ending sounds in a spoken word.

  2. 2

    Change the first sound

    Substitute an initial phoneme and say the new word while keeping the remaining sounds the same.

  3. 3

    Change the middle sound

    Swap the medial sound to make a different spoken word and compare what changed.

  4. 4

    Change the last sound

    Substitute the final sound and check that the result is a real word.

  5. 5

    Make, blend and check new words

    Add, remove or substitute one sound, blend the result and check that the new word makes sense.

How to use this roadmap: learn the idea, study examples, explain your thinking, then practise and check mastery.

Key concept

Learning goal: I can add, remove or change phonemes in spoken words to make new words.

  • Add, delete or substitute a sound in a one-syllable word to make a new word.
  • Change a middle sound to make a new word.
  • Change a final sound to make a new word.

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: adding, substituting or deleting phonemes in any position in one-syllable words with up to 4 phonemes
  • E2: substituting medial sounds in spoken words to make new words; for example, “pin”, “pen”, “pan”
  • E3: substituting final sounds in spoken words; for example, substitute the “t” in “pet” with “g” to form a new word “peg”
See the idea: Change a sound to change a spoken word

Change a sound to change a spoken word

Work through the example

  1. An adult says mat. Listen to its three sounds.
  2. Replace only the first sound with /s/. Keep the other sounds in order.
  3. Blend the changed sounds to say sat. Practise adding or removing a sound in separate oral tasks too.

Watch for this: Keep the sounds that were not changed. Sound manipulation is an oral skill, not just a letter swap.

Try it together

Without showing the spelling, ask the child to change the /p/ in pin to /t/.

Answer and teaching guidance

Tin. Listen for the sound change rather than a copied written answer.

An adult can read the directions, model the idea and accept an oral, pointed or drawn explanation where appropriate. For reading, spelling, handwriting or speaking tasks, observe the child doing the stated skill.

Common misconceptions
  • Changing letters silently — Say the sounds aloud.
  • Changing more than one sound — Change only the sound named.
  • Forgetting middle/final sounds — Practise start, middle and end positions.
Try these
  1. Sound switch — Change one starting sound to make a new word.
    mat → sat
  2. Take away — Delete the first sound and say what remains.
    smile → mile
  3. Middle sound change — Change /i/ in pin to /a/.
    pin → pan
Explain and apply

Substitute first sound

Change cat by replacing /c/ with /h/.

Answer and teaching guidance

The new word is hat. Only the first sound changes.

Substitute middle sound

Change sit by replacing /i/ with /a/.

Answer and teaching guidance

The new word is sat. The middle vowel sound changes.

Substitute final sound

Change cup by replacing /p/ with /t/.

Answer and teaching guidance

The new word is cut. The final sound changes.

Explain the change

What changed from fish to dish?

Answer and teaching guidance

The first sound changed from /f/ to /d/. The rest of the word stayed the same.

Check your understanding

Quick checks

  • Change mat to sat.
  • Delete /s/ from smile.
  • Change pin to pan.
  • What does substitute mean?
