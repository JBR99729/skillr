YEAR 1 ENGLISH

Change a sound to make a new word AC9E1LY10

Learning intention: I can add, replace or remove a spoken sound while keeping the others in order.

An adult can read directions and model the examples. Use a short turn or return to a section later.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Change one position, keep the rest

Adult says sip with no printed target shown. Say /s/ /i/ /p/. Add /l/ immediately after /s/: /s/ /l/ /i/ /p/. Blend to say slip. You added one sound inside the word; you did not replace a sound or move the new one to the front. Use one counter per sound to show the position.

Learn the skill

1. Add a sound

Keep every old sound and place one new sound exactly where asked. Say and blend the result. Practise one position at a time.

At + /s/ at the beginning gives sat. Ten + /t/ at the end gives tent. Sip + /l/ after /s/ gives slip. These have at most four sounds.

Try it · Adult says ram. Add /p/ at the end and say the new word.

Ramp: /r/ /a/ /m/ /p/. All the original sounds stay in order. Ask for the spoken word before revealing its spelling.

2. Replace one sound

Substitute means take one sound out and put a different sound in the same position. Leave the other sounds alone.

Change /m/ in mat to /s/: sat. Change the middle vowel in pin to the vowel in pen: pen. Change final /t/ in pet to /g/: peg.

Try it · Adult says cap. Replace its last sound with /t/ and say the new word.

Cat. /k/ and the short vowel stay; /p/ changes to /t/. Hear the child make the change rather than copy a displayed answer.

3. Remove one sound

Delete means take away the stated sound without adding a replacement. Touch the remaining counters and blend their sounds.

Remove first /s/ from stop: top. Remove /l/ from clap: cap. Remove final /t/ from tent: ten.

Try it · Adult says sand. Remove /n/, the sound just before the final /d/. What word remains?

Sad: /s/ /a/ /d/. Keep the final /d/. If needed, show four counters, remove the third and blend the others; record this support.

Common mistakes

“Add means replace.” Adding keeps the original sounds and creates one more position.

“Any sound can disappear.” Remove only the sound named.

“A silent written swap proves the skill.” Listen to the actual new spoken word.

10-MINUTE ACTIVITY

One change in a short spoken word

With printed spellings hidden, adult gives one instruction at a time: add /s/ before top; replace /m/ in mat with /s/; remove /l/ from clap. Check the spoken results stop, sat and cap afterward. Ask the child to show which counter changed.

Adjust the challenge Starter: model and guide one operation. Core: produce new words for clear beginning, middle and end changes. Stretch: explain a change between two familiar one-syllable words with no more than four sounds.

The displayed words guide the adult. Keep them out of the learner’s view for oral checks. Use short clean sounds, familiar vocabulary and ordinary accent variation; do not confuse letters with phonemes.

Three tricky questions

1. Is deleting /k/ from clap the same as deleting /l/?

No. Removing /k/ gives lap; removing /l/ gives cap. Position matters.

2. Can one sound use two written letters?

Yes. /sh/ is one sound in ship; a sound change need not be one letter change.

3. If cap becomes clap, did /k/ disappear?

No. /l/ was added after /k/; the original sounds remain.

FAQ

Should we teach every operation in one sitting?

No. Model one operation and position, give supported turns, then combine familiar changes when ready.

Do counters replace speaking?

They show sound positions. Observe the actual new spoken word for this oral skill, and note communication support.

See the idea: Change a sound to change a spoken word

Change a sound to change a spoken word

Work through the example

  1. An adult says mat. Listen to its three sounds.
  2. Replace only the first sound with /s/. Keep the other sounds in order.
  3. Blend the changed sounds to say sat. Practise adding or removing a sound in separate oral tasks too.

Watch for this: Keep the sounds that were not changed. Sound manipulation is an oral skill, not just a letter swap.

Try it together

Without showing the spelling, ask the child to change the /p/ in pin to /t/.

Answer and teaching guidance

Tin. Listen for the sound change rather than a copied written answer.

An adult can read the directions, model the idea and accept an oral, pointed or drawn explanation where appropriate. For reading, spelling, handwriting or speaking tasks, observe the child doing the stated skill.

Common misconceptions
  • Changing letters silently — Say the sounds aloud.
  • Changing more than one sound — Change only the sound named.
  • Forgetting middle/final sounds — Practise start, middle and end positions.
Try these
  1. Sound switch — Change one starting sound to make a new word.
    mat → sat
  2. Take away — Delete the first sound and say what remains.
    smile → mile
  3. Middle sound change — Change /i/ in pin to /a/.
    pin → pan

Substitute first sound

Change cat by replacing /c/ with /h/.

Answer and teaching guidance

The new word is hat. Only the first sound changes.

Substitute middle sound

Change sit by replacing /i/ with /a/.

Answer and teaching guidance

The new word is sat. The middle vowel sound changes.

Substitute final sound

Change cup by replacing /p/ with /t/.

Answer and teaching guidance

The new word is cut. The final sound changes.

Explain the change

What changed from fish to dish?

Answer and teaching guidance

The first sound changed from /f/ to /d/. The rest of the word stayed the same.

Curriculum alignment

AC9E1LY10: orally manipulate phonemes in spoken words by addition, deletion and substitution of initial, medial and final phonemes to generate new words (phonological awareness)

  • adding, substituting or deleting phonemes in any position in one-syllable words with up to 4 phonemes
  • substituting medial sounds in spoken words to make new words; for example, “pin”, “pen”, “pan”
  • substituting final sounds in spoken words; for example, substitute the “t” in “pet” with “g” to form a new word “peg”
