Manipulate Phonemes

I can add, remove or change phonemes in spoken words to make new words.

Topic GuideClassroom ViewWorksheetPracticeTest
Key concept

Learning goal: I can add, remove or change phonemes in spoken words to make new words.

  • Add, delete or substitute a sound in a one-syllable word to make a new word.
  • Change a middle sound to make a new word.
  • Change a final sound to make a new word.

Steps to use

Say word → Change sound → Say new word → Check meaning

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: adding, substituting or deleting phonemes in any position in one-syllable words with up to 4 phonemes
  • E2: substituting medial sounds in spoken words to make new words; for example, “pin”, “pen”, “pan”
  • E3: substituting final sounds in spoken words; for example, substitute the “t” in “pet” with “g” to form a new word “peg”
Common misconceptions
  • Changing letters silently — Say the sounds aloud.
  • Changing more than one sound — Change only the sound named.
  • Forgetting middle/final sounds — Practise start, middle and end positions.
Try these
  1. Sound switch — Change one starting sound to make a new word.
    mat → sat
  2. Take away — Delete the first sound and say what remains.
    smile → mile
  3. Middle sound change — Change /i/ in pin to /a/.
    pin → pan
Explain and apply

Substitute first sound

Change cat by replacing /c/ with /h/.

Answer and teaching guidance

The new word is hat. Only the first sound changes.

Substitute middle sound

Change sit by replacing /i/ with /a/.

Answer and teaching guidance

The new word is sat. The middle vowel sound changes.

Substitute final sound

Change cup by replacing /p/ with /t/.

Answer and teaching guidance

The new word is cut. The final sound changes.

Explain the change

What changed from fish to dish?

Answer and teaching guidance

The first sound changed from /f/ to /d/. The rest of the word stayed the same.

Check your understanding

Quick checks

  • Change mat to sat.
  • Delete /s/ from smile.
  • Change pin to pan.
  • What does substitute mean?
