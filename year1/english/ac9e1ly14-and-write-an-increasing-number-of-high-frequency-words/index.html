Key concept

Learning goal: I can read and write an increasing number of high-frequency words automatically.

  • Read an increasing bank of high-frequency words independently.
  • Write familiar high-frequency words accurately in meaningful sentences.

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: learning an increasing number of high-frequency words and reading them independently; for example, “one”, “have” and “pretty”
See the idea: Map the unusual part of a familiar word

Map the unusual part of a familiar word

Work through the example

  1. In said, s and d have their familiar sounds.
  2. The ai spelling represents the short /e/ sound in this word. This is the part to remember.
  3. Read said in a sentence, cover it, write it, then check the complete spelling.

Watch for this: Do not assume ai always has the same sound. Said is a word-specific spelling to learn.

Try it together

Read “Sam said hello.” Then cover said, write it and check.

Answer and teaching guidance

The word is said. Hear the child read it and check the written spelling; record any support.

An adult can read the directions, model the idea and accept an oral, pointed or drawn explanation where appropriate. For reading, spelling, handwriting or speaking tasks, observe the child doing the stated skill.

Common misconceptions
  • Memorise shape only — Map sounds and tricky letters.
  • Practise word alone forever — Use it in a sentence.
  • Ignore errors — Cover, write and check carefully.
Try these
  1. Word mapping — Say sounds and mark the tricky part in a high-frequency word.
    said
  2. Cover-write-check — Look, cover, write, then check.
    because
  3. Sentence use — Write a short sentence with the target word.
    They can play.
Explain and apply

Complete the sentence

Choose the high-frequency word: Mia was/were/be happy.

Answer and teaching guidance

was completes the sentence. Students should read the whole sentence and check that it sounds right.

Read without guessing

Read: The dog is small. Which word must be recognised quickly?

Answer and teaching guidance

The is a high-frequency word. Recognising it automatically helps students read the sentence smoothly.

Choose the right word

Complete: Can you/your/yes help me?

Answer and teaching guidance

you is correct. The sentence is asking another person for help.

Write from memory

Write the sentence: I have some apples. Which word might need extra practice?

Answer and teaching guidance

some is a high-frequency word that may not be easy to spell by sound alone, so it should be practised as a known word.

Check your understanding

Quick checks

  • Read: they.
  • Which part is tricky in said?
  • Use was in a sentence.
  • Why practise high-frequency words?

I am ready when I can

  • I can read an increasing bank of high-frequency words independently.
  • I can write familiar high-frequency words accurately in meaningful sentences.
