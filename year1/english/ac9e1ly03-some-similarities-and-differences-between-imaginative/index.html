Imaginative, Informative and Persuasive Texts

I can compare imaginative, informative and persuasive texts and choose one for a purpose.

Topic GuideClassroom ViewWorksheetPracticeTest
Key concept

Learning goal: I can compare imaginative, informative and persuasive texts and choose one for a purpose.

  • Tell story texts from texts that give opinions.
  • Choose a text that suits a purpose, such as finding information or reading a story.
  • Compare imaginative, informative and persuasive texts using visible features.

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: comparing and discussing texts, identifying some features that distinguish those that “tell stories” from those that “give opinions”
  • E2: selecting texts for a particular purpose or task; for example, a website that will give information about a learning area topic, a book that will tell a story about an animal
Common misconceptions
  • All texts tell stories — Some texts inform or persuade instead.
  • Opinion confused with fact — Opinion can be agreed or disagreed with.
  • No comparison language — Use same, different, both, but.
Try these
  1. Three-way sort — Sort cards into imaginative, informative and persuasive.
    story/fact/opinion
  2. Feature compare — Find one similarity and one difference between two text types.
    Both have titles; one uses facts.
  3. Purpose proof — Use a word or feature to justify the text type.
    because...
Explain and apply

Real or imagined

Read: A dragon carried a lunchbox to school. Could this happen in real life?

Answer and teaching guidance

No. Dragons and the event make it imaginative. This matches the IXL-style real-life check.

Information or persuasion

Read: Bees make honey. Read: You should protect bees. How are the texts different?

Answer and teaching guidance

The first gives information. The second tries to persuade the reader to do something.

Same topic, different purpose

Two texts are about dogs. One tells dog facts and one says dogs make the best pets. Which one gives an opinion?

Answer and teaching guidance

Dogs make the best pets is an opinion and has a persuasive purpose.

Compare features

Name one feature of a story and one feature of an information text.

Answer and teaching guidance

A story often has characters and events. An information text often gives facts, labels or topic words.

Check your understanding

Quick checks

  • Which text gives facts?
  • Which text has characters and plot?
  • Which text tries to convince?
  • Give one similarity between two text types.

I am ready when I can

  • I can tell story texts from texts that give opinions.
  • I can choose a text that suits a purpose, such as finding information or reading a story.
  • I can compare imaginative, informative and persuasive texts using visible features.
