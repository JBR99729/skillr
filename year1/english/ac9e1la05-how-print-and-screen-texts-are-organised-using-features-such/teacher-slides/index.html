Understand how print and screen texts are organised

Ready to project and teach

Teach it step by step

Work through these six teaching focuses in short sessions. Read, model, try and explain; return to any focus that needs more support.

Words to teach: A title names the whole text. A heading names a section. Contents lists sections and where they start. A link can take you to another page or section.

1. Titles and headings

Use the title to find the topic. Use a heading to find a part of the text.

Model together

Book title: “Our school garden”. Section headings: “Tools”, “Watering”, “Growing”.

Your turn: Which heading would help you find how the beds are watered?

Answer and teaching guidance

Watering. The title names the whole book; the heading names the relevant part.

2. Page numbers and contents

Find the section in the contents, then turn to its starting page and check the heading.

Model together

Contents: Tools — 2; Watering — 4; Growing — 6.

Your turn: Where does the Growing section start? How could you check you reached it?

Answer and teaching guidance

Page 6; check the page number and Growing heading. A contents entry points to information rather than giving all the information itself.

3. Images and layout

Look at where words, pictures and labels are placed. Use them together.

Model together

A page headed “Tools” places a picture beside the words “watering can”. A labelled diagram can show its handle and spout.

Your turn: How does placing the label beside the correct part help the reader?

Answer and teaching guidance

It connects the name with the visible part. A picture is not always decoration; check what information it helps explain.

4. Buttons, menus and swipes

Read a control’s label and learn what it does on that screen. Swipe or scroll actions depend on the app or text.

Model together

Model screen rules: Menu opens a list of sections. Next opens the next page. On this model, swiping left also opens the next page.

Your turn: You want to choose Tools from the section list. Which control would you use first?

Answer and teaching guidance

Menu, then Tools. The stated model rules make the answer clear. Other screens may work differently; do not teach a universal swipe direction.

5. Links, icons and spoken commands

A link or icon has a job. A spoken command can work when a device supports it and the setting is enabled.

Model together

Model rules: “Watering tips” opens those tips. A speaker button reads the page aloud. With voice control enabled, “Go back” returns to the previous screen.

Your turn: You want to hear the page, then return to the previous screen. Describe a route.

Answer and teaching guidance

Use the speaker button to hear it; use the enabled Go back voice command to return. A visible Back button could be another supported route. Discuss paper models separately from actual device use.

6. Compare books and screens

Compare the information and the route, rather than saying one format is always better.

Model together

In a book, contents says Watering starts on page 4. On the model screen, a Watering menu link opens the same section.

Your turn: Name one similar job and one different action.

Answer and teaching guidance

Both routes locate the Watering section. Turning to page 4 is a print action; selecting the named menu link is a screen action. Some digital books also have page numbers and contents.

Support, extend and check learning

Watch for this

Define the rules of each model screen. Swipes, buttons, menus, icons and verbal commands can vary. Never assume a spoken command works on every device. A paper navigation answer shows understanding of the model; observe actual use separately if you want evidence of device navigation.

Support

Use a familiar book and one simple model screen. Offer a single target section and two labelled controls. An adult can read the labels without choosing the route.

Extend within this skill

Give two possible routes to the same section. Compare the steps and explain which you would choose for that task.

Quick check

Use the contents model to find Watering. Explain how you would reach the same section on the model screen.

Expected evidence and next step

Turn to page 4 and check its heading in print; open Menu and choose Watering on the screen. Ask why the chosen feature helps. Accept another route only when the screen rules support it.

If the explanation is unclear, model a different example and try again. Record what the child can explain or do, and the support used; a paper score alone does not establish an observed speaking or performance skill.

An adult can read the directions and explain taught terms. Accept spoken, signed, pointed, AAC or scribed responses when these preserve the skill being checked. Short directions do not assume every child can independently decode all the words.

Work through the example

  1. The contents gives the name of each section and its starting page.
  2. To find nests, read “Nests — page 4”, then turn to page 4.
  3. A heading names that section. On a screen, a link or button can take you to a section.

Answer and teaching guidance

Page 6. Check its heading when you arrive.

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • Names feature but not job: Always say what the feature helps the reader do.
  • Confuses title and heading: Title names the whole text; heading names a part.
  • Ignores screen features: Buttons, links and swipes also organise texts.

Practice Thought & Formative Assessment

Use a fresh, code-aligned example. The student should show the exact word, sound, sentence, image feature, audience clue or text evidence that supports the answer.
