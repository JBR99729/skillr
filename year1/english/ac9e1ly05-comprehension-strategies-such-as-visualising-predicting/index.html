What students learn

1. Outcome Overview & Core Concepts

Comprehension grows when readers visualise, predict, connect, summarise and question using vocabulary, context and text structure.

Learning target: use comprehension strategies such as visualising, predicting, connecting, summarising and questioning when listening, viewing and reading to build literal and inferred meaning by drawing on vocabulary and growing knowledge of context and text structures

Big idea: Use strategies to build literal and inferred meaning

Learning routine: Preview → Predict → Read/listen → Connect → Question → Summarise

2. Subject-Specific Content & Key Examples

Question: If a character packs a raincoat and umbrella, you can infer it may rain because those objects are used in wet weather.

Prediction stop

Guided practice

  1. Prediction stop — Pause before the ending and predict with evidence.
    I think... because...
  2. Picture in mind — Describe what you visualise from a sentence.
    I see...
  3. Question and answer — Ask one literal and one inferred question.
    Who...? Why might...?
3. Common Student Misconceptions

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • Prediction with no evidence: Use a clue from the text.
  • Summary too detailed: Say the main idea, not every small event.
  • Inference as guess: Combine clue plus thinking.
4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence

4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence

Step 1: Explicit Teaching & Modelling

Name the target in child-friendly language and demonstrate one concrete example connected to context, vocabulary, text structure.

Step 2: Guided Analysis

Compare or practise a second example together. Students point to, say or show the clue.

Step 3: Collaborative / Independent Practice

Create or analyse one example together, then use a fresh familiar example independently.

Step 4: Formative Assessment

Use a one-minute check with a new example; require observable evidence rather than a guess.

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: identifying information and details from spoken informative texts
  • E2: building topic knowledge and learning new vocabulary before and during reading
  • E3: making predictions from the cover, from illustrations and at points in the text before reading on, and confirming and adjusting understanding after reading
  • E4: drawing inferences and explaining inferences using clues from the text
  • E5: making connections with existing knowledge and personal experiences

Revision Notes

Revision Notes

  • Remember: Use strategies to build literal and inferred meaning
  • Use the routine: Preview → Predict → Read/listen → Connect → Question → Summarise
  • Before answering: point to the clue in the spoken words, written sentence, image, page layout or text purpose.
  • Self-check: change the example slightly and see whether the same reasoning still works.
