Comprehension Strategies

I can predict, connect, visualise, summarise, question and infer to build meaning from a text.

What this skill means

Learning goal: I can predict, connect, visualise, summarise, question and infer to build meaning from a text.

  • Find information and details in spoken informative texts.
  • Build topic knowledge and learn useful vocabulary before and during reading.
  • Predict from covers, illustrations and text clues, then confirm or change the prediction.
  • Make an inference and explain the text clue that supports it.
  • Connect a text with what you already know or have experienced.

Steps to use

Preview → Predict → Read/listen → Connect → Question → Summarise

See it, then try it

Use a strategy on purpose

predictvisualiseconnectquestionsummarise

Worked example

Question: If a character packs a raincoat and umbrella, you can infer it may rain because those objects are used in wet weather.

Support thinking with text clues

literal: in the textinferred: text clue + thinkingsummary: main idea
Try these
  1. Prediction stop — Pause before the ending and predict with evidence.
    I think... because...
  2. Picture in mind — Describe what you visualise from a sentence.
    I see...
  3. Question and answer — Ask one literal and one inferred question.
    Who...? Why might...?
Common mix-ups
  • Prediction with no evidence — Use a clue from the text.
  • Summary too detailed — Say the main idea, not every small event.
  • Inference as guess — Combine clue plus thinking.
Check your understanding

Quick checks

  • What is a prediction?
  • What is an inference?
  • Ask one question about a text.
  • Summarise a familiar story in one sentence.

I am ready when I can

  • I can find information and details in spoken informative texts.
  • I can build topic knowledge and learn useful vocabulary before and during reading.
  • I can predict from covers, illustrations and text clues, then confirm or change the prediction.
  • I can make an inference and explain the text clue that supports it.
  • I can connect a text with what you already know or have experienced.
Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: identifying information and details from spoken informative texts
  • E2: building topic knowledge and learning new vocabulary before and during reading
  • E3: making predictions from the cover, from illustrations and at points in the text before reading on, and confirming and adjusting understanding after reading
  • E4: drawing inferences and explaining inferences using clues from the text
  • E5: making connections with existing knowledge and personal experiences
