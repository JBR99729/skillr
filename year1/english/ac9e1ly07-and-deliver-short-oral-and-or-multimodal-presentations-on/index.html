Short Oral Presentations

I can plan and deliver a short presentation with a clear beginning, middle and ending.

Topic GuideClassroom ViewPrintable Worksheet40-question PracticeTest
What this skill means

Learning goal: I can plan and deliver a short presentation with a clear beginning, middle and ending.

  • Report the result of a group activity clearly.
  • Explain how to do or make something in an organised order.
  • Give a short presentation using clear speech and topic words.
  • Change volume and pace to suit the purpose.
  • Give a reason when trying to persuade an audience.

Steps to use

Opening → Middle details → Concluding statement → Practise voice/gesture

See it, then try it

Presentation structure

Opening: Today I will talk about...Middle: First...Middle: Next...Closing: Thank you

Worked example

Question: “Today I will talk about frogs. Frogs need water. Thank you.” has an opening, middle information and closing.

Practise delivery, not just words

stand readyclear voicegesturesteady pacelook at audience
Try these
  1. Three-part talk — Plan a presentation using opening, middle and closing cards.
    opening / middle / closing
  2. Voice check — Practise saying one sentence too quiet, too loud and just right.
    classroom voice
  3. Gesture match — Choose one gesture that supports your topic.
    point / show / count
Common mix-ups
  • No closing — End with a final statement, not just stopping.
  • Reading too fast — Practise steady pace.
  • Random gesture — Use gesture that helps meaning.
Check your understanding

Quick checks

  • What does an opening do?
  • Name one delivery skill.
  • Why use topic vocabulary?
  • Give a closing sentence.

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: reporting the results of group activities
  • E2: explaining how to do or make something
  • E3: giving short oral presentations about areas of interest or content area topics, speaking clearly and with appropriate volume, and using content-specific vocabulary
  • E4: experimenting with volume and pace for particular purposes; for example, presenting information, retelling stories, and reciting rhymes and poems
  • E5: giving reasons why the class should learn a particular game
