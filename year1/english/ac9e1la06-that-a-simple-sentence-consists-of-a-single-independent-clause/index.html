Simple Sentences

I can recognise and build simple sentences that communicate one main event or idea.

Topic GuideClassroom ViewPrintable Worksheet40-question PracticeTest
What this skill means

Learning goal: I can recognise and build simple sentences that communicate one main event or idea.

  • Recognise that a simple sentence communicates one main event or idea.
  • Identify who or what is involved and what is happening.
  • Add useful details such as where, when or how.

Steps to use

Choose idea → Say sentence → Check it makes sense → Add boundary punctuation

See it, then try it

One sentence, one complete idea

The dog ran.Mia opened the box.The big leaf fell.

Worked example

Question: Is “ran to school” a complete sentence? No. It tells an action but does not say who ran.

Check who/what and what happened

Who or what?What happened?Does it make sense alone?
Try these
  1. Sentence or fragment — Sort groups of words into complete sentence and not complete yet.
    The cat slept. / under the mat
  2. Build one idea — Use a noun card and verb card to make a complete sentence.
    Bird + flew → The bird flew.
  3. Punctuation check — Add a capital letter and end mark to a simple sentence.
    sam ran → Sam ran.
Common mix-ups
  • Missing who/what — Add the person, place or thing doing the action.
  • Too many ideas — Keep one clear event or idea.
  • No boundary mark — Start with a capital and end with punctuation.
Check your understanding

Quick checks

  • Is “The frog jumped.” a sentence?
  • Fix: ran fast.
  • What does a sentence need to make sense?
  • Add punctuation: mia sang

I am ready when I can

  • I can recognise that a simple sentence communicates one main event or idea.
  • I can identify who or what is involved and what is happening.
  • I can add useful details such as where, when or how.
Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: knowing that a single event or idea can include a process, a happening or a state (verb), the participant or who or what is involved (noun group/phrase), and the surrounding circumstances (adverb group/phrase); for example, “Teddy (participant: who or what is involved) read (process: a happening) the book (participant: who or what is involved) in the library (circumstance: where he read).”
  • E2: understanding that simple sentences answer questions such as, “what is happening?” and “who or what is involved?” along with details such as “where?”, “when?”, “how?”
