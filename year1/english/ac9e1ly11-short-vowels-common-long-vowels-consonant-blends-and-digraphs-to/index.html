Learning journey

What you’ll learn, strand by strand

This topic is taught through 5 focused strands. Each strand builds one part of the skill before learners move into independent practice.

  1. 1

    Build simple words from sounds

    Connect individual speech sounds to letters and letter groups to build simple words.

  2. 2

    Blend and read simple words

    Combine sounds smoothly to read unfamiliar words instead of guessing from the first letter.

  3. 3

    Write from dictation and check

    Listen to a word, segment its sounds, write the graphemes and check the result.

  4. 4

    Read words with vowel teams

    Notice common vowel teams and use them when decoding and spelling.

  5. 5

    Read and write one- and two-syllable words

    Apply phonics knowledge across longer words by working through each syllable carefully.

How to use this roadmap: learn the idea, study examples, explain your thinking, then practise and check mastery.

Key concept

Learning goal: I can use vowels, blends and digraphs to read and write one- and two-syllable words.

  • Use letter-sound knowledge to read and write words with short and common long vowel sounds.
  • Read and write one-syllable words with common consonant digraphs and blends.
  • Blend sounds to read one- and two-syllable words.

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: using knowledge of letters and sounds to write words with short vowels; for example, “man”, and common long vowel sounds; for example, “time”
  • E2: using knowledge of letter sounds to write single-syllable words with consonant digraphs and consonant blends; for example, “wish” and “rest”
See the idea: Blend a digraph as one sound

Blend a digraph as one sound

Work through the example

  1. The letters sh work together for one sound in ship.
  2. Blend /sh/, /i/, /p/ to read ship. Do not add a separate /s/ and /h/.
  3. A blend is different: the f and r in frog keep two sounds. Learn the particular spelling pattern in each word.

Watch for this: Two letters sometimes represent one sound; they do not always do so.

Try it together

Read ship, then spell it using the sounds and their letters.

Answer and teaching guidance

Hear /sh/, /i/, /p/ and see the spelling s-h-i-p. Reading and spelling are checked separately.

An adult can read the directions, model the idea and accept an oral, pointed or drawn explanation where appropriate. For reading, spelling, handwriting or speaking tasks, observe the child doing the stated skill.

Common misconceptions
  • Digraph counted twice — sh/ch/th can make one sound.
  • Guessing from first letter — Look through the whole word.
  • Vowel missing — Every syllable needs a vowel sound.
Try these
  1. Pattern sort — Sort words by short vowel, long vowel, blend or digraph.
    ship / frog / cake
  2. Blend and write — Blend a word, then write the sounds in order.
    shop
  3. Two-syllable clap — Clap and read a two-syllable word.
    sun-set
Explain and apply

Find a digraph

Read: ship, chip, sip. Which word has sh?

Answer and teaching guidance

ship has sh. The two letters sh work together to make one sound at the start of the word.

Hear both blend sounds

Read: fog, frog, drum. Which word has the blend fr?

Answer and teaching guidance

frog has fr. In a consonant blend, both sounds are heard: /f/ then /r/.

Long vowel pattern

Choose the word with ai: ran, rain, red.

Answer and teaching guidance

rain has ai. The letters ai commonly make the long a sound in one-syllable words.

Blend a two-syllable word

Read sunset by parts: sun-set. How can you blend it?

Answer and teaching guidance

Read each syllable, then join them: sun-set becomes sunset. This builds from sound work into fluent word reading.

Check your understanding

Quick checks

  • What sound does sh make?
  • Read: frog.
  • Is a in cake short or long?
  • Why check the vowel?

I am ready when I can

  • I can use letter-sound knowledge to read and write words with short and common long vowel sounds.
  • I can read and write one-syllable words with common consonant digraphs and blends.
  • I can blend sounds to read one- and two-syllable words.
