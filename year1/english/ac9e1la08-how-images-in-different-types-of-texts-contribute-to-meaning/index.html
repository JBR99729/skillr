How Images Add Meaning

I can explain how images add meaning to different kinds of texts.

Topic GuideClassroom ViewWorksheetPracticeTest
Key concept

Learning goal: I can explain how images add meaning to different kinds of texts.

  • Compare different images of the same subject and explain what each image adds.
  • Follow meaning across a sequence of images.
  • Notice information shown in an image that is not stated in the written words.
  • Discuss images in stories and cultural accounts by First Nations Australian authors and illustrators.

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: comparing images from texts where images of the same subject are represented differently; for example, a cartoon image of an animal, a photograph of an animal and a digital image of an animal in an advertisement
  • E2: understanding how authors and illustrators build up meaning across a sequence of images
  • E3: understanding that some images convey meaning that is not included in the accompanying written text; for example, a diagram shows information about how parts of a plant are connected, which is not explained in the print text
  • E4: exploring images in stories and cultural accounts by First Nations Australian authors and discussing the impact this may have (teaching context)
Common misconceptions
  • Describing only — Explain how the image changes or supports meaning.
  • Ignoring words — Read words and image together.
  • One correct image only — Different images can support different meanings.

Explain and apply

Image adds meaning

A story says The child opened the gift. The picture shows a grin. What does the image add?

Answer and teaching guidance

The picture adds the child's feeling. The words tell the event; the image gives extra meaning.

Match image to purpose

Which image helps an information page about plant parts: a labelled plant diagram or a birthday party picture?

Answer and teaching guidance

The labelled plant diagram fits because it helps the reader identify parts of a plant.

Compare two image jobs

A map image shows a path; a recipe image shows folding dough. What does each image help readers do?

Answer and teaching guidance

The map helps with location and direction. The recipe image helps explain an action in a procedure.

Check image evidence

A picture shows a kitten under a chair, but the words say The room was empty. What should the reader notice?

Answer and teaching guidance

The image adds information that changes the reader's understanding, so words and images must be read together.

Check your understanding

Quick checks

  • What can an image add to words?
  • How can an image change mood?
  • Why read image and words together?
  • Give one detail an image might show.
