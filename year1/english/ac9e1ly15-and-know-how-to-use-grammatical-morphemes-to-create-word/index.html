Morphemes and Word Families

I can use morphemes and base words to build and read word families.

Topic GuideClassroom ViewWorksheetPracticeTest
Key concept

Learning goal: I can use morphemes and base words to build and read word families.

  • Build word families by adding common grammatical morphemes.
  • Find the base word inside a longer word and use the morpheme to help read its meaning.

Steps to use

Find base word → Add morpheme → Read new word → Explain meaning

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: building word families from common morphemes; for example, “play”, “plays”, “playing”, “played”, “playground”
  • E2: using morphemes to read words; for example, by recognising the base word in words such as “walk-ed”
Common misconceptions
  • Ending has no meaning — Explain what the morpheme changes.
  • Base word lost — Find the base word first.
  • Word family as rhymes — Word families here are about shared meaning parts.
Try these
  1. Word family build — Start with a base word and add endings.
    jump → jumps/jumped/jumping
  2. Meaning match — Match an ending to its meaning.
    -ed → happened before
  3. Sentence test — Use two words from the same family in sentences.
    I play. I played.
Explain and apply

Add an ending

Add -ing to play. What word do you make?

Answer and teaching guidance

playing is made from the base word play plus the ending -ing.

Show one action now

Which word means the action is happening now: help, helping or helps?

Answer and teaching guidance

helping means the action is happening now. The ending -ing changes how the word works in the sentence.

Find the base word

What is the base word in reading?

Answer and teaching guidance

read is the base word. The ending -ing has been added to make reading.

Make a word family

List three words in the play family.

Answer and teaching guidance

play, plays and playing belong to the same word family because they share the base word play.

Check your understanding

Quick checks

  • What is the base word in jumping?
  • Add -ed to look.
  • What does -s do in runs?
  • Make a word family for play.

I am ready when I can

  • I can build word families by adding common grammatical morphemes.
  • I can find the base word inside a longer word and use the morpheme to help read its meaning.
