Personal Connections to Literature

I can share a response to a literary text and connect it with my own experience.

Topic GuideClassroom ViewPrintable Worksheet40-question PracticeTest
What this skill means

Learning goal: I can share a response to a literary text and connect it with my own experience.

  • Ask questions about characters, settings and events.
  • Connect a text with your own experiences and explain the connection.
  • Show a response to a character or event through drawing or role-play.
  • Identify who is telling the story.

Steps to use

Listen/read → Notice part → Connect → Explain response

See it, then try it

Connect the text to your experience

Text: The child lost a toy.Connection: I lost my hat once.Response: I felt worried too.

Worked example

Question: “This reminds me of my first day at school” is a connection because it links the text to an experience.

Use respectful discussion language

I noticed...This reminds me of...I felt...I agree because...
Try these
  1. Connection sentence — Complete a sentence linking text to experience.
    This reminds me of...
  2. Feeling thermometer — Choose a feeling and explain which part of the text caused it.
    happy / worried / surprised
  3. Partner response — Listen to a partner and add a related idea.
    I also thought...
Common mix-ups
  • Retells only — Add your thought, feeling or connection.
  • Connection too vague — Name the part of the text that made the connection.
  • Interrupting discussion — Listen first, then respond.
Check your understanding

Quick checks

  • What is a personal connection?
  • Complete: This reminds me of ___.
  • How can a text make a reader feel?
  • Why should we listen to others’ responses?

I am ready when I can

  • I can ask questions about characters, settings and events.
  • I can connect a text with your own experiences and explain the connection.
  • I can show a response to a character or event through drawing or role-play.
  • I can identify who is telling the story.
Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: generating questions about characters, settings and events from books and sharing responses
  • E2: discussing different texts and offering opinions about how they reflect their own experiences
  • E3: expressing responses to characters and events in stories using drawing and role-play
  • E4: identifying who is telling the story in different texts
