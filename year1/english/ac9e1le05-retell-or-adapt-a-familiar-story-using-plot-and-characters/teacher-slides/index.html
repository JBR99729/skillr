Key conceptTeach and discuss

Learning goal: I can retell or adapt a familiar story while keeping its important plot and character ideas clear.

  • Describe a character using details from a familiar story.
  • Imitate a character's way of speaking or expressing an attitude.
  • Retell key events in order using speaking, drawing, performance or digital tools.

Retell first, then change one part

Work through the example

  1. Retell the main events in their order.
  2. To adapt the story, choose a part to change, such as the character or place.
  3. Keep the changed events connected. If the place changes, some actions may need to change too.

Watch for this: An adaptation makes a deliberate change; a retelling preserves the important events.

Try it together

Retell this story with a different character. Keep the lost-ball problem.

Answer and teaching guidance

Accept a clear retelling with the changed character and a connected ending.

An adult can read the directions, model the idea and accept an oral, pointed or drawn explanation where appropriate. For reading, spelling, handwriting or speaking tasks, observe the child doing the stated skill.

Curriculum examplesTeach and discuss

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: writing character descriptions
  • E2: imitating a characteristic piece of speech or dialogue, or the attitudes or expressions of favourite characters in texts
  • E3: retelling key events in stories using oral language, visual arts, digital tools or performance
Common misconceptionsTeach and discuss
  • Random changes — Keep enough familiar structure for the story to make sense.
  • Missing ending — A retell needs a clear ending.
  • Changes all parts at once — Change one or two parts deliberately.

Explain and applyTeach and discuss

Retell in order

Retell: Mia found a seed. Mia planted it. A flower grew. What happened first, second and last?

Answer and teaching guidance

The retell should keep the plot order so listeners recognise the familiar story.

Use characters and plot

If you act as the fox in a story, what words or actions help the audience know who you are?

Answer and teaching guidance

A strong answer uses character behaviour, voice, gesture or a simple prop tied to the plot.

Adapt one part

Change the setting of a familiar story while keeping the main character and problem.

Answer and teaching guidance

The adaptation should change one clear feature while preserving enough structure to remain recognisable.

Compare versions

Complete: In the original story ____. In my adapted story ____.

Answer and teaching guidance

The comparison shows what stayed the same and what changed in the retell or adaptation.
