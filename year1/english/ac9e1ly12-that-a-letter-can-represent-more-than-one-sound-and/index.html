Learning journey

What you’ll learn, strand by strand

This topic is taught through 5 focused strands. Each strand builds one part of the skill before learners move into independent practice.

  1. 1

    Letters represent sounds

    Understand that written letters and letter groups stand for speech sounds.

  2. 2

    Map sounds to graphemes

    Connect a heard sound to an appropriate letter or letter group when reading and spelling.

  3. 3

    Blend and segment words

    Move between whole words and their component sounds to support both decoding and spelling.

  4. 4

    Hear and count syllables

    Identify the beats or syllables in spoken words and notice the vowel sound in each syllable.

  5. 5

    Read and write words in syllable parts

    Use syllable boundaries to tackle longer words more accurately.

How to use this roadmap: learn the idea, study examples, explain your thinking, then practise and check mastery.

Key concept

Learning goal: I can recognise that letters can represent different sounds and every syllable has a vowel sound.

  • Recognise that one letter can represent different sounds in different words.
  • Recognise that the same sound can be represented by different letters.
  • Remember that every syllable contains a vowel sound.

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: recognising that letters can have more than one sound; for example, the letter “u” in “cut”, “put”, “use” and the letter “a” in “cat”, “father”, “any”
  • E2: recognising sounds that can be produced by different letters; for example, the “s” sound in “sat” and “cent”
See the idea: Hear the vowel sound in each syllable

Hear the vowel sound in each syllable

Work through the example

  1. Say sunset naturally, then clap sun and set: two syllables.
  2. Listen for a vowel sound in each part.
  3. A vowel letter can represent different sounds in different words. Count what you hear, not only the written letters.

Watch for this: Two written vowel letters may represent one vowel sound, as ai does in rain.

Try it together

Say robot. How many syllables do you hear?

Answer and teaching guidance

Two: ro-bot. Accept the usual pronunciation in the child’s accent.

An adult can read the directions, model the idea and accept an oral, pointed or drawn explanation where appropriate. For reading, spelling, handwriting or speaking tasks, observe the child doing the stated skill.

Common misconceptions
  • Letter has only one sound — Some letters represent different sounds in different words.
  • Counting written vowels only — Listen for vowel sounds.
  • Syllable without vowel — Every syllable needs a vowel sound.
Try these
  1. Sound compare — Compare a letter sound in two words.
    a: apple/table
  2. Syllable clap — Clap one- and two-syllable words.
    sun / tiger
  3. Vowel check — Circle or name the vowel sound in each syllable.
    robot
Explain and apply

Count vowel sounds

Clap cat. How many syllables do you hear?

Answer and teaching guidance

cat has one syllable because it has one vowel sound.

Letter, two sounds

Compare gem and go. What changes about the letter g?

Answer and teaching guidance

The letter g can represent a soft sound in gem and a hard sound in go.

Clap two parts

Clap sunset. How many syllables do you hear?

Answer and teaching guidance

sunset has two syllables: sun-set. Each syllable has a vowel sound.

Sort by vowel sound

Sort these words: cat, cake, bed, he. Which words have a short vowel sound?

Answer and teaching guidance

cat and bed have short vowel sounds. cake and he have long vowel sounds.

Check your understanding

Quick checks

  • Clap robot.
  • Can a letter make more than one sound?
  • What must every syllable contain?
  • Name a vowel letter.

I am ready when I can

  • I can recognise that one letter can represent different sounds in different words.
  • I can recognise that the same sound can be represented by different letters.
  • I can remember that every syllable contains a vowel sound.
