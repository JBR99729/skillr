Reasons for Likes and Preferences

I can explain my likes, dislikes and preferences with clear reasons.

Topic GuideClassroom ViewPrintable Worksheet40-question PracticeTest
What this skill means

Learning goal: I can explain my likes, dislikes and preferences with clear reasons.

  • Use because to give a reason for a like, dislike or preference.
  • Use comparison words such as better and faster to show how strongly you prefer something.

Steps to use

State preference → Give reason → Use evidence → Listen/respond

See it, then try it

Use because to explain a preference

I prefer the red coverbecause it is easier to seeI dislike the noisy endingbecause it distracts me

Worked example

Question: Which response gives a reason? “I like the poem because the rhyme is funny.” It says the preference and explains why.

Build a reasoned response

I like...I prefer...because...My reason is...
Try these
  1. Reason ladder — Choose a book, game or activity and add one clear reason.
    I prefer ___ because ___.
  2. Sort strong responses — Sort responses into ‘reason’ and ‘no reason’.
    I like it. / I like it because it is funny.
  3. Agree and build — Listen to a partner’s preference and add your own reason.
    I agree because...
Common mix-ups
  • No reason — Add because and a real reason.
  • Repeating the same words — Use like, dislike, prefer and reason language.
  • Reason does not match — Check the reason explains the choice.
Check your understanding

Quick checks

  • Complete: I prefer ___ because ___.
  • Which sentence gives a reason?
  • How can you politely disagree?
  • Why is “because” useful?

I am ready when I can

  • I can use because to give a reason for a like, dislike or preference.
  • I can use comparison words such as better and faster to show how strongly you prefer something.
Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: using words including “because” to introduce reasons for likes, dislikes and preferences
  • E2: exploring comparative words (adjectives) to express the degree of preference; for example, “better”, “faster”
