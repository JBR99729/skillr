Poems, Chants, Rhymes and Songs

I can listen to, discuss and create sound patterns in poems, chants, rhymes and songs.

What this skill means

Learning goal: I can listen to, discuss and create sound patterns in poems, chants, rhymes and songs.

  • Listen and respond to performance poetry, chants and songs from First Nations Australians.
  • Explore poetry, chants and songs from Asian cultures.
  • Listen to short poems such as haiku about familiar topics.
  • Imitate and invent alliteration, rhyme and other sound patterns.

Steps to use

Listen → Find sound pattern → Imitate → Invent → Perform

See it, then try it

Rhyme and alliteration are sound choices

rhyme: bee/treealliteration: silly snakebeat: clap-clap-rest

Worked example

Question: “busy buzzing bees” uses alliteration because several words begin with the /b/ sound.

Keep the sound pattern going

choose a soundrepeat itswap wordsperform with rhythm
Try these
  1. Sound hunt — Find repeated first sounds in a line.
    big blue bird
  2. Rhyme chain — Make a chain of words that rhyme.
    light / night / bright
  3. Invent a chant — Create a two-line chant with one repeated sound.
    Tap the table, tap the tin.
Common mix-ups
  • Alliteration = same letter only — Listen for same sound, not just same spelling.
  • Rhyme = same first sound — Rhyme is usually ending sound.
  • No performance — Say it aloud to hear the pattern.
Check your understanding

Quick checks

  • Which uses alliteration: lazy lion or cat hat?
  • Give a rhyme for day.
  • What is a chant?
  • Invent two words starting with /s/.

I am ready when I can

  • I can listen and respond to performance poetry, chants and songs from First Nations Australians.
  • I can explore poetry, chants and songs from Asian cultures.
  • I can listen to short poems such as haiku about familiar topics.
  • I can imitate and invent alliteration, rhyme and other sound patterns.
