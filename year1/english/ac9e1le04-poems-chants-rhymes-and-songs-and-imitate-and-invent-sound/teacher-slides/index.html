1. Outcome Overview & Core Concepts

Poems, chants, rhymes and songs often use sound patterns such as rhyme and alliteration. Students can imitate and invent those patterns.

Learning target: listen to and discuss poems, chants, rhymes and songs, and imitate and invent sound patterns including alliteration and rhyme

Big idea: Listen, imitate and invent alliteration and rhyme

Learning routine: Listen → Find sound pattern → Imitate → Invent → Perform

    big blue bird
  2. Rhyme chain — Make a chain of words that rhyme.
    light / night / bright
  3. Invent a chant — Create a two-line chant with one repeated sound.
    Tap the table, tap the tin.

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • Alliteration = same letter only: Listen for same sound, not just same spelling.
  • Rhyme = same first sound: Rhyme is usually ending sound.
  • No performance: Say it aloud to hear the pattern.

Practice Thought & Formative Assessment

Use a fresh, code-aligned example. The student should show the exact word, sound, sentence, image feature, audience clue or text evidence that supports the answer.

Quick checks

  • Which uses alliteration: lazy lion or cat hat?
  • Give a rhyme for day.
  • What is a chant?
  • Invent two words starting with /s/.

  • Hear alliteration
