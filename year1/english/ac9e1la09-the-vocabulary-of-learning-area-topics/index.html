Learn the skill

Six small steps, clear examples

Open the focus you need for today’s worksheet. Read, try and explain before moving on.

Learning goal: Help children connect learning words with their meanings.

Useful words: A topic is what we are learning about. Topic words help us name and explain ideas. A clue helps us work out a meaning.

1. What are topic words?

Topic words belong to what we are learning. Say the word, show its meaning, then use it in a short sentence.

Example

In a plant lesson, root, stem and leaf name parts. In a shape lesson, side and corner help us describe shapes.

Try it: Which belongs in our plant word group: leaf or wheel?

See answer and explanation

Leaf names a plant part. Wheel belongs to a different group here; the group depends on the stated topic.

2. Science topic words

Observe means look closely to find out. Roots take in water and hold a plant in place. A stem supports the plant’s leaves. A leaf is a plant part.

Example

“I observe the leaf” means I look at it carefully. “I water the roots” names the part taking in water.

Try it: A child looks closely at a plant with a magnifier. Does observe mean look closely or look away?

See answer and explanation

Look closely. The action gives a clue to the meaning. Show a real, safe plant and point to its parts; guessing from the sound of the word is not enough.

3. Geography topic words

A map shows where places are. A route is a way from one place to another. A landmark is a place or feature that helps us recognise where we are.

Example

On our pretend map, the route goes from the school gate, past the hall, to the garden. The hall is a landmark.

Try it: Which word names the way from the gate to the garden: route or leaf?

See answer and explanation

Route. Ask the learner to trace a route on a simple supplied map. A map symbol gets its meaning from that map’s key; symbols are not always the same.

4. Group and connect related words

Group words by meaning, not just by their first letters. Say what the words have in common. A word may fit more than one sensible group.

Example

Rain, wind and cloud are weather words. Root, stem and leaf are plant-part words.

Try it: For the group “weather”, which does not fit: rain, wind, pencil? Explain.

See answer and explanation

Pencil does not name weather. Rain and wind do. Ask for the group meaning rather than a spelling rule.

5. Use topic words precisely

Choose the word that matches the meaning. Nearby words, a picture or a real object can help. Check a new meaning with an adult.

Example

“Draw a line around the shape” uses around to explain the position. “The stem holds up the leaves” gives a clue about stem.

Try it: A plant part holds up the leaves. Is it the stem or the seed?

See answer and explanation

The stem. A seed can grow into a new plant, but it is not the part described here. The child can point, say or draw the taught part.

6. Topic words across subjects

The same word can have different meanings in different lessons. Read or hear the whole message before deciding. Repeat means do again; past means before now.

Example

“Turn the page” is an action. “It is my turn” means it is my time to take part. The sentences tell us which meaning fits.

Try it: In “We planted seeds in the past”, does past mean before now or later?

See answer and explanation

Before now. Use a short class timeline to show the meaning. Revisit familiar words in Maths, Science and English rather than testing untaught subject facts.

See the idea: Link a topic word to its meaning

Link a topic word to its meaning

Work through the example

  1. A topic word helps name something we are learning about.
  2. Explain the word in familiar language, then use it in a sentence about that topic.
  3. Check context. The same word can have different meanings in different situations.

Watch for this: Knowing a word means using it meaningfully, not only repeating it.

Try it together

Use the word shelter in a sentence about an animal’s needs.

Answer and teaching guidance

For example, “The rabbit needs a safe shelter.” Accept an accurate sentence and explanation.

An adult can read the directions, model the idea and accept an oral, pointed or drawn explanation where appropriate. For reading, spelling, handwriting or speaking tasks, observe the child doing the stated skill.

Check learning and offer support

Watch for this: A word that looks or sounds similar is not always related in meaning. Weather does not guarantee the same conditions every season or everywhere in Australia.

Check: Choose one taught topic word. Show what it means, use it in a short sentence and explain which learning topic it fits.

See answer and explanation

Look for a correct meaning and a relevant use, not perfect spelling. If the child names a word without showing its meaning, model it with a real object or clear example and try again.

Help: Offer two taught words and a real object or labelled picture. Model a short sentence, then let the learner make one.

Next challenge: Compare a familiar word in two short sentences with different meanings. Explain the clue that helps.

Local words and respect

Adults can introduce Country, Traditional Owners and Acknowledgement of Country using local community guidance. Country has cultural meaning beyond a place on a map. Check preferred local names and pronunciation; an Acknowledgement and a Welcome to Country are different. Do not invent cultural names or require children to share private family knowledge.
