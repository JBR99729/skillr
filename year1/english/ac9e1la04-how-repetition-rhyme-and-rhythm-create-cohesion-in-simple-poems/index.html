Repetition, Rhyme and Rhythm

I can hear and use repetition, rhyme and rhythm in poems, chants and songs.

Topic GuideClassroom ViewPrintable Worksheet40-question PracticeTest
What this skill means

Learning goal: I can hear and use repetition, rhyme and rhythm in poems, chants and songs.

  • Find repeated words and sentence patterns.
  • Hear end rhyme in poems.
  • Use rhythm and repetition to make a poem, chant or song sound connected.

Steps to use

Listen → Clap rhythm → Find repeat → Find rhyme → Perform

See it, then try it

Sound patterns make the text stick together

tap tap tapcat / hatagain and againbeat: clap-clap-pause

Worked example

Question: Which words rhyme: sun/run or sun/sock? Sun and run rhyme because they end with the same sound.

Perform the pattern, then invent one

repeat a linekeep the beatchange one rhyming word
Try these
  1. Clap the beat — Clap or tap the beat while saying a chant.
    tap tap pause
  2. Rhyme swap — Change one rhyming word and keep the pattern.
    cat, hat, mat
  3. Find the repeated line — Listen for a line or sound that comes back.
    Again! Again!
Common mix-ups
  • Rhyme = same first sound — Rhyme usually matches the ending sound.
  • Reading without rhythm — Clap or tap the beat first.
  • Missing repeated words — Listen for words or lines that come back.
Check your understanding

Quick checks

  • Which pair rhymes: play/day or play/pig?
  • Name one repeated word in a chant.
  • What is rhythm?
  • Invent a rhyming word for cat.

I am ready when I can

  • I can find repeated words and sentence patterns.
  • I can hear end rhyme in poems.
  • I can use rhythm and repetition to make a poem, chant or song sound connected.
Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: identifying patterns of repetition in texts; for example, repetition of sentence patterns such as “Have you seen …”
  • E2: discussing different poems and identifying rhyme; for example, end of line rhyme
