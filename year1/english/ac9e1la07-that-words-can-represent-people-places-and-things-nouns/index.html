Word Classes in Sentences

I can choose words for people, actions, qualities and details to make sentences precise.

Topic GuideClassroom ViewPrintable Worksheet40-question PracticeTest
What this skill means

Learning goal: I can choose words for people, actions, qualities and details to make sentences precise.

  • Recognise words for people, places and things; happenings and states; qualities; and details.
  • Make a sentence more precise by choosing useful describing words, detail words and exact verbs.

Steps to use

Read sentence → Find word → Ask its job → Name word class

See it, then try it

Word job cards

noun: dogverb: runsadjective: brownadverb: quickly

Worked example

Question: In “The small bird sang”, which word describes the bird? Small is an adjective because it tells what the bird is like.

Ask the job question

Who/what? → nounWhat happens? → verbWhat kind? → adjectiveHow/when/where? → adverb
Try these
  1. Word job sort — Sort word cards by job.
    jump / tree / soft / slowly
  2. Build a richer sentence — Add an adjective or adverb to a simple sentence.
    The dog runs → The brown dog runs quickly.
  3. Find in reading — Pick one sentence and mark a noun and a verb.
    Mia paints.
Common mix-ups
  • Word meaning only — Ask what job the word does in the sentence.
  • Adjective as noun — A describing word needs something to describe.
  • Missing verbs — Find what happens or what is.
Check your understanding

Quick checks

  • Name the noun in: The cat sleeps.
  • Name the verb in: Sam jumps.
  • Which word describes: red ball?
  • Add an adverb to: The dog ran.

I am ready when I can

  • I can recognise words for people, places and things; happenings and states; qualities; and details.
  • I can make a sentence more precise by choosing useful describing words, detail words and exact verbs.
Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: understanding that words or groups of words can represent the participants (nouns; for example, people, places, things) that are involved in various activities or processes (verbs of doing, saying, thinking, being) and the details or circumstances surrounding the activity (adjectives and adverbs that answer “when?”, “where?”, “how?”)
  • E2: recognising how a sentence can be made more specific by adding adjectives, adverbs and precise verbs
