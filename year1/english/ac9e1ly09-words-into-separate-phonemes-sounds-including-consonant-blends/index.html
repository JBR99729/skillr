Learning journey

What you’ll learn, strand by strand

This topic is taught through 5 focused strands. Each strand builds one part of the skill before learners move into independent practice.

  1. 1

    Hear a word as separate sounds

    Listen for each phoneme in a spoken word rather than treating the word as one sound.

  2. 2

    Stretch, count and segment sounds

    Say a word slowly, count its sounds and separate them in order.

  3. 3

    Keep sounds in the right order

    Preserve the sequence of phonemes, including blends at the beginning or end of a word.

  4. 4

    Segment longer spoken words

    Apply the same process to words with more sounds and more complex consonant combinations.

  5. 5

    Blend back and check

    Put the separated sounds back together to confirm that they reconstruct the original word.

How to use this roadmap: learn the idea, study examples, explain your thinking, then practise and check mastery.

Key concept

Learning goal: I can break spoken words into their separate phonemes, including words with blends and clusters.

  • Say each phoneme in order in a spoken word, including words with consonant blends or clusters.

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: saying sounds in order for a given spoken word; for example, “s-p-oo-n” and “f-i-s-t”
See the idea: Count spoken sounds, not letters

Count spoken sounds, not letters

Work through the example

  1. Say shop naturally. Say its sounds in order: /sh/, /o/, /p/.
  2. There are three sounds. The two letters sh represent one sound.
  3. Compare frog: /f/, /r/, /o/, /g/. The beginning blend has two separate sounds.

Watch for this: Letter counts, syllable claps and phoneme counts are different tasks. Do this check orally.

Try it together

With the spelling covered, ask an adult to say frog. Tap each sound, then blend it back.

Answer and teaching guidance

Four sounds in order: /f/, /r/, /o/, /g/. Accept ordinary accent variation.

An adult can read the directions, model the idea and accept an oral, pointed or drawn explanation where appropriate. For reading, spelling, handwriting or speaking tasks, observe the child doing the stated skill.

Common misconceptions
  • Counting letters not sounds — Listen for phonemes, not letter count.
  • Blend as one sound — /fr/ has /f/ and /r/.
  • Cannot blend back — Always say the word again after segmenting.
Try these
  1. Sound boxes — Push one counter for each sound.
    stop = /s/ /t/ /o/ /p/
  2. Blend hunt — Find words with two consonant sounds at the start.
    frog / swim
  3. Stretch and blend — Stretch sounds then say the word normally.
    /b/ /e/ /s/ /t/ → best
Explain and apply

Segment a digraph word

Say ship. Which sounds do you hear?

Answer and teaching guidance

The sounds are /sh/ /i/ /p/. The two letters sh represent one sound.

Segment a blend word

Say frog. Which separate sounds do you hear?

Answer and teaching guidance

The sounds are /f/ /r/ /o/ /g/. The blend has two consonant sounds.

Final cluster check

Say nest. What sounds come at the end?

Answer and teaching guidance

The final sounds are /s/ and /t/. Students should hear both sounds in the final cluster.

Tap the sounds

Tap once for each sound in clap.

Answer and teaching guidance

Clap has four sounds: /c/ /l/ /a/ /p/. This matches IXL-style sound segmentation.

Check your understanding

Quick checks

  • Segment “ship”.
  • How many sounds in “frog”?
  • What is a consonant blend?
  • Blend /s/ /t/ /o/ /p/.

I am ready when I can

  • I can say each phoneme in order in a spoken word, including words with consonant blends or clusters.
