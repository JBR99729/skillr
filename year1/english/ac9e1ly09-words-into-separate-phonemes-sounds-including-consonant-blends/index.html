YEAR 1 ENGLISH

Hear every sound in a word AC9E1LY09

Learning intention: I can separate spoken words into sounds and keep their order, including clusters.

An adult can read directions and model the examples. Use a short turn or return to a section later.

More examples ↓Help with gaps ↓10-minute activity ↓

WORKED EXAMPLE

One counter for each sound

Adult says shop without showing its spelling. Say its sounds: /sh/ /o/ /p/. Move three counters. In stop, say /s/ /t/ /o/ /p/ and move four counters. The two beginning sounds in stop remain separate. The printed sh in shop represents one sound. Say the whole word again to check.

Learn the skill

1. Listen and separate

A phoneme is one speech sound. Say a familiar word naturally, stretch where possible, then say each sound in order. Avoid adding an extra “uh” after consonants.

Adult models map: /m/ /a/ /p/, then blends back to map. Three counters stand for the three sounds.

Try it · Adult says fish with its spelling hidden. Say every sound and move one counter each.

/f/ /i/ /sh/: three sounds. Hear the actual response; the last sound is one /sh/. Counters support the oral task, not letter counting.

2. Keep the beginning cluster

A consonant cluster has adjacent consonant sounds. Listen for each one instead of treating the cluster as a single sound.

Flag: /f/ /l/ /a/ /g/. There are four sounds; /f/ and /l/ both come before the vowel.

Try it · Adult says swim without showing its spelling. Separate every sound, then blend back.

/s/ /w/ /i/ /m/: four sounds, in order. If /w/ is missed, model it and try again. Note the support rather than calling the repeated model independent.

3. Keep the ending cluster

Listen through to the end. A correct total alone is not enough: say each sound in its right place.

Nest: /n/ /e/ /s/ /t/. Both /s/ and /t/ need a counter.

Try it · Adult says lamp with spelling hidden. Say the sounds and identify the last two.

/l/ /a/ /m/ /p/: four sounds. The final two are /m/ /p/. Accept ordinary accent variation; do not substitute syllable claps for the phoneme response.

Common mistakes

“A letter is always one sound.” Listen first: sh can represent one sound.

“A cluster is one sound.” Keep each consonant, as /s/ and /t/ in stop.

“The correct number is enough.” Ask for the full ordered sound sequence, then blend back.

10-MINUTE ACTIVITY

Say, move and check

Adult chooses a few familiar spoken words such as map, fish, flag and nest, keeping spellings hidden. Child repeats each whole word, says its sounds with one counter each, then blends back. Use fewer words if attention or effort drops.

Adjust the challenge Starter: model a three-sound word. Core: keep both sounds in beginning and ending clusters. Stretch: try stamp, /s/ /t/ /a/ /m/ /p/, keeping all five sounds in order.

Use counters, large tokens or pointing as appropriate. Observe actual segmentation. Accent and speech access needs require thoughtful support; a written choice does not establish an oral response.

Three tricky questions

1. Does shop need four sound counters?

No. /sh/ /o/ /p/ has three sounds.

2. Can you leave out the last sound of nest?

No. /t/ is part of the spoken word.

3. Is one clap for stop the same task?

No. Stop is one syllable but has four phonemes.

FAQ

Should I show the spelling first?

For the oral check, hide it. Afterward, letters can help connect sounds to spelling.

Must every sound be stretched?

No. Some consonants are brief stop sounds. Say them cleanly without an added vowel.

Count spoken sounds, not letters

Work through the example

  1. Say shop naturally. Say its sounds in order: /sh/, /o/, /p/.
  2. There are three sounds. The two letters sh represent one sound.
  3. Compare frog: /f/, /r/, /o/, /g/. The beginning blend has two separate sounds.

Watch for this: Letter counts, syllable claps and phoneme counts are different tasks. Do this check orally.

Try it together

With the spelling covered, ask an adult to say frog. Tap each sound, then blend it back.

Answer and teaching guidance

Four sounds in order: /f/, /r/, /o/, /g/. Accept ordinary accent variation.

An adult can read the directions, model the idea and accept an oral, pointed or drawn explanation where appropriate. For reading, spelling, handwriting or speaking tasks, observe the child doing the stated skill.

Common misconceptions
  • Counting letters not sounds — Listen for phonemes, not letter count.
  • Blend as one sound — /fr/ has /f/ and /r/.
  • Cannot blend back — Always say the word again after segmenting.

Explain and apply

Segment a digraph word

Say ship. Which sounds do you hear?

Answer and teaching guidance

The sounds are /sh/ /i/ /p/. The two letters sh represent one sound.

Segment a blend word

Say frog. Which separate sounds do you hear?

Answer and teaching guidance

The sounds are /f/ /r/ /o/ /g/. The blend has two consonant sounds.

Final cluster check

Say nest. What sounds come at the end?

Answer and teaching guidance

The final sounds are /s/ and /t/. Students should hear both sounds in the final cluster.

Tap the sounds

Tap once for each sound in clap.

Answer and teaching guidance

Clap has four sounds: /c/ /l/ /a/ /p/.
