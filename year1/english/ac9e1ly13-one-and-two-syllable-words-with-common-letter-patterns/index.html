YEAR 1 ENGLISH

Spell words using familiar patterns AC9E1LY13

Learning intention: I can say, write and check one- and two-syllable words with common spellings.

An adult can read directions and model the examples. Use a short turn or return to a section later.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Use sounds, parts and a spelling check

Adult dictates sunset with the model hidden. Say sun–set. Spell the first familiar part s-u-n, then s-e-t. Join them: sunset. Read it back and compare with the standard spelling. Both parts matter; sounding out helps, while remembered and taught spelling patterns tell us which letters to choose.

Learn the skill

1. Map sounds to taught letters

Say the word, listen in order and choose known spellings. Keep both consonants in a blend and all letters of a digraph.

Ship uses sh for its first sound. Flag needs both f and l before a and g. Nest needs both s and t at the end.

Try it · Adult hides the screen and dictates clap. Write the whole word, then check it.

Clap: c-l-a-p. Keep /k/ and /l/ at the start. A spoken segmentation supports spelling, but the child must produce the written word.

2. Write both syllables

Say each part and spell it with taught patterns. Some standard spellings include letters you cannot infer from a syllable count alone.

Napkin: nap–kin, n-a-p-k-i-n. Rabbit: two spoken parts; its taught standard spelling is r-a-b-b-i-t. Notice bb explicitly.

Try it · Adult dictates sandpit with the spelling hidden. Write and check both parts.

Sand–pit: s-a-n-d-p-i-t. Retain the final d of sand and the first p of pit. Compare only after the child attempts the spelling.

3. Check the pattern and the whole word

Read your spelling back and compare with a reliable model after trying. Locate the exact difference and rewrite the full word. A correct sound can have several spellings.

Draft: “picnik”. Reference: picnic. Compare the last letters: this word ends with c, not k. Correct the whole word and read it again.

Try it · Compare draft “rabit” with reference rabbit. What must change?

Add the second b: rabbit. Hearing two syllables does not itself prove doubling. Learn and check the standard spelling in a useful word.

Common mistakes

“Every sound uses one letter.” Sh in ship represents one sound with two letters.

“Two syllables tell you every letter.” Use taught word spellings as well as spoken parts.

“Copying is a spelling-memory check.” Copying practises letter order; hide the model for a spelling attempt.

10-MINUTE ACTIVITY

Listen, write and compare

Adult chooses a few taught words such as flag, rain, napkin and sandpit, then hides the displayed spellings. Dictate each in a short meaningful sentence. Child says its parts, writes it, reads it back and compares with a model afterward.

Adjust the challenge Starter: use a taught sound or pattern card. Core: spell one- and two-syllable targets without copying. Stretch: explain one correction or apply familiar parts in bedtime or raincoat after those spellings are taught.

Keep dictation targets and answer models away from the learner during attempts. Record prompts and any reference used. A typed response can show spelling; assess handwriting separately.

Three tricky questions

1. Can sounding out choose between every spelling of /k/?

No. Common patterns and known standard word spellings help choose c, k or ck.

2. Is a missing second part acceptable in sunset?

No. Both sun and set belong in the whole word.

3. Must a correct first attempt be changed?

No. Check it and explain what is already correct.

FAQ

Should every word be unfamiliar?

No. Rehearse useful taught words and add new words supported by familiar patterns.

Why use a sentence in dictation?

It identifies the intended word and meaning, especially when words can sound alike.

Work through the example

  1. An adult says the word while its spelling is hidden.
  2. Say the syllables, then listen to the sounds in each part.
  3. Write the word using known patterns. Compare with the correct spelling after trying, and discuss any correction.

Watch for this: Copying a displayed word is useful practice but is not independent spelling from dictation.

Picnic: pic-nic. Check all six letters after the child writes the word.

Common misconceptions
  • Guessing without checking — Use sound and pattern knowledge.
  • Missing second syllable — Clap and spell both parts.
  • Pattern not checked — Compare with a known word.

Explain and apply

Check each syllable

Write rabbit. Which spelling keeps the two parts clear: rab-bit?

Answer and teaching guidance

Rabbit is correct. Say its two syllables to check the whole word, then compare with the taught spelling rabbit: it has bb in the middle. Hearing two syllables alone does not tell you to double b.

Build a compound word

Join sun and set. What word do you make?

Answer and teaching guidance

sun and set make sunset. Students can spell the word by writing both smaller words in order.

Choose a digraph spelling

Which spelling matches chicken: chikin, chicken or chiken?

Answer and teaching guidance

chicken is correct. It begins with ch and uses the common ck pattern after a short vowel sound.

Proofread by pattern

A student writes picnik. Which letter pattern should they check?

Answer and teaching guidance

picnic is correct. Students should check the final /k/ spelling pattern in the second syllable.
