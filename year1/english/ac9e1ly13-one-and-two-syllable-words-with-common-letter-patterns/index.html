AC9E1LY13: Spell one- and two-syllable words with common letter patterns

What students learn

1. Outcome Overview & Core Concepts

Spellers use common letter patterns and syllable parts to spell one- and two-syllable words.

Learning target: spell one- and two-syllable words with common letter patterns

Big idea: Spell one- and two-syllable words using common patterns

Learning routine: Say word → Segment sounds/syllables → Choose pattern → Write → Check

2. Subject-Specific Content & Key Examples

2. Subject-Specific Content & Key Examples

Use known patterns to spell

playrainshopsunsetjumping

Worked example

Question: Spell “sunset”. Clap sun-set, spell each part, then join the two syllables.

Break the word before writing

soundpatternsyllablewritecheck

Pattern word sort

Sort words with a common spelling pattern.

Guided practice

  1. Pattern word sort — Sort words with a common spelling pattern.
    play/day/stay
  2. Syllable spelling — Split a two-syllable word before spelling.
    sun-set
  3. Check aloud — Read your spelling back and listen for missing sounds.
    ship / shop
3. Common Student Misconceptions

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • Writing from memory only: Use sound and pattern knowledge.
  • Missing second syllable: Clap and spell both parts.
  • Pattern not checked: Compare with a known word.
4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence

4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence

Step 1: Explicit Teaching & Modelling

Name the target in child-friendly language and demonstrate one concrete example connected to writing.

Step 2: Guided Analysis

Compare or practise a second example together. Students point to, say or show the clue.

Step 3: Collaborative / Independent Practice

Create or analyse one example together, then use a fresh familiar example independently.

Step 4: Formative Assessment

Use a one-minute check with a new example; require observable evidence rather than a guess.

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: writing one- and two-syllable words containing known blends; for example, “bl” and “st”
Practice Thought & Formative Assessment

Practice Thought & Formative Assessment

Use a fresh, code-aligned example. The student should show the exact word, sound, sentence, image feature, audience clue or text evidence that supports the answer.

Quick checks

  • Spell a word that rhymes with day.
  • How do you spell sunset in parts?
  • Why say the word slowly?
  • Name one common pattern.

Mastery evidence

Revision Notes

Revision Notes

  • Remember: Spell one- and two-syllable words using common patterns

  • Before answering: point to the clue in the spoken words, written sentence, image, page layout or text purpose.
