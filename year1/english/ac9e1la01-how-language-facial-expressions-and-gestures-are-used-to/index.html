AC9E1LA01 • Year 1 English

Language, Faces and Gestures

I can use words, facial expressions and gestures to communicate clearly and choose useful open or closed questions.

Topic GuideClassroom ViewPrintable Worksheet40-question PracticeTest
What this skill means

Learning goal: I can use words, facial expressions and gestures to communicate clearly and choose useful open or closed questions.

  • Notice how words, facial expressions, gestures and body language change a message.
  • Recognise that First Nations Australian communication can include important symbols, gestures and body language.
  • Use words and body language to show emotions.
  • Tell the difference between a closed question and an open question.

Steps to use

Notice purpose → Choose words → Use voice/gesture → Check response

See it, then try it

Match language to the speaking purpose

Question: Can I join?Offer: I can help.Command: Stop there.Exclaim: Wow!

Students practise how the words, face, gesture and voice change for each purpose.

Worked example

Question: Which is a request? “Please pass the glue.” It politely asks someone to do something, so it is a request.

Say it two ways and notice the change

Could I have a turn?Give me a turn.May I help?

Choose the wording that fits the relationship and classroom situation.

Try these
  1. Purpose cards — Sort short spoken lines into question, offer, request, command and exclamation.
    Can I help? / Look out! / Please wait.
  2. Voice and face match — Say the same words with a calm request voice and then an excited exclamation voice.
    That is amazing!
  3. Polite classroom request — Change a command into a polite request.
    Move. → Could you please move?
Common mix-ups
  • Words only — Also notice face, gesture, voice and relationship.
  • Command instead of request — Use polite wording when asking someone to do something.
  • One voice for every purpose — Change volume, tone or gesture to match meaning.
Check your understanding

Quick checks

  • Which sentence is a question?
  • Turn “Give me the book” into a polite request.
  • What facial expression might match an exclamation?
  • Why does relationship matter when we speak?

I am ready when I can

  • I can notice how words, facial expressions, gestures and body language change a message.
  • I can recognise that First Nations Australian communication can include important symbols, gestures and body language.
  • I can use words and body language to show emotions.
  • I can tell the difference between a closed question and an open question.
Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: recognising the effects of words, signing, gestures and body language on the way communications are received by others
  • E2: understanding that symbols, gestures and body language are key components of communication in First Nations Australian communities (teaching context)
  • E3: viewing short films and discussing how characters use words and body language to convey emotions
  • E4: learning the difference between closed questions; for example, “Are you ready?”, and open questions; for example, “What made this text so exciting?”
