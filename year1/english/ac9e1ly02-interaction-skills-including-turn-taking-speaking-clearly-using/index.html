Turn-Taking and Active Listening

I can take turns, listen actively and build a conversation with ideas and questions.

Topic GuideClassroom ViewPrintable Worksheet40-question PracticeTest
What this skill means

Learning goal: I can take turns, listen actively and build a conversation with ideas and questions.

  • Take turns in pair and group talk.
  • Stay on topic, listen carefully and respond to what another person says.
  • Contribute ideas in class, group and pair discussions.
  • Interact appropriately with peers, teachers and visitors.
  • Ask useful open, closed, when, why and how questions.

Steps to use

Listen → Wait turn → Speak clearly → Respond to idea → Ask/question

See it, then try it

Conversation moves

I agree because...Can you explain?My idea is...I heard you say...

Worked example

Question: If a partner says “Plants need water”, a good response is “I agree because our plant drooped when dry.” It listens and builds on the idea.

Use one talk move at a time

look/listenwaitspeak clearlyrespondask
Try these
  1. Talk move practice — Use one sentence stem in a partner discussion.
    I agree because...
  2. Listening check — Repeat one idea your partner said.
    You said...
  3. Question build — Ask one question that helps the discussion continue.
    Why do you think...?
Common mix-ups
  • Waiting only to talk — Listen for the idea before responding.
  • Too quiet/fast — Use classroom volume and clear pace.
  • New idea ignores partner — Connect to what was already said.
Check your understanding

Quick checks

  • What is active listening?
  • Give a useful response stem.
  • Why take turns?
  • Ask a question that keeps talk going.

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: using turn-taking in group and pair work
  • E2: building a conversation by staying on topic, supporting other speakers, eliciting responses, listening supportively and attentively, asking relevant questions, providing useful feedback and prompting
  • E3: participating in informal and structured class, group and pair discussions about content area topics, ideas and information
  • E4: interacting appropriately with peers, teachers and visitors
  • E5: formulating different types of questions to ask a speaker, such as open and closed questions and “when”, “why” and “how” questions
