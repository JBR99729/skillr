YEAR 1 SCIENCE · AC9S1U03

Push, pull and predict

Describe the strength and direction of pushes and pulls. Predict changes to an object's motion or shape.

More examples ↓Help with gaps ↓10-minute activity ↓

Learning intention: Describe the strength and direction of pushes and pulls. Predict changes to an object's motion or shape.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Same car, a different push

Push a toy car gently to the right, then try a stronger push with the same car, start point and surface. Predict that it may travel farther after the stronger push. Observe what actually happens. A push on soft dough may change its shape instead.

ActionDirection or strengthPossible effect
Push a carTo the rightStarts moving right
Stronger push, otherwise similar trialGreater strengthMay travel farther
Press soft doughInwardsChanges shape

Learn the skill

1. Name the force and direction

A force is a push or pull. Describe which way it acts. Force direction and movement direction are not always the same.

Examples

Push a toy car away from your hand. Pull a toy wagon towards you using its handle.

Try it · A ball rolls right. A hand pushes against it to the left. Could it slow down?

Yes. A push opposite to its movement can slow or stop it.

2. Compare strengths carefully

Change the strength of the push while keeping the car, surface and starting place the same. Predict before trying.

TrialCar and surfacePush
ASame car and boardGentle
BSame car and boardStronger
Try it · Can we compare push strength if one trial uses a heavy car on a rug and the other a light car on a board?

The comparison is unclear because the car and surface changed too.

3. Watch motion and shape

A force may start, stop, speed up, slow down or turn an object. It may also bend, stretch or squash a suitable material.

ObjectActionObservation
Soft doughPress gentlyShape changes
Moving carOpposing pushMay slow or stop
Try it · Does every force make an object move to a new place?

No. An object may stay in place or change shape.

Common mistakes

“A stronger push always moves every object farther.” Compare the same object and conditions. Other conditions and obstacles can affect the result.

“Only pulling can change shape.” A push can squash dough; a pull can stretch suitable material.

10-MINUTE ACTIVITY

Predict two force effects

2 minutes: an adult checks a floor-level space and a sturdy toy car. 5 minutes: compare gentle and stronger pushes from the same start; then gently press soft dough. 3 minutes: describe direction, strength and the observed effects.

Adjust the challenge Support: point and say push or pull. Extend: explain which conditions must stay the same to compare push strength.

Use gentle forces away from people, edges and breakable objects. Use dough only if suitable for the child; do not eat it.

Three tricky questions

1. Can a force stop a moving object?

Yes. A force acting against motion can slow or stop it.

2. A pushed box does not move. Was there necessarily no push?

No. A push may act even when no movement is visible.

3. Can the same material change shape in more than one way?

Yes. Suitable dough can be squashed by pushing or stretched by pulling.

FAQ

Is a force just movement?

No. A force is a push or pull that can affect motion or shape.

Do we need force calculations?

No. Describe and compare familiar pushes and pulls using everyday language.

More teaching examples and curriculum coverage

Additional explanations and guided checks for AC9S1U03.

1. Describe pushes and pulls

A push acts away from the pushing contact; a pull draws towards the pulling contact. Opening a drawer by its handle uses a pull.

Try it: What action usually closes that drawer?

See answer and explanation

A push. Name the action and its direction.

2. Name the force direction

An arrow labelled “push” shows the direction of the force, not necessarily the whole path the object will travel.

Try it: An arrow marked push points right. Which direction is the force?

See answer and explanation

Right. Read the arrow's stated meaning.

3. Compare force strength carefully

With the same trolley and conditions, a stronger starting push can make it move faster. Surface, barriers and starting conditions also matter.

Try it: Does a stronger push always guarantee a greater distance?

See answer and explanation

No. Conditions can change the result; describe the given test.

4. Notice changes in motion

Forces can start, stop, slow or change an object's direction. An adult gently catching a rolling ball makes it stop.

Try it: What might a sideways tap change?

See answer and explanation

The direction of the ball's motion. Observe what actually happens.

5. Notice changes in shape

Squeezing soft dough changes its shape. The push does not turn it into a new material.

Try it: What changes when a sponge is squeezed?

See answer and explanation

Its shape. Compare before and after, using safe adult-approved materials.

Common mistakes and quick fixes
  • Forgetting direction — Use an arrow to show where the force acts.
  • Only saying move — Forces can also change shape, not only position.
  • Unfair comparison — Keep the object and surface the same when comparing strength.
