YEAR 1 SCIENCE · AC9S1U01

What do living things need?

Identify the needs of plants and animals. Explain how the place they live helps meet those needs.

More examples ↓Help with gaps ↓10-minute activity ↓

Learning intention: Identify the needs of plants and animals. Explain how the place they live helps meet those needs.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Water is one need, not every need

A potted plant near a window has access to light and air. Its roots take up water. A plant needs suitable growing conditions; watering alone does not replace light. Animals need suitable food, water and air, and places that protect them.

Living thingNeedWhat the place provides
Potted plantLightA suitable bright position
Garden birdWaterA clean water source
Garden birdShelterSuitable protected places

Learn the skill

1. Name the need

Start with what the living thing needs, rather than an object you would like to give it.

AnimalPlant
Air, water, suitable food and shelterAir, water, light and suitable growing conditions
Try it · Does a toy meet a rabbit’s need for water?

No. A toy may provide activity, but the rabbit still needs suitable drinking water.

2. Match a place to a need

Look for a feature that supplies a need. A birdbath may supply water; leafy cover may provide shelter.

Need → feature

Water → water source. Shelter → a suitable protected place.

Try it · Does shelter also provide every animal with food?

No. Check each need separately and consider the particular animal.

3. Compare and care

Living things share needs, but the amount of water and suitable food or shelter can differ. Observe and ask an adult how to care for them.

Two plants, different care

A cactus and a fern both need water. They may need different amounts and conditions.

Try it · Must every animal eat seeds?

No. Different animals need different foods. Never feed a wild animal just to test an idea.

Common mistakes

“Soil is a plant’s food.” Plants make their own food using light. Roots take up water and nutrients; soil can also support the plant.

“Water is all it needs.” Check all the needs. Do not remove a plant’s or animal’s necessities to test this.

10-MINUTE ACTIVITY

Build a needs map

2 minutes: choose a familiar plant or animal with an adult. 5 minutes: draw its home and label three features that help meet its needs. 3 minutes: explain each need → feature link.

Adjust the challenge Support: use picture choices. Extend: compare two living things and explain one difference in their needs.

Observe without disturbing wildlife. Follow an adult’s care guidance.

Three tricky questions

1. A pet has a roof over its enclosure. Have all its needs been met?

No. Shelter alone does not supply air, water and suitable food.

2. Two plants need different amounts of water. Does only one need water?

No. Both need water; their suitable conditions differ.

3. Can a rock provide shelter for a small animal?

It can provide a protected space for some animals. Observe without lifting or moving its shelter.

FAQ

Do plants eat soil?

No. Plants make their own food using light. Soil may supply water, nutrients and support.

What is a habitat?

The place a living thing lives, with features that can meet its needs.

More teaching examples and curriculum coverage

Additional explanations and guided checks for AC9S1U01.

1. Identify an animal's needs

Animals need air, water and food; suitable shelter protects them. A rabbit drinking meets its water need, while a bush may provide shelter.

Try it: Does drinking water also provide food?

See answer and explanation

No. Different needs must each be met.

2. Identify a plant's needs

Plants need water, air, light and room to grow. They make their own food; soil is not food and water does not replace light.

Try it: A watered plant is kept in a dark cupboard. What need is missing?

See answer and explanation

Light. Water alone cannot meet every need.

3. Connect needs to a place

A habitat is the place a living thing lives. A leafy pond edge may give a frog damp shelter and access to suitable food.

Try it: Is a bucket of water enough to meet every frog need?

See answer and explanation

No. Check food, air and suitable shelter as well as water.

4. Compare different living things

Needs are shared, but suitable food and shelter differ. A caterpillar eating leaves and a bird eating seeds use different food sources.

Try it: Should every animal be given the same food?

See answer and explanation

No. Food must suit the particular animal.

5. Use observations to care safely

Look without disturbing animals or damaging plants. With an adult, notice whether a known plant has suitable water and light; do not test needs by harming it.

Try it: What could you do if its soil seems dry?

See answer and explanation

Tell an adult and check the plant's care needs. Do not withhold necessary care for an experiment.

Common mistakes and quick fixes
  • Only naming the place — Explain which need the place provides.
  • One need is enough — Check several needs, not only food or water.
  • Forgetting plants — Plants also have needs: air, water and light.
