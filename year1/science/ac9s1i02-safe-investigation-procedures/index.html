YEAR 1 SCIENCE · AC9S1I02

Plan a safe investigation

Suggest and follow safe steps that help investigate a question and test a prediction.

More examples ↓Help with gaps ↓10-minute activity ↓

Learning intention: Suggest and follow safe steps that help investigate a question and test a prediction.

WORKED EXAMPLE

A safe plan for the toy-car question

Question: does the car travel farther on the board or rug? With an adult, choose a clear floor-level space. Use the same car and start mark. Try a similar gentle push on each surface, observe and record, then pack away.

OrderProcedureWhy
1Adult checks space and equipmentAvoid hazards
2Try the agreed comparisonTest the question
3Record, then pack awayKeep evidence and clear the space

Illustration for discussion. Any example measurements are supplied in the table, not measured from the artwork.

Learn the skill

1. Connect steps to the question

Choose actions that can provide the observation you need. Predict before testing.

We need to observe…

How far this car travels on each surface, not which colour children prefer.

Try it · Would measuring the car's colour test which surface lets it travel farther?

No. The plan needs observations of distance travelled.

2. Check safety first

An adult checks equipment and space. Stop and tell an adult if anything breaks or spills.

HazardSafer procedure
Objects near an edgeWork at floor level
People in the pathClear the test space
Broken toyStop and tell an adult
Try it · Should you continue with a broken toy because the test is nearly finished?

No. Stop and tell an adult.

3. Follow, record and reflect

Follow agreed steps in order. Keep the comparison useful, record results and explain a possible improvement.

Keep similar

Same car, starting place and push approach. Change the surface being compared.

Try it · Why use the same car in both trials?

It helps us compare the surfaces without also changing the car.

Common mistakes

“Safe steps can come after the test.” Check the plan and hazards before starting.

“Any safe activity answers the question.” The steps must also produce relevant evidence.

10-MINUTE ACTIVITY

Build a three-step plan

2 minutes: an adult reads the question with you. 5 minutes: draw a safe setup and arrange prepare → try → record cards. 3 minutes: explain one safety choice and one step that answers the question.

Adjust the challenge Support: order picture cards. Extend: identify a change that would make the comparison less useful.

Do not investigate heat, sharp tools, electricity or unknown substances in this activity.

Three tricky questions

1. The toy is safe, but a person is sitting in its path. Is the setup ready?

No. The space needs to be clear too.

2. Can a drawing help someone follow a procedure?

Yes, if it shows the equipment and steps clearly.

3. A spill occurs during the test. Should you record first?

Stop and tell an adult. Make the area safe before continuing.

FAQ

What is a procedure?

A clear set of steps to follow.

Can children suggest changes?

Yes. Discuss them with an adult and check safety before trying them.

Additional explanations and guided checks for AC9S1I02.

1. Connect the procedure to the question

To compare how a toy rolls on two surfaces, plan a guided test on those surfaces. A useful procedure answers the actual question.

Try it: Would changing the toy's colour test rolling distance on different surfaces?

See answer and explanation

No. Choose steps that collect evidence about distance and surface.

2. Check the safe setup

An adult approves the materials, clears the rolling space and explains the steps. Follow instructions; stop if conditions become unsafe.

Try it: What should you do if equipment breaks?

See answer and explanation

Stop, move away if needed and tell the adult. Do not continue using it.

3. Follow steps in order

Set up, predict, release, observe, record and pack away. Record the result after observing it, not before.

Try it: Why should you prepare a record before starting?

See answer and explanation

It helps capture each result clearly while leaving the result blank until observed.

4. Keep the comparison useful

Use the same toy and release point when comparing surfaces. An adult helps decide what to change and what to keep the same.

Try it: If both surface and starting height change, is the comparison clear?

See answer and explanation

No. You cannot tell which change affected the result.

5. Pack away and explain

Return safe equipment as directed, clear spills with adult help and explain how the steps tested your prediction.

Try it: Does wearing goggles replace following instructions?

See answer and explanation

No. Equipment supports safety; careful behaviour and adult guidance are still needed.

Common mistakes and quick fixes
  • Starting before safety — Read or suggest safety steps before touching equipment.
  • Steps out of order — Put the procedure in sequence before testing.
  • Procedure does not test prediction — Match each step to the question being investigated.
