Making and Recording Observations

Scientists record what they observe so evidence is not forgotten. Year 1 students can use drawings, words, counts, informal units or digital tools.

What students learn

Scientists record what they observe so evidence is not forgotten. Year 1 students can use drawings, words, counts, informal units or digital tools.

Learning routine: Observe → Measure informally → Record → Check detail → Share

Success looks like

  • Observe carefully
  • Record evidence
  • Use informal measurements
  • Choose record type
  • Share details
Concept model and worked application

Record observations in a useful way

drawingwordstallyblocksphoto

Informal measurement can use blocks, hand spans, steps or other equal units.

Worked example

Observation: The toy car rolled 8 hand spans. Record: car A — 8 hand spans — smooth floor. This gives more evidence than only writing “it went far”.

Choose a record that matches the observation

height → blockssound → wordsnumber → tallychange → before/after

The record should help someone else understand what was observed.

Explain your thinking: Say what each part of the model represents, then explain why the answer or conclusion follows.

Common misconceptions and traps
  • Memory only — Write, draw or count during the investigation.
  • Unequal units — Use similar informal units end-to-end or consistently.
  • Record lacks detail — Include what changed, how much, or what was noticed.
