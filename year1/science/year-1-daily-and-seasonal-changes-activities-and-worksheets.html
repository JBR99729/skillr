About This Year 1 Science Topic

Weather and seasonal patterns create changes in the environment that people can observe over time. These changes may include temperature, rainfall, wind, cloud cover, daylight, plant growth, flowering, fruiting and leaf colour.

In this topic, students make and record observations of daily weather and seasonal changes in local environments.

They explore how weather indicators and seasonal patterns help people plan activities, select suitable clothing and make decisions in everyday life.

Students investigate how changing weather and seasons affect plants, animals and humans, including animal behaviours such as hibernation and migration.

They also recognise the extensive knowledge held by First Nations Australians about weather patterns, seasonal changes, landscapes and concepts of time.

These activities support the Australian Curriculum Version 9.0 content descriptor AC9S1U02 and its associated elaborations.

Learning Intentions

Students will learn to:

  • identify common types of weather;
  • observe and record daily weather conditions;
  • identify changes that occur across seasons;
  • describe seasonal changes in plants;
  • describe how weather affects animals and humans;
  • explain how seasonal changes affect living things;
  • identify examples of hibernation and migration;
  • use weather information to plan activities;
  • choose suitable clothing for different weather;
  • compare daily weather and seasonal patterns;
  • recognise First Nations knowledge of weather and seasons;
  • communicate observations using drawings, charts and sentences.

Teacher Guide

Recommended Materials

  • daily weather chart;
  • weather symbol cards;
  • outdoor thermometer;
  • rain gauge;
  • wind sock or simple wind indicator;
  • season picture cards;
  • photographs of local plants across different seasons;
  • animal migration and hibernation picture cards;
  • clothing and activity cards;
  • calendars and observation journals;
  • drawing materials and clipboards;
  • community-approved First Nations seasonal resources.

Suggested Teaching Sequence

  1. Introduce common weather conditions and weather symbols.
  2. Begin a daily class weather observation chart.
  3. Discuss how weather can change from day to day.
  4. Introduce the concept of seasonal change.
  5. Observe how local plants change over time.
  6. Investigate how animals respond to seasonal changes.
  7. Explore hibernation and migration.
  8. Discuss how weather affects people and daily routines.
  9. Match clothing and activities to weather conditions.
  10. Explore First Nations knowledge of weather and seasons.
  11. Compare observations and identify simple patterns.
  12. Assess learning through drawings, explanations and charts.

Teacher Questions

  • What is the weather like today?
  • What evidence supports your observation?
  • How is today’s weather different from yesterday’s?
  • What changes do you notice in this plant?
  • What usually happens during this season?
  • How might this weather affect an animal?
  • Why do some animals hibernate?
  • Why do some animals migrate?
  • How does the weather affect our plans?
  • What clothing would be suitable today?
  • What patterns can you see in our weather chart?
  • How can seasonal knowledge help people care for Country?

Common Misconceptions

Weather and seasons mean the same thing

Explain that weather describes short-term atmospheric conditions, while seasons are recurring periods of the year associated with broader environmental patterns.

Every day in one season has the same weather

Use daily observations to show that weather can vary within the same season.

All animals hibernate in winter

Explain that some animals hibernate, some migrate and others remain active but change their behaviour.

Plants stop changing outside spring

Observe that plants can grow, flower, fruit, lose leaves or become dormant at different times.

A weather forecast is always certain

Explain that a forecast is a prediction based on current information and may change.

Year 1 Earth and Space Sciences Activities

Activity 1: Daily Weather Watch

Students observe cloud cover, temperature, wind and rainfall at the same time each day.

They record the conditions using symbols, drawings or short sentences and compare observations across the week.

Curriculum connection: AC9S1U02_E1

Activity 2: Seasonal Plant Observation

Select a tree or garden area to observe regularly.

Students record changes such as leaf colour, falling leaves, flowers, fruit or new growth.

Curriculum connection: AC9S1U02_E1

Activity 3: Plan for the Weather

Present different weather forecasts and possible activities.

Students choose suitable activities and explain how the weather information helped them decide.

Curriculum connection: AC9S1U02_E2

Activity 4: Hibernation and Migration Sort

Provide pictures of animals that hibernate, migrate or remain active during seasonal changes.

Students sort the animals and describe how each behaviour helps the animal respond to environmental changes.

Curriculum connection: AC9S1U02_E3

Activity 5: How Weather Affects Living Things

Compare examples of hot, cold, wet, dry and windy weather.

Students explain possible effects on plants, animals and humans.

Curriculum connection: AC9S1U02_E4

Activity 6: Dress for the Forecast

Give students clothing cards and a selection of weather forecasts.

Students choose suitable clothing and justify each choice.

Curriculum connection: AC9S1U02_E5

Activity 7: Weather Pattern Graph

Use class weather observations to create a picture graph or tally chart.

Students identify the most common conditions and discuss simple patterns.

Curriculum connection: AC9S1U02_E1 and AC9S1U02_E2

Activity 8: First Nations Seasonal Knowledge

Explore an appropriate local or regional First Nations seasonal calendar using authoritative, community-approved sources.

Students identify connections among weather, plants, animals, landscapes and seasonal activities.

Curriculum connection: AC9S1U02_E6

Activity 9: Time, Weather and Country

Discuss how changes in plants, animal behaviour and weather can indicate that a particular time or season is approaching.

Students create a labelled sequence showing one example.

Curriculum connection: AC9S1U02_E7

Activity 10: Seasonal Change Poster

Students choose one season and create a poster showing typical weather, plant changes, animal responses, clothing and activities.

They present the poster and explain the connections.

Curriculum connection: AC9S1U02

Differentiation

Support

  • use familiar weather photographs and symbols;
  • focus on one weather feature at a time;
  • provide labelled picture cards;
  • use oral responses before written recording;
  • provide sentence starters and word banks;
  • complete class charts together;
  • use matching and sorting tasks;
  • provide partially completed observation tables;
  • allow students to draw instead of writing full sentences.

Extension

  • compare weather data across several weeks;
  • calculate simple totals from a weather chart;
  • compare two seasons in detail;
  • research an unfamiliar migrating or hibernating animal;
  • explain how a weather change could affect a food chain;
  • create and present a short weather forecast;
  • compare local seasons with another region;
  • investigate changes in daylight across the year;
  • design a seasonal activity plan using evidence.

  • describe changes in plants across seasons;
  • explain how weather affects plants, animals and humans;

  • identify a simple pattern in weather observations;
  • recognise First Nations knowledge of weather and seasons.

Home Learning Ideas

  • What clothing is suitable?
  • What activity could we plan?
  • How might this weather affect a plant?
  • How might it affect an animal?
  • What seasonal change can you see?
  • What pattern have you noticed this week?
