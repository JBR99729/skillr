About This Year 1 Science Inquiry Topic

Planning and conducting investigations helps young students turn questions and predictions into practical science experiences. In this unit, students learn that investigations need clear steps, safe behaviour and suitable equipment.

Students follow teacher-guided procedures, investigate how objects move when pushed or pulled, discuss different ways to explore science questions, and suggest sensible steps for setting up and packing away equipment.

The focus is on safe participation, following instructions, making simple choices, cooperating with others and building confidence with practical science.

Learning Intentions

Students will learn to:

  • suggest safe ways to carry out a science investigation;
  • follow teacher instructions promptly;
  • use age-appropriate science equipment safely;
  • identify when sun safety is required;
  • follow steps in a guided investigation;
  • investigate how objects move when pushed or pulled;
  • discuss different ways to answer a science question;
  • suggest sensible setup and pack-up steps;
  • work cooperatively during practical activities;
  • help care for equipment and the learning environment.

Teacher Guide

Recommended Materials

  • plastic safety goggles;
  • child-sized aprons;
  • sun hats and sunscreen for outdoor investigations;
  • toy cars, balls and lightweight objects;
  • ramps or sturdy boards;
  • smooth and textured surfaces;
  • hoops, cones or masking tape for testing areas;
  • picture cards showing investigation steps;
  • equipment tubs or labelled trays;
  • observation sheets and pencils.

Suggested Teaching Sequence

  1. Introduce the purpose of safe procedures in science.
  2. Discuss common classroom and outdoor safety rules.
  3. Model how to use age-appropriate equipment correctly.
  4. Practise following short instructions in order.
  5. Introduce a guided push-and-pull investigation.
  6. Ask students to predict what may happen.
  7. Complete the investigation using clear, numbered steps.
  8. Discuss other ways the question could be investigated.
  9. Model safe equipment setup and pack-up.
  10. Reflect on which procedures helped the investigation run safely.

Teacher Questions

  • What could we do to stay safe?
  • Which equipment do we need?
  • How should this equipment be used?
  • What should we do first?
  • Why is it important to follow the steps in order?
  • What happens when this object is pushed?
  • What happens when it is pulled?
  • How else could we investigate this question?
  • What should stay the same during the test?
  • How can we set up without blocking walkways?
  • Where should each item go when we finish?

Core Safety Expectations

Listen before touching equipment

Students should wait until instructions are complete and permission is given before handling science materials.

Use equipment only as demonstrated

Model the correct use of each item and provide only equipment suitable for Year 1 students.

Keep the investigation area clear

Bags, chairs and unused materials should be moved away from walkways and practical testing areas.

Apply outdoor safety procedures

For outdoor work, follow school sun-safety requirements and identify boundaries before students begin.

Report spills or damaged equipment

Students should stop, move away and tell the teacher rather than attempting to manage an unsafe situation independently.

Common Misconceptions

Safety equipment removes every risk

Explain that goggles and aprons help reduce risk, but students must still follow instructions and use equipment carefully.

The steps can be completed in any order

Show how changing the order may make a test unfair, confusing or unsafe.

Only the teacher is responsible for safety

Reinforce that every student contributes by listening, moving carefully, using equipment properly and reporting problems.

There is only one way to investigate a question

Encourage guided discussion about different materials, locations, methods and observations that could be used.

Year 1 Planning and Conducting Activities

Activity 1: Safe or Unsafe?

Show picture cards of students using equipment, working outdoors or moving around an investigation area. Students sort each example as safe or unsafe and explain their reasoning.

Curriculum connection: AC9S1I02_E1

Activity 2: Science Safety Equipment Match

Students match goggles, aprons, hats and other age-appropriate items to situations in which they may be needed.

Curriculum connection: AC9S1I02_E1

Activity 3: Follow the Investigation Steps

Provide illustrated step cards for a simple investigation. Students place the cards in a sensible order before following the procedure.

Curriculum connection: AC9S1I02

Activity 4: Guided Toy Car Push Investigation

Students follow teacher-guided steps to push a toy car along a marked path, observe its movement and compare what happens with different pushes.

Curriculum connection: AC9S1I02_E2

Activity 5: Push or Pull Sorting

Students test or observe familiar objects and classify each movement as mainly caused by a push, a pull or both.

Curriculum connection: AC9S1I02_E2

Activity 6: How Else Could We Test It?

Present a question such as “Which ball rolls farther?” Students discuss different safe ways the class could investigate it.

Curriculum connection: AC9S1I02_E3

Activity 7: Choose the Best Investigation Method

Students compare two or three teacher-provided investigation ideas and select the safest and clearest method, explaining their choice.

Curriculum connection: AC9S1I02_E3

Activity 8: Equipment Setup Sequence

Students organise picture cards showing how to collect, carry and arrange equipment while keeping the workspace safe.

Curriculum connection: AC9S1I02_E4

Activity 9: Pack-Up Challenge

Small groups follow a pack-up checklist to return equipment, clear surfaces, check the floor and report damaged or missing items.

Curriculum connection: AC9S1I02_E4

Activity 10: Investigation Reflection

Students reflect on which procedures they followed, how they stayed safe and what they would improve in the next investigation.

Curriculum connection: AC9S1I02

Differentiation

Support

  • use visual safety symbols and picture-based step cards;
  • give one instruction at a time;
  • model each action before students begin;
  • pair students with a supportive partner;
  • use equipment trays with clear labels;
  • provide oral response and drawing options;
  • repeat a familiar investigation before introducing a new one.

Extension

  • ask students to suggest an extra safety rule;
  • have students explain why each step is necessary;
  • invite students to compare two possible investigation methods;
  • ask which conditions should stay the same for a fair comparison;
  • have students design a simple setup diagram;
  • ask students to create a pack-up checklist for classmates.

Assessment Ideas

Observe whether the student can:

  • suggest at least one relevant safety procedure;
  • follow teacher instructions promptly;
  • use age-appropriate equipment correctly;
  • follow a short sequence of investigation steps;
  • participate in a guided push-and-pull investigation;
  • describe how an object moved;
  • suggest another way to investigate a question;
  • help set up equipment safely;
  • pack away materials responsibly;
  • work cooperatively with others.

Quick Exit Ticket

  1. Name one safety rule.
  2. State one step you followed during the investigation.
  3. Explain one thing you did when packing away.

Home Learning Ideas

Families can support early investigation skills through safe, supervised activities using familiar household objects.

  • plan the steps for testing which toy rolls farther;
  • sort household actions into pushes and pulls;
  • create a safe setup checklist before an activity;
  • discuss what equipment would be suitable;
  • follow a simple three-step procedure in order;
  • practise returning materials to their correct places;
  • talk about another safe way to test the same question.

All home investigations should use safe, age-appropriate materials and direct adult supervision.
