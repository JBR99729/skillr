AC9S1H01 • Year 1 Science

Science in Daily Life

People use science every day when they observe patterns, make predictions and choose safe or useful actions.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

People use science every day when they observe patterns, make predictions and choose safe or useful actions.

Learning routine: Notice problem → Observe pattern → Predict → Choose action → Explain

Success looks like

  • Identify daily science
  • Use patterns
  • Make predictions
  • Connect to actions
  • Explain usefulness
Concept model and worked application

Daily science uses patterns

dark cloudsrain likelytake umbrella

Observation plus pattern helps us predict and choose an action.

shadow movessun changespredict later shadow

Patterns help us predict what may happen next.

Worked example

Question: Why might someone take an umbrella after seeing dark clouds? They use the observed weather pattern to predict rain and choose a useful action.

Use a pattern to make a practical prediction

observationpatternpredictionaction

Name the pattern, say what you predict, then explain why it matters.

Explain your thinking: Say what each part of the model represents, then explain why the answer or conclusion follows.

Guided learning activities

Weather decision

Use signs in the sky or forecast symbols to choose what to wear or carry.

Model: clouds → rain? → umbrella

Shadow pattern

Observe shadows at two times and predict what may happen later.

Model: morning shadow ≠ afternoon shadow

Home science

List times people use science at home, school or outdoors.

Model: cook • clean • garden • travel

Common misconceptions and traps
  • Prediction without pattern — Point to the observation or repeated pattern first.
  • Science only in labs — Science is also used in cooking, weather choices, gardening and safety.
  • Guess equals prediction — A prediction uses evidence or experience.

Quick questions

  • Give one example of science in daily life.
  • How can weather patterns help people?
  • What makes a prediction scientific?
  • Why is observing useful before acting?

Return to the concept model if an answer is uncertain. Use the model to explain, not just to guess.
