Concept model and worked application

Separate daily changes from seasonal changes

morningmiddaynight

Daily changes happen within one day: light, shadows, temperature and routines.

summerautumnwinterspring

Seasonal changes happen across months: weather patterns, daylight, plant changes and activities.

Worked example

Question: Is sunrise to sunset a daily or seasonal change? It is daily because it happens within one day and changes light and routines.

Connect the change to an everyday response

rain → umbrellahot day → hatwinter → coatdark → light

Explain both the environmental change and what people do because of it.

Explain your thinking: Say what each part of the model represents, then explain why the answer or conclusion follows.

Separate today's weather from a seasonal pattern

Describe environmental changes and connect them to everyday choices.

Worked example

Yesterday was rainy and today is sunny. Does that alone show that the season has changed?

  1. Describe what was observed on each day without guessing a whole season.
  2. Daily weather can change within the same season. Seasonal patterns are noticed over a longer time.
  3. Use today's observations to choose suitable clothing or an activity, and compare many days to discuss a seasonal pattern.

Watch for this mistake: One hot day is not enough to establish a seasonal change. Seasonal patterns also differ between places.

Try it, then explain

  1. Rain is falling before a walk. Name one sensible change to the plan and why.

    Hint

    Connect the change to the observed weather.

    Answer and explanation

    For example, wear a raincoat or use a sheltered route to stay dry.

  2. Which takes longer to observe: clouds moving this afternoon or a tree losing leaves over several weeks?

    Hint

    Compare the times stated.

    Answer and explanation

    The tree's change over several weeks.

Australian Curriculum elaborations

These curriculum examples extend the central model into varied contexts. They are learning contexts, not extra definitions to memorise.

E1

making and recording observations of phenomena such as changes to weather, seasonal changes to plants such as colour or dropping of leaves, and growth of flowers or fruit

Try and explain: A class photographs the same tree each week. Leaves gradually change colour and fall. What pattern could the record reveal?

Answer and teaching point

It can show a change over several weeks. Compare dated observations of the same tree rather than treating one fallen leaf as a whole seasonal pattern.

E2

noticing how daily weather indicators and seasonal patterns help us to make plans for activities in our daily lives

Try and explain: Clouds darken before an outdoor game. How could that observation help with planning?

Answer and teaching point

It gives a reason to check the weather and consider shelter or a changed plan. Dark clouds suggest possible rain but do not guarantee it.

E3

investigating how seasonal changes affect plants and animals, including animals that hibernate and migrate

Try and explain: Records show some birds leave an area at a similar time each year. Is that more likely a daily or seasonal pattern?

Answer and teaching point

A seasonal pattern, because the change recurs across the year rather than within a single day. Not all bird species migrate.

E4

investigating how changes in the weather affect plants and animals, including humans

Try and explain: A plant's soil becomes dry after several hot days. Name an observation and a possible response.

Answer and teaching point

Dry soil is the observation. Check the plant's watering needs and provide suitable care; do not assume every plant needs the same amount of water.

E5

exploring how people make clothing choices using predictions of weather or knowledge of seasonal changes

Try and explain: A forecast predicts a cold, wet morning. Explain a clothing choice using that information.

Answer and teaching point

A warm layer and rain protection could help keep a person warm and dry. The reason should connect the clothing to the predicted conditions.

E6

recognising the extensive knowledges of daily and seasonal changes in weather patterns and landscape held by First Nations Australians

Common misconceptions and traps
  • Weather equals season — Weather can change daily; seasons are longer patterns.
  • Only naming the season — Describe what changes and how it affects life.
  • Forgetting daily changes — Light, shadows and temperature can change within one day.
