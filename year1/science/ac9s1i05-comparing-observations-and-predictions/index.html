Comparing Observations and Predictions

After an investigation, scientists compare what they predicted with what they observed, listen to others’ observations and consider whether the investigation was fair.

What students learn

After an investigation, scientists compare what they predicted with what they observed, listen to others’ observations and consider whether the investigation was fair.

Learning routine: Read prediction → Compare observation → Check fairness → Ask next question

Success looks like

  • Compare prediction and observation
  • Listen to others’ observations
  • Consider fairness
  • Identify next questions
  • Use evidence
Concept model and worked application

Prediction and observation may match or differ

predictionobservationsame?different?

A different result is not failure. It gives evidence and can lead to a new question.

Worked example

Prediction: the big ball will roll farther. Observation: the small ball rolled farther. Comparison: my prediction was different, so I need to look at the evidence and ask why.

Fair investigations change only what is being tested

same objectsame surfacesame startchange one thing

Fairness helps observations answer the question properly.

Explain your thinking: Say what each part of the model represents, then explain why the answer or conclusion follows.

Guided learning activities

Prediction check

Compare a prediction with what happened and label same or different.

Model: predicted / observed

Fair or not fair

Judge simple investigation examples and explain what should stay the same.

Model: same ramp? same car?

Next question

Use an unexpected result to ask a new question.

Model: What could we test next?

Common misconceptions and traps
  • Wrong prediction means bad science — A different result can still teach something.
  • Ignoring other observations — Compare your evidence with others’ evidence.
  • Unfair test accepted — Check what changed and what stayed the same.
