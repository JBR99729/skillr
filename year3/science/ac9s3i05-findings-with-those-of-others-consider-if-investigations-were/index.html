YEAR 3 SCIENCE · AC9S3I05

Compare findings and draw a fair conclusion

Learning intention: Compare your findings with others, review fairness, ask further questions and draw a conclusion supported by evidence.

More examples ↓Help with gaps ↓10-minute activity ↓

WORKED EXAMPLE

Different readings can show the same pattern

Two groups compare a fabric sleeve with no sleeve. Each uses matching cups, 100 mL of water at 36°C, and a ten-minute test in a similar room. Their supplied final temperatures differ slightly, but both find less cooling with the sleeve. Compare the pattern and the procedure before deciding why readings differ.

GroupWith sleeve, after 10 minNo sleeve, after 10 min
A33°C28°C
B32°C27°C

Artwork provides context. Read the supplied records for exact counts and measurements; do not measure the illustration.

Learn the skill

1. Compare the findings, not just one number

Group A records 33°C versus 28°C. Group B records 32°C versus 27°C. Both have a 5°C difference between their two final readings. Since all cups started at 36°C, both comparisons show less cooling with the sleeve.

GroupTemperature decrease: sleeveTemperature decrease: no sleeve
A3°C8°C
B4°C9°C
Try it · Must one group be wrong because its final readings differ from the other group’s?

No. The groups show the same pattern. Method differences, measurement and real variation should be considered.

2. Review whether the comparison was fair

Check the changed factor, measured outcome and conditions kept similar. If a proposed repeat puts only the bare cup in front of a fan, airflow changes as well as the sleeve condition.

Fairness checkWhy it matters
Same starting temperature and volumeGives comparable starting conditions
Same cup type, place and timingReduces other differences
Sleeve condition is the intended changeHelps answer the sleeve question
Try it · Can a fan aimed at only one cup make the sleeve comparison harder to interpret?

Yes. Airflow could affect cooling too, so the two conditions no longer differ only in the intended way.

3. Draw a limited conclusion and ask next

Use the evidence to answer the original question, then identify what remains unknown. A conclusion is different from a new question.

Conclusion and next question

Conclusion: the fabric sleeve reduced cooling in these two comparisons. Further question: would another sleeve material reduce cooling by a different amount under the same conditions?

Try it · Do these results prove this sleeve keeps water warm forever?

No. They describe ten-minute comparisons under the recorded conditions.

Common mistakes

“Different findings mean somebody is careless.” First compare methods, instruments and conditions; keep all honest observations.

“A conclusion is any interesting new idea.” A conclusion answers the investigated question using evidence. Label further questions separately.

10-MINUTE ACTIVITY

Compare, check, conclude

2 minutes: read both groups’ records. 5 minutes: describe the shared pattern and explain one fairness check. 3 minutes: write a supported conclusion and one connected further question.

Adjust the challenge Support: use a same/different comparison frame. Extend: explain how changing both airflow and sleeve condition could lead to an unsupported conclusion.

Use supplied data. Any real comparison needs adult-prepared comfortably warm water, stable cups and suitable instruments. Experimental water is not for drinking.

Three tricky questions

1. A repeated result differs by 1°C. Should it be erased?

No. Keep the reading and record any genuine procedure issue.

2. Can the two groups support a similar conclusion even with different exact readings?

Yes. Compare the overall finding and the conditions, not only whether every number matches.

3. Is “What colour should our poster be?” the strongest further investigation question about cooling?

No. A question about material, thickness or conditions connects more directly to the cooling evidence.

FAQ

What belongs in a conclusion?

An answer to the original question, supported by the recorded evidence and limited to what was investigated.

What is a useful further question?

A connected question prompted by observations, differences in findings or something the current investigation did not test.

What students learn in AC9S3I05

Students learn to compare findings with those of others, consider if investigations were fair, identify questions for further investigation and draw conclusions. They use the visual model, precise vocabulary and evidence to explain and apply the concept.

Learning routine: Observe → Model → Investigate → Record → Explain

Success looks like

  • Recognise finding
  • Use fair test
  • Explain conclusion
  • Apply in a new context
  • Justify with evidence

Common misconceptions
  • Different result means someone is wrong — Chance variation or method differences may explain it.
  • Feature named without effect — Explain how the feature changes meaning or outcome.
  • One example treated as a rule — Test the idea with another example.
