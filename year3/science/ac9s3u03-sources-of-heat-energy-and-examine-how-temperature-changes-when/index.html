YEAR 3 SCIENCE · AC9S3U03

Follow the heat, read the temperature

Learning intention: Identify heat sources. Use temperature changes to explain heat-energy transfer between objects.

WORKED EXAMPLE

A cool spoon in warmer water

An adult places a cooler spoon in warmer water. In the supplied example, the spoon’s temperature rises while the water’s temperature falls. Heat energy transfers from the warmer water towards the cooler spoon. The numbers describe this example, not measurements from the illustration.

RecordWater temperatureSpoon temperature
Before contact36°C20°C
Later33°C29°C

Artwork provides context. Read the supplied records for exact counts and measurements; do not measure the illustration.

Learn the skill

1. Identify a heat source

The Sun, a fire and operating heating devices can supply heat energy. Friction can also warm objects. Identifying a source does not make it safe to touch.

ExampleHeat-energy source or transfer
Sunlight warms a surfaceEnergy arrives from the Sun
An operating heater warms nearby objectsElectrical energy supplies heating
Hands rubbed togetherRubbing can warm them
Try it · Is a blanket normally a heater that makes its own heat?

No. It mainly reduces heat transfer between the body and surroundings.

2. Track the transfer direction

For objects at different temperatures, heat energy transfers from warmer to cooler. In the example, the water loses energy while the cooler spoon gains some.

Direction in this example

Water at 36°C → heat energy → spoon initially at 20°C.

Try it · Did “cold” flow out of the spoon and make the water cooler?

No. Explain the transfer of heat energy from the warmer water.

3. Use a thermometer as evidence

A thermometer measures temperature, commonly in degrees Celsius. Temperature and heat energy are different ideas. Read both objects’ records and keep the units.

Measured changeInterpretation
Spoon: 20°C to 29°CIt became warmer
Water: 36°C to 33°CIt became cooler
Try it · Must touching objects reach the same temperature immediately?

No. Temperature changes can take time, and surroundings may also affect them.

Common mistakes

“Heat and temperature are identical.” Temperature describes how hot or cold something is; heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference.

“Cold energy moves into the warmer object.” Use warmer-to-cooler heat transfer to explain the change.

10-MINUTE ACTIVITY

Explain both temperature changes

2 minutes: read the supplied table. 5 minutes: draw and label a heat-transfer arrow, then describe what happened to each object. 3 minutes: explain which measurements support your account.

Adjust the challenge Support: compare only the starting temperatures first. Extend: explain why a later measurement might be useful before claiming the objects have reached the same temperature.

Use supplied data or teacher-prepared comfortably warm water. An adult handles equipment and checks temperature; never touch hot surfaces or use boiling water.

Three tricky questions

1. The spoon warmed. Does the water have to stay at its original temperature?

No. The water may cool as it transfers energy to the spoon and surroundings.

2. A metal and wooden object are in the same room. Does feeling different prove different temperatures?

No. Materials transfer heat differently; use suitable temperature measurements.

3. Can an insulating sleeve stop every bit of heat transfer forever?

No. It can reduce heat transfer; it does not provide perfect permanent insulation.

FAQ

What does °C mean?

Degrees Celsius, a unit used to report temperature.

Does this lesson require heat-energy calculations?

No. Use source, transfer direction and observed temperature changes to explain the examples.

What students learn in AC9S3U03

Students learn to identify sources of heat energy and examine how temperature changes when heat energy is transferred from one object to another. They use the visual model, precise vocabulary and evidence to explain and apply the concept.

Learning routine: Observe → Model → Investigate → Record → Explain

Success looks like

  • Recognise heat energy
  • Use temperature
  • Explain transfer
  • Apply in a new context
  • Justify with evidence

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

The content description and elaborations below show the curriculum ideas taught in this unit. Items marked as teaching context support lesson planning.

  • Content description: identify sources of heat energy and examine how temperature changes when heat energy is transferred from one object to another
  • E1: exploring how we sense heat and identifying sources of heat such as the sun, fire, electrical devices and geothermal springs
  • E2: using a thermometer to record temperature changes as evidence when heat energy is transferred
  • E3: observing and, with assistance, measuring, what happens when a cold object is placed in direct contact with a warm object and proposing explanations
  • E4: modelling the movement of heat from one object to another using drawing or role-play
  • E5: investigating how well heat is transferred by different types of materials such as metals, plastics and ceramics and identifying how materials are used to keep things hot and cold
  • E6: exploring how First Nations Australians developed clothing from animal skins such as possum furs and kangaroo skin cloaks that trap heat close to the body to stay warm (teaching context)
