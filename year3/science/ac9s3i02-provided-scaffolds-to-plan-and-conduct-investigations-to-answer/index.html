YEAR 3 SCIENCE · AC9S3I02

Use a scaffold to plan a fair test

Learning intention: Use a provided plan to investigate a question or prediction. Identify fair-test features and safe procedures.

More examples ↓Help with gaps ↓10-minute activity ↓

WORKED EXAMPLE

Plan before comparing cup sleeves

Question: does adding a fabric sleeve reduce cooling over ten minutes? Use the scaffold below before testing. Change the sleeve condition, measure temperature change, and keep the water volume, starting temperature, cup type, place and timing similar.

Planning promptExample plan
ChangeSleeve or no sleeve
MeasureWater temperature at start and after 10 min
Keep similarCup, 100 mL water, 36°C start, room and timing
SafetyAdult-prepared comfortably warm water; stable cups
Repeat and recordRepeat each condition; keep all readings

Artwork provides context. Read the supplied records for exact counts and measurements; do not measure the illustration.

Learn the skill

1. Identify what changes and what is measured

The changed factor is deliberately varied. The measured outcome is the observation used to answer the question.

RoleIn this investigation
ChangeWhether there is a sleeve
MeasureTemperature change over the same time
Try it · Should the water volume also change just because a sleeve is added?

No. That would make the sleeve comparison harder to interpret.

2. Plan the sequence and safety

Agree on the steps, equipment and response to hazards before starting. Follow the adult-approved procedure.

Procedure scaffold

Prepare matching cups → check starting conditions → start together → measure at agreed times → record → clean up.

Try it · A spill happens near the equipment. Should recording come first?

No. Stop, tell the adult and make the area safe.

3. Conduct, repeat and keep the record

Use the same procedure for repeats. Repeats help reveal variation; they do not guarantee identical results or permit unwanted results to be deleted.

Trial issueHonest response
A reading differsKeep it and check procedure
Timing was missedRecord the problem rather than inventing a value
Try it · Should a trial be erased because it does not support the prediction?

No. Keep the observation and describe any genuine method problem.

Common mistakes

“A fair test changes several important conditions together.” Change the intended factor while keeping relevant conditions similar.

“Safety is only checked after something goes wrong.” Plan safety first and keep checking throughout.

10-MINUTE ACTIVITY

Complete the planning scaffold

2 minutes: read the question. 5 minutes: fill in change, measure, keep similar, equipment and safety boxes. 3 minutes: ask a partner to identify one missing step before the plan is used.

Adjust the challenge Support: use provided choices for each box. Extend: explain how a poor control could produce a misleading result.

An adult prepares and checks comfortably warm water and stable equipment. No boiling water, mains electrical experiments or unsupervised heating.

Three tricky questions

1. Both cups have sleeves, but different water volumes. Does that test sleeve versus no sleeve?

No. The intended comparison is missing, and another condition changes.

2. Do three repeats guarantee the prediction will be supported?

No. Repeats help evaluate evidence; they do not choose the result.

3. Can a drawing be part of the planning scaffold?

Yes. Label equipment and positions, and keep the steps clear.

FAQ

What is a control condition here?

A relevant condition kept similar, such as the starting water volume.

Is taking turns the whole meaning of a fair test?

No. Taking turns is fair behaviour; scientific fairness also concerns the comparison and its conditions.

What students learn in AC9S3I02

Students learn to use provided scaffolds to plan and conduct investigations to answer questions or test predictions, including identifying the elements of fair tests, and considering the safe use of materials and equipment. They use the visual model, precise vocabulary and evidence to explain and apply the concept.

Learning routine: Observe → Model → Investigate → Record → Explain

Success looks like

  • Recognise changed variable
  • Use measured variable
  • Explain controlled variable
  • Apply in a new context
  • Justify with evidence

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

The content description and elaborations below show the curriculum ideas taught in this unit. Items marked as teaching context support lesson planning.

  • Content description: use provided scaffolds to plan and conduct investigations to answer questions or test predictions, including identifying the elements of fair tests, and considering the safe use of materials and equipment
  • E1: collaboratively identifying and ordering the steps in an investigation
  • E2: using a provided framework or graphic organiser to plan and identify what to change, what to keep the same and what to measure to make a test fair
  • E3: examining an example of a soil profile after soil has settled in water and planning an investigation to compare and contrast the components and particle sizes of different soils
  • E4: planning an investigation to determine which material is the best to keep substances cold
  • E5: discussing safety rules to follow when conducting investigations, such as following teacher instructions, manipulating equipment and materials with care and wearing appropriate personal safety gear, such as gloves, safety goggles and face masks when handling soils
  • E6: consulting with First Nations Australians to guide the planning of scientific investigations, including safety considerations for field investigations (teaching context)

Common misconceptions
  • Several factors changed — Change one tested factor at a time.
  • Feature named without effect — Explain how the feature changes meaning or outcome.
  • One example treated as a rule — Test the idea with another example.
