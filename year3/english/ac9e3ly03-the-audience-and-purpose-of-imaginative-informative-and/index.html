AC9E3LY03: Identify the audience and purpose of imaginative, informative and persuasive texts through their use of language features and/or images

identify the audience and purpose of imaginative, informative and persuasive texts through their use of language features and/or images

Topic guideWorksheetPracticeTest

1. Outcome Overview & Core Concepts

Students learn to identify the audience and purpose of imaginative, informative and persuasive texts through their use of language features and/or images. They connect a text or language feature to meaning and explain why a writer, speaker or text uses it. Key concepts include audience, purpose, language, image. Success requires a specific example or evidence.

Learning target: identify the audience and purpose of imaginative, informative and persuasive texts through their use of language features and/or images

2. Subject-Specific Content & Key Examples

Framing

A close-up can make an expression dominant; a wide shot can emphasise setting or isolation. Explain what the viewer is directed to notice.

Angle and power

A low camera angle can make a subject appear powerful, while a high angle may make the same subject appear vulnerable.

Salience

Size, placement, contrast and empty space guide attention. Identify the most salient element and explain how it shapes representation.

Modes work together

In video, image, spoken words, music and captions can reinforce or complicate one another. Analyse the combined effect.

3. Common Student Misconceptions

  • Feature spotting: Naming a camera angle, colour or sound is not analysis; explain its effect.
  • One fixed effect: Interpret the feature in context and alongside other modes.
  • Separate-mode lists: Explain how image, words and sound reinforce, contradict or qualify one another.
  • Retelling: Analyse how the representation was constructed, not only what it shows.

4. 4-Step Instructional Sequence

Step 1: Explicit Teaching & Modelling

Define the outcome-specific metalanguage (audience, purpose, language) and model: identify the feature, select evidence, explain its effect or purpose.

Step 2: Guided Analysis

Analyse a second example together: What is the choice? Where is the evidence? What does it do here?

Step 3: Collaborative / Independent Practice

Students annotate or compare a new example, then apply the concept independently in analysis, editing, speaking or composition.

Step 4: Formative Assessment

Use an unseen micro-text or creation task. Require precise evidence plus a justified explanation or deliberate crafting choice.

Curriculum Coverage & Elaborations

The content description is the required target. The elaborations below are taught as examples and contexts, not as disconnected checklist wording.

  • E1: identifying words, phrases and images intended to persuade listeners, viewers or readers
  • E2: identifying features of advertisements that target children
  • E3: identifying the purpose of an imaginative text; for example, identifying the purpose of a fable
