Mathematical Modelling in Practical Problems

What students learn

Mathematical modelling turns a practical situation into a diagram, table or number sentence, then connects the solution back to the context. This matters because three $4 notebooks cost $12 and five $2 pencils cost $10; the total is $22.

Some situations require more than one operation. Use this idea when a problem asks you to choose operations from the situation, and explain the result with mathematical model, strategy, interpret.

Learning intention: We are learning to use mathematical modelling to solve practical problems involving additive and multiplicative situations including financial contexts; formulate problems using number sentences and choose calculation strategies, using digital tools where appropriate; interpret and communicate solutions in terms of the situation.

Success criteria

  • I can represent situations.
  • I can formulate number sentences.
  • I can use multiple operations.
Key vocabulary
  • mathematical model — a diagram, number sentence or representation of a situation
  • strategy — a chosen method for solving a problem
  • interpret — explain what an answer means in its situation

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Using every number — Choose numbers and operations that match the question.
  • Stopping at a number — Interpret what the result means in context.
  • No check — Use estimation, inverse operations or a second representation.
Teaching sequence and quick check

Quick check: Model 4 packs of 6 shared among 3 children.

Expected response: Three notebooks at $4 each cost $12.

If incorrect: Choose numbers and operations that match the question.
