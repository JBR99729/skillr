AC9M3N06 • Year 3 Maths

Mathematical Modelling for Practical Problems

Understand a practical problem, represent the quantities, choose operations, solve, check the result and communicate what the answer means in context.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

Students use a modelling cycle: understand, represent, choose a strategy, calculate, check and communicate. They decide whether addition, subtraction, multiplication or division matches the structure of a practical problem.

Worked examples and teaching sequence
  1. Identify what is known and unknown.
  2. Represent the situation with a diagram, table or number sentence.
  3. Choose an operation from the problem structure.
  4. Calculate using an efficient strategy.
  5. Check reasonableness and interpret the answer in context.
Common misconceptions and quick check
  • Choosing an operation from a keyword alone.
  • Giving a numerical answer without its contextual meaning.
  • Failing to check whether a remainder changes the practical decision.

Quick check: A class packs 38 items equally into boxes of 6. Model the situation and explain what the remainder means.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Contexts include money, sharing, grouping, measurement and everyday planning. Students compare representations, justify operation choices, test solutions and communicate decisions based on the model.
