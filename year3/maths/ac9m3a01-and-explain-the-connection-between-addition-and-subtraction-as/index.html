AC9M3A01: Recognise and explain the connection between addition and subtraction as inverse operations, apply to partition numbers and find unknown values in number sentences

Learning goals: what you will learn

Addition and subtraction undo each other. A whole and two parts create related number sentences that can solve unknown values.

Learning routine: Identify whole and parts → Write related facts → Find unknown → Check with inverse

Success looks like

  • Identify whole and parts
  • Write fact families
  • Solve unknowns
  • Use inverse checks
  • Explain connections

Success criteria:

Prerequisite knowledge

Students should be comfortable with addition and subtraction facts to 20, simple equal groups and number sentences, and using symbols to represent an unknown or repeated relationship. Revisit a concrete or pictorial model first if the symbols are not yet meaningful.

Worked and modelled examples

Use a fact family for 37, 25 and 62

623725

The same whole and parts create two addition and two subtraction facts.

  1. Read every label and identify what each quantity or feature represents.
  2. Describe the relationship shown by the model.
  3. Check that the conclusion answers the original question.

Apply the same relationship to the new context, then justify the answer using the labels and evidence shown.

Common misconceptions and corrections
  • Reversing subtraction freely — Addition is commutative; subtraction usually is not.
  • Using the wrong whole — The largest amount is usually the whole in a fact family.
  • No inverse check — Substitute the unknown into the original sentence.

Independent practice

  1. Which fact is related to 37 + 25 = 62?

    • 62 − 37 = 25
    • 37 − 25 = 62
    • 62 + 25 = 37
    • 25 − 62 = 37

  2. Complete the unknown.

    Prompt: 47 + □ = 83

  3. What is the unknown in □ − 28 = 45?

    • 73
    • 17
    • 63
    • 83

  4. Write four related facts for 54, 36 and 90.

  5. Match the unknown sentence to its answer.

    Match: 29 + □ = 84 • □ − 18 = 42 • 96 − □ = 39 ↔ 57 • 60 • 55

  6. Complete the inverse check.

    Prompt: 96 − 57 = 39, and 39 + □ = 96.

Reasoning and problem-solving

  1. Create a fact-family puzzle with one hidden number and enough clues to find it.

  2. Solve 348 + □ = 725 using partitioning and an inverse operation. Explain each step.

Explain the reasoning, not only the final answer.

Assessment-style questions and review hints

  1. Explain why 84 − 29 can solve 29 + □ = 84.

  2. A student writes 62 − 25 = 37 and 25 − 62 = 37. Explain which fact is incorrect and why.

Teacher and parent guidance

For teachers

Model the idea with concrete or visual representations first, then ask students to explain the relationship in words before moving to symbolic work.

For parents and carers

Ask your child to show the idea with a drawing, objects or a simple example and explain how they checked the answer.

Read the topic guide and use the teacher slide for instruction. Students can then use the worksheet for written work, open Practice for supported feedback, or take the Test when they are ready.

Use reliable, community-approved sources and identify the specific Nation, People or community where the source does. Do not present diverse First Nations knowledges as one generic tradition.
