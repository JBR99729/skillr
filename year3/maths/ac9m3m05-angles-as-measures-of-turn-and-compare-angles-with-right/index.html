Angles and Turns

Recognise an angle as an amount of turn and compare angles as smaller than, equal to or larger than a right angle.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

Students identify turns in movement and shapes, use a right-angle benchmark and compare angle size independently of arm length or orientation.

Worked examples and teaching sequence
  1. Model quarter, half and full turns physically.
  2. Use a square corner as a right-angle benchmark.
  3. Overlay or compare openings.
  4. Describe each angle relative to a right angle.
Common misconceptions and quick check
  • Thinking longer arms make a larger angle.
  • Judging angle size by orientation.
  • Comparing shapes rather than the opening.

Quick check: Find three classroom angles and classify each relative to a right angle.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Contexts include body turns, corners, clock hands and shape features, with students constructing and comparing angles using a right-angle reference.
