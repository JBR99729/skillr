Angles as Measures of Turn

What students learn

An angle measures the amount of turn between two arms. A right angle is a useful benchmark for comparing smaller and larger turns. This matters because the length of the arms does not change the angle size.

Place or imagine a square corner at the vertex to compare. Use this idea when a problem asks you to find angles in everyday objects, and explain the result with angle, right angle, vertex.

Learning intention: We are learning to identify angles as measures of turn and compare angles with right angles in everyday situations.

Success criteria

  • I can identify vertices and arms.
  • I can recognise right angles.
  • I can compare smaller angles.
Key vocabulary
  • angle — a measure of turn between two directions
  • right angle — a quarter turn
  • vertex — the point where the two arms of an angle meet

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Longer arms mean bigger angle — Angle size depends on turn, not arm length.
  • Vertex ignored — The arms must meet at the vertex.
  • Shape size confused with angle — A small drawing can still show a large turn.
Teaching sequence and quick check

Quick check: Identify a right angle.

Expected response: A quarter turn is a right angle.

If incorrect: Angle size depends on turn, not arm length.
