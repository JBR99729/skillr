AC9M3N03 • Year 3 Maths

Add and Subtract with Place Value

Add and subtract two- and three-digit numbers by partitioning, rearranging and regrouping place-value parts, then estimate or use the inverse to check the result.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesWorksheetPracticeTest
What students learn in AC9M3N03

Students use place value to split numbers into hundreds, tens and ones, rearrange parts when useful and regroup equal values without changing the total. They choose efficient mental and written strategies rather than relying on a single procedure.

  • Partition two- and three-digit numbers into useful place-value parts.
  • Regroup 10 ones as 1 ten and 10 tens as 1 hundred, and reverse those exchanges when subtracting.
  • Add and subtract by place value, compensation or other efficient strategies.
  • Estimate before or after calculating to check reasonableness.
  • Use the inverse operation to check an answer.
Key vocabulary and models

Partition means split a number into useful parts. Regroup means rename an equal value, such as 10 ones as 1 ten. The inverse operation undoes another operation, so addition can check subtraction.

For 268 + 157, combine hundreds, tens and ones; 8 + 7 makes 15 ones, which can be regrouped as 1 ten and 5 ones.

Worked examples and teaching sequence
  1. Estimate: 268 + 157 is close to 270 + 160 = 430.
  2. Partition: 268 = 200 + 60 + 8 and 157 = 100 + 50 + 7.
  3. Regroup: 8 + 7 = 15 ones, then combine the new ten with the tens.
  4. Calculate: 300 + 110 + 15 = 425.
  5. Compensate: for 503 − 198, subtract 200 to get 303, then add 2 to get 305.
  6. Check: use addition to verify a subtraction result where appropriate.
Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Content description: add and subtract two- and three-digit numbers using place value to partition, rearrange and regroup numbers to assist in calculations without a calculator.

  • E1: use base-ten materials, diagrams and expanded notation to partition and regroup numbers while preserving value.
  • E2: compare strategies such as place-value partitioning and compensation and explain why both produce the same result.
  • E3: use inverse relationships between addition and subtraction to check calculations and missing-value problems.
  • E4: estimate totals and differences and use the estimate to judge whether an exact answer is reasonable.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Regrouping without equal exchange: trade exactly 10 ones for 1 ten or 10 tens for 1 hundred.
  • Losing a compensation adjustment: if 198 was treated as 200, correct the difference by 2.
  • Misaligning place values: combine hundreds with hundreds, tens with tens and ones with ones.
  • No reasonableness check: estimate before or after calculating.
