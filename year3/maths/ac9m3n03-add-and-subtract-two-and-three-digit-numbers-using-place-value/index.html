Add and subtract two- and three-digit numbers

Use place value to partition and regroup two- and three-digit numbers. To calculate 352 - 178, rename 352 as 2 hundreds, 14 tens and 12 ones, then subtract each part to get 174.

Learn the skill

Small steps, clear examples

Learning goal: Partition numbers, exchange equal values and choose a calculation strategy that suits the numbers, without a calculator. Explain each change and check the result.

Before you start: Know addition and subtraction facts to 20 and the value of hundreds, tens and ones.

Useful words: partition: split a number into parts; regroup: exchange equal values; difference: the amount left or the gap between two amounts.

1. Partition and rearrange to add

Split numbers into useful parts. You can add corresponding places or collect a friendly pair first.

200 + 10030 + 406 + 2

Worked example: 236 + 142 = (200 + 100) + (30 + 40) + (6 + 2) = 378. Every part is still included.

For 48 + 27, split 27 into 2 and 25: 48 + 2 = 50, then 50 + 25 = 75. Both routes preserve the total.

Watch for this: Keep place values attached to digits: 3 hundreds is 300, not 3.

Try it: Solve 324 + 153 by partitioning.

Check the answer and explanation

300 + 100 = 400; 20 + 50 = 70; 4 + 3 = 7. Total 477.

2. Subtract parts or count up

Subtraction can remove an amount or find the distance between two numbers. Counting up is useful when the numbers are close.

Worked example: 368 − 124 = 368 − 100 − 20 − 4 = 244.

To find 503 − 487, count from 487 to 500: 13; then to 503: 3. The difference is 16. Check 487 + 16 = 503.

Watch for this: Counting up gives a difference; do not report the final endpoint as the answer.

Try it: Find 402 − 386 by counting up.

Check the answer and explanation

386 to 400 is 14; 400 to 402 is 2. Difference 16.

3. Regroup when adding

Ten ones have the same value as one ten. Ten tens have the same value as one hundred.

3 H + 11 T + 15 O4 H + 2 T + 5 O425

Worked example: 267 + 158 has 15 ones, 11 tens and 3 hundreds before exchanging. Rename 15 ones as 1 ten and 5 ones. Now there are 12 tens: rename these as 1 hundred and 2 tens. The result is 425.

Use base-ten blocks or a place-value drawing to track both exchanges. Nothing is added to the original total.

Watch for this: A regrouped ten belongs in the tens column, not as an extra one.

Try it: Work out 186 + 247 and explain an exchange.

Check the answer and explanation

433. Thirteen ones become one ten and three ones; thirteen tens then become one hundred and three tens.

4. Regroup to subtract, including through zero

When there are too few units in one place, exchange from a larger place. Reduce the donor place as you do so.

Worked example: rename 402 as 3 hundreds, 9 tens and 12 ones. First exchange one hundred for ten tens, then one of those tens for ten ones. Now subtract 178: 12 − 8 = 4 ones; 9 − 7 = 2 tens; 3 − 1 = 2 hundreds. Answer 224.

Watch for this: Do not subtract the smaller digit from the larger digit independently in each column.

Try it: Find 503 − 276. Show the renamed starting number.

Check the answer and explanation

503 = 4 hundreds + 9 tens + 13 ones. Subtract 2 hundreds, 7 tens and 6 ones to get 227.

5. Adjust to friendly numbers and correct

A nearby round number may be easier to use. Keep track of how the adjustment affects the answer.

Worked example: 198 + 47 = 200 + 47 − 2 = 245. Adding 2 to 198 made the temporary total 2 too large.

For 352 − 198, subtract 200 instead: 352 − 200 = 152. Two too many were removed, so restore 2: 154. Another valid route adds 2 to both numbers: 354 − 200 = 154.

Watch for this: Adding two to both numbers preserves a difference; adding two to both addends increases a sum by four.

Try it: Use a friendly number to solve 463 − 199.

Check the answer and explanation

463 − 200 = 263; restore 1 to get 264.

6. Choose, explain and check a strategy

Read what the situation asks before calculating. Use a drawing, number line, partition or written working that makes your reasoning clear.

Worked example: a club has 245 badges, receives 138, then gives out 176. First find 245 + 138 = 383. Then 383 − 176 = 207 badges left.

Check the second step with the inverse: 207 + 176 = 383. An estimate of about 250 + 140 − 180 = 210 makes 207 plausible; the exact inverse check confirms the calculation.

Watch for this: An estimate can expose a large error, but it does not prove every digit of an exact answer.

Try it: A library has 326 books and lends 148. How many remain? Check with addition.

Check the answer and explanation

178 books. 178 + 148 = 326.

Common mistakes and quick fixes
  • Keep place values attached to digits: 3 hundreds is 300, not 3.
  • Counting up gives a difference; do not report the final endpoint as the answer.
  • A regrouped ten belongs in the tens column, not as an extra one.
  • Do not subtract the smaller digit from the larger digit independently in each column.
  • Adding two to both numbers preserves a difference; adding two to both addends increases a sum by four.
  • An estimate can expose a large error, but it does not prove every digit of an exact answer.
