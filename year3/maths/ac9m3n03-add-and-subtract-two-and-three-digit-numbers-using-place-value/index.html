Add and Subtract with Place Value

What students learn

Place value lets us split numbers into hundreds, tens and ones, regroup equal values and choose efficient addition or subtraction strategies. This matters because combine hundreds, tens and ones, then regroup 15 ones as 1 ten and 5 ones.

Subtract a friendly number, then adjust. Use this idea when a problem asks you to use compensation for subtraction, and explain the result with partition, regroup, inverse.

Learning intention: We are learning to add and subtract two- and three-digit numbers using place value to partition, rearrange and regroup numbers to assist in calculations without a calculator.

Success criteria

  • I can partition numbers.
  • I can regroup accurately.
  • I can add three-digit numbers.
Key vocabulary
  • partition — split a number into useful place-value parts
  • regroup — rename a quantity without changing its value
  • inverse — an operation that undoes another operation

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Regrouping without equal exchange — Trade 10 ones for 1 ten or 10 tens for 1 hundred.
  • Losing the adjustment — Compensation must be corrected at the end.
  • No reasonableness check — Estimate before or after calculating.
Teaching sequence and quick check

Quick check: Solve 268 + 157 by partitioning.

Expected response: 268 + 157 = 425.

If incorrect: Trade 10 ones for 1 ten or 10 tens for 1 hundred.
