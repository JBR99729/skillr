Addition and Subtraction with Place Value

Add and subtract two- and three-digit numbers by partitioning, regrouping and using place value to keep quantities equivalent.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

Students partition numbers into hundreds, tens and ones, combine like place values, regroup when useful and check results with estimation or the inverse operation.

Worked examples and teaching sequence
  1. Represent each number by place value.
  2. Choose a partitioning or jump strategy.
  3. Regroup without changing the number's value.
  4. Calculate and check whether the answer is reasonable.

Example: 268 + 157 = (200+100) + (60+50) + (8+7) = 425.

Common misconceptions and quick check
  • Combining digits from different place values.
  • Losing a ten or hundred during regrouping.
  • Accepting an unreasonable answer without estimating.

Quick check: Solve 403 − 178 and show where regrouping occurs.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Teaching contexts include open number lines, split strategies, place-value materials, compensation and inverse checking in practical addition and subtraction problems.
