AC9M3N03 • Year 3 Maths

Add and Subtract with Place Value

Place value lets students partition, rearrange and regroup hundreds, tens and ones so two- and three-digit addition and subtraction can be solved efficiently without a calculator.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

Learning intention: add and subtract two- and three-digit numbers using place value to partition, rearrange and regroup numbers to assist in calculations without a calculator.

  • Partition numbers into hundreds, tens and ones in standard and useful non-standard forms.
  • Regroup 10 ones as 1 ten and 10 tens as 1 hundred without changing value.
  • Choose strategies such as partitioning, compensation, bridging and inverse checking.
  • Estimate before or after calculation to judge whether an answer is reasonable.

Concept boundary: flexible place-value strategies are the target; a single formal written algorithm is not required as the only method.

Key vocabulary and representations

To partition is to split a number into useful parts. To regroup is to rename a quantity without changing its value. An inverse operation undoes another operation and can check an answer.

Use place-value charts, base-ten blocks, open number lines, bar models and equations to connect concrete, visual and symbolic reasoning.

Teaching progression and worked examples
  1. Partition two- and three-digit numbers in multiple equivalent ways.
  2. Add or subtract place-value parts and regroup when needed.
  3. Introduce compensation with nearby friendly numbers.
  4. Check with estimation and an inverse operation.

Example 1: 268 + 157 = (200 + 100) + (60 + 50) + (8 + 7) = 300 + 110 + 15 = 425.

Example 2: 503 − 198 can be thought of as 503 − 200 + 2 = 305.

Teacher language: “How can we partition the numbers? What equal exchange is needed? Is there a friendly number that makes the calculation easier?”

Curriculum elaborations and how they are taught
  • Use place-value materials and diagrams to partition, rearrange and regroup quantities before recording number sentences.
  • Compare different addition/subtraction strategies and explain why they give the same result.
  • Use compensation and bridging strategies with two- and three-digit numbers.
  • Estimate and use inverse operations to assess the reasonableness of calculated answers.
Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Regrouping without equal exchange: physically trade 10 ones for 1 ten or 10 tens for 1 hundred.
  • Losing the compensation adjustment: record both the friendly-number change and the correction.
  • Combining unlike places: align or label hundreds, tens and ones.
  • No reasonableness check: make a quick estimate before accepting the result.
Support, Core and Extend
  • Support: keep base-ten materials visible and use numbers requiring one regrouping at a time.
  • Core: choose and explain an efficient strategy for mixed two- and three-digit addition/subtraction.
  • Extend: solve one calculation in two different ways and compare efficiency while staying within Year 3 number ranges.
Quick check and mastery evidence
  1. Solve 268 + 157 by partitioning. Expected: 425.
  2. Solve 503 − 198 using compensation. Expected: 305.
  3. Explain why 10 ones can be renamed as 1 ten. Evidence: recognises equal value.

Mastery means flexibly partitioning/regrouping, selecting a suitable strategy and checking whether the result is sensible.
