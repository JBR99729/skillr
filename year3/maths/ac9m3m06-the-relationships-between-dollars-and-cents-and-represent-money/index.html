Dollars and Cents

Use 100 cents = 1 dollar to read, represent and compare money amounts and make equivalent combinations of Australian coins and notes.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

Students connect dollars and cents, write money notation correctly, make equivalent values and solve simple comparison and change situations.

Worked examples and teaching sequence
  1. Build values with coins.
  2. Exchange 100 cents for $1.
  3. Represent one amount in several combinations.
  4. Compare amounts using consistent notation.

Example: $2.50 = 250 cents.

Common misconceptions and quick check
  • Treating $2.5 as 2 dollars 5 cents.
  • Assuming more coins always means more value.
  • Forgetting 100 cents equals one dollar.

Quick check: Show two different combinations worth $3.40.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Contexts include shopping, coin combinations, equivalent values, dollars-to-cents relationships and reasoning about totals and simple change.
