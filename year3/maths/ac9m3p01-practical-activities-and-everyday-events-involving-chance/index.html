What students learn in AC9M3P01

Chance language describes how possible an event is. Certain events must happen, impossible events cannot happen, and likely or unlikely events depend on the situation.

Learning routine: List outcomes → Check conditions → Place on chance scale → Explain evidence

Success looks like

  • Identify chance events
  • List outcomes
  • Use certain/impossible
  • Use likely/unlikely
  • Explain reasoning

Concept models and worked thinking

Reasoning must use the possible outcomes or known conditions.

  1. Read every label and identify what each quantity or feature represents.
  2. Describe the relationship shown by the model.
  3. Check that the conclusion answers the original question.

Analyse a bag of counters

red × 6blue × 2green × 0

Red is likely, blue is possible but unlikely, and green is impossible if there are no green counters.

Apply the same relationship to the new context, then justify the answer using the labels and evidence shown.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

The content description and elaborations below show the curriculum ideas taught in this unit. Items marked as teaching context support lesson planning.

  • Content description: identify practical activities and everyday events involving chance; describe possible outcomes and events as ‘likely’ or ‘unlikely’ and identify some events as ‘certain’ or ‘impossible’ explaining reasoning
  • E1: predicting what could happen next in practical activities that involve an element of chance, considering possible outcomes and using terms such as “likely” or “unlikely” to explain their predictions
  • E2: classifying a list of everyday events or sorting a set of event cards according to how likely they are to happen, using the language of chance and giving reasons for classifications; discussing how impossible outcomes cannot ever happen, uncertain outcomes are affected by chance as they may or may not happen whereas certain events must always happen, so they are not affected by chance
  • E3: making predictions and testing what would happen; for example, if 10 names were put in a box, and names were then drawn out one at a time and replaced after each selection, discussing how likely it would be after 10 selections that all 10 names were drawn from the box or that one name was drawn multiple times
  • E4: role-playing being a chatbot or virtual assistant responding to a user about the likelihood of events; for example, using preset questions on cards relating to the likelihood of events, role-playing in pairs responding as a virtual assistant, giving reasons for their response

Avoid this trap

  • Likely means certain — Likely events can still fail to occur.
  • Impossible based on opinion — Check the actual possible outcomes.
  • No conditions stated — Chance can change when the situation changes.
