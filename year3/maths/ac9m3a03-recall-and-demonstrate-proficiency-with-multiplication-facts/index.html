Multiplication Facts for 3, 4, 5 and 10

What students learn

Facts for 3, 4, 5 and 10 can be built from patterns, arrays and known facts. Each multiplication fact connects to related division facts. This matters because the array shows 28 ÷ 4 = 7 and 28 ÷ 7 = 4.

Use nearby known facts rather than counting from one. Use this idea when a problem asks you to use known facts to derive new ones, and explain the result with multiplication fact, related division fact, proficiency.

Learning intention: We are learning to recall and demonstrate proficiency with multiplication facts for 3, 4, 5 and 10; extend and apply facts to develop the related division facts.

Success criteria

  • I can recall 3s facts.
  • I can recall 4s facts.
  • I can recall 5s and 10s.
Key vocabulary
  • multiplication fact — a known multiplication statement
  • related division fact — a division statement made from the same fact family
  • proficiency — being accurate, efficient and flexible
Worked examples

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Repeated addition too slow — Use patterns and known facts for recall.
  • Division fact does not match total — The dividend is the whole product.
  • Mixing 4s and 5s patterns — Use arrays or doubling to verify.
Teaching sequence and quick check

Quick check: What is 7 × 4?

Expected response: 7 × 4 = 28.

If incorrect: Use patterns and known facts for recall.
