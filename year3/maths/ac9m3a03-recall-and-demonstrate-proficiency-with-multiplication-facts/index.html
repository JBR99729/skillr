AC9M3A03: Multiplication Facts for 3, 4, 5 and 10

Students build fluency with 3, 4, 5 and 10 multiplication facts and connect each fact to related division facts using equal groups, arrays and fact families.

Topic guideTeacher SlidesWorksheetPracticeTest
What students learn in AC9M3A03

Students recall 3, 4, 5 and 10 times facts and use the same factors and product to build related division facts.

  • Recall 3, 4, 5 and 10 times facts with increasing fluency.
  • Represent multiplication with equal groups, arrays and skip counting.
  • Use multiplication facts to derive related division facts.
  • Partition arrays to use known facts for harder facts.
Key models and vocabulary
  • factor — a number being multiplied
  • product — the result of multiplication
  • quotient — the result of division
  • array — objects arranged in equal rows and columns
  • related division fact — a division equation using the same parts and whole as a multiplication fact
Teaching progression and worked examples
  1. Fact family: 4 × 6 = 24, so 24 ÷ 4 = 6 and 24 ÷ 6 = 4.
  2. Use known facts: for 3 × 8, think 3 × 4 = 12 and double it to get 24.
  3. Skip count check: 5 × 7 = 35 can be checked by 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35.

Teacher language: “What are the factors? What is the product? Which division facts use the same three numbers?”

Curriculum coverage and elaborations
  • Content description: recall and demonstrate proficiency with multiplication facts for 3, 4, 5 and 10; extend and apply facts to develop the related division facts
  • E1: recalling multiplication facts for 3, 4, 5 and 10
  • E2: using arrays and equal groups to explain multiplication facts
  • E3: applying multiplication facts to develop related division facts

Common mistakes to watch for
  • Counting by ones: return to skip counting and known facts.
  • Swapping product and factor in division: identify the whole/product first.
  • Writing unrelated division facts: keep the same factors and product in the fact family.
Support, core and extend
  • Support: use counters, arrays and skip-count tracks.
  • Core: recall mixed 3, 4, 5 and 10 facts and write related division facts.
  • Extend: partition arrays to derive larger facts from known facts.
Quick check and mastery evidence
  1. Write the multiplication and division fact family for 4, 7 and 28.
  2. Use a known fact to solve 3 × 9 and explain the strategy.

Mastery means students can recall the facts, represent them, derive division facts and explain the relationship.
