AC9M3ST03 • Year 3 Maths

Guided Statistical Investigations

A statistical investigation connects a question to relevant data, a suitable representation and a conclusion supported by the evidence.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

Learning intention: conduct guided statistical investigations involving the collection, representation and interpretation of data for categorical and discrete numerical variables with respect to questions of interest.

Students move through a coherent cycle: ask, collect, represent, interpret and communicate.

Concept boundary: focus on guided investigations with simple categorical and discrete numerical data; formal inference is not required.

Key vocabulary and representations

Statistical investigation — a question-driven process for collecting and interpreting data. Question of interest — the question the data is intended to answer. Conclusion — a statement supported by the collected evidence.

Teaching progression and worked examples
  1. Ask a focused question.
  2. Plan and collect relevant data.
  3. Represent the data clearly.
  4. Interpret patterns and differences.
  5. Answer the original question with evidence.

Example: investigate “Which playground activity is most popular in our class?” Survey the class, record categories, graph frequencies and use the graph to answer the question.

Curriculum elaborations and how they are taught

Guide students through complete investigations using familiar questions, explicit collection plans, appropriate tables or graphs, interpretation prompts and evidence-based conclusions.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Irrelevant data: connect every collected value to the question of interest.
  • Poor display choice: choose a representation that makes the needed comparison visible.
  • Unsupported conclusion: point to the exact data that supports the statement.
Support, Core and Extend
  • Support: use a teacher-provided question and partially completed collection table.
  • Core: complete the full guided cycle and communicate a conclusion.
  • Extend: compare two representations or refine the original question after reviewing the data.
Quick check and mastery evidence

Give a small data set and its question of interest. Mastery means the student chooses a suitable display, interprets the data correctly and writes a conclusion that directly answers the question.
