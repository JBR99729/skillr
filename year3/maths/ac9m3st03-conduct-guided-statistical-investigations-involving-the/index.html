Prerequisite knowledge

Students should be able to sort information into categories, read simple tables or displays and compare counts. Revisit what each label, category and scale represents before analysing a data display.

Concept teaching

Key idea: A statistical investigation begins with a question and uses collected data to form evidence-based conclusions while recognising variation.

Learning routine: Question → Plan → Collect → Represent → Interpret → Report

statistical investigation
a process of asking, collecting, displaying and interpreting data
question of interest
the focused question an investigation answers
conclusion
a statement supported by the collected data
Worked and modelled examples

Follow the investigation cycle

Question→Collect→Organise→Display→Interpret→Report

Each stage should connect back to the original question.

  1. Read every label and identify what each quantity or feature represents.
  2. Describe the relationship shown by the model.
  3. Check that the conclusion answers the original question.

Describe patterns and differences without claiming more than the data shows.

Apply the same relationship to the new context, then justify the answer using the labels and evidence shown.

Common misconceptions and corrections
  • Question and data mismatch — Collect data that directly answers the question.
  • Conclusion without evidence — Quote frequencies or comparisons.
  • Claiming cause — A small descriptive investigation may show association, not cause.

Independent practice

  1. What is the first stage of a statistical investigation?

    • ask a clear question
    • draw any graph
    • write a conclusion first
    • choose the favourite answer

  2. Complete the cycle.

    Prompt: Question → collect → organise → display → □ → report

  3. Which conclusion uses evidence?

    • Bus was most common with 8 responses
    • Bus is always best
    • Everyone must use a bus
    • The graph looks nice

  4. Write a statistical question about a countable Year 3 classroom variable.

  5. Match the stage to the action.

    Match: collect • represent • interpret ↔ look for patterns • record responses • create a table or graph

  6. Complete the evidence sentence.

    Prompt: The category with 8 responses had □ more than the category with 5.

Reasoning and problem-solving

  1. Plan a complete statistical investigation, including question, variable, collection method, display and conclusion format.

  2. Compare two possible conclusions from one graph and decide which is better supported by evidence.

Explain the reasoning, not only the final answer.

Assessment-style questions and review hints

  1. Explain how variation can appear in repeated class survey results.

  2. A student collects favourite fruit data to answer 'How many books are read?' Explain the mismatch.

Curriculum alignment

The content description and elaborations below show the curriculum ideas taught in this unit. Items marked as teaching context support lesson planning.

  • Content description: conduct guided statistical investigations involving the collection, representation and interpretation of data for categorical and discrete numerical variables with respect to questions of interest
  • E1: creating a poster, flowchart or infographic that describes the process of statistical investigation, and the components, the tools and the types of data that can be collected, represented and interpreted for a purpose
  • E2: collaboratively working through a whole class investigation by choosing a question of interest, using an efficient collection method and recording collected data; interpreting the data in terms of the question
  • E3: planning and carrying out investigations that involve collecting data; for example, narrowing the focus of a question such as, “Which is the most popular breakfast cereal?” to “Which is the most popular breakfast cereal among Year 3 students in our class?”
  • E4: conducting a whole class statistical investigation into the best day to hold an open day for parents by creating a simple survey; collecting the data by asking the parents, representing and interpreting the results, and deciding as a class which day would be best
  • E5: investigating seasonal calendars of First Nations Australians by collecting data and creating frequency tables and spreadsheets based on environmental indicators; creating one-to-one data displays about frequency of environmental indicators for the current season (teaching context)
