What students learn

A statistical investigation begins with a question and uses collected data to form evidence-based conclusions while recognising variation. This matters because each stage should connect back to the original question.

Describe patterns and differences without claiming more than the data shows. Use this idea when a problem asks you to interpret variation in results, and explain the result with statistical investigation, question of interest, conclusion.

Learning intention: We are learning to conduct guided statistical investigations involving the collection, representation and interpretation of data for categorical and discrete numerical variables with respect to questions of interest.

Success criteria

  • I can ask statistical questions.
  • I can plan data collection.
  • I can represent data.
Key vocabulary
  • statistical investigation — a process of asking, collecting, displaying and interpreting data
  • question of interest — the focused question an investigation answers
  • conclusion — a statement supported by the collected data

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Question and data mismatch — Collect data that directly answers the question.
  • Conclusion without evidence — Quote frequencies or comparisons.
  • Claiming cause — A small descriptive investigation may show association, not cause.
Teaching sequence and quick check

Quick check: Write a statistical question.

Expected response: First ask a clear statistical question.

If incorrect: Collect data that directly answers the question.
