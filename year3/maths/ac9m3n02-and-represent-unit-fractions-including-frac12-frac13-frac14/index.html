Unit Fractions

Represent unit fractions as one equal part of a whole and build multiples by combining equal fractional parts.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

Students partition wholes into equal parts, name unit fractions such as one-half, one-third and one-quarter, place fractions on number lines and combine equal parts to make related fractions.

Worked examples and teaching sequence
  1. Confirm the whole.
  2. Partition it into equal-sized parts.
  3. Name one part using the denominator.
  4. Combine repeated unit fractions and represent them on a number line.
Common misconceptions and quick check
  • Counting unequal pieces as fractions.
  • Thinking a larger denominator means a larger unit fraction.
  • Changing the whole while comparing fractions.

Quick check: Draw 3/4 as three copies of 1/4 and locate it on a 0-to-1 number line.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Contexts include folding, fraction strips, collections and number lines, with attention to equal partitioning, unit-fraction language and repeated fractional parts.
