Unit Fractions and Multiples

Fractions describe equal parts of a whole. Students recognise unit fractions, build their multiples in different representations and combine same-denominator parts to complete a whole.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

Learning intention: recognise and represent unit fractions including one-half, one-third, one-quarter, one-fifth and one-tenth and their multiples in different ways; combine fractions with the same denominator to complete the whole.

  • Identify 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/5 and 1/10 as one of equal parts of a whole.
  • Represent multiples such as 3/4 or 7/10 using regions, strips, collections and number lines.
  • Keep the denominator fixed when combining equal-sized parts.
  • Recognise when the total number of same-denominator parts completes one whole.

Concept boundary: the focus is unit fractions and their multiples with familiar denominators; formal unlike-denominator fraction addition is not a Year 3 target.

Key vocabulary and representations

A unit fraction has numerator 1. The denominator tells how many equal parts make one whole. The numerator tells how many of those equal parts are selected.

Use fraction strips, folded paper, area models, equal collections and number lines. Always identify the whole before naming the fraction.

Teaching progression and worked examples
  1. Partition one whole into equal halves, thirds, quarters, fifths and tenths.
  2. Name one part as the corresponding unit fraction.
  3. Build multiples by counting equal unit-fraction parts.
  4. Combine same-denominator parts and recognise when all parts make the whole.

Example 1: Divide a strip into 4 equal parts and shade 3: the shaded amount is 3/4.

Example 2: 2/5 + 3/5 makes 5/5, which is one whole, because all five equal fifths are present.

Teacher language: “What is the whole? Are the parts equal? How many equal parts make the whole? How many are selected?”

Curriculum elaborations and how they are taught
  • Fold, cut and partition shapes to create familiar unit fractions and discuss the need for equal parts.
  • Represent the same fraction using different models such as a strip, collection and number line.
  • Build multiples of unit fractions by iterating one equal part.
  • Combine fractions with the same denominator to determine the missing part needed to complete a whole.
Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Unequal parts are called fractions: require equal partitioning of the same whole.
  • Adding denominators: same-sized parts keep the same denominator; count the selected parts.
  • Ignoring the whole: label or show the whole before comparing fractions.
  • Thinking a larger denominator means a larger unit fraction: for the same whole, more equal parts means each part is smaller.
Support, Core and Extend
  • Support: use physical fraction strips and halves/quarters before introducing fifths and tenths.
  • Core: represent unit fractions and multiples in at least two ways and complete wholes with same-denominator parts.
  • Extend: locate familiar fractions on a number line and explain equivalence to one whole without introducing unlike-denominator algorithms.
Quick check and mastery evidence
  1. Show one-third of a strip. Expected: 1 of 3 equal parts.
  2. What fraction is 3 shaded parts out of 4 equal parts? Expected: 3/4.
  3. Complete 2/5 to make one whole. Expected: add 3/5.

Mastery means consistently identifying the whole and equal parts, representing familiar fractions and combining same-denominator parts accurately.
