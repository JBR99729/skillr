AC9M3N02 • Year 3 Maths

Unit Fractions and Multiples

Recognise and represent halves, thirds, quarters, fifths and tenths and their multiples; combine fractions with the same denominator to complete one whole.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesWorksheetPracticeTest
What students learn in AC9M3N02

Students identify a clearly defined whole, divide it into equal parts and name one part as a unit fraction. They build multiples of unit fractions and combine fractions with the same denominator by counting equal-sized parts.

  • Recognise 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/5 and 1/10 as one equal part of a whole.
  • Represent multiples such as 3/4 and 7/10 with shapes, strips, collections and number lines.
  • Check that parts are equal and that models refer to the same whole.
  • Combine fractions with the same denominator.
  • Find the missing fraction needed to complete one whole.
Key vocabulary and models

A unit fraction has numerator 1. The denominator tells how many equal parts make the whole; the numerator tells how many of those equal parts are selected.

For 3/4, the whole is divided into 4 equal parts and 3 are selected. For 2/5 + 3/5, five fifths are collected, so the total is one whole.

Worked examples and teaching sequence
  1. Identify the whole: students must know what object, collection or interval counts as one whole.
  2. Check equal parts: unequal pieces cannot be named as equal fractional parts.
  3. Name one part: one of 5 equal parts is 1/5.
  4. Build multiples: 4 copies of 1/5 make 4/5.
  5. Complete the whole: 2/5 needs 3/5 more because 5/5 = 1.
Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Content description: recognise and represent unit fractions including one-half, one-third, one-quarter, one-fifth and one-tenth and their multiples in different ways; combine fractions with the same denominator to complete the whole.

  • E1: fold, partition, draw or use digital models to create equal halves, thirds, quarters, fifths and tenths.
  • E2: represent unit fractions and multiples using area models, length models, collections and number lines while keeping the whole explicit.
  • E3: combine equal-sized fractional parts with the same denominator and explain how many parts make one whole.
  • E4: solve missing-part problems such as identifying the fraction needed to complete 3/4 or 6/10 to one whole.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Unequal parts called fractions: verify that every part is equal.
  • Adding denominators: when the parts are the same size, count numerators and keep the denominator.
  • Ignoring the whole: identify and keep the same whole before comparing or combining models.
  • Confusing denominator with amount selected: denominator counts all equal parts in the whole; numerator counts selected parts.
