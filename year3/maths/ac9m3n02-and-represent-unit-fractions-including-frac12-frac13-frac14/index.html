Unit Fractions and Multiples

What students learn

The denominator tells how many equal parts make the whole. Fractions with the same denominator can be combined by counting equal parts. This matters because the whole is split into 4 equal parts and 3 parts are selected.

Five fifths make one whole. Use this idea when a problem asks you to complete a whole using the same denominator, and explain the result with unit fraction, denominator, equivalent whole.

Learning intention: We are learning to recognise and represent unit fractions including one-half, one-third, one-quarter, one-fifth and one-tenth and their multiples in different ways; combine fractions with the same denominator to complete the whole.

Success criteria

  • I can identify unit fractions.
  • I can represent multiples.
  • I can use equal parts.
Key vocabulary
  • unit fraction — a fraction with 1 as its numerator
  • denominator — the number showing how many equal parts make one whole
  • equivalent whole — all equal parts combined to make one

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Unequal parts called fractions — The parts must be equal.
  • Adding denominators — When parts are the same size, count numerators and keep the denominator.
  • Ignoring the whole — Fractions must refer to a clearly identified whole.
Teaching sequence and quick check

Quick check: Show one-third of a strip.

Expected response: One-third is 1 of 3 equal parts.

If incorrect: The parts must be equal.
