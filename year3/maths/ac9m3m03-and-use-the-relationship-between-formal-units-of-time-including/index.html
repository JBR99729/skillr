AC9M3M03 • Year 3 Maths

Time Units and Duration

Seconds, minutes, hours and days have fixed relationships. Students use these relationships and familiar benchmarks to estimate and compare how long events last.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

Learning intention: recognise and use the relationship between formal units of time including days, hours, minutes and seconds to estimate and compare event duration.

  • Know that 60 seconds = 1 minute, 60 minutes = 1 hour and 24 hours = 1 day.
  • Select a sensible unit for short, medium and long events.
  • Estimate duration using familiar time benchmarks before measuring or comparing.
  • Express equivalent simple durations using related units where appropriate.

Concept boundary: focus on unit relationships and duration; complex elapsed-time algorithms are not the central target.

Key vocabulary and representations

Duration is how long an event lasts. A formal unit is a standard time unit such as second, minute, hour or day. To convert is to express the same duration using another related unit.

Useful representations include timelines, labelled unit chains and event cards ordered from shortest to longest.

Teaching progression and worked examples
  1. Match familiar events to seconds, minutes, hours or days.
  2. Build the relationships 60 s = 1 min, 60 min = 1 h and 24 h = 1 day from clocks and daily routines.
  3. Estimate an event, then time or verify it.
  4. Compare events by expressing them in a common useful unit.

Example: 2 minutes = 120 seconds because each minute contains 60 seconds. A 2-hour movie lasts longer than a 90-minute lesson because 2 hours = 120 minutes.

Teacher language: “Which unit makes sense? What relationship connects these units? Can we express both durations in the same unit?”

Curriculum elaborations and how they are taught
  • Use classroom routines and timed activities to build benchmarks for seconds, minutes and hours.
  • Relate days and hours using a 24-hour day and familiar daily events.
  • Estimate and then compare durations, explaining why the chosen unit is appropriate.
  • Use multiplication or division with simple whole-number relationships to express equivalent durations.
Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Using base 100 for time: reinforce 60 seconds per minute and 60 minutes per hour.
  • Choosing an unsuitable unit: seconds suit short events; days suit much longer events.
  • Comparing only the numerals: compare after considering the units.
  • Confusing a time of day with duration: ask “When?” versus “How long?”
Support, Core and Extend
  • Support: use event cards, timers and a visible time-unit relationship chart.
  • Core: estimate, measure and compare durations using appropriate formal units.
  • Extend: express simple durations in another related unit and justify which unit communicates the comparison most clearly.
Quick check and mastery evidence
  1. How many seconds are in 2 minutes? Expected: 120 seconds.
  2. Which unit suits a school lesson? Expected: minutes.
  3. Which is longer: 90 minutes or 2 hours? Expected: 2 hours/120 minutes.

Mastery means selecting suitable units, using the standard relationships accurately and comparing durations with clear reasoning.
