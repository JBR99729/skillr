AC9M3M03 • Year 3 Maths

Units of Time and Duration

Recognise and use relationships between days, hours, minutes and seconds to estimate and compare event duration.

Topic guideTeacher deckWorksheetPracticeTest

What students learn

Students build a usable understanding of formal units of time rather than memorising one answer pattern. They explain ideas, recognise them in new situations and apply them independently.

  • Relate seconds, minutes, hours and days.
  • Estimate, measure and compare durations.
  • Use concrete examples, pictures, oral explanations and written responses.
  • Estimate reading time and plan sequences of events such as a class party.
  • Choose the correct strategy in mixed tasks.

Essential relationships

  • 60 seconds = 1 minute
  • 60 minutes = 1 hour
  • 24 hours = 1 day
  • 7 days = 1 week
  • 12 months = 1 year (extension)

Teacher note: time is not base 100. Revisit the groups of 60 often.

Choose a sensible unit

Use seconds for a blink or sprint, minutes for brushing teeth or recess, hours for a movie or school day, days for a camp and days/weeks for a holiday. Ask: “Could another unit work?” and “Which unit communicates the duration best?”

Estimate → measure → compare

  1. Choose a unit.
  2. Estimate the duration.
  3. Measure with a sand timer, stopwatch or digital timer.
  4. Compare estimate and measurement.
  5. Explain whether the estimate was reasonable.

Try a reading passage, 10 star jumps, packing a bag, tidying a desk or a short puzzle.

Real-life scenarios

  • School: Maths 9:10–10:00 = 50 minutes.
  • Sport: 4:30 PM + 15-min warm-up + 30-min skills + 10-min cool-down = 5:25 PM.
  • Cooking: toast → minutes; slow cooking → hours.
  • Travel: nearby walk → minutes; city drive → hours; holiday → days/weeks.
  • Reading: estimate and time a set passage.
  • Class party: estimate games and see whether the plan fits the available time.

Conversions and comparison

  • Hours → minutes: ×60; 2 h = 120 min.
  • Minutes → seconds: ×60; 3 min = 180 s.
  • Weeks → days: ×7; 3 weeks = 21 days.
  • Reverse simple conversions by dividing by the matching relationship.

Always write the unit. Convert durations to the same unit before comparing.

Elapsed time and AM/PM

Count on using a timeline: start → friendly hour → remaining minutes → total. Example: 2:15 PM to 4:45 PM = 2 h 30 min. AM is midnight to before noon; PM is noon to before midnight. 12 noon = 12 PM; 12 midnight = 12 AM. Detailed clock relationships continue in AC9M3M04.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Content description: recognise and use the relationship between formal units of time including days, hours, minutes and seconds to estimate and compare the duration of events.

  • E1: estimate how long it takes to read a set passage and share the duration.
  • E2: plan a sequence of events from estimated durations, such as class-party activities.
  • E3: read or set digital devices to the minute or second, including countdown timers.
  • E4: use sand/digital timers to check estimates such as 1, 3 and 5 minutes.
  • E5: explore First Nations Australian cultural accounts of cycles of time involving the sun, moon and stars (teaching context).

Common mistakes

  • Using 100 minutes per hour instead of 60.
  • Writing 75 minutes as 1:75 instead of 1 h 15 min.
  • Comparing unlike units without converting.
  • Choosing awkward units, such as 7,200 seconds for a 2-hour trip.
  • Mixing up AM/PM or noon/midnight.
  • Subtracting clock digits instead of reasoning on a timeline.
  • Giving a correct answer without explaining why.
  • Mixing up before/after or longer/shorter.

Student FAQ

Hours to minutes? ×60. Minutes to seconds? ×60. Compare units? Convert to the same unit. Elapsed time? Time between start and finish. Leap year? 366 days with February 29 (extension). A decade is 10 years and a century is 100 years.

Quick guided practice

  2. 3 weeks = 21 days
  3. 180 s = 3 min
  4. 6:00–7:30 PM = 1 h 30 min
  5. Best unit for a sneeze = seconds
