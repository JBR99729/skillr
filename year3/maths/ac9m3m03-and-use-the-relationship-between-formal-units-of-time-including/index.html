AC9M3M03: Units of Time and Duration

Recognise and use relationships between days, hours, minutes and seconds to estimate and compare the duration of events.

Topic guideTeacher deckWorksheetPracticeTest

What students learn in AC9M3M03

This unit builds a usable understanding of formal units of time. Students explain the idea, recognise it in new examples and apply it in practice rather than memorising one answer pattern.

  • Relate seconds, minutes, hours and days.
  • Use concrete examples, pictures, oral explanations and written responses.
  • Estimate how long it takes to read a passage and check the estimate.
  • Plan events such as a class party by estimating activity durations.
  • Finish with mixed tasks requiring students to choose a strategy.

Essential time relationships

  • 60 seconds = 1 minute
  • 60 minutes = 1 hour
  • 24 hours = 1 day
  • 7 days = 1 week
  • 12 months = 1 year (useful extension)

Teacher emphasis: clock time is not base 100. Revisit the 60-second and 60-minute relationships often.

Choose a sensible unit

Match the unit to the event: a blink or short sprint → seconds; brushing teeth or recess → minutes; a movie or school day → hours; a camp → days; a holiday → days or weeks. Ask, “Could another unit work?” and “Which unit communicates the duration most clearly?”

Estimate, measure and compare

  1. Choose a sensible unit.
  2. Estimate the duration.
  3. Measure with a sand timer, stopwatch or digital timer.
  4. Compare estimate and measurement.
  5. Explain whether the estimate was reasonable.

Try reading a passage, 10 star jumps, packing a bag, tidying a desk or completing a short puzzle.

Real-life scenarios for kids

  • School: Maths 9:10–10:00 = 50 minutes.
  • Sport: 4:30 PM + 15-min warm-up + 30-min skills + 10-min cool-down = 5:25 PM.
  • Cooking: toast uses minutes; slow cooking may use hours.
  • Travel: nearby walk → minutes; another-city drive → hours; holiday → days/weeks.
  • Reading: estimate and time a set passage.
  • Class party: estimate games and decide whether the plan fits the available time.

Conversions and comparison

  • Hours → minutes: ×60. Example: 2 h = 120 min.
  • Minutes → seconds: ×60. Example: 3 min = 180 s.
  • Weeks → days: ×7. Example: 3 weeks = 21 days.
  • For simple reverse conversions, divide by the matching relationship.

Attach a unit to every answer. Convert two durations to the same unit before comparing them.

Elapsed time: count on

Use a timeline: start time → jump to a friendly hour → count remaining minutes → add the jumps. Example: 2:15 PM to 4:45 PM = 2 h 30 min. Detailed analog/digital clock relationships continue in AC9M3M04.

AM, PM and longer-unit enrichment

AM is midnight to before noon; PM is noon to before midnight. 12 noon = 12:00 PM and 12 midnight = 12:00 AM. Extension: a common year has 365 days; a leap year has 366; a decade is 10 years; a century is 100 years.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Content description: recognise and use the relationship between formal units of time including days, hours, minutes and seconds to estimate and compare the duration of events.

  • E1: estimate how long it takes to read a set passage and share the duration.
  • E2: plan a sequence of events from estimated durations, such as class-party activities.
  • E3: read or set time on digital devices to the minute or second, including countdown timers.
  • E4: use sand and digital timers to check estimates such as 1, 3 and 5 minutes.
  • E5: explore First Nations Australian cultural accounts of cycles of time involving the sun, moon and stars (teaching context).

Common mistakes to watch for

  • 1 hour = 100 minutes ✗; it is 60 minutes.
  • 75 minutes = 1:75 ✗; regroup to 1 h 15 min.
  • Comparing unlike units without converting first.
  • Choosing awkward units, such as 7,200 seconds for a 2-hour trip.
  • Mixing up AM/PM, especially noon and midnight.
  • Subtracting clock digits instead of reasoning on a timeline.
  • Giving an answer without being able to explain why it works.
  • Mixing up before/after or longer/shorter.

Student FAQ

Hours to minutes? Multiply by 60. Minutes to seconds? Multiply by 60. Compare different units? Convert to the same unit. Elapsed time? Time between start and finish. Leap year? 366 days, with February 29.

Quick guided practice

  1. 2 hours = 120 minutes
  2. 3 weeks = 21 days
  3. 180 seconds = 3 minutes
  4. 6:00 PM–7:30 PM = 1 h 30 min
  5. Best unit for a sneeze = seconds
