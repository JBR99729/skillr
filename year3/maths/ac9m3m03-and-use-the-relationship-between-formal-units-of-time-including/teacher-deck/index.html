AC9M3M03 • Year 3 Maths

Units of Time & Duration

Seconds • minutes • hours • days • weeks • months • years

Teacher goal: build meaning first, then relationships, estimation, conversion and real-life problem solving.

Learning intentions

  • Choose sensible units for different events.
  • Use 60 s = 1 min, 60 min = 1 h, 24 h = 1 day and 7 days = 1 week.
  • Estimate, measure and compare durations.
  • Count forward to solve simple elapsed-time situations.

Success check: students can explain why a unit is sensible.

Warm-up: Which unit?

Blink
seconds
Eat lunch

Ask: Could another unit work? Which is most useful?

The time ladder

  • 60 seconds = 1 minute
  • 60 minutes = 1 hour
  • 24 hours = 1 day
  • 7 days = 1 week
  • 12 months = 1 year
  • 10 years = decade • 100 years = century

Important misconception: time is not base 100.

Seconds in real life

Use seconds for very short events: a blink, clap, jump, short sprint or countdown.

Estimate then measure: How long do 10 star jumps take?

Minutes in real life

Brushing teeth ≈ 2 minutes. A brain break ≈ 5 minutes. Recess is usually described in minutes.

Reasoning: Which is longer, 2 minutes or 100 seconds?

Hours, days and weeks

  • Movie → about 2 hours
  • School day → several hours
  • Library loan → perhaps 2 weeks = 14 days
  • Weekend → 2 days

AM and PM

  • AM: midnight through morning before noon.
  • PM: noon through afternoon and evening before midnight.
  • 12 noon = 12:00 PM.
  • 12 midnight = 12:00 AM.

Elapsed time: count on

  1. Find the start time.
  2. Jump to a friendly hour.
  3. Count the remaining minutes.
  4. Add the jumps.

Example: 2:15 PM → 4:45 PM = 2 h 30 min.

School timetable scenario

Maths starts at 9:10 AM and ends at 10:00 AM.

Elapsed time = 50 minutes.

Lunch starts at 12:40 PM and lasts 40 minutes.

Finish = 1:20 PM.

Draw a timeline instead of subtracting clock digits.

Sport scenario

Training starts 4:30 PM → 15-min warm-up → 30-min skills → 10-min cool-down.

Finish time = 5:25 PM.

Ask students which section is longest and how they know.

Cooking scenario

  • Toast → minutes
  • Muffins → tens of minutes
  • Slow-cooked meal → hours

Connect maths to recipe instructions and timers.

Travel scenario

Nearby walk → minutes. Drive to another city → hours. Holiday → days or weeks.

Would you normally describe a 2-hour trip as 7,200 seconds? Why not?

Estimate → measure → compare

Estimate how long it takes to read a short passage. Measure with a timer. Compare the estimate and actual duration.

This directly supports AC9M3M03 elaboration E1.

Plan a class party

Choose 3–4 activities. Estimate each duration. Put them in an order. Check whether the plan fits the available time.

This directly supports elaboration E2 and makes duration purposeful.

Digital and sand timers

Set a digital timer for 1, 3 or 5 minutes. Estimate when the time is nearly finished, then check.

Supports E3–E4: setting devices and checking estimates.

Common misconceptions

  • 1 hour = 100 minutes ✗
  • 75 minutes = 1:75 ✗
  • AM means light and PM means dark ✗
  • All months have the same number of days ✗
  • Elapsed time is ordinary digit subtraction ✗

Guided practice

  • 2 h = 120 min
  • 3 weeks = 21 days
  • 180 s = 3 min
  • 6:00 PM to 7:30 PM = 1 h 30 min
  • Best unit for a sneeze = seconds

FAQ

  • Hours to minutes? Multiply by 60.
  • Minutes to seconds? Multiply by 60.
  • Leap year? 366 days, with February 29.
  • Elapsed time? Time between a start and finish.
  • Why compare in the same unit? Equal-sized units make the comparison fair.

Exit ticket

  1. Best unit for a 30-minute TV episode?
  2. Minutes in 4 hours?
  3. Days in 3 weeks?
  4. Longer: 150 seconds or 2 minutes?
  5. Name one time mistake and correct it.

Answers: minutes; 240 min; 21 days; 150 seconds; explanations vary.
