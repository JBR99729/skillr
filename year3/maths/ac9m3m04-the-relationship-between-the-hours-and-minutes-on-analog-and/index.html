AC9M3M04: Telling Time on Analogue and Digital Clocks

Describe the relationship between hours and minutes on analog and digital clocks, read time to the nearest minute, and extend confident learners into clock geometry.

Topic guideTeacher deckWorksheetPracticeTest

What students learn in AC9M3M04

This unit keeps the original goal: students build a clear, usable understanding of hours and minutes on analogue and digital clocks. They explain the idea, recognise it in new examples and apply it rather than memorising one clock picture.

  • Describe the relationship between hour and minute hands.
  • Read and represent time to the nearest minute or five-minute interval.
  • Connect analogue time, digital notation and spoken time language.
  • Explain why the hour hand moves continuously between hour numbers.
  • Use concrete clocks, pictures, oral explanations and written responses before independent practice.
  • Finish with mixed examples so students choose a strategy.

Core curriculum and Year 4 extension

Core AC9M3M04 Reading clocks to the nearest minute, connecting representations and explaining hand movement.

Optional Year 4 extension Clock-circle geometry, degrees travelled and angles between hands.

Experienced-teacher guidance: AC9M3M04 is a Year 3 code. With Year 4 students, use the core as retrieval and fluency, then add geometry as enrichment. The angle formula is not required for core mastery.

Meet the analogue clock

Hour hand

Usually shorter and often thicker. It rotates once in 12 hours: 30° per hour or 0.5° per minute.

Minute hand

Usually longer. It rotates once in 60 minutes: 6° per minute.

Second hand

Usually long and thin. It rotates once in 60 seconds. Designs vary, so teach purpose and movement as well as appearance.

Minute map: 12→00, 1→05, 2→10, 3→15, 4→20, 5→25, 6→30, 7→35, 8→40, 9→45, 10→50, 11→55.

A reliable routine for reading time

  1. Read the minute hand: count by 5s, then individual ticks.
  2. Identify the hour that has just passed; the hour hand may be between numbers.
  3. Write the hour before the colon and two minute digits after it, such as 7:05.
  4. Say the time in words: 7:05 is 5 minutes past 7.
  5. Check whether the hour-hand position is reasonable.

Examples: 12:15 = quarter past 12; 6:30 = half past 6; 12:45 = quarter to 1; 8:58 = 2 minutes to 9.

Real-life scenarios for students

  • School: read Maths, recess and assembly times.
  • Transport: match an analogue clock to a bus departure such as 8:20.
  • Sport: explain why the hour hand at 4:45 is close to 5.
  • Cooking: set a timer and predict the minute-hand position.
  • Appointments: interpret 9:05, 9:50 and “ten minutes to ten”.
  • Class transitions: compare the current clock with pack-up time.

Clock geometry: clocks are circles

Extension: add this only after accurate clock reading is secure.
  • A full clock face is 360°.
  • 12 equal hour sections give 30° between adjacent numbers.
  • 60 minute ticks give 6° per tick.
  • The hour hand moves 0.5° per minute; minute and second hands move 6° per minute/second.
15 minutes

90°, a quarter-turn.

30 minutes

180°, a half-turn.

3:00 and 9:00

90° smaller angle.

6:00

180° straight angle.

Numbers 2 to 5 have 3 spaces: 3 × 30° = 90°. The hands overlap 11 times in 12 hours, an enrichment fact.

Finding the angle between the hands

For advanced learners, let H be the hour and M the minutes. Treat 12 as H = 0.

d = |30H − 5.5M|
smaller angle = min(d, 360° − d)

The second step corrects a common error. At 9:00, d = 270°, but the smaller angle is 90°.

3:15: |90 − 82.5| = 7.5°.
8:20: |240 − 110| = 130°.
12:15: use H = 0, giving 82.5°.

Suggested teaching sequence

  1. Identify hands and count by 5s around a model clock.
  2. Read hour, half-hour and quarter-hour times.
  3. Move to five-minute intervals and exact minute marks.
  4. Connect analogue, digital and spoken forms.
  5. Teach explicitly that the hour hand moves continuously.
  6. Apply clock reading to timetables, buses, cooking, sport and appointments.
  7. For confident Year 4 students, add the geometry and then the formula.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

The original curriculum coverage remains intact.

  • Content description: describe the relationship between the hours and minutes on analog and digital clocks, and read the time to the nearest minute.
  • E1: represent and read time on an analog clock using markings and hand positions, to the nearest minute or five-minute interval.
  • E2: connect analog and digital time and use language such as quarter past, quarter to and minutes past.
  • E3: read analog clocks throughout the day and connect hour-hand position with minute-hand travel.

Common mistakes to watch for

  • Rushing to a rule before checking a clock model or drawing.
  • Reading the hour hand as the minute hand.
  • Thinking the minute hand on 7 means 7 minutes instead of 35.
  • Leaving the hour hand exactly on a number after minutes pass.
  • Writing 7:5 instead of 7:05.
  • Calling 3:45 quarter past 3 instead of quarter to 4.
  • Counting printed numbers rather than spaces for angles.
  • Forgetting the smaller-angle check after |30H − 5.5M|.
  • Using the formula before understanding the clock.
  • Getting a correct answer but being unable to explain why.
  • Mixing up before/after, past/to, longer/shorter or hours/minutes.

Student FAQ

Which hand shows the hour? The shorter hand. Why is it between numbers? It moves continuously.

Exact minutes? Count by 5s, then the remaining ticks. Why 7:05? Digital minutes use two digits.

Degrees between numbers? 30°. Per minute tick? 6°. Smaller angle? Compare d with 360° − d.
