AC9M3M04 • Year 3 Maths

Analog and Digital Time to the Minute

Analog and digital clocks represent the same time in different ways. Students connect the movement of the minute and hour hands and read time to the nearest minute.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

Learning intention: describe the relationship between the hours and minutes on analog and digital clocks and read time to the nearest minute.

  • Recognise that one full turn of the minute hand is 60 minutes and advances the hour hand by one hour.
  • Read the minute hand using individual minute marks, not only five-minute intervals.
  • Read the hour hand as moving gradually between hour numerals.
  • Write matching digital times with two minute digits, for example 8:05.

Concept boundary: the target is reading and representing time to the minute; complex timetable and elapsed-time calculations are separate learning goals.

Key vocabulary and representations

An analog clock uses hands on a dial. Digital time uses digits separated by a colon. The minute hand completes one revolution each hour while the hour hand moves gradually to the next hour.

Use clock faces, matching digital-time cards and annotated minute scales to show the same time in different forms.

Teaching progression and worked examples
  1. Review the 60 minute marks and the 5-minute positions around a clock face.
  2. Read exact minute marks between the familiar five-minute points.
  3. Track the hour hand between numerals as minutes pass.
  4. Match analog displays to digital notation and explain both hand positions.

Example 1: At 3:27 the minute hand is on the 27th minute mark and the hour hand is a little past 3, not at 4.

Example 2: Five minutes past eight is written 8:05. The zero is required because the minutes field has two digits.

Teacher language: “How many minutes past the hour? Which hour has already begun? Where should the hour hand be between the two numerals?”

Curriculum elaborations and how they are taught
  • Relate minute-hand movement to 60 equal minute intervals around the clock.
  • Observe how the hour hand moves continuously while the minute hand travels around the dial.
  • Match analog and digital representations for times that are not multiples of five minutes.
  • Use classroom schedules and real clocks to read and record times to the nearest minute.
Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Counting only by fives: use individual minute marks for exact times.
  • Reading the hour hand as the next hour: identify the hour that has already started.
  • Writing 7:4 instead of 7:04: always use two digits for minutes.
  • Swapping the hands: compare hand length and function before reading.
Support, Core and Extend
  • Support: use a labelled teaching clock with all minute marks and colour-coded hands.
  • Core: read and make analog times to the minute and record the matching digital form.
  • Extend: explain how the hour hand position changes across an hour and correct an inaccurate clock representation.
Quick check and mastery evidence
  1. Read a clock showing 3:27. Expected: 3:27.
  2. Write five minutes past eight digitally. Expected: 8:05.
  3. At 11:42, where should the hour hand be? Evidence: between 11 and 12, closer to 12 than at the start of the hour.

Mastery means coordinating both hands, reading exact minute marks and translating reliably between analog and digital representations.
