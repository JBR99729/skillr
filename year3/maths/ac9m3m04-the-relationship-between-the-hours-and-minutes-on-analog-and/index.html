Learn the skill

Small steps, clear examples

Learning goal: Read the minute marks and the moving hour hand together. Connect the same time on an analogue clock, in digital notation and in words.

Before you start: Count by fives; recognise hours, minutes and familiar half- and quarter-hour times.

Useful words: analogue: a clock with hands; digital: time written with digits; past: minutes after an hour; to: minutes before the next hour.

1. Connect hands, hours and minutes

The longer hand shows minutes. A full trip around the 60 minute marks is one hour. The shorter hour hand moves gradually as those minutes pass.

Worked example: at 3:30, the minute hand reaches 6, marking 30 minutes. The hour hand is halfway from 3 to 4 because half of the hour has passed.

The printed 6 names an hour position, but for the minute hand that position means 6 × 5 = 30 minutes.

Watch for this: Do not leave the hour hand exactly at 8 when half the hour has passed.

Try it: At 8:30, where should each hand be?

Check the answer and explanation

Minute hand at 6; hour hand halfway from 8 to 9.

2. Read five-minute benchmarks

Count minutes around the face: 1 means 5, 2 means 10, through 11 meaning 55. At 12 the next hour begins with 00 minutes.

Worked example: the minute hand at 9 shows 45 minutes. With the hour hand between 2 and 3, the time is 2:45. It is still in the hour that began at 2.

Use known quarter-hour positions as anchors: 3 gives 15 minutes, 6 gives 30, and 9 gives 45.

Watch for this: Read the hour most recently passed, not the next hour being approached.

Try it: Read the time with the minute hand at 7 and the hour hand between 5 and 6.

Check the answer and explanation

5:35. Seven groups of five minutes make 35.

3. Count individual minute marks

Count by fives to the previous numbered position, then count the extra one-minute intervals.

Worked example: at 4:23, the minute hand is three small marks after 4. Four groups of five make 20, then three more make 23. The hour hand is between 4 and 5.

At 9:58, start at the 11 position: 55 minutes, then count three more marks. The hour is still 9, though the short hand is close to 10.

Watch for this: Count spaces or minute steps from the benchmark, not the benchmark mark twice.

Try it: A minute hand is two small marks after 8. The hour hand lies between 6 and 7. What time is it?

Check the answer and explanation

6:42. Eight groups of five give 40 minutes; add two.

4. Connect analogue and digital notation

In hour:minute notation, the minutes use two digits. Both hands must agree with the written time.

Worked example: 7:05 means five minutes after 7. Put the minute hand at 1 and the hour hand just past 7. At 7:50 the hour hand is much closer to 8.

A 12-hour clock face alone does not show morning or evening; a routine or am/pm label provides that context.

Watch for this: The zero in 7:05 holds the tens-of-minutes place.

Try it: Explain the difference between 7:05 and 7:50.

Check the answer and explanation

They are five and fifty minutes past 7. The minute hand is at 1 and 10 respectively; the hour hand moves further towards 8 for 7:50.

5. Use past and to correctly

“Past” refers to the hour that began. “To” counts the remaining minutes until the next hour.

Worked examples: 12:15 is quarter past 12; 12:45 is quarter to 1. For 4:40, 60 − 40 = 20 minutes remain, so say 20 minutes to 5.

The same time can be called 40 minutes past 4. Keep the next-hour change clear when using “to”.

Watch for this: Quarter to 8 means 7:45, not 8:45.

Try it: Write 8:47 using “past” and “to”.

Check the answer and explanation

47 minutes past 8; 13 minutes to 9.

6. Draw, check and explain both hands

Place the minute hand accurately, then position the hour hand by how far through the hour it is.

Worked example: to draw 5:52, place the minute hand two small marks after 10. The hour hand is between 5 and 6, close to 6 but not at it.

Read a classroom clock to the nearest minute, write its digital time and explain both hands. To check a claimed drawing, inspect the short hand as well as the longer one.

Watch for this: A correct minute hand with an inconsistent hour hand is not a correct complete clock.

Try it: Draw 10:38. Explain why the hour hand is past halfway from 10 to 11.

Check the answer and explanation

The minute hand is three marks after 7. Since 38 is more than 30 minutes, the hour hand is beyond halfway from 10 to 11.

Common mistakes and quick fixes
  • Do not leave the hour hand exactly at 8 when half the hour has passed.
  • Read the hour most recently passed, not the next hour being approached.
  • Count spaces or minute steps from the benchmark, not the benchmark mark twice.
  • The zero in 7:05 holds the tens-of-minutes place.
  • Quarter to 8 means 7:45, not 8:45.
  • A correct minute hand with an inconsistent hour hand is not a correct complete clock.
Check understanding and choose the next step
  1. Write 25 minutes past 3 digitally.
  2. Write 6:48 using “to”.
  3. At 11:59, has the hour hand reached 12?
Answers and next step
  1. 3:25.
  2. 12 minutes to 7.
  3. No. It is very close to 12 but still in the hour that began at 11.

If an answer is uncertain, reopen the matching focus, use its model and explain a fresh example before the test.

Support: Use a geared clock so both hands move together. Start with five-minute benchmarks, then add individual minute marks.

Extend within this topic: Draw three times during the same hour, including one after :45. Explain the changing hour-hand position and name each time in two ways.

An adult can read the directions aloud. Let the learner show reasoning with objects, a drawing, pointing or a spoken explanation where appropriate. For an actual measuring, creating or data-collection task, the learner still performs and records the stated skill; provide suitable supervision.
