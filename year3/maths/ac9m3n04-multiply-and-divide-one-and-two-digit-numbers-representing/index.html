What students learn

Multiplication combines equal groups and division shares or groups equally. Arrays and partitioning make the structure of a problem visible. This matters because partition 14 into 10 and 4, then combine 60 and 24.

The same array represents related multiplication and division facts. Use this idea when a problem asks you to connect multiplication and division, and explain the result with array, factor, quotient.

Learning intention: We are learning to multiply and divide one- and two-digit numbers, representing problems using number sentences, diagrams and arrays, and using a variety of calculation strategies.

Success criteria

  • I can use arrays.
  • I can partition multiplication.
  • I can model division.
Key vocabulary
  • array — objects arranged in equal rows and columns
  • factor — a number multiplied by another number
  • quotient — the result of a division

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Unequal groups — Multiplication and equal sharing require equal groups.
  • Partitioning only one factor incorrectly — Keep one factor fixed and split the other into useful parts.
  • Multiplication and division disconnected — Use related facts to check answers.
Teaching sequence and quick check

Quick check: Solve 6 × 14 by partitioning.

Expected response: 6 × 14 = 84 because 6 × 10 + 6 × 4 = 60 + 24.

If incorrect: Multiplication and equal sharing require equal groups.
