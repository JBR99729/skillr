AC9M3N04 • Year 3 Maths

Multiplication and Division Strategies

Represent multiplication and division with equal groups, arrays and partitioning, then use known facts to solve one- and two-digit calculations.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

Students connect equal groups, arrays, repeated addition and sharing/grouping division. They partition larger factors into known facts and use multiplication/division relationships to check solutions.

Worked examples and teaching sequence
  1. Model the problem with groups or an array.
  2. Identify a known fact or useful partition.
  3. Combine partial products or quotients.
  4. Check using the inverse relationship.

Example: 6 × 14 = 6 × 10 + 6 × 4 = 84.

Common misconceptions and quick check
  • Using unequal groups.
  • Confusing sharing division with subtraction.
  • Partitioning a factor but omitting a partial product.

Quick check: Use an array or partitioning to solve 4 × 18, then write a related division fact.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Contexts include arrays, equal groups, number-line jumps, sharing and grouping problems, partitioning strategies and links between multiplication and division.
