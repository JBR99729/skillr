AC9M3N04 • Year 3 Maths

Multiplication and Division Strategies

Multiplication combines equal groups and division shares or groups equally. Students represent problems with equations, diagrams and arrays and use flexible calculation strategies.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

Learning intention: multiply and divide one- and two-digit numbers, represent problems using number sentences, diagrams and arrays, and use a variety of calculation strategies.

  • Interpret multiplication as equal groups or arrays and division as equal sharing or grouping.
  • Use known facts and partitioning to calculate with one- and two-digit numbers.
  • Connect multiplication and division as related operations using the same quantities.
  • Represent and explain the strategy with an equation, array, diagram or verbal reasoning.

Concept boundary: the emphasis is conceptual and strategic computation; formal long multiplication/division algorithms are not required.

Key vocabulary and representations

An array arranges objects in equal rows and columns. A factor is a number being multiplied. A quotient is the result of division.

Use arrays, equal-group drawings, bar models, number lines and fact-family triangles to keep multiplication and division connected.

Teaching progression and worked examples
  1. Build equal groups and arrays and record multiplication equations.
  2. Read the same arrays as sharing/grouping division situations.
  3. Partition a two-digit factor into friendly parts and combine partial products.
  4. Use related multiplication facts to solve/check division.

Example 1: 6 × 14 = 6 × 10 + 6 × 4 = 60 + 24 = 84.

Example 2: From 6 × 14 = 84, derive 84 ÷ 6 = 14 and 84 ÷ 14 = 6.

Teacher language: “What are the equal groups? Which factor could we partition? Which related fact can check the division?”

Curriculum elaborations and how they are taught
  • Represent multiplication and division stories with arrays, diagrams and number sentences.
  • Partition one factor into useful parts to extend known facts to two-digit calculations.
  • Compare sharing and grouping interpretations of division using concrete materials.
  • Use inverse relationships and fact families to check calculations and explain reasoning.
Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Unequal groups: rebuild the model so every group has the same quantity.
  • Partitioning both factors inconsistently: keep one factor fixed while splitting the other into useful parts.
  • Multiplication and division are disconnected: use the same array to state related facts.
  • Remainder ignored in a grouping context: interpret the context and account for all items, without overextending beyond the problem.
Support, Core and Extend
  • Support: use counters and small arrays linked directly to known facts.
  • Core: choose a representation and strategy for mixed multiplication/division problems and explain the connection.
  • Extend: solve a two-digit multiplication in two partitioning ways and compare efficiency, remaining within Year 3 expectations.
Quick check and mastery evidence
  1. Solve 6 × 14 by partitioning. Expected: 84.
  2. Use 6 × 14 = 84 to write a related division fact. Expected: 84 ÷ 6 = 14 or 84 ÷ 14 = 6.
  3. Draw an array for 4 × 7 and explain both multiplication and division interpretations. Evidence: equal rows/columns and related facts.

Mastery means representing equal-group structure, using efficient strategies and connecting multiplication with division.
