Concept modelTeach from the board

Key idea: Metric units must match the attribute and size of the item. Familiar benchmarks help make sensible estimates.

Learning routine: Identify attribute → Choose unit → Use benchmark → Estimate → Check

metric unit
a standard unit such as metre, kilogram or litre
attribute
the feature being measured, such as length, mass or capacity
estimate
a sensible measurement made before measuring exactly
Elaboration examplesCopied content

The content description and elaborations below show the curriculum ideas taught in this unit. Items marked as teaching context support lesson planning.

  • Content description: identify which metric units are used to measure everyday items; use measurements of familiar items and known units to make estimates
  • E1: examining the packaging on supermarket items to determine the metric unit used to describe the mass or capacity of the contents
  • E2: identifying items that have a mass of one kilogram or 500 grams, or a capacity of one litre or 500 millilitres and using these benchmarks to estimate the mass or capacity of other things, explaining their reasoning
  • E3: estimating the height of a tree by comparing it to the height of their friend and quoting the result as “the tree is about 3 times as tall”; estimating the capacity of a fish tank by using a litre milk carton as a benchmark
  • E4: choosing and using metres to estimate the dimensions of the classroom

Assessment and reviewReady for practice
  • Unit does not match attribute — Length, mass and capacity use different units.
  • Large unit for tiny object — Choose a unit that gives a useful number.
  • Estimate without benchmark — Refer to a familiar measured item.

  1. Explain why kilometres are not a useful unit for pencil length.

  2. A student estimates a chair has a mass of 5 m. Explain both errors.

Review hint: Follow the routine “Identify attribute → Choose unit → Use benchmark → Estimate → Check” and use the worked model to check whether your answer is reasonable.
