AC9M3M01 • Year 3 Maths

Choosing Metric Units and Estimating

Metric units must match the attribute and size of an item. Familiar measured benchmarks help students make estimates that are sensible rather than guesses.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

Learning intention: identify which metric units are used to measure everyday items and use measurements of familiar items and known units to make estimates.

  • Match length with millimetres, centimetres, metres or kilometres as appropriate.
  • Match mass with grams or kilograms and capacity with millilitres or litres.
  • Use a familiar measured object as a benchmark before estimating.
  • Explain why a chosen unit and estimate are reasonable for the attribute and scale.

Concept boundary: focus on choosing units and estimating from benchmarks; formal unit-conversion algorithms are not the target of this code.

Key vocabulary and representations

Metric unit is a standard unit such as centimetre, metre, gram, kilogram, millilitre or litre. An attribute is the feature being measured, such as length, mass or capacity. An estimate is a sensible approximate measurement supported by evidence.

Useful benchmarks include a pencil at about 15 cm, a doorway at about 2 m, a small drink bottle around 500 mL and a bag of flour around 1 kg. Benchmarks should be checked with real measuring instruments before students rely on them.

Teaching progression and worked examples
  1. Name the attribute: decide whether the question is about length, mass or capacity.
  2. Choose the unit: select a unit that gives a useful-sized number for the object.
  3. Recall a benchmark: compare with a familiar item whose measurement is known.
  4. Estimate, then measure: record the estimate with a unit and check it with an instrument.
  5. Reflect: explain whether the estimate was too high, too low or close and why.

Example: A pencil is better estimated in centimetres than metres. If a familiar pencil is about 15 cm long, a slightly longer pencil might reasonably be estimated at about 18 cm before measuring.

Teacher language: “What attribute are we measuring? Which unit gives a useful number? What measured benchmark can help you?”

Curriculum elaborations and how they are taught

The topic explicitly teaches the content description by connecting everyday objects to appropriate metric units and by using known measured items as reference points for estimation.

  • Sort everyday measurement situations by attribute and justify an appropriate metric unit.
  • Build a classroom benchmark collection by measuring familiar objects and recording their approximate measurements.
  • Estimate unknown measurements by comparing with one or more known benchmarks, then measure to evaluate the estimate.
  • Discuss why the same attribute may need a smaller or larger unit depending on the object or distance.
Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Unit does not match the attribute: first name whether the quantity is length, mass or capacity.
  • A large unit is chosen for a tiny object: choose a unit that gives a useful-sized number.
  • An estimate is a random guess: require students to name the benchmark used.
  • A unit is omitted: every estimate and measurement must include its unit.
Support, Core and Extend
  • Support: provide real objects, labelled measuring tools and a small benchmark card.
  • Core: independently choose a unit, estimate from a benchmark, measure and explain the difference.
  • Extend: compare two plausible units for the same attribute and explain which is more useful, without introducing formal conversion procedures as the main goal.
Quick check and mastery evidence
  1. Choose a suitable unit for a pencil length. Expected: centimetres.
  2. Choose a suitable unit for the mass of a school bag. Expected: kilograms.
  3. A door is about 2 m high. Use this to estimate a wall height and explain your reasoning. Evidence: a plausible estimate explicitly linked to the 2 m benchmark.

Mastery means selecting units by attribute and scale, making benchmark-based estimates and communicating each result with an appropriate unit.
