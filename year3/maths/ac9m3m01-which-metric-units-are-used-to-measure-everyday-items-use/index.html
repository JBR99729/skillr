AC9M3M01 • Year 3 Maths

Choosing Metric Units

Choose sensible metric units for everyday length, mass and capacity, then use measurements to compare objects accurately.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

Students decide whether centimetres or metres, grams or kilograms, and millilitres or litres are appropriate for an item. They estimate first, measure carefully and compare results using the same attribute and unit.

  • Match units to length, mass and capacity.
  • Explain why a unit is suitable.
  • Estimate before measuring.
  • Compare measurements with correct units.
Worked examples and teaching sequence
  1. Identify the attribute being measured.
  2. Choose a sensible unit for the size of the item.
  3. Estimate a likely measurement.
  4. Measure and record the number with its unit.
  5. Compare objects only after checking the units match.

Example: A pencil is better described in centimetres than metres; a classroom length is usually more useful in metres.

Common misconceptions and quick check
  • Choosing a unit because its number looks convenient rather than because it suits the attribute.
  • Leaving the unit off an answer.
  • Comparing numbers measured in different units without converting or remeasuring.

Quick check: Choose an appropriate unit for the mass of a school bag and explain why.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

This unit develops practical measurement judgement. Teaching contexts include selecting units for familiar objects, estimating before measuring, explaining unit choices, and comparing measured quantities in meaningful classroom and everyday situations.
