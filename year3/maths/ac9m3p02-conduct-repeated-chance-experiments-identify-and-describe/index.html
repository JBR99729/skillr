Repeated Chance Experiments

Repeated chance experiments produce results that can vary. More trials give more evidence about patterns, but exact counts are not guaranteed. This matters because the frequencies show what happened in this set of trials.

What students learn

Repeated chance experiments produce results that can vary. More trials give more evidence about patterns, but exact counts are not guaranteed. This matters because the frequencies show what happened in this set of trials.

A fair chance process can still produce different results in separate runs. Use this idea when a problem asks you to compare two trial sets, and explain the result with chance experiment, trial, variation.

Learning intention: We are learning to conduct repeated chance experiments; identify and describe possible outcomes, record the results, recognise and discuss the variation.

Success criteria

  • I can identify outcomes.
  • I can conduct repeated trials.
  • I can record frequencies.
Key vocabulary
  • chance experiment — a repeatable action with an uncertain result
  • trial — one performance of a chance experiment
  • variation — differences in results between repeated sets of trials

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Expecting perfect equality — Equal chances do not guarantee equal results in a small trial.
  • Changing the device — Keep the experiment conditions consistent.
  • Recording from memory — Tally each trial immediately.
Teaching sequence and quick check

Quick check: List coin outcomes.

Expected response: The possible coin-toss outcomes are heads and tails.

If incorrect: Equal chances do not guarantee equal results in a small trial.
