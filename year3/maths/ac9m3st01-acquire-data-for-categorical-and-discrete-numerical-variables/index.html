AC9M3ST01 • Year 3 Maths

Collecting and Recording Data

A useful data investigation begins with a clear question, then collects and records only the data needed to address that purpose.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

Learning intention: acquire data for categorical and discrete numerical variables to address a question of interest or purpose by observing, collecting and accessing data sets; record the data using appropriate methods including frequency tables and spreadsheets.

Students distinguish categories from countable numerical values, gather relevant observations and record them accurately.

Concept boundary: focus on simple categorical and discrete numerical data; continuous measurement distributions are not the target.

Key vocabulary and representations

Categorical variable — sorts responses into groups. Discrete numerical variable — takes countable number values. Frequency — how often a category or value occurs. Use tally tables, frequency tables and simple spreadsheets.

Teaching progression and worked examples
  1. Pose a clear question.
  2. Decide what data answers it.
  3. Collect or access the data consistently.
  4. Record tallies and frequencies accurately.

Example: “How many books did each student read this month?” produces discrete numerical data; “Which genre do you prefer?” produces categorical data.

Curriculum elaborations and how they are taught

Students observe, survey or access small data sets, choose an appropriate recording method, check that totals match the number of observations and use spreadsheets where they support accurate organisation.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Collecting before defining the question: write the question first.
  • Mixing categories and counts: identify the variable type explicitly.
  • Losing tallies: transfer each tally group carefully and check the total.
Support, Core and Extend
  • Support: use a prepared question and recording table.
  • Core: choose and record an appropriate small data set.
  • Extend: compare two possible collection methods and justify the more useful one.
Quick check and mastery evidence

Give a question and ask what data should be collected and how it should be recorded. Mastery requires a relevant variable, accurate recording structure and a correct frequency total.
