Efficient Mental Addition and Subtraction

Known facts such as doubles, complements to 10 and fact families can be extended to tens and hundreds for efficient mental calculation. This matters because the digits keep the same relationship while place value changes.

What students learn

Known facts such as doubles, complements to 10 and fact families can be extended to tens and hundreds for efficient mental calculation. This matters because the digits keep the same relationship while place value changes.

Adjust to a friendly number, then compensate. Use this idea when a problem asks you to use compensation and bridging, and explain the result with mental strategy, compensation, friendly number.

Learning intention: We are learning to extend and apply knowledge of addition and subtraction facts to 20 to develop efficient mental strategies for computation with larger numbers without a calculator.

Success criteria

  • I can extend facts to tens.
  • I can use bridging.
  • I can use compensation.
Key vocabulary
  • mental strategy — a way to calculate without writing every step
  • compensation — adjust numbers to calculate, then correct the adjustment
  • friendly number — a nearby number that is easier to use

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Counting by ones — Use a known fact or place-value relationship.
  • Compensation not reversed — If you add extra, subtract it later.
  • Strategy chosen by habit — Choose the method that fits the numbers.
Teaching sequence and quick check

Quick check: Use 7 + 8 to solve 70 + 80.

Expected response: 70 + 80 = 150.

If incorrect: Use a known fact or place-value relationship.
