Elaboration examplesCopied content

The content description and elaborations below show the curriculum ideas taught in this unit. Items marked as teaching context support lesson planning.

  • Content description: estimate the quantity of objects in collections and make estimates when solving problems to determine the reasonableness of calculations
  • E1: estimating how much space a grid paper representation of a large number such as 20 200 will take up on the wall and how much paper will be required
  • E2: estimating the number of people in a large gathering; for example, school assembly, using known numbers, such as how many students per class
  • E3: choosing which place value they would estimate to for different situations; for example, they would estimate to the nearest 10 when estimating how many dots on a ladybird or they would estimate to the nearest 1000 when estimating crowd sizes at a venue
  • E4: checking the reasonableness of an addition calculation by using two- and three-digit numbers to the nearest 10 or hundred to estimate; for example, using 200 + 400 = 600 to estimate and check the solution to the calculation 219 + 385

What is the big idea in this topic?
An estimate is a sensible approximate value based on evidence. Benchmarks, equal groups and rounded numbers help judge whether an exact answer is reasonable.
What routine should I use?
Choose benchmark → Group → Estimate → Calculate if needed → Check reasonableness
How do I know I am ready to move on?
You can use benchmarks, group collections, round to friendly numbers, judge reasonableness, explain estimates and explain how your model or calculation supports the answer.
Assessment and reviewReady for practice
  • Random guessing — Use a benchmark or grouping strategy.
  • Estimate treated as exact — Use words such as about or approximately.
  • Ignoring the estimate after calculating — Compare the exact result with the estimate.

  1. Estimate 725 − 289, then explain how the estimate helps check the exact answer.

  2. A student estimates 398 + 205 as 400 + 20. Explain what was overlooked.

Review hint: Follow the routine “Choose benchmark → Group → Estimate → Calculate if needed → Check reasonableness” and use the worked model to check whether your answer is reasonable.
