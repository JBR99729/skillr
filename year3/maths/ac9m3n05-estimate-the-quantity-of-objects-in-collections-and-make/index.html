Estimating Collections

Estimate quantities by using benchmarks, equal-sized groups and sampling, then check how close the estimate is to the actual count.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

Students estimate rather than guess randomly. They use known groups, visual benchmarks and sample regions to make a reasoned estimate before counting to evaluate accuracy.

Worked examples and teaching sequence
  1. Look for structure in the collection.
  2. Identify or create a benchmark group such as 10.
  3. Estimate how many benchmark groups fit.
  4. Adjust for leftovers and record an approximate quantity.
  5. Count to check and discuss the difference.
Common misconceptions and quick check
  • Calling an unsupported guess an estimate.
  • Using groups of noticeably different sizes.
  • Expecting an estimate to equal the exact count.

Quick check: Explain a strategy for estimating a jar containing about 150 counters without counting one by one first.

Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Contexts include classroom collections, grouped displays and environmental quantities, with students describing the benchmark or grouping strategy that supports an estimate.
