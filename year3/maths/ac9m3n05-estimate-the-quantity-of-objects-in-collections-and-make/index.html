AC9M3N05 • Year 3 Maths

Estimating Quantities and Answers

An estimate is a sensible approximate value supported by evidence. Benchmarks, grouping and friendly numbers help students estimate collections and judge whether exact calculations are reasonable.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

Learning intention: estimate the quantity of objects in collections and make estimates when solving problems to determine the reasonableness of calculations.

  • Use known benchmark groups to estimate larger collections.
  • Round or adjust numbers to friendly values before calculating.
  • Use words such as about and approximately to distinguish estimates from exact answers.
  • Compare an exact result with an estimate to decide whether the calculation is reasonable.

Concept boundary: estimation is used for sensible approximation and checking; formal rounding rules to many place values are not the central target.

Key vocabulary and representations

An estimate is a sensible approximate value. A benchmark is a known amount used for comparison. A result is reasonable when it makes sense for the quantities and situation.

Useful representations include grouped collections, ten-frames, arrays, number lines and calculation chains showing exact numbers adjusted to friendly numbers.

Teaching progression and worked examples
  1. Estimate small collections by recognising familiar groups rather than counting every object.
  2. Scale a known benchmark group across a larger collection and adjust for leftovers.
  3. Estimate calculations by replacing numbers with nearby friendly values.
  4. Calculate exactly and compare the result with the estimate.

Example 1: A collection arranged as three groups of about 10 and one group of about 8 contains about 40 objects.

Example 2: 398 + 205 is about 400 + 200 = 600. The exact answer 603 is close to 600, so it is reasonable.

Teacher language: “What benchmark can help? Is your estimate meant to be exact? Does the final answer sit near the estimate?”

Curriculum elaborations and how they are taught
  • Estimate collections by using equal or approximately equal groups as benchmarks.
  • Compare estimates made with different grouping strategies and discuss which are more efficient or reliable.
  • Estimate sums and differences using nearby friendly numbers before calculating exactly.
  • Use the estimate after calculation to identify implausible answers and reconsider the strategy if needed.
Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Random guessing: require a benchmark, group or friendly-number reason.
  • Treating an estimate as exact: use approximation language explicitly.
  • Counting every object when estimation is requested: identify a useful sample/group first.
  • Ignoring the estimate after calculating: make comparison with the exact answer a final required step.
Support, Core and Extend
  • Support: use clearly grouped collections and friendly two-digit numbers.
  • Core: estimate a collection or calculation, explain the strategy, then compare with the exact result.
  • Extend: compare two different estimates and justify which benchmark or friendly-number choice is more useful without turning the task into formal error analysis.
Quick check and mastery evidence
  1. Estimate 398 + 205. Expected: about 600.
  2. Why is 603 a reasonable exact answer for 398 + 205? Expected: it is close to the estimate of 600.
  3. Estimate a displayed collection using groups rather than counting one by one. Evidence: a plausible estimate linked to a named benchmark/grouping strategy.

Mastery means producing evidence-based estimates and using them actively to judge the reasonableness of exact calculations.
