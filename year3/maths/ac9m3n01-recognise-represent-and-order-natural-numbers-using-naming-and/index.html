AC9M3N01 • Year 3 Maths

Numbers Beyond 10 000

Recognise, represent and order natural numbers beyond 10 000 by reading place value from the greatest place first and using zeros as place holders.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesWorksheetPracticeTest
What students learn in AC9M3N01

Students read, write, represent, rename, compare and order natural numbers beyond 10 000. They connect each digit to its place value and explain why a zero can be essential even when it contributes no quantity at that place.

  • Read and write numerals and number names beyond 10 000.
  • Represent numbers with place-value charts and expanded notation.
  • Rename a number without changing its value.
  • Compare numbers from the highest place value first.
  • Order sets of large natural numbers and justify the first place where they differ.
Key vocabulary and models

Place value is the value a digit has because of its position. A numeral is a written representation of a number. A natural number is a whole number used for counting.

For 42 507: 4 represents 40 000, 2 represents 2 000, 5 represents 500, 0 holds the tens place and 7 represents 7 ones.

Worked examples and teaching sequence
  1. Read: group digits into thousands and ones periods: 42 507 is forty-two thousand five hundred and seven.
  2. Represent: write 42 507 = 40 000 + 2 000 + 500 + 7.
  3. Rename: 15 704 can be described as 15 thousands, 7 hundreds and 4 ones, or 157 hundreds and 4 ones.
  4. Compare: for 42 507 and 42 750, the ten-thousands and thousands match; compare hundreds next, so 42 750 is greater.
  5. Order: repeat the same highest-place comparison to place several numbers in ascending or descending order.
Curriculum coverage and elaborations

Content description: recognise, represent and order natural numbers using naming and writing conventions for numerals beyond 10 000.

  • E1: read and write large numerals and connect spoken number names with written numerals, including numbers containing zero digits.
  • E2: use place-value materials, charts and expanded notation to represent and rename numbers in equivalent ways.
  • E3: compare and order large natural numbers by locating the first place value where the numerals differ.
  • E4: apply large-number understanding in meaningful contexts such as populations, distances, collections and data displays.

Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Dropping a zero: a zero can hold an empty place, as in 42 507.
  • Comparing from the ones: begin at the highest place value.
  • Reading each digit separately: read the number in periods and then connect the words to place value.
  • Renaming changes the number: verify each renaming by recombining the place-value parts.
