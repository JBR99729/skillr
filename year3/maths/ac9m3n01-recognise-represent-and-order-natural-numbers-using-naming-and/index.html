AC9M3N01 • Year 3 Maths

Numbers Beyond 10 000

Large natural numbers are read, written, represented and ordered through place value. Zero can hold an empty place without changing the positions of the other digits.

Topic GuideTeacher SlidesPractice SheetPracticeTest
What students learn

Learning intention: recognise, represent and order natural numbers using naming and writing conventions for numerals beyond 10 000.

  • Read and write numbers with ten-thousands, thousands, hundreds, tens and ones.
  • Use place-value representations to explain what each digit is worth.
  • Recognise zero as a placeholder in numbers such as 42 507.
  • Compare and order numbers from the highest place value first.

Concept boundary: the focus is natural-number naming, representation and ordering; negative numbers and formal decimal place value are not part of this target.

Key vocabulary and representations

Place value is the value a digit has because of its position. A numeral is the written digit representation of a number. Natural numbers are whole numbers used for counting.

Use place-value charts, expanded notation, number names, number lines and comparison symbols to connect representations.

Teaching progression and worked examples
  1. Build large numbers on a place-value chart and read each place from left to right.
  2. Connect numeral, number name and expanded place-value form.
  3. Include numbers with zero placeholders in different places.
  4. Compare numbers from the highest place first and order sets on a number line.

Example 1: 42 507 is forty-two thousand five hundred and seven. The zero shows there are no tens.

Example 2: Compare 42 507, 42 750 and 40 725. The first two have 42 thousand, so compare hundreds: 42 750 is greatest.

Teacher language: “What is the highest place? What value does this digit represent? Which place decides the comparison first?”

Curriculum elaborations and how they are taught
  • Read and write large numerals and number names, including numbers containing zero placeholders.
  • Represent the same natural number using place-value materials, charts, expanded forms and number lines.
  • Order sets of large numbers by comparing from the highest place value.
  • Use real data or quantities to give meaning to numbers beyond 10 000 while preserving the Year 3 place-value focus.
Common misconceptions and quick fixes
  • Dropping a zero: use a place-value chart to show the empty place.
  • Comparing from the ones: begin with the highest place.
  • Reading digits separately: group the number into meaningful place-value periods.
  • Believing more digits always means a particular order within equal-length numerals: compare the place values systematically.
Support, Core and Extend
  • Support: use place-value cards and charts with one place added at a time.
  • Core: read, write, represent, compare and order numbers beyond 10 000 including zero placeholders.
  • Extend: create numbers satisfying constraints such as “between 42 500 and 42 800 with 0 tens” and justify the result.
Quick check and mastery evidence
  1. Read 63 409 aloud. Expected: sixty-three thousand four hundred and nine.
  2. What value does the 5 have in 42 507? Expected: 500.
  3. Order 42 507, 42 750 and 40 725. Expected: 40 725, 42 507, 42 750.

Mastery means moving confidently among number names, numerals and place-value models and ordering numbers using place-value reasoning.
