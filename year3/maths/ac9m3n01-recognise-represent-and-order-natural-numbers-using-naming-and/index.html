Numbers beyond 10 000

Read, represent and order numbers beyond 10 000 using place value.

Learn the skill

1. Read and write large numbers

Group the digits from the right in threes: ones, thousands and then millions. Read the groups from left to right. Within each group, the places repeat: hundreds, tens and ones. In 42 507, 42 means forty-two thousands, and 507 means five hundred and seven.

Try it · Write “thirty-six thousand and nine” as a numeral. Explain the zeros.

36 009. The hundreds and tens places contain zero; the 9 stays in the ones place.

2. Connect digits, places and values

Move one place to the left and a unit is worth ten times as much. A digit and its value are different: in 63 409, the digit 4 has value 400.

Try it · What is the value of the 7 in 47 305? Write the expanded form.

7 000. The expanded form is 40 000 + 7 000 + 300 + 5.

3. Rename without changing the total

Exchange one larger unit for ten of the next smaller unit. The appearance changes; the total stays the same.

Try it · Rename 24 350 as hundreds, tens and ones. Give another valid partition.

243 hundreds + 5 tens + 0 ones; also 24 thousands + 3 hundreds + 5 tens. Both total 24 350.

Common mistakes

Ignoring a zero in a number. Use a place-value chart so each digit stays in the correct column.

Learn the skill

Small steps, clear examples

Learning goal: A digit gets its value from its place. Read, build, rename and order large whole numbers, keeping every zero in its correct place.

Before you start: Count in tens, hundreds and thousands; read four-digit numbers.

Useful words: digit: one symbol from 0 to 9; place value: what a digit is worth in a number; rename: show the same total using different-sized units.

1. Read and write large numbers

Group the digits from the right in threes: ones, thousands and then millions. Read the groups from left to right. Within each group, the places repeat: hundreds, tens and ones. In 42 507, 42 means forty-two thousands, and 507 means five hundred and seven.

Worked example data
Ten-thousandsThousandsHundredsTensOnes
42507

Worked example: “Fifty-two thousand and eighty” is 52 080. Write 52 in the thousands group and 080 in the final group. The zeros hold the hundreds and ones places.

For 425 006, read four hundred and twenty-five thousand and six. For 1 234 567, the groups mean one million, two hundred and thirty-four thousand, five hundred and sixty-seven. Spaces separate the three-digit groups.

Numbers can also describe long time scales. For the curriculum example “more than 60 000 years”, read sixty thousand and retain “more than”: the number is a lower reference, not an exact count.

Watch for this: Do not squeeze out internal zeros or read 52 080 as 52 800.

Try it: Write “thirty-six thousand and nine” as a numeral. Explain the zeros.

Check the answer and explanation

36 009. The hundreds and tens places contain zero; the 9 stays in the ones place.

2. Connect digits, places and values

Build the number with place-value counters. Point to each counter group while saying its value, then write the numeral.

Watch for this: A zero marks an empty place; it does not make the other digits move.

Try it: What is the value of the 7 in 47 305? Write the expanded form.

3. Rename without changing the total

Exchange one larger unit for ten of the next smaller unit. The appearance changes; the total stays the same.

15 thousands7 hundreds4 ones

Worked example: 15 704 is 15 thousands + 7 hundreds + 4 ones. Exchange the thousands for hundreds: 15 thousands = 150 hundreds, so the number is also 157 hundreds + 4 ones.

Check by rebuilding: 157 × 100 + 4 = 15 704. A second correct partition is 15 000 + 700 + 4.

Watch for this: Write the size of every unit. “157 hundreds” is different from “157”.

Try it: Rename 24 350 as hundreds, tens and ones. Give another valid partition.

Check the answer and explanation

243 hundreds + 5 tens + 0 ones; also 24 thousands + 3 hundreds + 5 tens. Both total 24 350.

4. Predict changes across zero places

When ten ones collect, exchange them for one ten. The same grouping rule continues through hundreds and thousands.

Worked example: one more than 19 999 is 20 000. The extra one completes each smaller group in turn. Ten more than 19 990 also makes 20 000, while one more makes 19 991. One hundred more than 19 950 is 20 050: ten hundreds regroup as a thousand.

To find one less than 30 000, exchange through the empty places: the answer is 29 999.

Watch for this: “One more” and “one ten more” give different answers.

Try it: Find one more than 10 099, ten more than 10 099 and one less than 10 000.

Check the answer and explanation

10 100; 10 109; 9 999. State which unit you added or removed each time.

5. Compare and order numbers

Compare the highest place first. If it matches, move one place right until the digits differ.

Worked example: compare 48 090 and 48 900. Both have 48 thousands. At the hundreds place, 0 is less than 9, so 48 090 < 48 900.

Order 39 999, 40 009, 40 090 and 39 909: 39 909, 39 999, 40 009, 40 090. Explain the first different place in each comparison.

Watch for this: The greatest ones digit does not necessarily make the greatest number.

Try it: Order 52 080, 52 008 and 52 800 from greatest to smallest.

Check the answer and explanation

52 800, 52 080, 52 008. Compare hundreds, then tens.

6. Place numbers on lines and compare numeral systems

A number line uses equal spaces for equal numerical changes. Work out the step before naming an unlabelled mark.

Worked example: 40 000 to 41 000 is 1 000. Four equal spaces make steps of 250: 40 000, 40 250, 40 500, 40 750, 41 000.

Another numeral system can group differently. In Japanese writing, 一 names one, 十 ten, 百 hundred, 千 thousand and 万 ten thousand. Compare the grouping unit 万 with the space used to group three digits in 10 000; the amount is the same.

Watch for this: Count spaces between marks, not the marks themselves.

Try it: A line runs from 60 000 to 62 000 in four equal spaces. Name every mark.

Check the answer and explanation

60 000, 60 500, 61 000, 61 500, 62 000. Each space is 500.

Common mistakes and quick fixes
  • Do not squeeze out internal zeros or read 52 080 as 52 800.
  • A zero marks an empty place; it does not make the other digits move.
  • Write the size of every unit. “157 hundreds” is different from “157”.
  • “One more” and “one ten more” give different answers.
  • The greatest ones digit does not necessarily make the greatest number.
  • Count spaces between marks, not the marks themselves.
Check understanding and choose the next step
  1. Write “forty thousand and six”.
  2. Rename 18 230 using hundreds and tens.
  3. Which is greater: 70 090 or 70 900? Explain.
Answers and next step
  1. 40 006.
  2. 182 hundreds and 3 tens: 18 200 + 30.
  3. 70 900; its hundreds digit is greater.
