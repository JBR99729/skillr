Learning goals: what you will learn

Large numbers are organised in groups of three digits. Each digit has a place value, and the same number can be renamed without changing its total value.

Learning routine: Read the periods → Build place value → Rename → Compare → Order → Explain

Success looks like

  • Read numbers beyond 10 000
  • Write number names
  • Use place value
  • Rename numbers
  • Compare and order

Success criteria:

  • Read numbers beyond 10 000
  • Write number names
  • Use place value
  • Rename numbers
  • Compare and order
Prerequisite knowledge

Students should be comfortable reading and representing whole numbers, using place value, and explaining simple addition, subtraction or equal-group relationships from earlier years. Begin with a short concrete or visual recall task before moving to Numbers Beyond 10 000.

Concept teaching

Key idea: Large numbers are organised in groups of three digits. Each digit has a place value, and the same number can be renamed without changing its total value.

Learning routine: Read the periods → Build place value → Rename → Compare → Order → Explain

place value
the value a digit has because of its position
numeral
a written symbol or group of digits that names a number
natural number
a whole number used for counting
Worked and modelled examples

Read and represent 42 507

ten-thousands4
thousands2
hundreds5
tens0
ones7

42 507 is forty-two thousand five hundred and seven. The zero keeps the tens place.

  1. Read every label and identify what each quantity or feature represents.
  2. Describe the relationship shown by the model.
  3. Check that the conclusion answers the original question.

Compare numbers from the highest place first

42 50742 75040 725

42 750 is greatest

Compare ten-thousands first, then thousands, hundreds, tens and ones.

Apply the same relationship to the new context, then justify the answer using the labels and evidence shown.

Common misconceptions and corrections
  • Dropping a zero — A zero can hold an empty place, as in 42 507.
  • Comparing from the ones — Start at the highest place value.
  • Reading every digit separately — Read the number in periods: thousands, then ones.

Independent practice

  1. Which numeral means forty-two thousand five hundred and seven?

    • 42 507
    • 4 257
    • 425 070
    • 42 057

  2. Complete the place-value statement.

    Prompt: 63 409 has □ ten-thousands, □ thousands, □ hundreds, □ tens and □ ones.

  3. Which number is greatest?

    • 48 900
    • 48 090
    • 48 009
    • 40 899

  4. Write 52 080 in words and explain the role of the zero digits.

  5. Match each numeral to its number name.

    Match: 23 006 • 32 060 • 230 006 ↔ two hundred and thirty thousand and six • twenty-three thousand and six • thirty-two thousand and sixty

  6. Complete the expanded form.

    Prompt: 47 305 = 40 000 + 7 000 + □ + 5

Reasoning and problem-solving

  1. Create three different renamings of 24 350 using thousands, hundreds, tens and ones. Explain why the value stays the same.

  2. Write clues for a five-digit mystery number with 7 in the thousands place and 0 in the tens place. Make the clues identify exactly one number.

Explain the reasoning, not only the final answer.

Assessment-style questions and review hints

  1. Order 39 999, 40 009, 40 090 and 39 909 from smallest to largest.

  2. A student says 50 206 is the same as 5 026 because both use 5, 2 and 6. Explain the error.

Review hint: Follow the routine “Read the periods → Build place value → Rename → Compare → Order → Explain” and use the worked model to check whether your answer is reasonable.

Teacher and parent guidance

For teachers

Model the idea with concrete or visual representations first, then ask students to explain the relationship in words before moving to symbolic work.

For parents and carers

Ask your child to show the idea with a drawing, objects or a simple example and explain how they checked the answer.

Read the topic guide and use the teacher slide for instruction. Students can then use the worksheet for written work, open Practice for supported feedback, or take the Test when they are ready.

Curriculum alignment

The content description and elaborations below show the curriculum ideas taught in this unit. Items marked as teaching context support lesson planning.

  • Content description: recognise, represent and order natural numbers using naming and writing conventions for numerals beyond 10 000
  • E1: moving materials from one place to another on a place value model to show renaming of numbers; for example, 1574 can be shown as one thousand, 5 hundreds, 7 tens and 4 ones, or as 15 hundreds, 7 tens and 4 ones
  • E2: using the repeating pattern of place value names and spaces within sets of 3 digits to name and write larger numbers: ones, tens, hundreds, ones of thousands, tens of thousands, hundreds of thousands, ones of millions, tens of millions; writing, for example, four hundred and twenty-five thousand as 425 000
  • E3: predicting and naming the number that is one more than 99, 109, 199, 1009, 1099, 1999, 10 009 ... 99 999 and discussing what will change when one, one ten and one hundred is added to each
  • E4: comparing the Hindu-Arabic numeral system to other numeral systems; for example, investigating the Japanese numeral system, 一、十、百、千、万
  • E5: comparing, reading and writing the numbers involved in the more than 60 000 years of First Peoples of Australia’s presence on the Australian continent through time scales relating to pre-colonisation and post-colonisation
